What Admetia is

An independent, unofficial tool built for personal use. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or connected to any university or business school, testing body, employer or government.

It gives general information. It is not admissions, careers, tax, legal or immigration advice, and it cannot predict a decision about your own case. Where a page touches immigration, it describes the published routes for a passport group; in the UK, advice on an individual’s case may only be given by regulated advisers.

Every page that states a figure, a rule or a rating also says where it came from and when it was read. Where nothing reliable could be found, the page says so instead of guessing.

How the calculators score

There are three models behind seven calculators.

  • MBA. A points-based admissions model across 43 business schools: 35 on a shared scale and 8 modelled individually. It offers two readings. As published evaluates score benchmarks directly; Corrected scores test thresholds as continuous ranges, which affects Columbia, Stanford, NYU and Yale.
  • Business master’s. A purpose-built model for pre-experience master’s in Management, Finance and Marketing. It enforces each programme’s hard eligibility rules first (degree requirements, minimum quantitative credits, language level, work-experience caps), then weighs your profile under that school’s own emphasis. Where a school publishes its weights or a points table (St. Gallen, Mannheim, TUM) the model uses them as written.
  • Computing master’s. A rule-first model for 26 programmes in the UK, Europe and the US across Computer Science, Data Science and AI, and conversion master’s. It models the conversion rules that disqualify applicants who already hold a computing degree, first-class and prerequisite-module bars, and five years of pooled applicant reports under sample-size thresholds for the UK and European programmes. The US programmes are modelled from their published rules only.

What the results mean, in the calculators’ own words, is on the master’s page under How the scoring works, and every results page carries a key to its verdicts. The MBA key gives the model author’s own stated odds, not measured outcomes; the master’s and computing keys give rules and a ranking, never a percentage.

Cross-scale test conversions (GMAT 10th edition, GMAT Focus, GRE) and international grade conversions are percentile-based approximations.

Deadlines. Each programme’s dates were read on the school’s own page on 23 September 2026 for the 2026–27 cycle, and each entry shows when it was last checked. Once the oldest check is more than 120 days old, every results page opens with a warning that the dates are getting old.

Programme Directory

The Programme Directory lists the 136 programmes the three models score: 67 business master’s, 26 computing master’s and 43 MBAs. Every requirement is what the school publishes and keeps its source tag. Three things are Admetia’s own and are labelled as such on the page:

  • “Our bar” is the calculator’s own threshold, shown with its rank in the track. No school publishes a points requirement, and it is not an admission rate.
  • Fees are copied from the research in the school’s own currency for the intake shown, and are never converted. Programmes with no fee in the research say “Not in our data”.
  • Deadlines move. Confirm on the official admissions page before you plan around one.

Career Explorer

The Career Explorer is built from 13 branch reports and an Italian pay add-on, researched on 9 October 2026. The research text is printed as written: nothing is summarised, and long pages get contents lists and collapsible sections instead.

  • The rating scales (hours, stress, people, quantitative, entry difficulty), the background-fit matrix, the researchers’ assumptions, the full list of gaps and every report’s sources are on the Explorer’s research sources page.
  • Each field and role page opens with the gaps that touch it, under “Verify before relying”. Each field page ends with the report’s own research notes: its scope, what was thin or could not be opened, and its sources.
  • Pay is in local currency and never converted. Tier lists reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact.
  • The Career Compass ranks roles using the Explorer’s own ratings and one editorial line of tags per role. It quotes the research for its explanations and makes no request beyond the page’s own files.

Hiring, calendar and toolkit

How hiring works (each country’s hiring page and the comparison) describes the way most graduates actually get their first job in each country rather than every possible path. Each line cites the file’s own sources or is marked “Our reading”; a part with nothing reliable is left out. Graduate outcomes come from Eurostat for the countries it covers, from the ILO’s modelled estimates through the World Bank elsewhere, and from a few national figures read by hand. The three groups are labelled and never ranked against each other.

The recruiting calendar shows 15 windows on one season’s axis. Months, flags and eligibility are Admetia’s reading of the research; each row quotes the passages it rests on, and portal links were opened on 10 October 2026. Its “Sources and limits” section explains which figures in the research were not used and why.

The application toolkit templates are Admetia’s own layouts. The school and country rules beside them are quoted from the research. The section budgets for motivation letters are labelled as Admetia’s suggestion: schools publish limits and prompts, not shares.

Interview prep labels each exercise always, usually, often or sometimes, from the research’s own tables. The story builder’s checks are rules of thumb drawn from employers’ guidance, and no employer publishes a rubric.

The Atlas: countries, visas and life

The Atlas covers 46 countries: 25 in Europe (the UK, Switzerland, Norway and Iceland included) and 21 outside it, with Hong Kong and Taiwan as separate entries, and 200 city hubs. A country guide is split into views (cities and hubs, getting hired, visas and permits, working there, life there, first weeks) with the whole guide also available on one page.

  • Every statement is a claim tagged data, employer-stated, practitioner consensus or anecdotal, with its source and the date it was read. Numbered notes at the foot of each view list them.
  • Demand levels are judgements. Dominant, strong, present and marginal summarise named employers, official statistics and specialist reports. None is a count of vacancies, and a level with no source behind it shows as “Not rated”.
  • Key figures (GDP per head, economy size, unemployment, growth, population) come from one source for all 46 countries, the IMF World Economic Outlook.
  • Visas and permits are shown for a passport group: EU / EEA / Swiss, UK, US or another passport. A route is in scope only if Europe is one end, so a US passport looking at Singapore is told it is outside Admetia’s scope. The section says when it was checked and when it is due again, and turns into a warning once that date passes. An unknown situation is marked as not settled, not guessed.
  • Each country’s research notes (the briefs it draws on, what is not verified yet, its verification log and its full numbered source list) are in that country’s “Sources and research” view.
  • Map data. Country shapes are from Natural Earth (public domain), drawn as Italy recognises boundaries; no position on any boundary is implied. They are simplified for the screen, so coastlines and borders are approximate.

Research library

The research behind the site is published with it as plain Markdown files, which open as text in the browser. Start from the library index, which lists every file with a one-line summary, suggested reading orders, the evidence tags and the dates.

  • decisions/ What to study, when, and whether at all.
  • careers/ One file per career family (the older, Europe-first library).
  • branches/ The 13 branch reports behind the Career Explorer, and their index.
  • getting-in/ Admissions, applications, recruiting and interview cases.
  • money/ Costs, pay and scholarships.
  • places/ Countries, cities, visas and logistics across countries.
  • countries/ One brief per Atlas country: hubs, employers, pay, standing.
  • visas_immigration/ Each country’s full visa research, in Italian, with its sources and open questions.
  • evidence/ How to read numbers, base rates, hypotheses and trends.
  • verification/ Verification logs and the claims and freshness registers.
  • glossary.md Terms and abbreviations.

The library is working material: it is longer, rougher and more candid about its own limits than the pages built from it, and parts of it are older than the pages that cite it.

English and Italian

EN · IT in the top strip switches the whole site, and the choice is remembered in this browser. English is the default. Translating never changes a score: only the words are swapped.

The Career Explorer’s research text, the application templates and the study cards stay in English, so that figures, sources and caveats do not change; their menus and labels are translated. Each country’s full visa research in the library is in Italian.

Privacy and saved answers

Admetia runs in your browser. There are no accounts, no cookies and no lead capture.

  • Calculator answers are saved only in this browser’s local storage, and only so a refresh does not lose them.
  • The Atlas remembers one thing, the passport group you picked.
  • The Career Compass, the calendar filters and your interview stories are saved the same way. A Compass share link puts your answers in the link itself, and only when you choose to copy one.
  • Filters in the Programme Directory and a hiring plan live in the address after “#”, so a list can be bookmarked or shared. They never include a calculator answer.
  • “Clear everything” at the foot of the page removes the calculators’ answers and the Atlas passport. The Compass has its own “start over”.
  • Visits are counted anonymously: which page, never what you type. Browsers that send Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control are never counted, and anyone can untick “Count my visit” in the footer.
