1. What this branch is

Data science is the work of using statistics, programming and domain knowledge to answer questions and make predictions from data, so that someone can decide something: ship a feature, approve a loan, stock a warehouse, continue a clinical trial. The title "data scientist" now covers three quite different jobs: (1) analytics or product data science, where you run experiments and explain what changed and why; (2) machine-learning (ML) or modelling data science, where you build predictive models such as forecasts, recommendations, fraud scores; and (3) research or applied science, a mostly PhD-track job inventing new methods. Beside these sit older, more specialised professions that are data science in everything but name: operations research (optimisation), statistics and biostatistics (clinical trials, official statistics), and the modelling teams of banks and insurers. In October 2026 the market is two-speed: the US Bureau of Labor Statistics projects data scientist employment to grow 35% from 2025 to 2035 and a median pay of US$120,230 (May 2025), yet generalist "data scientist" postings and entry-level hiring have weakened as work shifts to ML/AI engineers and as LLM-based tools automate routine analysis. The first job is the hard part; pay for those who get in is high, especially in the US.

2.1 What "data scientist" means in 2026, and how the title has split

The title was popularised around 2012 as "the sexiest job" combining statistics, coding and business sense. By 2026 it has fragmented. Large companies now run separate ladders: for example Levels.fyi lists Meta data scientist levels IC3-IC8 with a different ladder for machine learning engineers, and a career-advice source describes the split as product/analytics data science, ML data science and analytics data science, noting the clean split only holds at companies with enough headcount to specialise (resumegeni and codingshuttle pieces, 2026; low-quality sources, treat as description not evidence). In practice:

  • Analytics / product data science: experiments, metrics, causal inference (methods for working out whether X caused Y rather than merely correlating with it). Closest to a product manager. Little model building.
  • ML / modelling data science: forecasting, recommendation, pricing, churn, fraud. Python, feature engineering (turning raw data into inputs a model can use), model evaluation. Increasingly overlaps with ML engineering.
  • Research / applied science: new methods, papers, often a PhD.

Effect of LLMs and AutoML (automated model building). The evidence is mixed and partly vendor-sourced:

  • BLS still projects 35% growth for data scientists, 2025-35, 24,800 openings a year, median pay US$120,230, and 275,600 jobs in 2025 (BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook, accessed 9 Oct 2026). BLS projections are produced from historical trends and may not capture a sudden AI shock.
  • Indeed Hiring Lab data, as relayed by Interview Query and Pin (secondary, late 2025): data scientist postings were up about 15% over three years while tech postings overall were more than 30% below pre-pandemic levels as of late October 2025; roughly 45% of Indeed data and analytics postings contained AI-related terms by end-2025. I could not open the original Indeed report.
  • A vendor dataset (Interview Query, 2026, weak) reports interview activity for data scientist roles falling from 1,763 sessions a month in September 2025 to 772 in June 2026 (-56%) while ML Engineer and AI Engineer demand rose. A Refonte Learning post claims generalist data scientist listings fell about 25% year on year on LinkedIn with AI engineer postings up over 70% (unverified, could not confirm against LinkedIn).
  • Stanford's "Canaries in the Coal Mine" paper (Brynjolfsson, Chandar and Chen, August 2025), using ADP payroll data, found employment of 22-25 year-olds in the most AI-exposed occupations fell about 13% relative to less exposed ones, while older workers in the same jobs were stable. Software developers and customer service were the headline examples; data science was not isolated in what I could verify. Authors call it consistent with, not proof of, AI effects.
  • Meta, July 2026: Alex Schultz, previously CMO and head of Analytics (which includes data science), became Meta's first chief data officer, and he cited an internal Analytics Agent as Meta's most widely used AI agent (The Star / Spokesman-Review, 1-2 July 2026). It signals that routine analytics is being automated at the largest employers.

Net reading: tools like AutoML and LLM coding assistants have made writing a basic model or query cheap, so entry hiring has narrowed toward people who can frame a business problem, design experiments and judge model output. Employers' demand is shifting to ML/AI engineering. Demand for statistically rigorous people (causal inference, experimentation, regulated modelling) looks sturdier than demand for "can fit a random forest".

2.2 Product and experimentation data science

  • Definition: Using A/B tests (randomised experiments comparing a new version of a product with the current one), metric design and causal inference to guide product decisions.
  • In practice: you define success metrics for a feature, size a two-week test on checkout, write the SQL for conversion lift, check for pitfalls (novelty effects, network effects, multiple comparisons), and present a go/no-go to the product team. Also: diagnosing metric drops, forecasting growth, building north-star metrics.
  • Employers: tech and consumer platforms (Meta, Google, Netflix, Airbnb, Uber, DoorDash, Stripe, Pinterest, Snap among the top payers on Levels.fyi, 2026; in Europe Booking.com, Spotify, Zalando, Delivery Hero, Wolt, Revolut, Monzo; in Asia Grab, Rakuten, PayPay), plus e-commerce, fintech, gaming and media.

2.3 Machine-learning / modelling data science

  • Definition: Building predictive or optimisation models that feed a product or an operational decision: demand forecasting, recommendation, dynamic pricing, customer churn, fraud detection, predictive maintenance.
  • In practice: framing the target variable, assembling features from messy tables, training and validating models, handing them to engineers for deployment (MLOps), monitoring drift. Increasingly includes LLM-based features (classification, extraction, search).
  • Employers: big tech and scale-ups; retail and e-commerce; telecoms; energy and utilities (Enel is the highest-paying Italian employer on Levels.fyi's Italian data scientist page, 2026, small sample); manufacturing; media.

2.4 Decision science and operations research

  • Definition: Operations research (OR) uses mathematical optimisation (finding the best allocation under constraints), simulation and queueing theory to improve operations; "decision science" is the business-facing label for the same family plus behavioural and econometric methods.
  • In practice: route and schedule optimisation, inventory and network design, workforce rostering, airline pricing and revenue management (how many seats to sell at which price), warehouse and delivery simulation, energy dispatch. Tools: solvers (Gurobi, CPLEX, OR-Tools), Python, simulation packages, sometimes AMPL/Julia.
  • Employers: logistics and e-commerce operators, airlines, retailers, utilities and grid operators, manufacturers, defence and government analysts, and software vendors who sell planning engines. BLS says operations research analysts work mostly in finance and insurance (26%), professional/technical services (22%), management of companies (9%), federal government (6%) and manufacturing (4%) (BLS, May 2025). I did not verify specific employers' OR teams in this round (e.g. Amazon's supply-chain optimisation group); examples named in role section 3.3 are general industry knowledge.

2.5 Statistics and biostatistics

  • Definition: Designing studies and analysing data with rigorous inference. Biostatistics applies this to health: clinical trials, epidemiology, public health. Official statistics produces national numbers (inflation, employment, census).
  • In practice: writing statistical analysis plans for a trial, sample-size calculation, randomisation, analysing endpoints, interacting with regulators; or producing survey estimates and quality-controlling national statistics. Tools: R and SAS dominate pharma, with CDISC data standards (SDTM/ADaM) as the industry format.
  • Employers: pharma and biotech (e.g. GSK has a 2026 statistics/biostatistics graduate programme in Siena, Italy, requiring an MSc with a biostatistics focus, R assessed, and fluent English and Italian), contract research organisations (CROs, firms that run trials for drugmakers: IQVIA, Parexel and others), regulators, hospitals and universities, and national statistics offices: ISTAT (Italy), ONS (UK), Eurostat (EU). ISTAT opened a 195-post permanent recruitment in 2026 (54 researchers, 46 technologists, 95 technical collaborators); researcher/technologist applications closed 22 January 2026 (ISTAT/press, accessed Oct 2026). BLS: statisticians are most often employed by the federal government (16%), R&D in life/physical/engineering sciences (15%) and healthcare (8%).

2.6 Data science in financial services and insurance

  • Definition: Credit scoring (predicting the chance a borrower defaults), fraud detection, customer analytics, insurance pricing and claims, anti-money-laundering models, plus model validation.
  • In practice: models are regulated and must be explainable and documented; the work includes model risk governance, bias testing and sign-off by independent validators. Credit-risk modelling (probability of default, IFRS 9 provisioning) is a mature specialism. In insurance, predictive pricing models sit next to the actuarial function: Solvency UK/II requires the actuarial function to opine on underwriting policy, so pricing is where actuaries and data scientists collide (Novarica 2016 warned of friction; vendor Akur8 argues for hybrid "actuarial data scientists"; low-quality sources).
  • Employers: retail and commercial banks, card issuers (Capital One), fintechs (Monzo, Revolut), insurers (Admiral, Aviva, Allianz, Generali, AXA). BLS: insurance carriers employ 10% and credit intermediation 6% of US data scientists.
  • Versus actuaries and quants: actuaries are exam-qualified professionals responsible for reserves and capital; quants price and trade in markets (see Finance, Quant branch). Bank data scientists mostly model customers and risk, not markets.

2.7 Research and applied science

  • Definition: "Applied scientist" (Amazon's title; similar titles elsewhere: research scientist, scientist) builds new or heavily customised models and often publishes. Economist and "economic data scientist" roles in tech use econometrics (statistics for economic data, esp. causal) to study pricing, marketplaces and advertising. Cross-reference Economics.
  • In practice: reading papers, prototyping models, running offline and online evaluations, writing internal or external papers, sometimes shipping production code.
  • Employers: Amazon, Google, Meta, Microsoft, Apple, large marketplaces and platforms, research labs; AI labs sit in the AI branch. Amazon's US applied scientist pay is US$247k (L4) to US$413k (L6) in total compensation (Levels.fyi, 9 Oct 2026, 107 submissions).

2.8 Data science consulting

  • Definition: Building analytics and AI solutions for client companies, as a practice inside a consultancy (McKinsey QuantumBlack, BCG X), a Big Four or IT-services analytics team (Deloitte, Accenture, Capgemini), or a specialist boutique (Reply and Bip in Italy).
  • In practice: 3-6 month client projects, mixing modelling with stakeholder management; heavy emphasis on PowerPoint and getting the client to adopt the model.
  • Employers: strategy consultancies, Big Four, IT integrators, boutiques. Clients are banks, energy companies, retailers, public bodies.

Commentary:

  • Regional pay gap. US big tech pays 2-3 times European equivalents in total compensation (Meta IC3 US$153k, Google L3 US$196k vs. Germany all-levels median EUR 77.6k and Italy EUR 46k all-levels, small sample). Within Europe, London and Zurich lead (London median GBP 91k, Zurich CHF 116k), Germany follows with Berlin scale-ups paying more than the national median (Berlin EUR 113k), and Italy trails: junior Italian data scientist pay is about EUR 29-42k gross (aggregators), with Milan higher than the rest of Italy. The gap partly reflects Levels.fyi sample bias to big tech, which makes Europe look better than it is for the typical local employer.
  • Asia/Middle East hubs. Singapore (median SGD 120k, n=128) is the strongest local market; Grab pays about SGD 160k; Tokyo's median is JPY 8.76m (n=16) but a few firms (PayPay, Sakana AI) pay JPY 18-21m; Hong Kong (HKD 803k, n=12) and Dubai (AED 437.6k, n=20) have very thin data and wide uncertainty. Banks and government-linked agencies (GovTech SGD 117k) are major employers in Singapore. Dubai and Gulf roles are growing around government AI programmes (not verified here).
  • Hours. Where hours differ the most is consulting and startups (45-60) vs. public sector and pharma (36-45). Banks sit in the middle but with regulation-driven spikes. Evidence base: consensus and anecdotes; no systematic survey.
  • Italy specifically. Fewer product-led tech employers; most data science jobs are in banks (Intesa, UniCredit), insurers (Generali), energy (Enel, Eni), consultancies (Accenture, Deloitte, Reply, Bip) and public bodies (ISTAT). Salaries are quoted in RAL (gross annual); 13th/14th-month payments should be checked in an offer. Many strong Italian graduates move to London, Zurich or Amsterdam after 2-3 years.
  • Autonomy and AI: Employers with large experimentation cultures give more freedom; regulated environments give less. LLM tools reduce routine coding everywhere but entry-level roles are most exposed.

A reality check on pay, hours and tiers

Every figure here is dated and approximate, copied from the research with its source and year. Pay is in local currency and never converted. Tier lists reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact. Three limits apply to almost every page:

  • Asia and Gulf pay across almost all branches rests on aggregators, small self-reported samples (Levels.fyi has 12-40 submissions in some cities) or recruiter blogs. Tokyo is the thinnest.
  • Hours and stress are researchers' estimates from practitioner accounts, prep sites and a few surveys (mainly M&A). No report found a systematic hours survey by role family.
  • Gated recruiter guides (Hays, Robert Walters, Michael Page tables outside Italy, Management Consulted, CIPD, PMI) could not be read. Many pay figures therefore come via secondary summaries, as the reports note.

The full list of gaps, the rating scales and the researchers’ assumptions are on the sources page.
