1. What this branch is

Artificial intelligence careers are about building, studying and governing systems that learn from data, from classic machine-learning models to the large language models (LLMs) behind chat assistants and agents. Most jobs are engineering jobs: training and deploying models, building products on top of foundation models, and running the GPU infrastructure that serves them. A smaller set are research, safety and evaluation roles at frontier labs, universities and AI safety institutes, and a growing set are customer-facing, product and governance roles that turn AI into deployed, compliant products. Pay at the frontier is extreme for a small elite, while the entry-level market is tough and crowded, and much AI data-labelling work is low-paid and precarious; the reports below separate these realities rather than averaging them.

Scope notes: researched in two parts (building AI systems; research, frontier and governance roles). General software engineering, data science and data analytics have their own reports. Part 2 drew most of its pay-band evidence from published US job postings, many of them Anthropic's because that employer publishes ranges; treat those figures as one employer's data points, not as the market, and see the gaps list in ../index.md.

Part 1: Building AI systems

This part of the AI branch covers the people who build, run and ship AI systems rather than those who do frontier research, safety, policy or product strategy (those are in part 2). In practice it means five kinds of work: training and deploying machine-learning models (machine learning engineering), building products on top of large language models (LLM application or "AI engineering"), running the platforms and GPU clusters that models train and run on (MLOps and AI infrastructure), making AI see and act in the physical world (computer vision, robotics, edge AI), and producing the human-generated data that models learn from (annotation, RLHF, AI training work). The first four are engineering jobs with a strong programming core; the fifth is a very different, mostly contract-based labour market. The field has a split personality in 2026: a small elite at frontier labs earns exceptional pay, while the entry-level market for ordinary graduates is tougher than the headlines suggest.

P1-2.1 The employer landscape in 2026

Frontier labs. These are the companies training the most capable general models: OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind (inside Alphabet), Meta's AI groups, xAI, and in Europe Mistral AI (Paris). The Stanford AI Index 2026 reports that industry produced over 90% of notable frontier models in 2025, and the State of AI Report 2026 describes the frontier as a three-way race between Anthropic, OpenAI and Google. The State of AI Report also estimates that OpenAI's and Anthropic's combined annualised revenue reached about US$105 billion by late summer 2026, up from about US$30 billion at the start of the year (a report estimate, not audited figures). Labs hire engineers in large numbers, not only researchers: inference, training infrastructure, data pipelines, evaluation tooling and applied/customer-facing engineering are all engineering jobs. Mistral reportedly has more than 1,000 employees in 2026 and a valuation of about EUR 21 billion after a September 2026 Samsung stake (Wikipedia, secondary).

Big tech AI groups. Google, Meta, Microsoft, Amazon, Apple and NVIDIA employ the largest absolute number of ML engineers, mostly on products (search ranking, ads, recommendations, assistants, cloud AI services) rather than on frontier models. The State of AI Report 2026 says the four US hyperscalers guide to roughly US$733 billion of combined 2026 capital spending, mostly data centres and chips, which is what funds the infrastructure jobs.

AI infrastructure and chip companies. NVIDIA is the dominant GPU maker; others include AMD, Google (TPUs), Amazon (Trainium/Inferentia, built by Annapurna Labs), Cerebras, plus "neoclouds" such as CoreWeave that rent GPUs. The State of AI report notes CoreWeave had 1.5 GW of active power at the end of Q2 2026. These employers hire engineers who write low-level GPU code, build compilers, network clusters and operate fleets.

AI-native startups and application companies. Companies built around foundation models: model providers beyond the big labs (Cohere, for example), coding tools, search, legal, health and customer-service agents, and data/MLOps vendors (Databricks, Hugging Face and similar). The State of AI report finds that among companies with US$1-20 million in annualised revenue, AI-native firms grew 256% year over year at the 75th percentile versus 90% for firms adding AI to existing software, though it warns this does not prove AI adoption causes the growth. Startups are where most "AI engineer" jobs sit, with high variance in pay and survival.

Enterprises adopting AI. Banks, insurers, pharma, retailers, manufacturers and telecoms run internal AI teams for fraud detection, credit risk, demand forecasting, document processing, drug discovery and copilots. The Stanford AI Index 2026 reports 88% organisational AI adoption (a survey-based measure). Euronews (24 July 2026, as summarised in the project's earlier tech file, not read in full) reports that 59% of AI-labelled job titles in Germany and 58% in the Netherlands sit outside tech occupations, which fits the picture of AI work spreading into ordinary firms.

Consultancies and IT services. Accenture, Capgemini, Deloitte, McKinsey QuantumBlack, BCG X, Infosys, TCS, Reply (strong in Italy), Almawave and others implement AI for clients. Mistral announced a partnership with Accenture in February 2026 (Wikipedia, secondary). Work is client-driven: you adapt existing models to a client's data, systems and politics.

State-linked and sovereign AI. In the Gulf, Abu Dhabi's G42 runs Stargate UAE (announced 22 May 2025 with OpenAI, Oracle, NVIDIA, SoftBank and Cisco; operations expected in 2026); in July 2026 the US reclassified the UAE to a group easing advanced-chip export rules (Wikipedia on G42, secondary). G42 also announced European sovereign-AI partnerships, including Italy, in May 2026, and reportedly pledged up to US$40 billion in Italy in June 2025 (a pledge, not delivered investment). Saudi Arabia has a state AI company, Humain; I could not retrieve a reliable source on it, so details are unverified.

P1-2.2 The hiring market and pay dynamics (dated data)

  • Two markets. Levels.fyi (9 Oct 2026) shows US total compensation for an entry-level software engineer of about US$367K at Anthropic, US$249K at OpenAI (L2), US$227K at Scale AI (L3), US$205K for NVIDIA machine-learning engineers (IC1), US$208K at Google (L3, ML engineer) and US$187K at Meta (E3, ML engineer). Across all US ML engineers the median is US$280K (25th percentile US$200K, 75th US$386K, 90th US$495K). At the same time the median for the title "AI Engineer" is US$152K (25th US$110K, 75th US$211K, 90th US$280K). The gap between those two numbers is the real story: "AI engineer" at an ordinary company is a well-paid but normal software salary, while ML roles at the top labs pay several times more.
  • Private-equity caveat. OpenAI's and Anthropic's pay includes shares in private companies. Levels.fyi lists an OpenAI all-level median of about US$880K and an Anthropic median of about US$883K, but these depend on the (illiquid, valuation-dependent) value of equity and on a small, self-selected sample. Treat them as paper values, not guaranteed income.
  • Entry-level squeeze. SignalFire's State of Tech Talent 2026 (22 June 2026) reports entry-level hiring down about 65% at "Tech Majors" and about 76% at early-stage startups versus 2019 (SignalFire is a venture firm using proprietary data). The same report says the share of AI/ML engineers within engineering hiring is up 39% since ChatGPT's 2022 launch, research engineers up 28%, forward-deployed engineers up 30%. Indeed Hiring Lab (8 July 2026) found US software-development postings about 27.5% below February 2020 but up nearly 15% since late February 2025, with 71% of the May 2025 to May 2026 increase coming from senior roles. The Stanford Digital Economy Lab paper "Canaries in the Coal Mine?" (revised 12 Aug 2026) reports that employment of 22-25-year-olds in AI-exposed occupations is 19% below where it would be if it had tracked less-exposed peers (ADP payroll data, US). Net: demand for experienced people who can ship AI is strong; demand for people with no experience is weak, and "AI engineer" is no exception.
  • US pull and talent flows. The AI Index 2026 says the number of AI researchers and developers moving to the US has fallen 89% since 2017, including an 80% drop in the last year, and that US private AI investment reached US$285.9 billion in 2025, more than 23 times China's recorded US$12.4 billion (the report notes China's figure understates government-guided funds). This helps explain why Europe, the UK and the Gulf are courting talent.
  • Europe is cooler. Bitkom (3 Sep 2026) counts 79,000 unfilled IT positions in Germany in 2026, down from 149,000 in 2023; the earlier project file notes press coverage that entry-level conditions have tightened. UK ML-engineer advertised salaries are noisy (see 3.1).
  • Elite wars. Press reports of individual mega-offers (tens or hundreds of millions of dollars to a few researchers) are a signal about a tiny elite, not about engineers in general; I did not verify specific offers.
  • Working hours. Wired (23 July 2025), the New York Times (28 September 2025) and NPR (26 October 2025), as cited on Wikipedia's "996 working hour system" page, report US AI startups adopting 9am-9pm, six-day schedules. That is journalistic reporting about some startups and not a measurement of the industry.

P1-2.3 Sub-areas

Machine learning engineering. Taking models from experiment to production: preparing data, training and fine-tuning models, evaluating them, packaging and deploying them, monitoring drift. Employers: every big tech company, labs, quant funds (Jane Street, IMC and similar hire ML researchers and engineers), banks, insurers, pharma, logistics, retail.

Applied AI / LLM application engineering ("AI engineer"). Building products on foundation models via APIs: retrieval-augmented generation (RAG: letting a model look up your documents before answering), tool-using agents (models that call software functions and take multi-step actions), prompt design, fine-tuning (further training a model on your own examples), and evaluation (automated tests of output quality). Employers: AI-native startups, SaaS companies, enterprises, consultancies, and the labs' own "applied AI" teams (Anthropic lists Applied AI Engineer roles in London, Paris, Tokyo, Bengaluru and elsewhere).

MLOps, ML platform and AI infrastructure. MLOps is DevOps for ML: pipelines, experiment tracking, model registries, monitoring. ML platform teams build internal tools so other teams can train and deploy. AI infrastructure covers GPU clusters, distributed training, and inference (running trained models to answer requests) optimisation: batching, caching, quantisation (using lower-precision numbers to run faster), GPU kernels (small programs that run on the GPU, written in CUDA or Triton). Employers: labs, hyperscalers, NVIDIA, neoclouds, any company with a serious ML platform.

Computer vision, robotics and edge/embedded AI. Models that process images, video, lidar and sensor data, and increasingly robot-control models; "edge" means running on small devices (cars, cameras, drones, factory machines, medical scanners) with tight limits on power and latency. Employers: automotive and autonomous driving (Waymo, Tesla, Mobileye, Wayve in London), industrial automation, defence and drones (Anduril, Skydio and European equivalents), medical imaging (Siemens Healthineers, GE HealthCare, Philips and startups), robotics startups and chipmakers. The company names here are well-known examples; I did not verify their current hiring.

AI data work. People who label images, rank model responses, write ideal answers, grade reasoning or build test sets. Two layers: low-paid micro-task work through platforms, and higher-paid expert contracting (doctors, lawyers, coders, mathematicians). Employers: Scale AI (with Outlier and Remotasks), Surge AI (DataAnnotation), Mercor, Turing, Invisible, Appen, TELUS and the labs' own data-operations teams. See 3.5 for pay and precarity.

Part 2: Research, frontier and governance roles

This part of the AI branch covers the jobs that decide what AI systems can do and how they are allowed to be used. These are the people who invent and train the models (research scientists and research engineers), the people who try to make them safe and measure their dangers (alignment, interpretability, evaluations and red teaming), the people who put them inside customers' businesses (forward-deployed engineers, solutions architects, AI consultants), the people who write the rules and manage the legal and ethical risk (governance, policy, responsible-AI and compliance staff), and the product managers who decide what gets built. Part 1 of the branch covers the more conventional engineering jobs (ML engineers, LLM application builders, MLOps, computer vision and robotics, data and annotation work).

Two things shape everything below. First, the top end of this field is small, very well paid and extremely selective: a few dozen organizations in a handful of cities (San Francisco Bay Area, London, Zurich, Paris, New York, Seattle) employ most frontier researchers, and the Stanford AI Index 2026 says industry produced more than 90% of notable frontier models in 2025. Second, the "AI governance" side is the opposite shape: it is a young, fast-growing, mostly non-technical-or-semi-technical profession driven by regulation (the EU AI Act, Italy's national AI law), with many more entry points and much lower pay.

AI research (the "science" side). Definition: producing new methods or knowledge about how models learn and behave, for example a new training recipe for reasoning or a theory of why a method works. At a frontier lab (a company building the most capable general-purpose models, such as Anthropic, Google DeepMind, Meta's Superintelligence Labs and FAIR, Mistral, OpenAI, xAI) you run experiments on models from small to huge, track results and write internal documents; at a university you also teach, write grants and supervise students. Employers: frontier labs; big-tech research groups (Microsoft Research, NVIDIA, Apple, Amazon); AI companies (Cohere, Databricks, Scale AI, Sakana AI in Tokyo); universities and institutes. In Europe the main academic network is ELLIS (a pan-European network of AI research units); in Italy the main public programme is FAIR (Future Artificial Intelligence Research), a nonprofit foundation funded through the national recovery plan (PNRR) with one hub, ten "spokes" and seven transversal projects (FAIR website, read October 2026), plus IIT, FBK, and universities such as Politecnico di Milano, Sapienza and Bocconi. Industry is where the compute is: the AI Index 2026 reports that new AI PhDs in the US and Canada rose 22% from 2022 to 2024 but the whole increase went to academic jobs, not industry.

Research engineering. Definition: building and running the software that lets researchers train and test models: distributed training code across thousands of GPUs, reinforcement-learning (RL) environments, data pipelines, experiment tracking, evaluation harnesses. In practice the line between "research engineer" and "research scientist" at frontier labs is blurry: several lab postings (OpenAI "Research Engineer / Research Scientist" combined titles, Anthropic RL research engineer roles, xAI and Cohere "Member of Technical Staff" titles) describe one shared job of designing experiments, RL tasks and reward signals and diagnosing why models improve, not just plumbing. Employers: the same labs, plus NVIDIA, Meta, Microsoft, Databricks, Scale AI and well-funded startups.

AI safety, alignment, interpretability and evaluations. Alignment research tries to make models do what their developers and users intend and avoid catastrophic behaviour. Interpretability tries to reverse-engineer what happens inside a model. Evaluations ("evals") measure what a model can do, including dangerous capabilities (cyber attacks, biological misuse); red teaming means adversarially attacking a model or product to find failures. Employers fall in four groups: labs' safety teams (Google DeepMind, OpenAI and Anthropic all post dedicated safety research roles in October 2026, as do Scale AI and xAI); government institutes (the UK AI Security Institute, AISI, formed November 2023 and renamed in February 2025, with more than 100 technical staff and a stated budget of about GBP 66 million per year; the US Center for AI Standards and Innovation, CAISI, at NIST, which publishes evaluations of foreign AI models, the latest dated 17 September 2026; the EU AI Office's AI Safety unit); nonprofits (METR, Apollo Research, Redwood Research); and academic groups. Scale note: 80,000 Hours estimated in 2022 only about 300 people (90% interval 76 to 536 full-time equivalents) worked on technical approaches to catastrophic AI risk; the field has grown but is still small and entry "can be very competitive" (page updated 17 September 2026).

Forward-deployed engineering, AI solutions engineering and AI consulting. Definition: customer-facing technical work that gets AI to function inside a specific organization. A forward-deployed engineer (FDE) is a software engineer who embeds with a customer, builds production systems on the vendor's platform and feeds lessons back to the product; Palantir pioneered the role (internally "Delta"), and the Pragmatic Engineer (12 August 2025) describes solutions architects as more advisory, FDEs as more hands-on and long-term, and consultants as one-off recommenders. Employers with open FDE or deployment-strategist roles in the October 2026 job feeds: Palantir, Databricks, OpenAI, Anthropic, Cohere, Scale AI and Sakana AI, across the US, London, Paris, Munich, Zurich, Madrid, Tokyo, Seoul, Singapore, Sydney and the Gulf (Anthropic also lists "Applied AI Architect" roles in Milan); plus cloud providers and consultancies (McKinsey/QuantumBlack, BCG X, Accenture, Deloitte and similar; I could not open a primary page for these, so role details are general knowledge, unverified).

AI governance, policy, responsible AI and compliance. Definition: writing, interpreting and implementing the rules and internal controls around AI, from drafting national policy to running a model-risk review at a bank, building an inventory of AI systems for EU AI Act compliance, or writing a lab's product-use policy. Employers: regulators and governments (the European Commission's AI Office, with more than 125 staff across six units on its page; national authorities; UK DSIT/AISI; US agencies); think tanks (GovAI and others); corporate responsible-AI and compliance teams (big tech, banks such as Citi); Big Four, consultancies and law firms (Andersen in Italy announced an AI governance and compliance department in June 2026, per Milano Finanza); standards bodies (CEN-CENELEC JTC 21, ISO/IEC SC 42; general knowledge). The IAPP-Credo AI AI Governance Profession Report (16 April 2025; 671 respondents) found governance sits in many places: privacy and legal/compliance each lead it in 22% of organizations, IT 17%, data governance 10%; about half of governance staff sit in ethics, compliance, privacy or legal teams.

The EU AI Act timeline as of October 2026, after the "AI Omnibus" simplification (political agreement 7 May 2026, in force 27 July 2026, per the European Commission's AI Act page): prohibited practices and AI literacy duties since 2 February 2025; general-purpose AI model duties since 2 August 2025; most of the Act applies from 2 August 2026; a new prohibition on nudification apps and child sexual abuse material from 2 December 2026; high-risk systems in areas such as biometrics, employment, education and migration delayed to 2 December 2027; high-risk AI embedded in regulated products to 2 August 2028. The AI Office enforces rules on general-purpose models; national authorities share enforcement from 2 August 2026. Italy also passed its own AI law (Law 132/2025, in force 10 October 2025); I could not read which authorities it designates (widely reported as AgID and ACN, unverified). artificialintelligenceact.eu (updated 31 August 2026) lists the same later dates.

AI product management. Definition: deciding what AI products to build and how to ship them when the technology is probabilistic. What is distinctive versus general PM (covered in another branch): outputs vary, so quality is defined through evals; researchers' timelines are uncertain; capabilities change every few months so roadmaps are short; cost per query is a first-order constraint; and safety, abuse and trust issues are part of the product. October 2026 postings show the spread: OpenAI lists PMs for API agents, core models, integrity, youth and multimodal safety; Scale AI "Senior AI Product Manager" roles including cybersecurity and finance agents; Databricks PMs for its AI platform plus an associate-PM new-graduate programme; Cohere PMs for agents and integrations; Anthropic "Product Manager, Claude" and specialised PMs. Employers: labs, big tech, AI startups, enterprise software.

Short commentary.

  • Labs vs everything else. The labs pay multiples of other employers and give access to scarce compute, but they hire few people, and many roles are senior (Anthropic's applied roles ask for 4-6+ years). Anthropic, for example, posts a London "Applied AI Engineer, Startups" role at GBP 225-255K base but requires 4+ years of experience. Equity is private and illiquid.
  • Big tech remains the main route for sizeable entry-level cohorts and structured levels, but the Stanford and SignalFire data above show fewer new-graduate seats than in 2019.
  • Startups vary from excellent learning to financial and hours risk; the reported 996-style cultures (Wired, NYT, NPR, 2025) are a signal about a subset.
  • Enterprise AI teams are slower and pay less, but offer reasonable hours, domain depth and, in Europe, often the largest number of ML roles outside tech. Compliance (EU AI Act, model risk management in banks) adds paperwork but also creates specialised careers.
  • Consulting/IT services give breadth and quick exposure to many sectors at the cost of long hours and less depth; useful as a first job in Italy, where Reply, Accenture, Capgemini and similar firms are major employers of graduates (general knowledge; not verified here).
  • Industrial/automotive offers stable careers with real-world impact, but often lower pay and slower tooling than software firms.

Regional differences

  • US vs Europe. Using the Levels.fyi ML engineer medians (9 Oct 2026): US US$280K vs Germany EUR 88,465, France EUR 75,949, Zurich CHF 152,282, UK GBP 103,596, Italy EUR 32,471. Rough ratio: the US median is about 3 times Germany's and about 8 times Italy's in nominal terms (my arithmetic, ignoring exchange-rate and cost-of-living differences, and sample bias that favours well-paid US big-tech submitters). Italy's figure is based on few submissions and should be treated as indicative only.
  • Italy. Eurostat (extract April 2026, via the earlier project file) puts ICT specialists at 3.8% of employment in Italy, near the bottom of the EU. The earlier file also states Italy has about 8.7% of the EU's ICT specialists. Italian AI industry revenue reached EUR 1.2 billion in 2024, up 58% (Wikipedia, secondary, flagged by that page as possibly unreliable). Italian-language LLM projects (Minerva at Sapienza; Velvet by Almawave) and the AI4I institute in Turin exist (Wikipedia, secondary). Most ML roles are in Milan (banking, insurance, consulting, fashion/retail analytics), Turin (automotive, AI4I), Rome (public sector, defence/aerospace, research) and Bologna (industrial, CINECA supercomputing). Anthropic's job listing surfaced a Milan location, but I could not confirm the role type; the earlier file does not cover it. Italian pay is low relative to peers, which pushes strong graduates abroad (a widely repeated pattern in practitioner discussions).
  • Paris and London lab hubs. Paris is the home of Mistral (more than 1,000 staff, Wikipedia secondary) and has offices of other labs (Anthropic lists an Applied AI Engineer, Enterprise role in Paris; DeepMind lists Paris among its locations). London hosts Google DeepMind and Anthropic offices (DeepMind and Anthropic listings) and Wayve. Amsterdam: no reliable data found in this research (IMC and Flow Traders in the earlier file show quant-firm roles).
  • Gulf. Abu Dhabi's G42, Stargate UAE (announced May 2025; operations expected 2026), MBZUAI and sovereign-cloud projects mean heavy investment in AI infrastructure, and the US reclassified the UAE in July 2026 to ease advanced-chip exports (Wikipedia on G42).   • Asia. Singapore: ML engineer median S$141,805, SWE S$114,992 (Levels.fyi). Tokyo: SWE median JPY 8.05M, markedly below Singapore or London in dollar terms; Anthropic and DeepMind list Tokyo offices. Bengaluru and Mumbai: Anthropic lists Applied AI roles there; India's role as a global capability-centre (GCC) hub is general knowledge, not verified here. Hong Kong and China: no reliable data found; China's AI sector is large and competitive, with DeepSeek's R1 having briefly matched the top US model in February 2025 (Stanford AI Index 2026).

Commentary. Labs and big tech pay several times more than government, academia and nonprofits for comparable technical skill, but nonprofits like METR post US salaries ($328,000-$578,000 for evaluation roles) competitive with big tech. Within the labs, posted bands overlap heavily (OpenAI's $380,000-$500,000 research bands sit inside Anthropic's wider $350,000-$850,000 interpretability band), so no single lab sets the market. Governments offer stability and hybrid working at lower pay. Consulting and enterprise buyers are where the volume of governance and solutions jobs is, because every regulated company must comply with the EU AI Act from August 2026. In forward-deployed roles, labs pay more than consultancies for the same hours (Palantir, Databricks and Scale AI sit between) but ask for more experience.

Regional differences.

  • US versus Europe: London lab postings (Anthropic GBP 260,000-630,000; Cohere CA$250,000-535,000) are high by UK standards, but the UK research scientist median on Levels.fyi is GBP 116,501, Switzerland CHF 119,153, and UK AISI tops out at GBP 145,000, so the lab premium over the local market is large. Italy's software engineer median is EUR 39,258 total and I found no Italian frontier-lab research roles; ambitious Italians work in London, Zurich, Paris, the US or remotely.
  • London, Zurich, Paris: London hosts DeepMind, Anthropic, OpenAI and Cohere offices, AISI, Apollo and the London Initiative for Safe AI (host of Pivotal and LASR); Zurich hosts DeepMind, OpenAI and Anthropic postings; Paris hosts Mistral, DeepMind, Cohere and customer roles at Anthropic, OpenAI and Databricks. Brussels is the centre of EU policy; Washington DC of US policy (CAISI, NIST, Horizon placements, lab policy roles).
  • Italy: strong universities and the FAIR network give PhD and postdoc routes but low pay; governance and compliance demand is rising with Law 132/2025 and the EU AI Act (Il Sole 24 Ore reports Hays seeing more searches for AI governance specialists); I found no priced Italian posting beyond Anthropic's Milan role (which requires Italian).
  • Gulf and Asia: published pay is thin. Levels.fyi (self-reported) shows G42 research scientists at a median AED 429,653 (tax-free), UAE software engineers AED 337,547, Singapore research scientists SGD 90,299 and Japan about JPY 10.5 million; MBZUAI, TII and Sakana AI advertise roles without pay. Palantir, Databricks, OpenAI, Cohere and Scale AI post FDE or applied-AI roles in Tokyo, Seoul, Singapore, Sydney, Abu Dhabi, Dubai, Riyadh and Doha without ranges. China has its own frontier labs (not covered). Hong Kong: no reliable data found.
  • Immigration and talent flows: the AI Index 2026 reports that the number of AI researchers and developers moving to the US fell 89% since 2017, including 80% in the last year. Horizon does not sponsor visas and many fellowships need local work authorisation; Anthropic Fellows do not sponsor visas; Astra offers visa support; the UK AISI can sponsor visas for technical roles.

A reality check on pay, hours and tiers

Every figure here is dated and approximate, copied from the research with its source and year. Pay is in local currency and never converted. Tier lists reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact. Three limits apply to almost every page:

  • Asia and Gulf pay across almost all branches rests on aggregators, small self-reported samples (Levels.fyi has 12-40 submissions in some cities) or recruiter blogs. Tokyo is the thinnest.
  • Hours and stress are researchers' estimates from practitioner accounts, prep sites and a few surveys (mainly M&A). No report found a systematic hours survey by role family.
  • Gated recruiter guides (Hays, Robert Walters, Michael Page tables outside Italy, Management Consulted, CIPD, PMI) could not be read. Many pay figures therefore come via secondary summaries, as the reports note.

The full list of gaps, the rating scales and the researchers’ assumptions are on the sources page.
