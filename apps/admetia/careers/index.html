The thirteen fields

FieldDepthRole familiesWhat it is
FinanceDeep (4 parts)29Moving, pricing, investing and protecting money: investment banking and advisory (M&A, ECM, DCM/LevFin, restructuring), sales & trading and research, the buy side (asset management, hedge funds, PE, VC, private credit, allocators, private banking), and corporate finance, risk, compliance, quant and fintech. Top pay, but concentrated in a few very selective, long-hours seats.
Computer ScienceDeep (2 parts)14Building software products (backend, frontend, mobile, games, QA, solutions engineering) and running the systems underneath (DevOps/SRE, cloud, embedded, data engineering, enterprise IT, IT consulting, research). The widest pay spread of any branch; the hardest entry-level market in a decade.
Artificial IntelligenceDeep (2 parts)11Engineering AI systems (ML, LLM applications, MLOps/infrastructure, computer vision/robotics, data and feedback work) and the frontier around them (research science and engineering, safety and evaluations, forward-deployed roles, governance and policy, AI product). Extreme pay for a small elite; crowded entry.
Data ScienceDeep7Using statistics and models to make decisions: product experimentation, ML modelling, operations research, biostatistics, credit/fraud/pricing models in banking and insurance, PhD-track applied science, consulting.
Data AnalyticsDeep8Turning business data into reports, dashboards and answers: data/business analysts, BI developers, analytics engineers, marketing/product analysts, operations analysts, HR analytics, data governance, analytics consulting. The most accessible technical entry door.
CybersecurityDeep10Defending organisations: SOC monitoring, incident response and forensics, threat intelligence, penetration testing, application and cloud security, architecture, GRC/audit, OT security, consulting and the CISO track. Real demand at mid-level, but a tough first job despite "skills gap" headlines.
ManagementMedium6General business careers inside organisations: graduate leadership programmes, operations management, project/programme management, HR, in-house strategy and BD, public and non-profit management.
Logistics & Supply ChainMedium7Getting goods made, bought, stored and moved: procurement, demand/supply planning, logistics and freight forwarding, warehousing, shipping and maritime, supply chain analytics and systems, consulting.
AccountingMedium7Recording, checking and reporting financial information: external audit, tax, financial and management accounting/controlling, internal audit, forensic accounting, sustainability reporting. Qualification-driven and region-specific.
Marketing (incl. sales)Medium8Creating demand and winning customers: brand management, digital/performance, content and social, product marketing, CRM, market research, agency roles, and sales and business development.
EconomicsMedium7Being hired as an economist: central banks, international organisations, government and statistics offices, economic consulting, tech-sector economists, think tanks, academia and pre-docs. PhD-heavy at the top.
Management ConsultingMedium (extra)5Advising organisations on strategy, operations and transformation: MBB and tier-2 strategy firms, Big 4/Accenture, specialist boutiques, technology consulting, in-house strategy.
Product Management & StartupsMedium (extra)5Deciding what software gets built (PM, APM programmes, TPM and product ops) and building young companies (startup operator roles, founding).

No fourteenth branch was added. The two branches newcomers ask about most that the brief did not list are Sales & Business Development and Human Resources. Rather than spend a sub-agent on a separate report, sales and BD were added as a full role family in Marketing (3.8: SDR/BDR → account executive, key account management, with OTE pay), and HR as a full role family in Management (3.4). Sales is the larger entry door for business graduates and is the one to promote to its own report if the site needs it.

Read this before you rely on the explorer

A reality check on pay, hours and tiers

Every figure here is dated and approximate, copied from the research with its source and year. Pay is in local currency and never converted. Tier lists reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact. Three limits apply to almost every page:

  • Asia and Gulf pay across almost all branches rests on aggregators, small self-reported samples (Levels.fyi has 12-40 submissions in some cities) or recruiter blogs. Tokyo is the thinnest.
  • Hours and stress are researchers' estimates from practitioner accounts, prep sites and a few surveys (mainly M&A). No report found a systematic hours survey by role family.
  • Gated recruiter guides (Hays, Robert Walters, Michael Page tables outside Italy, Management Consulted, CIPD, PMI) could not be read. Many pay figures therefore come via secondary summaries, as the reports note.

The full list of gaps, the rating scales and the researchers’ assumptions are on the sources page.
