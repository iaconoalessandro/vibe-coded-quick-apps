What you actually do

  • SDR/BDR (software): find potential customers, send emails, call and message them, and book meetings for account executives. Example: 40-60 outbound touches a day, aiming for a handful of meetings a week (activity targets vary by company).
  • Account executive (AE): run demos, negotiate, and close deals against an annual quota (target). Pipeline reviews every week.
  • Key account manager (FMCG): manage a retailer relationship: negotiate promotions and listings, forecast volumes, handle conflicts, jointly plan with brand teams.
  • Luxury retail/clienteling and pharma sales (brief): luxury sales advisors build lasting client relationships; pharma sales representatives (Italy: "informatore scientifico del farmaco") visit doctors; both are regulated or high-training roles. Tools: CRM (Salesforce, HubSpot), sales engagement software, LinkedIn Sales Navigator, call recording, increasingly AI assistants.

A typical day and week

SDR: prospecting blocks, calls and sequences, CRM updates, daily activity reviews. AE: demos, proposal writing, follow-ups, internal approvals. KAM: retailer meetings, promotion planning, monthly performance reviews. Month-end and quarter-end are pressure peaks.

Hours, stress and lifestyle

Typical 40-50 hours; peaks 55-60 at quarter-end (BLS: many sales reps work more than 40 hours; quotas add stress; Prospects: sales hours are "generally long" with early starts and late finishes). Stress 4/5: pay depends on quota. Travel varies from none (inside sales) to heavy (field and key account). Hybrid common in SaaS.

Human vs. quantitative profile

People/communication: 5/5. Quantitative/technical: 2/5. Persuasion, resilience and discipline dominate; you need pipeline math and basic product knowledge, not advanced statistics.

Compensation (approximate)

  • Entry (SDR): US median SDR OTE $80K (about $55K base, $25K variable, 68:32 split), 60% of SDRs at quota (Bridge Group, February 2025, 351 B2B companies). A secondary source citing RepVue shows median base about $60K and OTE about $85K (unverified). UK sales executive basic £25,000-£28,000 (Prospects, September 2025). Italy: inside sales €25-35k with bonus 10-20%; field sales €30-35k; sales account €35-40k (Michael Page Italy 2026, <5 years); medical sales rep €30-40k with 15-20% bonus. Hong Kong BD manager HK$30-55k per month (Morgan McKinley 2026).
  • ~5 years (AE/KAM): US median AE OTE $200K (up from $190K in 2024), median quota $960K (4.6 times OTE), 48% of reps hit quota, ramp time 6.2 months, average experience at hire 3.7 years (Bridge Group AE report, 23 June 2026, 158 companies). RepVue medians via a secondary source (unverified): SMB AE about $130K OTE, mid-market $175K, enterprise $265K. Italy key account manager €55-70k (5-10 years, bonus 10-20%); pharma key account manager €65-90k (bonus 15-20%). Singapore key account manager S$95k at 5-10 years; Hong Kong key account manager (sales) HK$35-50k per month.
  • Senior: Italy national account manager €75-85k, sales director €85-100k (bonus 25-35%), commercial director over €95k; UK senior sales over £100k with commission (Prospects, September 2025); Singapore head of sales/BD director S$160-300k. Commission structure (SaaS): about 50/50 base/variable for AEs; median commission about 11.5% of annual contract value at quota (Bridge Group 2024 AE report via secondary summary, not re-verified).

Career path

SDR (12-24 months; average SDR tenure 1.9 years) to AE (SMB) to mid-market/enterprise AE (3-6 years) to sales manager/director (6-10) to VP sales / chief revenue officer. SDR promotions are scarcer: promotions were 16% of SDR attrition in 2024, down from 34% in 2020 (Bridge Group 2025). FMCG: sales rep/assistant KAM to KAM to national account manager to commercial/sales director.

Exit opportunities

After 2-3 years: AE, customer success, product marketing, sales operations, recruitment, marketing. After 7-10 years: sales director, VP, founder, general manager, or partnership/strategy leadership.

Tier list of employers

  • Tier 1 (by brand and training): Salesforce, Oracle, SAP, Microsoft, Google Cloud, HubSpot-type scale-ups; FMCG commercial rotations at P&G, Unilever, Nestle; luxury maisons for clienteling.
  • Tier 2: mid-size SaaS, staffing, telecom, medical device, B2B industrial.
  • Tier 3: commission-heavy, high-churn, low-base start-ups and call-centre-style outbound shops. Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact.

How to enter

  • Degrees: any; business, marketing or economics help, technical degrees for technical/scientific sales (BLS: technical/scientific reps typically need a bachelor's). Pharma sales often prefers life-science degrees.
  • Entry: SaaS SDR programmes at large vendors; FMCG sales graduate schemes (also parts of Unilever UFLP and P&G tracks); retail management trainee programmes; UK Level 4/6 sales apprenticeships.
  • Calendar: SDR hiring is rolling and frequent; FMCG schemes follow the autumn cycle.
  • Assessments: role-plays, cold-call exercises, personality tests, a final manager interview.
  • Certifications: Salesforce Administrator/Trailhead and HubSpot sales certificates are optional proof; few firms require them.
  • Skills: resilience, written outreach, listening, CRM familiarity.
  • Mistakes: ignoring commission plan details and quota realism; accepting a role with a low base and high quota.
  • Alternative routes: customer support or retail to SDR; SDR to marketing or product marketing. Entry difficulty: 2/5 for SDR/inside sales; 4/5 for FMCG graduate sales schemes and KAM jobs at top firms.

Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for

Variable pay and rejection; a 48% attainment rate for AEs and 60% for SDRs means many people miss targets (Bridge Group); SDR roles are the most exposed to AI automation (AI SDRs were first measured at 1% of respondents in 2025, a trend to watch). Not for people who need stable income, dislike targets or cold outreach, or want little interpersonal conflict.

A reality check on pay, hours and tiers

Every figure here is dated and approximate, copied from the research with its source and year. Pay is in local currency and never converted. Tier lists reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact. Three limits apply to almost every page:

  • Asia and Gulf pay across almost all branches rests on aggregators, small self-reported samples (Levels.fyi has 12-40 submissions in some cities) or recruiter blogs. Tokyo is the thinnest.
  • Hours and stress are researchers' estimates from practitioner accounts, prep sites and a few surveys (mainly M&A). No report found a systematic hours survey by role family.
  • Gated recruiter guides (Hays, Robert Walters, Michael Page tables outside Italy, Management Consulted, CIPD, PMI) could not be read. Many pay figures therefore come via secondary summaries, as the reports note.

The full list of gaps, the rating scales and the researchers’ assumptions are on the sources page.
