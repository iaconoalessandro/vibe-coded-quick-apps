What you actually do

You make the research possible and fast. Typical work across labs: designing RL environments and coding tasks, building reward signals and verifiers, running training experiments on frontier models and diagnosing why models do or do not improve, and building the pipelines that let research iterate quickly. October 2026 examples: Anthropic's Research Engineer, Code RL and Interpretability roles; OpenAI Research Engineer roles in retrieval and search, frontier evals and environments, Codex and privacy; Scale AI's ML Research Engineer, ML Systems; xAI and Cohere "Member of Technical Staff" roles in training infrastructure, RL frameworks and inference. Other work: distributed training across GPU clusters, profiling and CUDA kernels, data platforms for RL, evaluation tooling.

A typical day and week

Debugging a failed training run or a slow job; reviewing a colleague's pull request; pairing with a researcher on a new experiment; writing infrastructure code; on some teams an on-call rotation (one Anthropic cybersecurity RL posting mentions a possible on-call rotation). A week mixes coding (60-70%), meetings with researchers, and diagnosing experiments.

Hours, stress and lifestyle

Typical 45-55 hours; peaks 60-70 near launches or when a big run breaks (estimate). Stress 3-4/5: expensive runs make failures costly and on-call can interrupt evenings. On-site or hybrid: postings I read name an office city (San Francisco, Palo Alto, New York, London, Paris, Toronto, Zurich). Travel: low.

Human vs. quantitative profile

People/communication: 3/5. Quantitative/technical: 5/5. Collaboration with researchers is constant, but the hard part is distributed systems, performance and ML engineering.

Compensation (approximate)

  • US employer postings, base ranges (October 2026): OpenAI Research Engineer (San Francisco) $250,000-$445,000, Frontier Evals & Environments $295,000-$380,000, Retrieval & Search $293,000-$585,000, Codex $380,000-$500,000; Anthropic Code RL $500,000-$850,000, Interpretability $315,000-$560,000 (5-10+ years' experience), Applied AI Research Engineer $300,000-$400,000; Scale AI ML Research Engineer, ML Systems $189,600-$237,000, senior/staff $290,400-$363,000; Databricks Staff ML Engineer $190,000-$285,000; xAI Member of Technical Staff $180,000-$440,000; Cohere Lead MTS, Inference Infrastructure $235,000-$325,000. The spread is wide because titles map to different levels.
  • London and Europe: Anthropic Research Engineer (RL) GBP 260,000-630,000; Cohere Senior ML Systems Engineer, London CA$250,000-535,000 (about US$175,000-$375,000, my conversion); OpenAI research roles in London and Paris: no pay printed.
  • Levels.fyi (9 October 2026): research engineers across companies, median US$240,000 total (25th $115,000; 75th $369,000; 90th $660,000); Google research engineer shows one level, L5 at $620,000 (base $233,000). Anthropic software engineers: entry $367,000 total (base $250,000), senior $598,000, staff $1.13 million; OpenAI software engineer L2 $249,000, L3 $346,000, L5 $960,000; Palantir software engineer median $230,000 (711 submissions).
  • Big tech entry: a new-graduate or junior ML engineer at Google/Meta earns roughly $190,000-$310,000 total (derived from the research scientist ladders above at L3/E3-E4 levels; this is an upper-ish proxy).
  • Switzerland: research scientist median CHF 119,153 (proxy). Italy: software engineer median EUR 39,258; no data for research engineers. UAE software engineer median AED 337,547 (282 submissions; 90th AED 540,000; Levels.fyi). Mistral (Paris): software engineer median US$104,124 (small sample, Levels.fyi).

Career path

Software or ML engineer (0-3 years) -> research engineer (2-5) -> senior/staff research engineer (5-10) -> research engineering manager or technical lead. Many labs also convert strong research engineers into researchers; postings for "Staff+ Research Engineer", "Research Manager" and (at OpenAI) "Research Engineer / Research Scientist" shared titles show the ladder.

Exit opportunities

After 2-3 years: ML infrastructure roles at big tech, AI startups (often at higher equity), HPC/quant firms. After 7-10 years: head of infrastructure, founding engineer, CTO at an AI startup, or staff engineer at a cloud/GPU company.

Tier list of employers (alphabetical within each tier)

  • Tier 1: Anthropic, Google DeepMind, Meta Superintelligence Labs, NVIDIA (for performance engineering), OpenAI. Distinguishing: frontier-scale clusters and the hardest infrastructure problems.
  • Tier 2: Amazon, Apple, Cohere, Databricks/MosaicML-style training companies, Microsoft, Mistral, Scale AI, xAI, strong AI startups.
  • Tier 3: research engineering at universities, institutes and corporate labs (smaller clusters), national supercomputing centres (for example CINECA in Italy; general knowledge).

Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact.

How to enter

A bachelor's or master's in computer science, maths or physics works; Anthropic's postings require a bachelor's or equivalent experience and say publications are not necessary (I did not verify the same wording elsewhere). PhD not required but helpful. The practical filter is evidence of strong engineering plus familiarity with LLMs: shipping projects with PyTorch/JAX, reproducing a paper, contributing to open-source training libraries (reimplementing a small transformer, writing a fast attention kernel). Experience is usually required: postings call for 5-10+ years (Anthropic Interpretability) or "depends on level"; entry-level research engineer roles exist mainly at big tech and through fellowships (see 3.3) and internships. Interviews: Python coding, ML-infrastructure system design, take-home or pair programming on experiments, research discussion. Certifications: not valued. Common mistakes: notebook-level ML projects, ignoring systems fundamentals (networking, GPUs), underestimating coding-interview preparation. Alternative routes: backend or HPC software engineers; cybersecurity engineers moving into RL environments for security (Anthropic's cyber RL postings); non-target-school candidates through open source. Entry difficulty: 4/5.

Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for

You are a support function for researchers: credit goes to the papers' authors and priorities can change quickly. On-call and launch crunches exist. Pay at the top is very high but concentrated in equity of private firms. Not a fit for people who want to set the research agenda, dislike infrastructure work, or cannot live in a few expensive cities. Not a fit for those wanting a standard regulated-industry career with predictable hours.

A reality check on pay, hours and tiers

Every figure here is dated and approximate, copied from the research with its source and year. Pay is in local currency and never converted. Tier lists reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact. Three limits apply to almost every page:

  • Asia and Gulf pay across almost all branches rests on aggregators, small self-reported samples (Levels.fyi has 12-40 submissions in some cities) or recruiter blogs. Tokyo is the thinnest.
  • Hours and stress are researchers' estimates from practitioner accounts, prep sites and a few surveys (mainly M&A). No report found a systematic hours survey by role family.
  • Gated recruiter guides (Hays, Robert Walters, Michael Page tables outside Italy, Management Consulted, CIPD, PMI) could not be read. Many pay figures therefore come via secondary summaries, as the reports note.

The full list of gaps, the rating scales and the researchers’ assumptions are on the sources page.
