What you actually do

You design and price customised products and the supporting ideas for sales and clients. A typical equity derivatives structurer designs payoffs such as barrier options, worst-of structures, cliquets, digitals and autocallables (a note that redeems early if the underlying stays above a level), sometimes hybrids that mix equity, rates and FX, or quantitative investment strategies (QIS); then prices them with quants, checks they can be hedged, sizes capital and funding costs, and produces term sheets and pitch material (Citi, Selby Jennings and other postings, via search results). Compliance with product regulation (PRIIPs, MiFID II, benchmark rules) is part of the job. In rates, credit and FX, "solutions" or "origination" teams do similar work for liability management (helping issuers), hedging and structured notes. J.P. Morgan's markets programme includes "Structuring & Origination" as a distinct track (job-board listings, unverified).

A typical day and week

Morning: check volatility, correlation, dividend and funding levels; respond to sales requests (price this note for a private bank); build or adjust payoffs in a pricing tool (Python/Excel/internal libraries); coordinate with traders on hedge costs and with quants on model changes; prepare term sheets and pitches; afternoon calls with clients or private banks; review with legal/compliance. Multiple live requests at once; pitch deadlines.

Hours, stress and lifestyle

Hours: 50-60 per week typically, 65+ near product launches or pitch deadlines (practitioner consensus, unverified). Stress: 3/5. Hybrid arrangements and some remote work are more possible than on a trading floor because it is less tied to the market clock. Travel: client visits, particularly in Europe and Asia where the retail structured product market is large (France, Italy, Switzerland, Hong Kong, Singapore).

Human vs. quantitative profile

People/communication: 3/5. Quantitative/technical: 4/5. You need to explain complex payoffs simply and be rigorous in pricing and risk.

Compensation (approximate)

No recruiter table for structurers alone could be opened. Selby Jennings' 2025 NYC guide has no separate structuring row; equity derivatives (trading and sales) are the nearest: trading analysts US$175-275k total, associates US$250-450k, VPs US$400-850k (Selby Jennings, 2025). UK/Italy/Asia: no reliable data found. The Selby Jennings 2024 quant guide shows sell-side quant bands for London (Associate/AVP total £85-150k; VP total £135-250k) and Hong Kong/Singapore (associate US$120-140k total, VP US$220-250k), which sit near structurer/strat pay but are not the same job (2024).

Career path

Analyst (2-3 years) → Associate → VP (about 6-8 years, often hired laterally from a quant or structurer role) → Director → MD (head of structuring for a product or region). Many structurers start as quants/strats, graduates of mathematics and engineering programmes, and move into structuring; others rotate from sales or trading.

Exit opportunities

After 2-3 years: trading, sales, quant roles at banks, insurance product teams, private bank product teams. After 7-10 years: head of structuring, derivatives sales at a hedge fund, product control, fintech/robo-advice product design, or asset management product development.

Tier list of employers

Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact.

  • Tier 1: Goldman Sachs, J.P. Morgan, Morgan Stanley, Citi, Bank of America and the strongest European derivatives houses: BNP Paribas and Société Générale (known for equity derivatives and structured products), Barclays, Deutsche Bank, UBS.
  • Tier 2: Nomura, HSBC, Natixis, Crédit Agricole CIB, other global banks on selected products, large Italian banks (Intesa Sanpaolo, UniCredit) and Mediobanca for Italian retail certificates.
  • Tier 3: regional banks, brokers and small specialist houses.

How to enter

Strong maths/finance background (mathematics, engineering, physics, quantitative finance MSc such as Bocconi's MAFinRisk, whose placement page lists Mediobanca, Intesa Sanpaolo and UniCredit among recruiters in 2023-25 and says most graduates go into trading, risk, sales or structuring). Entry points: summer analyst programmes (structuring track), graduate programmes, strats/quant teams. Skills: derivatives pricing (Black-Scholes, volatility surfaces), Python, a clear way of explaining payoffs. CFA is not a signal; an MFE/quantitative master's is. Common mistakes: focusing on the maths with no communication; ignoring regulation; applying only to the biggest firms. Alternative routes: quant analyst/strat first, then structuring; ex-trader/associate moves; product control/valuation roles with strong derivatives knowledge. Entry difficulty: 4/5.

Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for

The seats are few; structured product demand depends on volatility, rates and investor risk appetite; pressure to produce "something new" for pitches; the role is in the middle (sales wants cheaper, risk wants safer); regulators scrutinise retail products; and pay is typically below top traders. Not a good fit if you dislike repetitive pricing work, if you want direct P&L ownership, or if you are not comfortable with derivatives mathematics.

A reality check on pay, hours and tiers

Every figure here is dated and approximate, copied from the research with its source and year. Pay is in local currency and never converted. Tier lists reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact. Three limits apply to almost every page:

  • Asia and Gulf pay across almost all branches rests on aggregators, small self-reported samples (Levels.fyi has 12-40 submissions in some cities) or recruiter blogs. Tokyo is the thinnest.
  • Hours and stress are researchers' estimates from practitioner accounts, prep sites and a few surveys (mainly M&A). No report found a systematic hours survey by role family.
  • Gated recruiter guides (Hays, Robert Walters, Michael Page tables outside Italy, Management Consulted, CIPD, PMI) could not be read. Many pay figures therefore come via secondary summaries, as the reports note.

The full list of gaps, the rating scales and the researchers’ assumptions are on the sources page.
