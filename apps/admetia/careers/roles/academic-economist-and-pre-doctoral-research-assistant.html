What you actually do

Academic economist: research (write and publish papers), teach, supervise students, referee papers and apply for grants. Pre-doc: a research assistant who cleans data, runs regressions in Stata/R/Python, reads literature and sometimes co-authors; the work is a one- to two-year apprenticeship before a PhD application.

A typical day and week

Faculty (illustrative composite): two days of teaching/office hours, two days of research, seminar on one afternoon, refereeing and admin. Pre-doc: coding for the principal investigator, weekly meeting, reading papers.

Hours, stress and lifestyle

Faculty 45-60 hours with flexible scheduling; peaks at job-market season, grant deadlines and tenure review. Stress 3/5 for pre-docs; 4/5 for the PhD job market and tenure track (author judgement; EJMR-style anecdotes not used). Travel: conferences. Pre-docs 40-50 hours.

Human vs. quantitative profile

People/communication: 3/5. Quantitative/technical: 5/5. Research requires technical depth and writing; teaching and collaboration require communication.

Compensation (approximate)

Faculty: AEA Universal Academic Questionnaire reports average starting salary for assistant professors at PhD-granting institutions at USD 154,288 in 2024-25; another table shows USD 157,017 (AEA Papers and Proceedings 2025, 115:724-727; the two figures differ and the basis, nine- vs twelve-month, is unclear). Payscale's generic estimate is lower (about USD 74,863, not comparable). Pre-docs (2026 postings): Harvard (Stantcheva) USD 50,000-55,000; USC USD 42,436 plus benefits; London Business School GBP 43,250, up to two years; LSE STICERD GBP 36,513-41,565 (rising to 44,468); an older Harvard/Yale/HBS fellowship offered USD 66,000. PhD stipends: Italian borsa about EUR 18,433 gross in 2026/27 at Brescia (university board decision, March 2026); Bocconi PhD EUR 27,000 year 1, EUR 23,000 later (library research); UKRI GBP 21,805 from 1 October 2026 (library research); LBS GBP 32,154 tax-free (library research). Italy faculty pay: no reliable data found. Asia/Gulf faculty pay: no reliable data found.

Career path

Pre-doc (1-2 years) -> PhD (US 5-6 years; Europe 3-5; library notes Bocconi 4 years) -> Assistant professor (tenure track, 6-7 years to tenure; US) -> Associate professor with tenure -> Full professor. In the UK, lecturer -> senior lecturer -> reader/professor. Italy: PhD -> postdoc/RTD-A/RTD-B -> associate professor via national qualification and local competition (system details: unverified in my sources).

Exit opportunities

After a PhD or 2-3 years on the market: industry (tech, consulting), central banks, IOs. ProMarket analysis: around two-thirds of PhDs took academic jobs in 2009, now roughly half, while tech rose from 1% of placements in the 2010s to 12% in the 2020s (ProMarket, accessed October 2026). After tenure: policy roles, central banks, consulting affiliate work.

Tier list of employers

Departments are conventionally ranked by research output (practitioner perception): Tier 1: MIT, Harvard, Chicago, Princeton, Stanford, Yale, Berkeley, LSE, Oxford, TSE, PSE and similar. Tier 2: other top-50 research universities. Tier 3: teaching-oriented colleges and universities. Pre-doc tiers: Fed/NBER/Harvard/Chicago-affiliated programmes versus smaller departments. Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact.

How to enter

Degrees: PhD required for faculty; bachelor's or master's for pre-docs (Fed RA, IMF RAP, think-tank RA also count as pre-doc-like). Calendar of the academic job market (typical practice; AEA says interviews will occur online in 2026): candidate profiles and listings on JOE (Job Openings for Economists, the AEA's listings site) open in the late summer; applications are due in the autumn; first-round interviews historically ran at the January ASSA meetings (now online), followed by campus visits ("flyouts") in January-March and offers in spring. Hiring conditions: JOE listings in the second half of 2024 were down 4.6% from 2023 and 22.8% from 2022; the first half of 2025 was down 14.4% from 2024 and 39% from 2023 (AEA Committee on the Job Market, July 2025). The December 2025 department-chair survey attributed weakness to US university shocks (endowment tax, grant cuts, indirect-cost reductions, visa restrictions); I could not read the hiring count from the PDF. Interview formats: job market paper presentation (job talk), research conversations with faculty, teaching statement. Skills: econometrics, one's subfield, coding, writing. Common mistakes: starting a PhD without research experience or pre-doc; weak letters; no job market paper. Alternative routes: pre-docs (Harvard, USC, LBS, LSE and others; NBER predoc job announcement 2026), master's with thesis, central bank RAs. I found no systematic placement rate for pre-docs. Entry difficulty: 5/5 for top-programme tenure-track seats; 3/5 for pre-docs.

Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for

5-6+ years of stipend-level pay; a weak 2025-26 job market (listings down 39% vs 2023 by mid-2025); US-visa uncertainty; tenure review stress; pre-docs are low-paid and temporary. Not a good fit if you need income soon, dislike open-ended research, or want applied impact quickly.

A reality check on pay, hours and tiers

Every figure here is dated and approximate, copied from the research with its source and year. Pay is in local currency and never converted. Tier lists reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact. Three limits apply to almost every page:

  • Asia and Gulf pay across almost all branches rests on aggregators, small self-reported samples (Levels.fyi has 12-40 submissions in some cities) or recruiter blogs. Tokyo is the thinnest.
  • Hours and stress are researchers' estimates from practitioner accounts, prep sites and a few surveys (mainly M&A). No report found a systematic hours survey by role family.
  • Gated recruiter guides (Hays, Robert Walters, Michael Page tables outside Italy, Management Consulted, CIPD, PMI) could not be read. Many pay figures therefore come via secondary summaries, as the reports note.

The full list of gaps, the rating scales and the researchers’ assumptions are on the sources page.
