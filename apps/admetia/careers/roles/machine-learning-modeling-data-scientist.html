What you actually do

You turn a business prediction problem into a working model. Examples: forecasting demand for 50,000 products per store so replenishment can order stock; ranking items on a home page; predicting which customers will cancel and testing which retention offer works; scoring transactions for fraud. Tasks: gather and clean data, feature engineering, train and compare models (gradient-boosted trees such as XGBoost or LightGBM remain the workhorse for tabular data; deep learning for text/images), validate offline and online, document, hand over to engineers or deploy yourself, and monitor for model drift (performance decay as the world changes). Increasingly: prototype LLM-based features (classifying support tickets, extracting fields from documents) and evaluate them. Tools: Python, SQL, scikit-learn, PyTorch, Spark, cloud platforms, experiment trackers, Git.

A typical day and week

Morning stand-up with the ML engineer and product owner; debugging data quality; training runs and code review; mid-week presentation of model results to the business owner; periodic retraining and model-monitoring reviews. About half the time is data work, not modelling (consensus).

Hours, stress and lifestyle

40-50 typical, 55-60 peak around launches or incidents (consensus). Stress 3/5; on-call is uncommon unless the model is customer-facing (consensus). Hybrid is common; project cycles of 1-3 months.

Human vs. quantitative profile

People/communication: 3/5. Quantitative/technical: 4/5. You must be genuinely fluent in statistics and code; explaining model limits to stakeholders is also needed, but less than in product roles.

Compensation (approximate)

  • US: BLS median for data scientists US$120,230 (May 2025); P10 US$67,240; P90 US$199,130. Levels.fyi top-company data above apply when the job is at big tech. Amazon applied scientist (the research-leaning cousin) L4 US$247k (Levels.fyi, 9 Oct 2026). Non-tech employers (insurers, retailers) pay far less than the Levels.fyi medians suggest; BLS is the better guide for the average market.
  • UK: banks' data science graduate schemes advertise GBP 42,000 (NatWest, 12-month data science programme, 2027 intake) and GBP 48,500 (Lloyds Banking Group Data Science & AI scheme, 2 years); Capital One UK GBP 46,500-55,500 plus GBP 5,000 joining bonus on its technology programme (employer listings, September 2026). Mid-career London: GBP 60-90k (Pocketwise, a general site, weak); senior GBP 70-100k nationally.
  • Italy: junior roughly EUR 29-42k gross (Glassdoor Feb 2026 EUR 26-33k; NettoCalc Milan EUR 42,000 for 0-3 years, a weaker aggregator); Generali Italia listing for a junior innovation and data analytics role EUR 36,000 (undated). Mid-career Italy and senior: no reliable data found beyond Levels.fyi's Italy 75th percentile EUR 60k and 90th EUR 74k (all levels, n=23). Hays Italy's 2026 guide gives EUR 56,360 as the average RAL across all professions surveyed, not data science.
  • Switzerland (for reference): Zurich senior data scientist median CHF 150,748 (n=16); all-levels CHF 116,413 (Levels.fyi, 9 Oct 2026). Singapore: median SGD 120k (all levels); Skillup (2026, MyCareersFuture data from March 2024) gives SGD 60k at 0-2 years, 105k at 2-5, 140k at 5-8, 180k at 8+ (base).

Career path

Junior/associate data scientist (0-2 years) → data scientist (2-5) → senior (5-8) → staff/principal or lead (8+). Many people move to "ML engineer" or "AI engineer" at the mid-level because that title currently carries more demand.

Exit opportunities

After 2-3 years: ML engineer, AI engineer, data scientist at a larger employer, product analytics, quant research (with strong maths, see Finance). After 7-10 years: lead/head of data science, ML engineering management, AI product, consulting, founder.

Tier list of employers

Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact.

  • Tier 1: big tech ML organisations and top ML-driven scale-ups (Google, Meta, Netflix, Stripe, Spotify, Zalando, Booking.com). Large data, mature MLOps, ML-engineer colleagues; very selective.
  • Tier 2: banks and insurers with real analytics functions (Capital One, JPMorgan, Lloyds, NatWest, Admiral, Allianz), retailers with data platforms, telecoms, energy majors (Enel, Eni), larger Italian and European corporates. Real problems and good training, slower tooling, more governance.
  • Tier 3: consultancies' delivery teams, mid-market firms and IT-services outsourcers where "data scientist" can mean reporting and light modelling.

How to enter

  • Degrees: bachelor's in computer science, statistics, mathematics, physics, engineering or economics; a master's in data science, statistics, machine learning or an applied field is the common path and is expected for most European roles. A PhD is not needed for most modelling roles. UK banks typically require a 2:1 in a STEM subject (NatWest). Lloyds states no minimum degree classification but expects demonstrable technical skills and does not sponsor visas.
  • Programmes: NatWest Graduate Trainee Data Science (12 months, GBP 42,000, 2027 intake); Lloyds Data Science and AI graduate scheme (2 years, 2 year-long placements incl. fraud and credit risk); Capital One UK technology programme; UK bank intakes typically run September to January with applications closing in autumn, so 2027 intake rounds are running now. Admiral's analytics/data science scheme (Cardiff): two 25-month programmes, 2026 intake deadline 26 Oct 2025 (listing).
  • Interviews: online assessment (logic, maths), SQL and Python coding test, then case or technical interviews on statistics and model evaluation; take-home projects are common at start-ups. Italian consultancies describe an online test, SQL/Python technical interview and optional case (Glassdoor candidate reports on Accenture and Reply, anecdotal).
  • Certifications: cloud ones (AWS, Azure, Google) help only slightly; kaggle rankings and projects matter more. Skills: Python, SQL, scikit-learn and gradient boosting, validation and leakage, basic cloud and Git, communication. Portfolio: an end-to-end project with deployment (even a small API), a forecasting or fraud project on public data, a GitHub repo with clean code.
  • Common mistakes: modelling without checking the data, leakage, optimising accuracy instead of the business metric, building huge deep-learning models where a simple baseline works.
  • Alternative routes: analyst → data scientist; software engineers moving into ML; non-target schools can use competitions, open-source contributions and internships at mid-sized firms.
  • Entry difficulty: 4/5.

Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for

Much of the day is data cleaning, and many models never reach production; the title is being replaced by ML/AI engineer, which asks for software skills; entry hiring has tightened (see 2.1). Not a good fit if you dislike coding or messy data, or want research freedom (that is 3.6).

A reality check on pay, hours and tiers

Every figure here is dated and approximate, copied from the research with its source and year. Pay is in local currency and never converted. Tier lists reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact. Three limits apply to almost every page:

  • Asia and Gulf pay across almost all branches rests on aggregators, small self-reported samples (Levels.fyi has 12-40 submissions in some cities) or recruiter blogs. Tokyo is the thinnest.
  • Hours and stress are researchers' estimates from practitioner accounts, prep sites and a few surveys (mainly M&A). No report found a systematic hours survey by role family.
  • Gated recruiter guides (Hays, Robert Walters, Michael Page tables outside Italy, Management Consulted, CIPD, PMI) could not be read. Many pay figures therefore come via secondary summaries, as the reports note.

The full list of gaps, the rating scales and the researchers’ assumptions are on the sources page.
