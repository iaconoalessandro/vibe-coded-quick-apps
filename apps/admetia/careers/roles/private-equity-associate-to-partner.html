What you actually do

Associates build LBO (leveraged buyout) models, review confidential information memoranda, run due diligence calls with consultants, accountants and lawyers, manage data rooms, write investment committee memos (often 30-40 pages), and work with banks on financing. After the deal, you monitor portfolio companies (monthly KPI packs, board materials). VPs and principals source deals and lead execution; partners decide on the investment and raise funds.

A typical day and week

Morning: model updates and calls with management or advisers; afternoon: diligence sessions or lender discussions; evenings and weekends: IC memo and model. Weeks differ: a "live deal" can mean 80-100 hours near signing, while portfolio monitoring and sourcing weeks are calmer.

Hours, stress and lifestyle

55-70 hours typical, 80-100 during live deals (a recruiter's guide says 60-80 regularly with longer stretches at closing, Wall Street Careers, 2026; lower mid-market is often 50-60, practitioner estimate). Stress 4/5. Travel to portfolio companies and targets. Hybrid is partly possible, but most junior work is in the office.

Human vs. quantitative profile

People/communication: 4/5. Quantitative/technical: 3/5. Modelling must be solid, but the job at VP level is judgment, negotiating with sellers, managing management teams and winning deals.

Compensation (approximate)

US: Associates (typically hired after 2-3 years in banking) earn base US$140-175k and first-year total US$250-400k+ (Leland, July 2025); Glassdoor averages US$246,462 total with a 25th-75th percentile range of US$186,657-332,138 (February 2026, via Wall Street Careers); one third-party estimate puts mega-funds at US$325-425k and middle-market at US$250-340k (Refonte Learning, 2026, an estimate). Carry is rare at associate level and begins around senior associate/VP (Wall Street Prep, via Wall Street Careers; recruiter material). Heidrick's 2025 North America survey (656 respondents, 19 November 2025) found half of respondents had base increases of 10% or less and three-quarters said bonuses are discretionary; the public page gives no dollar tables. UK: associates total £170-300k in London (Jobrise, 2026; low-reliability estimate; an older Emolument survey from March 2020 gave £100k+ base, bonus up to £36k, and carry up to £300k, stale). Italy: the PageGroup survey puts "M&A analyst/associate/manager (investment banking, private equity cluster)" at €27-60k gross below five years, up to about €100k at 5-10 years and above €100k beyond, with bonus 50-100% (via We Wealth, December 2024); this mixes banking and PE, so treat as a floor. Continental Europe: Heidrick's Europe survey (302 participants, 11 December 2025) shows managing partners with an average base of €339k and bonus of €459k (finews); other levels not public. Hong Kong/Singapore: managing partners average US$891k, up 14% (Heidrick 2025 APAC/Middle East survey via Gulf News, 6 April 2026), with average carry allocations of US$5.487 million; Middle East managing partners average US$750k total cash (up from US$624k); associate-level 2025 data: no reliable data found (older Heidrick data is stale). Tokyo: one recruiter listing for an associate to senior associate at a global PE fund quoted ¥14-17 million (Morgan McKinley, undated listing).

Career path

Analyst (pre-MBA, at some funds 0-2 years), associate (2-3 years), senior associate or VP (3-6 years), principal/director (6-10), partner/managing director (10-15+). Promotion timelines have widened as holding periods lengthened to about seven years (Bain, 2026).

Exit opportunities

After 2-3 years: MBA, move to a bigger or different fund, growth equity, venture, corporate development, portfolio company operating roles. After 7-10 years: partner at the same or another fund, start a fund or independent sponsor, CFO or CEO of a portfolio company, family office.

Tier list of employers

Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact. Mega-fund (largest funds, global, biggest deals, most structured): Blackstone, KKR, Apollo, Carlyle, TPG, CVC, EQT, Bain Capital, Advent, Warburg Pincus, Permira, Hellman & Friedman, Thoma Bravo, Silver Lake. Upper-middle-market / large mid-market: Leonard Green, Veritas, Francisco Partners, Apax, Ardian, PAI Partners, Bridgepoint, Cinven. Middle-market: regional and sector funds such as Riverside, HIG, Audax; in Italy Investindustrial, Clessidra, Wise Equity, Xenon, Ambienta, and the state-backed Fondo Italiano d'Investimento. Lower middle-market / independent sponsors: small funds with fewer resources and more operational involvement, where hiring is less structured.

How to enter

Almost all associate hires at top funds come from top-tier banks' M&A or leveraged-finance analyst classes, strategy consulting (especially in Europe), or occasionally other funds. US "on-cycle" recruiting means that large funds interview junior bankers very early (historically within months of starting as an analyst) for jobs 18-24 months ahead, through headhunters such as Henkel, Amity, CPI and Oxbridge, in processes that can go from call to offer in days. This was disrupted in 2025: JPMorgan warned that analysts accepting future-dated PE jobs in their first 18 months could be fired; on 11-12 June 2025 Apollo and General Atlantic announced they would not formally interview for the class of 2027 (Banking Dive, 12 June 2025); recruiting resumed on 5 January 2026 with Blackstone, Apollo, KKR and Thoma Bravo among a dozen-plus firms interviewing (reporting via PE Insights/Business Insider summaries); Apollo says it fills under half of its associate class on-cycle. Several firms now allocate about half their classes to off-cycle hiring (Prospect Rock Partners). The 2028-class calendar has not been reported; an undated forum post says it had not started (anecdote). In Europe and Asia, I found no source documenting a fixed on-cycle calendar; listings from European mid-market funds look rolling and year-round and ask for 1-3 years in investment banking, strategy consulting or M&A boutiques (job listings via CFA Institute career centre, Munich/Frankfurt/Zurich). That suggests continental mid-market PE is off-cycle, but this is my inference, not documented. Certifications: CFA is not required; some value a Master's. Skills: LBO modelling tests, case studies, a view on industries. Common mistakes: applying without any transaction experience, weak understanding of leverage, thinking MBA alone is a route (the earlier project file notes that post-MBA PE seats go almost exclusively to people with prior PE or elite M&A experience). Alternative routes: transaction services (Big 4), strategy consulting (Bain, BCG, McKinsey), credit funds, restructuring, or joining a smaller or independent sponsor and moving up. Entry difficulty: 5/5.

Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for

Pay is very high but bonuses are discretionary; realised carry depends on exits that are now slow (distributions under 15% of NAV for four years, Bain 2026). Junior work is repetitive modelling and diligence. Partnership seats are few: many associates exit. Not for you if you want predictable hours, hate negotiating, or want to run operations rather than make bets.

A reality check on pay, hours and tiers

Every figure here is dated and approximate, copied from the research with its source and year. Pay is in local currency and never converted. Tier lists reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact. Three limits apply to almost every page:

  • Asia and Gulf pay across almost all branches rests on aggregators, small self-reported samples (Levels.fyi has 12-40 submissions in some cities) or recruiter blogs. Tokyo is the thinnest.
  • Hours and stress are researchers' estimates from practitioner accounts, prep sites and a few surveys (mainly M&A). No report found a systematic hours survey by role family.
  • Gated recruiter guides (Hays, Robert Walters, Michael Page tables outside Italy, Management Consulted, CIPD, PMI) could not be read. Many pay figures therefore come via secondary summaries, as the reports note.

The full list of gaps, the rating scales and the researchers’ assumptions are on the sources page.
