What you actually do

On the research track, you write working papers (pre-publication drafts) on topics such as inflation, labour markets or bank lending, estimate models in Stata, R, Python or MATLAB, present at internal seminars and external conferences and try to publish in journals; the Fed Board states its economists present at conferences, publish in peer-reviewed journals and circulate working papers (Fed Board, October 2026). On the policy track, you maintain forecasting models (for example a nowcast, an estimate of today's GDP or inflation from the latest data) and write briefing notes for the policy committee (FOMC at the Fed, Governing Council at the ECB, MPC at the Bank of England). The ECB graduate economist works on analytical and policy work, improves analytical tools, prepares groundwork for policy decisions and communications and represents the ECB externally (ECB graduate page, 2026). Research assistants (RAs) at the Fed prepare data for memos, run literature searches, draft limited-complexity analytical memos and handle data requests (Fed RA page, October 2026). Supervisory economists review banks' risk models and stress tests.

A typical day and week

Illustrative composite, not a measured schedule. Monday: update the data pipeline and nowcast in R or Python; Tuesday: draft two pages for the governor's briefing; Wednesday: seminar where a colleague's working paper is critiqued; Thursday: coding the model or regressions; Friday: meeting with the policy division. In the weeks before a rate decision, forecast rounds and briefing deadlines tighten. Researchers have weeks of protected time for papers.

Hours, stress and lifestyle

Typical 40-50 hours, peak 55-60 around forecast rounds and policy meetings (author judgement from practitioner descriptions; no survey found). Stress 2/5 for research, 3/5 for policy or crisis periods. Flexibility is good: most central banks offer hybrid work, and travel is occasional (conferences, committee meetings). Fed DC jobs are in-person in Washington (Fed Board, October 2026).

Human vs. quantitative profile

People/communication: 3/5. Quantitative/technical: 4/5. Research economists spend most time alone on code and writing; policy economists must turn technical results into short memos for non-economists, so writing matters as much as modelling.

Compensation (approximate)

US: Fed Board does not publish role pay on the pages I read; its 2025 FR salary structure runs from FR-16 (USD 36,400-44,400) up to FR-31 (up to USD 295,200), with no mapping to RA or PhD-economist jobs on the page (Fed Board salary page, 2025). A 2026 San Francisco Fed (a regional Reserve Bank) listing shows USD 80,000 for an economic research associate (Built In listing, 2026, snippet-level); a university career page cites about USD 60,000 for Fed RAs from Glassdoor (undated, unverified). Euro area: ECB Economist Graduate Programme, band E/F step 1, EUR 4,982 net per month plus benefits (about EUR 59,800 net per year over 12 months; ECB page, 2026 cycle). UK: Bank of England graduate programme GBP 33,525 (Leeds, 2026 listing, TargetJobs) and GBP 36,500 in London/32,750 Leeds for 2025 (Prospects); the Economist stream salary was not separated; the Bank's PhD Research Programme was advertised at GBP 60,000 but was not recruiting for 2025/26 (BoE research careers page, snippet-level). Italy: Bank of Italy Esperto (graduate-level) starting pay is reported at EUR 50,126 gross per year (money.it, 18 December 2025; secondary and unverified; the official notice does not state pay). Asia: HKMA Graduate Economist, HK$ 49,000-50,000+ fixed monthly pay plus lump-sum on completion (HKMA vacancy and programme page, 2026; currency symbol shown as "$"). ~5 years and senior: no reliable data found for any central bank; Fed senior pay can reach the upper FR grades, but role mapping is not published.

Career path

RA/analyst (0-2 years, often leaving for a PhD) -> economist (PhD, year 0-5) -> senior economist (5-10) -> principal economist/adviser -> division head/director. At the ECB the graduate programme lasts two years with an option for a third (ECB page). Bank of Italy is a permanent civil-service-style career entered by competitive exam (concorso).

Exit opportunities

After 2-3 years (RA): PhD programmes (the Fed says over half of RAs enter graduate programmes, mainly economics or finance PhDs), government or consulting. After 7-10 years (economist): IMF/World Bank, bank chief-economist roles, academia, economic consulting, tech.

Tier list of employers

Tier 1 (research-heavy, global policy influence): Federal Reserve Board and New York Fed, ECB, Bank of England, BIS. Tier 2: other Reserve Banks, Bank of Italy, Bundesbank, Banque de France, Bank of Japan, MAS, HKMA, Bank of Canada. Tier 3: smaller or emerging-market central banks and financial supervisors, where analytical depth, publishing culture and PhD intensity vary widely. Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact. Gulf central banks and sovereign entities: no reliable data found on entry routes or pay.

How to enter

Degrees: PhD for research economist (ECB graduate economist requires a master's plus PhD in economics completed in the window, EU nationality, C1 English; Fed economist requires a PhD or dissertation in progress); bachelor's or master's for RA/analyst (Fed RA: bachelor's or master's in economics, statistics, maths, finance, data science or similar, US citizenship required); master's for many policy-analyst roles. Programmes and calendar: Fed RA applications Aug 17-Oct 15 per the Fed page, interviews Oct-Nov and Feb-May, rolling offers, start in June/July, two-year term. ECB Economist Graduate Programme 2026: application closed 6 January 2026, up to four posts, two-year fixed term from 1 September, written exercise, presentation, interview; the generalist graduate programme (19 places in total) is a separate track for master's graduates (library research, ECB page). BIS Graduate Programme: contract up to 11 months, salary net of Swiss income tax, advertised in spring; PhD fellowship in late summer (BIS pages). HKMA Graduate Economist: two-year programme, postgraduate degree in economics, finance or similar, 2026 deadline 9 January 2026, essay (about 300 words), aptitude test, written test, interview. Bank of Italy: Esperto economics profile December 2025 notice, 45 places, master's at 105/110 or above in listed classes (LM-77, LM-56, LM-16, LM-52, LM-62), 8,307 applications (about 185 per place), 60-question preselection, top 500 to written, top 400 to oral including English; separate PhD research fellowships for economists (six fellows, 12 months renewable once, October 2025 call). Interview formats: technical presentation of research, written exercise, policy memo. Skills: econometrics (time series, panel, causal inference), Stata/R/Python/MATLAB, clear writing. Common mistakes: applying with a master's to PhD-only research seats; missing the narrow window; ignoring nationality rules (EU-only at ECB; US-citizen only at the Fed RA). Alternative routes: RA jobs at the Fed or central bank, then PhD; for Italians, a concorso is the route and grade floors are hard. Entry difficulty: 4/5 (RA/analyst) to 5/5 (research PhD seats at tier 1).

Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for

Policy impact is often indirect; senior decisions are made by committees. Research seats are PhD-gated, and the best ones are scarce (ECB: four economist posts in 2026). Pay is below consulting and tech, and in Italy it is tied to public scales. Public-exam routes (Bank of Italy) filter on grades and test performance more than on skills. Not a good fit if you want fast pay growth, client-facing work, or are not willing to commit to years of technical writing.

A reality check on pay, hours and tiers

Every figure here is dated and approximate, copied from the research with its source and year. Pay is in local currency and never converted. Tier lists reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact. Three limits apply to almost every page:

  • Asia and Gulf pay across almost all branches rests on aggregators, small self-reported samples (Levels.fyi has 12-40 submissions in some cities) or recruiter blogs. Tokyo is the thinnest.
  • Hours and stress are researchers' estimates from practitioner accounts, prep sites and a few surveys (mainly M&A). No report found a systematic hours survey by role family.
  • Gated recruiter guides (Hays, Robert Walters, Michael Page tables outside Italy, Management Consulted, CIPD, PMI) could not be read. Many pay figures therefore come via secondary summaries, as the reports note.

The full list of gaps, the rating scales and the researchers’ assumptions are on the sources page.
