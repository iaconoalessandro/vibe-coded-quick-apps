What you actually do

Analysts generate long and short ideas, build earnings models, track "what the market expects" versus what is happening, meet companies and experts, and size trades with the PM; in pods, analysts run a sector for a PM and can get their own capital after proving themselves. PMs set the risk budget, trade daily and are measured on profit and loss (P&L), often daily. Event-driven analysts read legal documents; macro people follow central banks and flows; credit analysts model recoveries.

A typical day and week

Start around 6:00-7:00 with news and overnight positions; market open, constant trading and risk checks; earnings calls after close; evenings on models. Friday: weekly P&L review. A bad month tightens the PM and trims analysts' allocation. Strategy notes: fundamental long/short is the slower, research-heavy end; macro and RV are market-driven and episodic; merger arbitrage involves reading deal documents and monitoring regulators; distressed involves months-long legal processes and negotiation.

Hours, stress and lifestyle

55-70 hours typical; 70-80+ in earnings season and market crises (practitioner estimate; no survey found). Stress 5/5 at pods (daily P&L, strict loss limits, quick firing), 4/5 at slower fundamental funds. Mostly in the office. Travel is modest, except for company or expert meetings. The risk is job insecurity rather than raw hours.

Human vs. quantitative profile

People/communication: 3/5. Quantitative/technical: 4/5. You need conviction and the ability to argue with a PM, but numbers, probabilities and risk dominate.

Compensation (approximate)

Survey (almost 3,000 respondents across financial services, 2025 pay, eFinancialCareers Compensation & Lifestyle Report 2026, reported 25 August 2026): hedge fund junior (analyst and associate) average total US$227k; seniors (VP/director) more than three times that; managing directors US$2.2 million; base salaries rarely above US$300k; no breakdown by city. Entry programmes: Millennium's Equities Analyst Program (run with UBS: a first year in UBS equity research, then a Millennium PM team) listed a New York base of US$125,000 in May 2025 (job listing via MIT CDO; I could not open the live page); Point72 Academy summer internship base of US$120-140k prorated (Point72 posting) and a New York Academy listing at about US$125,000 (Indeed, unverified). Mid-career: pod-level estimates of US$300-700k for a New York analyst and US$500k-1.5 million+ for a senior analyst (Quantt Millennium guide, 2026; estimates from public and anecdotal sources); a former Balyasny analyst says senior analysts on a good pod make US$1-3 million in a normal year (X post as relayed by eFinancialCareers; anecdote). PMs commonly receive 12-25% of their P&L; top PMs earn tens of millions; one report says offers above US$100 million have been made to star PMs (Hedgeweek/WSJ, relayed by search; unverified details). Johnson projected hedge fund bonuses +2.5% to 10%+ for 2025 (AI-CIO). Europe, Asia, Gulf: no reliable breakdown found; the pay surveys exist but are paywalled.

Career path

Analyst (0-3 years), senior analyst (3-6), PM or junior PM with own capital (5-10, if at all), senior PM. At single-manager funds the path is analyst, senior analyst, sector head, partner. Most pods work on a "up or out" basis.

Exit opportunities

After 2-3 years: move between funds, go to long-only asset managers (often for lifestyle), PE/growth equity, a corporate role, or business school. After 7-10 years: start your own fund (rarely with outside money now), family-office CIO, allocator, operating roles, or a long-only PM role.

Tier list of employers

Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact. Tier 1: multi-manager platforms (Millennium, Citadel, Balyasny, Point72, ExodusPoint, Schonfeld) and elite single managers (Bridgewater, D. E. Shaw, Elliott, Viking, Marshall Wace, Brevan Howard in Europe). Tier 2: well-known mid-sized fundamental or macro funds, such as Lansdowne, Egerton, Capula, Pharo, Hillhouse in Asia. Tier 3: small, specialist and emerging managers (often under US$1 billion), where resources are thinner and survival risk is higher. Italian hedge fund jobs are few: Milan has some boutiques, but London and Switzerland are the main European hubs (practitioner view; unverified).

How to enter

Graduate programmes are rare but growing at platforms: Point72 Academy is a paid ten-month programme (eight months of training, two of rotations with teams) for students graduating between December 2026 and July 2027, with more than 230 graduates placed as of March 2026 (Point72 posting, via search); Millennium's analyst programme and 2027 summer internships (applications opened 3 August 2026, closed 3 September 2026) asked for a GPA of 3.5+ (listing); Citadel and Citadel Securities took about 350 interns out of more than 115,900 applicants, an acceptance rate of about 0.36% (Fortune via AlphaMaven, undated); Balyasny received over 40,000 applications for about 100 intern places (Pensions & Investments, undated). Most hires still come from sell-side research, investment banking, consulting or long-only funds after two to four years. Degrees: finance, economics, maths, engineering, accounting; STEM is valued at systematic or data-heavy pods. Certifications: CFA is optional; track record matters more. Build: a documented stock-picking record, a published pitch, or a student fund. Common mistakes: pitching only large caps with consensus views, no short idea, ignoring risk. Alternative routes: sell-side research, a credit or restructuring job, or an internal move from a bank's markets desk. Entry difficulty: 5/5.

Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for

Pay is extreme in the best cases and zero in the worst: pods cut people quickly after drawdowns; bonuses depend on a PM you do not control; there are few seats for graduates. March 2026 shows the model can lose money for the largest names. Not for you if you need stability, dislike public daily scoreboards or are not comfortable being wrong in front of colleagues several times a week.

A reality check on pay, hours and tiers

Every figure here is dated and approximate, copied from the research with its source and year. Pay is in local currency and never converted. Tier lists reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact. Three limits apply to almost every page:

  • Asia and Gulf pay across almost all branches rests on aggregators, small self-reported samples (Levels.fyi has 12-40 submissions in some cities) or recruiter blogs. Tokyo is the thinnest.
  • Hours and stress are researchers' estimates from practitioner accounts, prep sites and a few surveys (mainly M&A). No report found a systematic hours survey by role family.
  • Gated recruiter guides (Hays, Robert Walters, Michael Page tables outside Italy, Management Consulted, CIPD, PMI) could not be read. Many pay figures therefore come via secondary summaries, as the reports note.

The full list of gaps, the rating scales and the researchers’ assumptions are on the sources page.
