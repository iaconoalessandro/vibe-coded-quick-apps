What you actually do

Analyse borrowers (usually PE-owned mid-sized companies): model cash flows and leverage, study covenants and legal documents, speak with sponsors and management, write credit memos for the investment committee, negotiate terms with other lenders and lawyers, and monitor the portfolio after funding (quarterly compliance and amendment requests). Special-situations teams do restructuring, rescue financing and distressed purchases; asset-based finance teams analyse pools of receivables or consumer loans and value collateral.

A typical day and week

Review new deals from sponsors, build a model, speak to lawyers about the term sheet, and prepare an IC memo. During a closing, 14-hour days; in monitoring weeks, updating covenants, calling borrowers, and approving amendments. In stressed periods (2026 software loans) much time goes on "watch-list" names.

Hours, stress and lifestyle

50-65 hours; 70-80 around closings (practitioner estimate). Stress 4/5, higher in 2026 with valuation and redemption pressure. Mostly in-office; some travel to sponsors.

Human vs. quantitative profile

People/communication: 3/5. Quantitative/technical: 3/5. Downside analysis, documentation and sponsor relationships matter more than complex maths.

Compensation (approximate)

US: a posted direct lending associate role lists base US$125-150k excluding bonus (Marathon Fund, listing); a recruiter guide gives entry-level base US$90-130k with 30-80% bonus (Wall Street Careers, 2026); a Wall Street Oasis benchmark summary (forum, unverified) puts Tier-1-city associates at about US$300k total (US$150k base plus US$150k bonus) and VPs at about US$505k. Heidrick's first North American private credit survey (297 respondents, 10 June 2025) says carry commonly vests over five years, clawbacks are rare, and deferral is common above the most junior levels (20-30% of bonus). One recruiter blog says a direct-lending principal averages about US$2.9 million of carry over a five-year fund cycle (attributed to Heidrick; not verified). UK/Europe: Dartmouth Partners' 2025 guide has London figures for analyst to director (paywalled) and says Paris and Frankfurt pay is "typically 10-20% lower"; actual numbers: no reliable data found. Italy: no reliable data found (Milan private credit teams are small; Tikehau Capital, for example, advertises a private equity internship in Italy without pay). Johnson projected 2025 private credit bonuses +5-10% (AI-CIO), and a secondary report says -2.5% to -7.5% for 2026 (unverified). Asia/Gulf: no reliable data found.

Career path

Analyst (0-2), associate (2-4), VP (4-7), principal/director (7-10), partner/MD (10+). Carry usually starts around VP/principal.

Exit opportunities

After 2-3 years: another credit fund, PE, restructuring advisory, bank leveraged finance, hedge fund credit. After 7-10 years: partner, head of a strategy, CIO of an insurer's credit book, CFO/treasury roles, start own lending platform.

Tier list of employers

Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact. Tier 1 (large platforms with proprietary origination and insurance capital): Apollo, Ares, Blackstone Credit & Insurance, HPS (now part of BlackRock), Blue Owl, Oaktree, Sixth Street, Golub, Goldman Sachs Asset Management; in Europe ICG, Hayfin, Arcmont, Tikehau, Pemberton. Tier 2: mid-sized specialist lenders and the credit arms of PE and asset managers (Barings, Churchill, Muzinich, Marathon). Tier 3: small, regional and BDC-style lenders, where deal flow is less proprietary.

How to enter

Hired mainly from leveraged finance, DCM (debt capital markets), restructuring and credit research at banks, and from Big 4 transaction services; some firms hire analysts directly from undergraduate programmes (e.g., Ares and large platforms' analyst classes, check each firm's site). In Europe, internships at Tikehau or Muzinich in Milan, Paris and London are a way in. Certifications: CFA is respected for credit; the CAIA is a secondary signal for alternatives. Skills: credit analysis, loan documents, covenant logic, financial modelling. Common mistakes: treating credit like equity (upside) rather than downside protection; ignoring legal documents. Alternative routes: bank credit risk, rating agencies, restructuring and insolvency practice. Entry difficulty: 4/5.

Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for

The asset class is in its first major test since scale; if defaults rise, the junior staff of weaker platforms are the first to be cut (headhunter: jobs down up to 25% in 2024). Upside is capped by the loan's interest rate, so pay depends on fees and scale. Not for you if you dislike legal documents, prefer fast-moving equity stories, or dislike the monitoring of problem loans.

A reality check on pay, hours and tiers

Every figure here is dated and approximate, copied from the research with its source and year. Pay is in local currency and never converted. Tier lists reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact. Three limits apply to almost every page:

  • Asia and Gulf pay across almost all branches rests on aggregators, small self-reported samples (Levels.fyi has 12-40 submissions in some cities) or recruiter blogs. Tokyo is the thinnest.
  • Hours and stress are researchers' estimates from practitioner accounts, prep sites and a few surveys (mainly M&A). No report found a systematic hours survey by role family.
  • Gated recruiter guides (Hays, Robert Walters, Michael Page tables outside Italy, Management Consulted, CIPD, PMI) could not be read. Many pay figures therefore come via secondary summaries, as the reports note.

The full list of gaps, the rating scales and the researchers’ assumptions are on the sources page.
