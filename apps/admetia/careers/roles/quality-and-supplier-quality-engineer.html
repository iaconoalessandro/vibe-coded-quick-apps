What you actually do

You keep products and bought-in parts within specification: write inspection plans, analyse defects and customer complaints to their root cause, audit production processes and suppliers, approve new parts before production, and maintain the quality-management system for certification. Prospects lists the core as setting procedures and specifications, monitoring performance with statistical reports, leading lean and Six Sigma improvement and ensuring compliance with standards such as ISO 9001:2015 (December 2024). Supplier-quality engineers do this at suppliers' plants: JLR's Supplier Technical Assistance team answers for supplier quality across its vehicles (JLR, 2026). US occupational data file "quality engineer" and "supplier quality engineer" under validation engineers, a branch of industrial engineering (O*NET, 2025). Tools: statistical process control, root-cause methods, measurement equipment, audit checklists. Example: a customer returns brackets with cracked welds; you quarantine the stock, trace the batch to one welding cell, find a worn fixture, agree the fix with production and send the customer the corrective-action report.

A typical day and week

Sourced tasks at officer level: inspecting and testing products and processes, checking that standards are met, recording and reporting findings, updating quality procedures and assessing the effect of process changes (National Careers Service, quality control officer, read October 2026). An engineer's week adds the morning review of yesterday's defects, a containment action on the line, a call or visit to a supplier about a failed batch, an internal audit and a customer report (illustrative; not sourced).

Hours, stress and lifestyle

  • Hours: 37-42 a week, with evenings and weekends on shifts (National Careers Service). Prospects says early starts, late finishes and shift work are common in manufacturing; BLS notes overtime to meet production deadlines. No figure for incident weeks was found.
  • Stress: 3/5 (my judgement).
  • Lifestyle: on site. Supplier-quality roles travel to suppliers; how often: no reliable data found.

Human vs. quantitative profile

People/communication: 3/5. Quantitative/technical: 4/5. The technical side is statistics (process capability, sampling, measurement error) and reading drawings; the people side is telling a production manager or a supplier that a batch does not ship.

Compensation (approximate)

  • UK: JLR's graduate programme, which contains the supplier-quality pathway, pays GBP 32,000 plus a GBP 2,000 joining bonus, then GBP 39,000 after two years (2026 intake, closed). Toyota Manufacturing UK graduates start on GBP 34,756 plus overtime (no separate quality scheme is listed). Outside schemes: quality control officers earn GBP 25,000 as starters and GBP 40,000 when experienced (National Careers Service); quality managers start at GBP 23,000-28,000 and can pass GBP 60,000 in manufacturing, aerospace and automotive (Prospects, December 2024).
  • US: industrial engineers, the group that contains quality engineers, had a median of US$102,440 in May 2025, with the lowest 10% under US$74,370 (BLS; not an entry figure). Quality control inspectors, the job that needs no degree, had a median of US$48,570. ASQ's 2025 salary survey is for members only and could not be opened.
  • Italy: no entry survey for this role was found; the metalworking floors and AlmaLaurea averages in 3.8 apply.

Career path

Quality technician or engineer (0-3 years) → senior or supplier-quality engineer (3-6) → quality manager (6-10) → head of quality or plant management. The ladder follows Prospects, which says appointments reach board level; the years are my estimate.

Exit opportunities

  • After 2-3 years: procurement (supplier development), production management, continuous improvement (3.8).
  • After 7-10 years: quality director, plant manager, certification bodies and auditing, self-employed consultancy (Prospects).

Tier list of employers

  • Tier 1: automotive, aerospace, medical-device and pharmaceutical manufacturers, where quality is regulated or audited by the customer: JLR and Toyota (verified); Rolls-Royce, Airbus, Bosch, Brembo and Leonardo (general knowledge; not verified).
  • Tier 2: food, consumer-goods and electronics manufacturers.
  • Tier 3: small suppliers with a single quality person.
  • Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact.

How to enter

  • Degrees: for the engineer title, a STEM degree in practice. JLR's supplier-quality pathway takes a 2.2 in engineering, materials science, manufacturing or any STEM discipline, and does not sponsor visas; its manufacturing engineering pathway takes engineering degrees only; 90% of O*NET respondents say a bachelor's degree is required. Quality management more widely is open to all graduates according to Prospects, which prefers engineering, food science, applied science or business with quality modules. Business and economics graduates therefore enter through quality-systems, audit and food or service-sector assurance roles, not as engineers.
  • Certifications: these come later. ASQ's Certified Quality Engineer requires eight years of experience, of which a bachelor's degree waives four and a master's five (exam fee US$550; ASQ, read October 2026). The CQI's practitioner grade needs two years of quality work and 30 hours of training (Prospects; the CQI's own page could not be opened). Auditor courses for ISO 9001 or IATF 16949 help (general knowledge; not sourced here).
  • Common mistakes: expecting design work; quality is about process discipline.
  • Non-target routes: start as a quality control assistant or technician, or take the UK Level 4 Quality Practitioner apprenticeship (National Careers Service).
  • Entry difficulty: 2/5.

Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for

You stop shipments other departments want to send, and the job is noticed mainly when something fails. Shifts follow production. Inspection itself is being automated: BLS expects 3% growth for quality control inspectors from 2025 to 2035 as technology takes over inspection tasks, so the secure work is in analysis, supplier management and audits. Many starting posts expect prior quality or industry experience (Prospects). Poor fit: people who avoid conflict or dislike documentation.

A reality check on pay, hours and tiers

Every figure here is dated and approximate, copied from the research with its source and year. Pay is in local currency and never converted. Tier lists reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact. Three limits apply to almost every page:

  • Asia and Gulf pay across almost all branches rests on aggregators, small self-reported samples (Levels.fyi has 12-40 submissions in some cities) or recruiter blogs. Tokyo is the thinnest.
  • Hours and stress are researchers' estimates from practitioner accounts, prep sites and a few surveys (mainly M&A). No report found a systematic hours survey by role family.
  • Gated recruiter guides (Hays, Robert Walters, Michael Page tables outside Italy, Management Consulted, CIPD, PMI) could not be read. Many pay figures therefore come via secondary summaries, as the reports note.

The full list of gaps, the rating scales and the researchers’ assumptions are on the sources page.
