What you actually do

Policy, programme or operational management in government or NGOs: draft briefings and policy options, manage budgets and grants, run service-delivery teams, handle ministerial or board questions, and monitor funded programmes. Tools: Word/Excel/PowerPoint, case-management systems, grant-management and monitoring frameworks.

A typical day and week

Meetings with stakeholders, writing and clearing briefings through several approval layers, managing a team's casework, and responding to parliamentary or press queries. Hours are mostly regular.

Hours, stress and lifestyle

  • Hours: 37-45 per week; peaks 50+ around budgets, crises or elections (practitioner consensus).
  • Stress: 2/5 typically (3/5 for politically exposed posts or field NGOs).
  • Lifestyle: stable; hybrid common in the UK and EU institutions; field NGOs involve deployment.

Human vs. quantitative profile

People/communication: 3/5 to 4/5. Quantitative/technical: 2/5. Writing, process knowledge and stakeholder management matter more than modelling.

Compensation (approximate)

  • UK Fast Stream: first-year £31,554 and fourth-year £40,098 in 2025-26 (Institute for Government); a 2026-27 award was not found. The 2026 campaign was expected to open mid-October 2026 and run for four weeks (Commons written answer, May 2026); start dates in September of the following year. Eligibility is restricted by nationality (see below).
  • EU institutions: a new AD5 official earns a basic €6,152.64 gross per month at step 1, about €7,137 with the 16% expatriation allowance in Brussels or Luxembourg (pay table in force from 1 July 2025, via redopositor.com; press quotes €5,000-€5,400, so the figure is secondary). Blue Book traineeship grant €1,538.16 a month (25% of AD5 step 1, per research/careers/public-policy-and-academia.md).
  • Italy: no reliable data found on funzionario pay for the 2026 concorsi (the Ministry of Economy and Finance sought 485 funzionari in a concorso that closed 27 Jan 2026, per the public-policy file; secondary).
  • US federal and NGOs: no reliable data found (not researched this pass).

Career path

UK Fast Stream: scheme years 1-4 → Grade 7 / deputy director in about 10-12 years (general knowledge; not sourced here). EU: AD5 → AD7/AD8 in 8-12 years → head of unit. NGOs: officer → programme manager → country director.

Exit opportunities

  • After 2-3 years: another ministry, consultancy, regulators, think tanks, large corporates' public affairs.
  • After 7-10 years: head of unit/director, international organisations, consulting in the public sector, social enterprises.

Tier list of employers

  • Tier 1: Fast Stream, EU institutions (Commission, Parliament, ECB), UN family via YPP or JPO, central banks (Banca d'Italia: 45 Esperti places, 8,307 applications in Dec 2025 competition, per the public-policy file).
  • Tier 2: national ministries and agencies, major INGOs, development banks.
  • Tier 3: local government, small NGOs, charities.
  • Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact.

How to enter

  • Degrees: any, with a 2:2 or above for most Fast Stream schemes; EPSO AD5 requires a three-year degree, reasoning tests, an EU-knowledge test and an essay; Italian concorsi require a laurea and written tests.
  • Programmes: UK Fast Stream (generalist and specialist schemes); EPSO AD5 competition (EPSO/AD/427/26: 174,727 applicants, 1,490 reserve-list places, Italy 45% of applicants; reasoning tests planned from November 2026); Blue Book traineeship (five months, about 1,000 places per session); contract-agent database.
  • Nationality: the Fast Stream is effectively closed to EU graduates who arrived after 2020 unless they have settled or pre-settled status (Civil Service Careers, as noted in the public-policy file).
  • Calendar: Fast Stream applications open each autumn (2025: 10 October); EPSO competitions are announced irregularly.
  • Assessment: online tests, e-tray exercises, assessment centre (Fast Stream); multiple-choice reasoning for EPSO.
  • Certifications: not required; PRINCE2 or APM can help for programme-delivery roles.
  • Common mistakes: treating AD5 as a safe plan (about 0.85% selection); leaving no fall-back.
  • Non-target routes: Blue Book traineeship, contract agents, NGOs' entry roles, temporary civil-service roles, local government graduate schemes.
  • Entry difficulty: 5/5 for Fast Stream and AD5; 2-3/5 for local government and many NGOs.

Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for

Lower pay than the private sector, slow decisions, political exposure, and long selection cycles. NGOs often pay little and rely on short grants. Poor fit: people who need fast promotion, high pay or clear individual attribution of results.

A reality check on pay, hours and tiers

Every figure here is dated and approximate, copied from the research with its source and year. Pay is in local currency and never converted. Tier lists reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact. Three limits apply to almost every page:

  • Asia and Gulf pay across almost all branches rests on aggregators, small self-reported samples (Levels.fyi has 12-40 submissions in some cities) or recruiter blogs. Tokyo is the thinnest.
  • Hours and stress are researchers' estimates from practitioner accounts, prep sites and a few surveys (mainly M&A). No report found a systematic hours survey by role family.
  • Gated recruiter guides (Hays, Robert Walters, Michael Page tables outside Italy, Management Consulted, CIPD, PMI) could not be read. Many pay figures therefore come via secondary summaries, as the reports note.

The full list of gaps, the rating scales and the researchers’ assumptions are on the sources page.
