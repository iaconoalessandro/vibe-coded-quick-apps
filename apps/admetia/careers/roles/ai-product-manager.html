What you actually do

You define what an AI product should do and how to measure it: owning strategy, roadmap and delivery for a product area, turning model capabilities into usable experiences with metrics, experiments and research, and working with design, engineering, data science, research and marketing. Distinctive AI-PM work: defining evals that stand for product quality, deciding when a model is "good enough", managing cost per request, handling prompt and model changes, working with safety teams on misuse (OpenAI lists PMs for multimodal safety and integrity; Scale AI for cybersecurity and finance agents; Anthropic for Safeguards and Public Sector), and shaping what research should build next.

A typical day and week

User and data review; writing a product requirements document with eval criteria; syncing with engineers and researchers; reviewing launch readiness with safety teams; customer calls; prioritisation meetings. Weekly rhythm: launch planning, metrics reviews, and unscheduled escalations when model behaviour changes.

Hours, stress and lifestyle

Typical 45-55 hours, peaks 60-70 around launches (estimate). Stress 3-4/5: fast-changing technology, high visibility, and launch deadlines. Hybrid at big tech; frontier-lab and AI-startup roles are mostly on-site in San Francisco, New York or Seattle (Cohere's PM roles list Toronto). Travel: low to moderate.

Human vs. quantitative profile

People/communication: 5/5. Quantitative/technical: 3/5. You need to read experiment results and understand model limitations, but the main skill is alignment of people and judgment.

  • Levels.fyi (9 October 2026), US total comp: all employers median $230,000 (25th $169,000; 75th $330,000; 90th $450,000); Google median $402,000 (L4 $308,000, L5 $391,000, L6 $588,000); Meta E4 $265,000, E5 $418,000, E6 $674,000; Microsoft median $253,000; Anthropic $407,000; OpenAI $750,000; Palantir $180,000. Career path

Associate PM / technical program manager (0-3 years) -> product manager (2-5) -> senior PM (5-8) -> group PM or head of product (8-12) -> VP/Chief Product Officer. Also: engineers, data scientists and consultants lateral into AI PM after 3-6 years.

Exit opportunities

After 2-3 years: PM roles in other tech companies, startup product leads. After 7-10 years: head of product, founder, general manager, venture capital.

Tier list of employers (alphabetical within each tier)

  • Tier 1: Frontier labs (Anthropic, Google DeepMind, OpenAI), Google and Microsoft AI products, Meta.
  • Tier 2: AI-native startups (Cohere, Mistral, Perplexity-type companies; general knowledge), Adobe, Databricks, NVIDIA, Salesforce, Scale AI.
  • Tier 3: AI product teams at banks, pharma, consultancies and enterprise software firms.

Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact.

How to enter

Rarely a first job at a lab (Anthropic's PM, Claude posting prefers 8+ years and products with millions of users), but associate-PM doors exist: Databricks' 2027 new-graduate APM and Meta's rotational PM level. Typical routes: associate PM programmes at big tech, engineer-to-PM, data scientist-to-PM, consultant-to-PM, startup founder. Degrees: any; computer science, engineering and business dominate; an MBA helps at some companies. Skills: designing evaluations, writing a spec with acceptance criteria for probabilistic behaviour, using LLM APIs, understanding cost and latency. Portfolio: shipped side projects, a teardown of an AI product, a prototype with an evaluation set. Interviews: product sense, execution, analytics, behavioural and technical-fluency. Certifications: little weight. Common mistakes: confusing demos with products; neglecting evals and safety; chasing hype over user needs. Alternative routes: technical program managers and customer success/solutions staff moving into product; non-target-school candidates through startup experience. Entry difficulty: 4/5 at frontier labs and top big tech; 3/5 elsewhere.

Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for

Roadmaps get overturned when models change; you are accountable for outcomes you do not control; and AI PM is a crowded aspiration, so many candidates compete for few roles. Not a fit for people who need stable, long-horizon plans, or who dislike ambiguity about technical feasibility. Not a fit for those who want to do the technical work themselves.

A reality check on pay, hours and tiers

Every figure here is dated and approximate, copied from the research with its source and year. Pay is in local currency and never converted. Tier lists reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact. Three limits apply to almost every page:

  • Asia and Gulf pay across almost all branches rests on aggregators, small self-reported samples (Levels.fyi has 12-40 submissions in some cities) or recruiter blogs. Tokyo is the thinnest.
  • Hours and stress are researchers' estimates from practitioner accounts, prep sites and a few surveys (mainly M&A). No report found a systematic hours survey by role family.
  • Gated recruiter guides (Hays, Robert Walters, Michael Page tables outside Italy, Management Consulted, CIPD, PMI) could not be read. Many pay figures therefore come via secondary summaries, as the reports note.

The full list of gaps, the rating scales and the researchers’ assumptions are on the sources page.
