What you actually do

Run a shift or a department of a warehouse or fulfilment centre: staffing, safety, picking and packing productivity, inbound and outbound flow, inventory accuracy, and customer service levels. You use a WMS, labour management dashboards and daily KPIs, and you lead operatives, often through supervisors. Example: a pick-path jam delays outbound trailers; you re-allocate 12 people, change the batching and clear the backlog before the trucks leave.

A typical day and week

Shift start briefing and safety talk; walk the floor, review KPIs; solve problems (equipment faults, absenteeism, damage); meet with the next shift. On a 4-on, 3-off or 12-hour pattern; peak season (November-December, the pre-Christmas "Q4 peak") means extra shifts and compulsory overtime.

Hours, stress and lifestyle

45-55 hours on shift patterns; 60+ in peak; nights and weekends common. Amazon postings describe on-site work, 10-12 hours of standing and walking per day, some roles 12-hour shifts, 3x2 pattern in Brazil, 4-on/3-off in Australia (Amazon postings, 2025-26). Stress: 4/5. No remote work. Travel is limited to the site (relocation is common); UK programmes expect mobility and a driving licence (DHL, K+N).

Human vs. quantitative profile

People/communication: 5/5. Quantitative/technical: 2/5. It is leadership of a large front-line workforce; the numeracy is KPIs, rate calculations and basic process analysis.

Compensation (approximate)

  • US: Amazon Area Manager listing in Portland US$70,000-90,900 (2025 listing, one data point); Amazon Canada Area Manager graduate program C$69,000 base plus sign-on and equity (2025). Senior operations manager US$142,688-182,700 (Patterson, California listing). Third-party aggregators vary widely (US$60,000-85,000), and are unreliable. BLS warehouse/logistics-sector median for logisticians US$63,530 (transportation & warehousing, May 2025) is a different job family.
  • UK: DHL Supply Chain graduate GBP 30,500 (2027 start); K+N contract logistics graduates around GBP 28,500 (project support listing, 2026); experienced logistics/distribution managers GBP 41,000-65,000 (Prospects). Amazon UK area manager pay: no reliable data found.
  • Italy: no reliable data found for graduate-level warehouse managers. Operational logistics clerks EUR 22,000-28,000 (Randstad listing).
  • Singapore: no reliable data found.

Career path

Shift supervisor / area manager (0-2) → operations manager / site department head (2-5) → general manager of a site (5-9) → regional operations director (9-14) → VP operations. Transfers into continuous improvement, planning, or network design are common.

Exit opportunities

After 2-3 years: continuous improvement, planning, transport management, or retail operations. After 7-10 years: site general manager, regional operations director, supply chain director in a retailer, consulting (operations), or operations lead in start-ups or quick-commerce.

Tier list of employers

Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact.

  • Tier 1 (best structured training, brand value, scale): Amazon operations (high scale and volume, exposure to robotics, but tough hours), DHL Supply Chain, Kuehne+Nagel contract logistics, GXO, big grocery chains' DCs, P&G/Unilever manufacturing distribution.
  • Tier 2: Regional 3PLs, fashion and retail distribution (Zalando, Inditex), pharma logistics.
  • Tier 3: Small 3PLs, local distributors; pay and training weaker, autonomy higher.

How to enter

Any degree (logistics, business, engineering) or none; the key hiring signal is leadership, resilience and shift flexibility. Programmes: Amazon Area Manager programme (entry-level, on-site role), DHL Supply Chain graduate programme (any degree, no minimum grade, mobile across UK sites), Maersk contract logistics management graduate programme (logistics, business or related degree, driving licence; 2026 intake closed in May), Kuehne+Nagel graduate programmes. Calendar: Amazon, DHL and 3PLs hire year-round with some autumn graduate cycles. Certifications: not required; lean or Six Sigma yellow/green belt helps; IOSH/NEBOSH-type safety certificates are valued in the UK (unverified); CILT for later career. Skills: Excel, KPI reading, basic WMS, leadership. Common mistakes: underestimating physical and shift demands; thinking this is an office job. Alternative routes: team leader from the floor with part-time study; military logistics veterans are well regarded (unverified). Entry difficulty: 2/5.

Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for

Long shifts, nights and weekends; heavy exposure to injuries, strikes and burnout; automation reduces the number of operative roles, and Amazon-style productivity metrics are controversial (unions are critical, reported). Not a good fit for people who want desk-based, predictable hours or who dislike being on their feet.

A reality check on pay, hours and tiers

Every figure here is dated and approximate, copied from the research with its source and year. Pay is in local currency and never converted. Tier lists reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact. Three limits apply to almost every page:

  • Asia and Gulf pay across almost all branches rests on aggregators, small self-reported samples (Levels.fyi has 12-40 submissions in some cities) or recruiter blogs. Tokyo is the thinnest.
  • Hours and stress are researchers' estimates from practitioner accounts, prep sites and a few surveys (mainly M&A). No report found a systematic hours survey by role family.
  • Gated recruiter guides (Hays, Robert Walters, Michael Page tables outside Italy, Management Consulted, CIPD, PMI) could not be read. Many pay figures therefore come via secondary summaries, as the reports note.

The full list of gaps, the rating scales and the researchers’ assumptions are on the sources page.
