What you actually do

You take positions, using the fund's capital, based on your judgement of macro, rates, FX, credit, commodities or equities. A junior "analyst" or "associate trader" at a macro or multi-manager fund supports a PM by building trade ideas, running risk, doing data work and executing; a PM runs their own book with risk limits and a P&L target. A discretionary macro trader reads central banks, economic data, positioning and flows and then expresses views through futures, swaps, options and FX. Pod-shop PMs usually have strict stop-loss limits; macro funds (Brevan Howard, Rokos, Caxton, Soros-type) allow larger views. Tools: Bloomberg, in-house risk tools, Python, execution through banks.

A typical day and week

Early start tracking Asia and Europe; reviewing positions and risk; research on themes; calls with sell-side salespeople and strategists; trades around data releases and central bank meetings; end-of-day risk review. Weeks revolve around data, central bank meetings and position management.

Hours, stress and lifestyle

Hours: 55-70 typical; 70+ in stress periods (practitioner consensus; no survey). Stress: 5/5: your P&L is your livelihood and hitting a drawdown limit can end the seat quickly. Pay is variable. Lifestyle: office-based (funds are hybrid for analysts, but PMs sit near risk), London, New York, Greenwich, Hong Kong, Singapore, Dubai and Geneva hubs.

Human vs. quantitative profile

People/communication: 2/5. Quantitative/technical: 4/5. The job is mostly decision-making under uncertainty and risk management; the network (to sell-side and to talk to) matters, but it is a solo-ish performance role.

Compensation (approximate)

  • US: Selby Jennings (2024): fund PM base US$200-300k, payout 10-20% of P&L; quant trader at a prop/HFT firm base US$150-200k and 30-50% of P&L. Top hedge fund quant PhD hires US$300-750k total. Johnson Associates (May 2026) projects hedge fund incentives +2.5% to 10%+ in 2026 with wide variation; macro, commodities, prop and quant "outperforming," event-driven under; a "talent war for star PMs" and growing pass-through fees pushing pay up.
  • UK: Selby Jennings (2024): fund PM base £120-250k, payout 5-20% of P&L.
  • Asia, Italy: no reliable data found (Italy has few discretionary macro funds). Hong Kong/Singapore quant researcher bands in the 2024 guide are US$120-240k total for top hedge funds with PhDs, not typical for discretionary roles.
  • Entry level: junior analyst seats at hedge funds are usually filled by people with 1-4 years at a bank, so there is no "entry" salary table; a London buy-side execution role was advertised at £70-90k and mid-to-senior traders at hedge funds at £150-250k in job ads (search results; unverified).

Career path

Typical path: bank analyst/associate (2-5 years in sales, trading or research) → hedge fund analyst or junior trader (2-4 years) → PM (with risk capital) → senior PM / CIO. Others start in prop trading or at a pod shop. Many do not make PM: seats are scarce and the attrition rate is high. eFinancialCareers' 2026 analysis of 15 star traders from 2016 found about a third had moved to hedge funds as senior PMs or unit heads; only four of the 15 still worked in banking (23 February 2026; small sample). In credit and distressed, bank MDs have moved to Verition and Schonfeld (same source).

Exit opportunities

After 2-3 years: another fund, a different strategy, the sell side, a prop firm or long-only manager. After 7-10 years: run your own fund (rare), family office CIO, senior PM at larger multi-manager, asset management or advisory, or leave finance. Non-competes in Europe can force 6-12 months (or up to three years in extreme cases) of gardening leave, according to Selby Jennings (2024).

Tier list of employers

Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact.

  • Tier 1 (multi-manager and top macro funds): Millennium, Citadel, Point72, Balyasny, Brevan Howard, Rokos, Caxton, Bridgewater (systematic-macro hybrid), Soros Fund Management. Differences: capital, risk infrastructure, pay upside, strict limits.
  • Tier 2: Schonfeld, Verition, ExodusPoint, Marshall Wace (macro and equity), Haidar, Qube (systematic), regional macro funds.
  • Tier 3: smaller funds, family offices, emerging managers in Dubai, Hong Kong, Singapore, London.

How to enter

There is almost no direct graduate entry into discretionary PM roles. The route is: a strong sell-side desk seat (Tier 1 or Tier 2), strong P&L or an idea-generating track record, relationships with funds (via sales), and a headhunter. Some funds hire graduates as analysts (smaller intake; often via referral) or from investment banking/research. Skills: market view, risk discipline, scenario thinking, Python for analysis, and the ability to run a track record. Certifications: none required; CFA is helpful for fundamental equity. Mistakes: leaving a bank desk too early; building no P&L record; ignoring risk management. Alternative routes: prop trading, personal-account track record (rare), starting at a multi-manager as an analyst after a research or quant background. Entry difficulty: 5/5.

Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for

High attrition; careers hinge on performance and capital allocators' patience; the 2025-26 pod-shop market has strict drawdown limits and long non-competes; compensation concentrates in a few stars; moves cost months on gardening leave; and Johnson Associates flags that AI firms and proprietary trading firms are competing for quantitative talent. Not a good fit if you want stable pay, work-life balance, or if you are uncomfortable with a personal P&L; also not a fit if you want a team environment or have little tolerance for losses.

A reality check on pay, hours and tiers

Every figure here is dated and approximate, copied from the research with its source and year. Pay is in local currency and never converted. Tier lists reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact. Three limits apply to almost every page:

  • Asia and Gulf pay across almost all branches rests on aggregators, small self-reported samples (Levels.fyi has 12-40 submissions in some cities) or recruiter blogs. Tokyo is the thinnest.
  • Hours and stress are researchers' estimates from practitioner accounts, prep sites and a few surveys (mainly M&A). No report found a systematic hours survey by role family.
  • Gated recruiter guides (Hays, Robert Walters, Michael Page tables outside Italy, Management Consulted, CIPD, PMI) could not be read. Many pay figures therefore come via secondary summaries, as the reports note.

The full list of gaps, the rating scales and the researchers’ assumptions are on the sources page.
