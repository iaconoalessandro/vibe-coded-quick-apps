What you actually do

Work in a team for a client on a project of weeks to months: analyse inventory, design a distribution network, reduce procurement spend, redesign an S&OP process, assess tariff exposure, or implement a planning system. Deliverables are models, slide decks and implementation plans. For detail on firms, hours and recruiting see the management consulting report.

A typical day and week

Client site or video workshops, analysis in Excel/Alteryx/Python, slides for steering committees, weekly status review. Travel to client sites 3-4 days a week is common in the UK, Germany and the US; Italy and Gulf projects are often local or regional (management consulting report).

Hours, stress and lifestyle

50-60 hours typical, 60-70 at deadlines (practitioner norms; see management consulting report); stress 4/5; travel heavy; remote only partly.

Human vs. quantitative profile

People/communication: 4/5. Quantitative/technical: 4/5.

Compensation (approximate)

  • US: Big 4 consulting analyst base US$78-112k and Accenture Strategy about US$105k (Road to Offer / PrepLounge 2026, via the management consulting report); operations-specific figures not separated.
  • UK: Big 4 and Accenture graduate base roughly GBP 32-37k, Capgemini Invent GBP 51k (PrepLounge UK 2026, via the management consulting report).
  • Italy: no reliable data found; indicative EUR 26-30k at Deloitte/Accenture Italy (Indeed Italy, self-reported, management consulting report).
  • Senior: Big 4 manager US$105-150k base; UK senior consultant/manager GBP 92-125k (management consulting report). Supply-chain-specific bands: no reliable data found.

Career path

Analyst/consultant (0-2) → senior consultant (2-4) → manager (4-7) → senior manager/director (7-10) → partner. Many leave before manager level.

Exit opportunities

After 2-3 years: supply chain roles in industry (planning, procurement, strategy), MBA. After 7-10 years: head of supply chain or transformation at a corporate, private-equity operations partner, or partner track.

Tier list of employers

Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact.

  • Tier 1: McKinsey, BCG, Bain operations practices, Kearney (supply chain strength; unverified ranking).
  • Tier 2: Big 4 operations practices, Accenture Operations, Capgemini Invent, Alvarez & Marsal, AlixPartners.
  • Tier 3: Boutique planning/ERP consultancies and local firms (BIP, Reply in Italy; see the management consulting report).

How to enter

Engineering, business, economics, supply chain; a master's helps; relevant internship in planning or procurement. Programmes and calendar: see the management consulting report (UK Big 4 recruitment is rolling, opening around September with deadlines in spring). Certifications: ASCM CSCP is a modest plus; no certification is needed at strategy firms. Skills: Excel/PowerPoint, structured problem solving, supply chain fundamentals, SQL/Alteryx. Common mistakes: applying only to MBB; treating supply chain as a purely technical subject. Alternative routes: 2-3 years in industry planning or procurement, then lateral hire into an operations practice; technology-implementation roles at integrators. Entry difficulty: 4/5 for MBB, 3/5 for Big 4/Accenture operations practices.

Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for

Long hours and travel, an up-or-out culture, work on slides rather than operations, and a slowdown since 2023 with AI changing junior work (management consulting report). Not a good fit for people who want to own a real operation, need stable hours, or dislike presenting.

A reality check on pay, hours and tiers

Every figure here is dated and approximate, copied from the research with its source and year. Pay is in local currency and never converted. Tier lists reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact. Three limits apply to almost every page:

  • Asia and Gulf pay across almost all branches rests on aggregators, small self-reported samples (Levels.fyi has 12-40 submissions in some cities) or recruiter blogs. Tokyo is the thinnest.
  • Hours and stress are researchers' estimates from practitioner accounts, prep sites and a few surveys (mainly M&A). No report found a systematic hours survey by role family.
  • Gated recruiter guides (Hays, Robert Walters, Michael Page tables outside Italy, Management Consulted, CIPD, PMI) could not be read. Many pay figures therefore come via secondary summaries, as the reports note.

The full list of gaps, the rating scales and the researchers’ assumptions are on the sources page.
