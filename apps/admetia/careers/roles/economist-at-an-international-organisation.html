What you actually do

IMF country economists draft parts of Article IV reports, a yearly assessment of a member country's economy, and estimate fiscal, monetary and external-sector scenarios; the IMF Economist Program rotates PhDs through country surveillance and programmes, policy analysis and research for flagship publications (IMF EP page, October 2026). World Bank economists write country economic memoranda, evaluate project impact and advise governments on reforms. OECD economists draft country surveys and statistical series. Tools: Stata, R, Python, spreadsheets and macro models; deliverables are reports, briefing notes and missions where you meet ministry officials.

A typical day and week

Illustrative composite. Weeks at HQ: data work, drafting a chapter and internal review meetings; mission weeks (one to two weeks, several times a year) mean travel to the member country for meetings with the finance ministry and central bank. A deadline-heavy "report week" can run late.

Hours, stress and lifestyle

Typical 40-50 hours, peaks 55-60 during missions and report deadlines (author judgement). Stress 3/5. Travel is substantial for IMF/World Bank (several missions a year; the IMF programme specifically includes surveillance missions) and lower at the OECD. Remote/hybrid is common but HQ-based. IO staff often live abroad on expatriate benefits.

Human vs. quantitative profile

People/communication: 4/5. Quantitative/technical: 4/5. You must negotiate with ministry officials and convey politically sensitive messages, not only run models.

Compensation (approximate)

IMF: the most recent public salary table I could retrieve is dated 1 May 2017: entry-level PhD economists sit at grade A11 (USD 95,090-142,610), economists at A12-A13 (A12 USD 108,670-163,000; A13 USD 124,620-186,950); these are old and current pay is higher (IMF Annual Report Web Table 3.4, 2017; 2025 table not retrievable). IMF salaries are generally not subject to national income tax and carry an internal staff assessment; I could not confirm current detail (unverified, general knowledge). World Bank: net-of-tax scales; US headquarters, effective 1 July 2023: GF USD 101,700-189,000 (midpoint 145,300), GG USD 131,900-245,000 (midpoint 188,500), GH USD 177,600-330,000 (World Bank Group net salary scale, 2023). FY27 scales exist but the PDF was unreadable (World Bank FY27 scales, 2026). Economists enter around GF (YPP is GF-level). OECD: Young Associates EUR 3,886 per month exempt from French income tax, plus leaving allowance around EUR 21,000 (OECD YAP page via Wayback capture of 1 September 2026, library research); senior OECD economist pay: no reliable data found. EU institutions: AD5 basic EUR 6,152.64 per month from 1 July 2025 (Council doc 16220/25, library research); net about EUR 4,859 with no allowances and EUR 5,870 with expatriation allowance (AMLA worked example, a Frankfurt EU agency; Brussels may differ by a few per cent). EIB: graduate programme at the minimum of grade level 3; EUR 56,125 reported by a search snippet (unverified). Italy: Italian JPO (Junior Professional Officer, state-funded) pay is set by the host organisation: no reliable data found. ~5 years and senior: IMF grades A12-A13 (2017 bands above) for economists and A14+ for division chiefs.

Career path

Research analyst or intern (0-2 years) -> Economist (PhD entrant, IMF Economist Program, 3 years) -> Senior Economist (5-8) -> Division/unit chief or advisor (10-15) -> Director. World Bank YPP: graduate degree plus 2-6 years' experience, two-year programme with three 8-month rotations, then a role at least a further 5 years on completion (World Bank YPP page, October 2026, via library research).

Exit opportunities

After 2-3 years: PhD, ministries, central banks, consulting. After 7-10 years: senior posts in governments (finance ministry, central bank), regional banks, private-sector chief economist roles, academia.

Tier list of employers

Tier 1: IMF, World Bank, OECD (policy influence, high selectivity). Tier 2: regional development banks (EIB, EBRD, ADB, IDB, AfDB), BIS, specialised UN bodies (UNCTAD, UNDP economics units). Tier 3: smaller UN agencies, NGOs and consultancies working on IO projects. Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact.

How to enter

Degrees: PhD for IMF Economist Program (recent PhD or within one year of completing, member-country national, under 34 when joining, apply by 4 December 2026 for September 2027 start; three-year programme with two 18-month assignments); bachelor's for IMF Research Analyst Program (recent bachelor's, GPA at least 3.5/4.0, two-year contract in Washington, apply by 30 December 2026) and OECD Young Associates (bachelor's only, no master's or PhD allowed, OECD-member nationality, GPA 3.0/4.0, 8-12 participants a year, applications Nov-Dec); master's plus 2-6 years for World Bank YPP (2027 cohort window was 1-30 September 2026, which has closed; start September 2027). UN YPP: bachelor's accepted, age 32 or younger, but only nationals of listed countries; Italy was not on the 2026 list (careers.un.org, October 2026). Italians can use the state-funded JPO programme (45 Italian places in 2026, born 1 January 1995 or later, master's; deadline was 5 May 2026). Interview formats: IMF EP has a preliminary interview, paper submission with three references, panel interview and written assessment; offers in February. Skills: macro and public finance, econometrics, writing, languages. Common mistakes: assuming a master's can enter the Economist Program (it cannot); missing narrow annual windows; ignoring nationality rules. Alternative routes: IMF RAP, OECD internships (about EUR 1,000 per month), EU Blue Book traineeships, development-bank graduate programmes (EIB GRAD, under two years since graduation), national secondments. Entry difficulty: 5/5 (Economist Program, YPP; no published acceptance rates).

Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for

Extremely selective with narrow windows and nationality gates; IO pay is high but the cost of living in Washington, Paris or Luxembourg takes a chunk; slow, consensus-based work; heavy travel; promotion is slow past mid-grades; UN hiring was down (external recruitment to geographical posts was only 104 in 2024, from the library report). Not a good fit if you dislike bureaucracy or cannot relocate.

A reality check on pay, hours and tiers

Every figure here is dated and approximate, copied from the research with its source and year. Pay is in local currency and never converted. Tier lists reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact. Three limits apply to almost every page:

  • Asia and Gulf pay across almost all branches rests on aggregators, small self-reported samples (Levels.fyi has 12-40 submissions in some cities) or recruiter blogs. Tokyo is the thinnest.
  • Hours and stress are researchers' estimates from practitioner accounts, prep sites and a few surveys (mainly M&A). No report found a systematic hours survey by role family.
  • Gated recruiter guides (Hays, Robert Walters, Michael Page tables outside Italy, Management Consulted, CIPD, PMI) could not be read. Many pay figures therefore come via secondary summaries, as the reports note.

The full list of gaps, the rating scales and the researchers’ assumptions are on the sources page.
