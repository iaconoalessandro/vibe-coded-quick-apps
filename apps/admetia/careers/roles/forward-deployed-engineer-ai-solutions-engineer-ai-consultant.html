What you actually do

You take a general-purpose model or data platform and make it solve a customer's real problem: production applications, agents and connectors (for example MCP servers, which let models use tools), evaluation sets, and reusable deployment patterns fed back to product teams. Examples: Anthropic's London FDE role embeds with enterprise customers with 25-50% travel, and its Applied AI Architect role is pre-sales; OpenAI lists FDE roles by industry (healthcare, legal, financial services) and for government and security customers; Palantir's FDE and Deployment Strategist roles run at customer sites including defence and government; Databricks separates FDEs (hands-on builds), Deployment Strategists and Delivery Solutions Architects (post-sales). Consultants at McKinsey/QuantumBlack or BCG X combine strategy with delivery (general knowledge, unverified for specific firms).

A typical day and week

On site or on video call with the client's engineers; scoping a use case with a business owner; building a prototype (Python, APIs, vector databases, evaluation sets); presenting to executives; writing up reusable patterns for the product team. Monday-Thursday at the client, Friday at base is a common rhythm at Palantir-style firms (practitioner commentary; unverified).

Hours, stress and lifestyle

Typical 45-55 hours; peaks of 60-70 during go-lives or customer escalations (estimate). Stress 4/5: client-driven deadlines, ambiguity, and responsibility for production systems. Travel: 25-50% (Anthropic FDE posting); Palantir expects about 25% on site and another employer cited up to 50% (Pragmatic Engineer, 12 August 2025). Hybrid; some customer sites are hard to reach (factories, air-gapped networks).

Human vs. quantitative profile

People/communication: 4/5. Quantitative/technical: 4/5. This is the role where you must both code and persuade; consulting-style roles lean to 5/5 for people and 3/5 technical.

Career path

Software engineer or consultant (0-3 years) -> FDE/solutions engineer (2-5) -> senior FDE or solutions architect (5-8) -> staff/principal, engineering manager or customer-engineering lead (8+). Postings across employers show the ladder: FDE, senior FDE, "Manager, Forward Deployed Engineering", "Staff FDE" and pre-sales programme leads.

Exit opportunities

After 2-3 years: product engineering, startup founding engineer, consulting, solutions-architect roles at cloud providers. After 7-10 years: head of customer engineering, chief technology officer at an AI startup, partner-track consulting, or founder (customer exposure is a classic startup apprenticeship).

Tier list of employers (alphabetical within each tier)

  • Tier 1: Palantir (the role's originator) and frontier labs (Anthropic, Google Cloud/DeepMind customer engineering, OpenAI).
  • Tier 2: AI-native startups (Cohere, Ramp, Lindy and others cited by the Pragmatic Engineer), cloud providers (AWS, Google Cloud, Microsoft), Databricks, Salesforce, Scale AI, Snowflake; consultancies' AI arms (QuantumBlack, BCG X, Accenture, Deloitte; general knowledge).
  • Tier 3: systems integrators, regional consultancies and in-house AI enablement teams at banks and pharma.

Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact.

How to enter

The role is rarely a first job at a frontier lab: Anthropic's FDE postings want 4+ years (London) and 8+ (Paris), its Architect roles 5+. But Palantir and Databricks have earlier-career doors: Palantir's feed lists New Grad and Internship tracks for Forward Deployed Software Engineer and Deployment Strategist (New York, London, Chicago, Seoul, Washington DC, Paris and others; US new-grad FDE pay about $135,000-$145,000, estimated in the posting), and Databricks lists new-graduate product roles. Paths: software engineer who has built LLM applications (Part 1 branch) plus customer exposure; consultant who can code. A bachelor's or equivalent is enough per Anthropic; computer science, engineering, data or maths are common, but economics, management and finance graduates enter via consulting and sales engineering. Portfolio: two or three real applications on LLM APIs (agent with tool use, retrieval, evaluation set) and a short case study; cloud certifications help for solutions-architect roles at cloud firms (not at labs). Interviews: Python technical interview, customer scenario role-play, system design, executive communication. Language: several European roles require local fluency (Anthropic Milan Italian C1, Paris native French; OpenAI lists Spanish-speaking roles in London and Madrid), an opening for Italians and Francophones. Common mistakes: treating it as pure coding; promising capabilities models cannot deliver; no evidence of production experience. Alternative routes: management consultants and data scientists who upskill in coding; technical account managers. Entry difficulty: 3/5 at consulting and integrators; 4/5 at frontier labs and Palantir.

Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for

Travel and context-switching are real; the Pragmatic Engineer notes the role can feel like two jobs (customer delivery and platform engineering) and that expectations vary. You live with customers' messy data and politics, and your success depends on things outside your control. Consultancies add up-or-out pressure. Not a fit for people who want deep research time, dislike travel, or prefer a single focused technical problem. Not a fit if you cannot present to executives.

A reality check on pay, hours and tiers

Every figure here is dated and approximate, copied from the research with its source and year. Pay is in local currency and never converted. Tier lists reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact. Three limits apply to almost every page:

  • Asia and Gulf pay across almost all branches rests on aggregators, small self-reported samples (Levels.fyi has 12-40 submissions in some cities) or recruiter blogs. Tokyo is the thinnest.
  • Hours and stress are researchers' estimates from practitioner accounts, prep sites and a few surveys (mainly M&A). No report found a systematic hours survey by role family.
  • Gated recruiter guides (Hays, Robert Walters, Michael Page tables outside Italy, Management Consulted, CIPD, PMI) could not be read. Many pay figures therefore come via secondary summaries, as the reports note.

The full list of gaps, the rating scales and the researchers’ assumptions are on the sources page.
