What you actually do

You take work off the founder's or a leader's desk: build the financial model and the monthly dashboard, run the company's quarterly goals, prepare investor updates and board packs, analyse pricing or a new market, run special projects (a first enterprise sales process, an international launch, an acquisition integration), and fill gaps (hiring, contracts, partnerships). Tools: Excel/Google Sheets, SQL, a BI tool, Notion/Slack, presentation software. Deliverables are models, memos, operating rhythms (weekly metrics reviews), and tracked projects.

A typical day and week

A day might start with a metrics check-in with the CEO, then a data pull, then a customer or partner call, then a draft of the board pack; there is no fixed job description, and priorities change weekly. A week includes the leadership meeting, an investor-update draft, one cross-functional project push, and ad hoc "fire drills".

Hours, stress and lifestyle

50-60 hours typical; peak 70+ before board meetings, fundraises and launches. Stress 4/5 (close to the founder's cash and survival worries; the startup may run out of money). Mostly in-office at early-stage startups.

Human vs. quantitative profile

People/communication: 4/5. Quantitative/technical: 3/5. In practice you must earn trust quickly with founders and employees and also build models and read metrics well enough to be useful.

Compensation (approximate)

US: no reliable survey found for founder's associate. Product-flavoured chief of staff roles at companies under ~200 staff: US$110-135k (LaunchNotes vendor guide, 2025; unsourced). FounderPath's SaaS salary benchmark (109 salaries, February 2026) shows a median chief-of-staff base around US$116k; its early-stage bottom-quartile (~US$30k) looks like a data error. A seed-stage entry-level figure around US$81k appears on a recruiter site citing an unverified "COS Network 2025 Salary Report". UK/Europe: no reliable data found for role titles; for context, a junior business hire at seed in Europe earned €50-70k cash plus options of about 5-15% of salary per year (Index Ventures, 2018 data, stale). LBS MiM graduates entering tech had a mean starting base of about £35-36k in 2024-25, below consulting (LBS MiM employment reports 2024 and 2025, via prior Admetia research). Italy: no reliable data found; expect Milan startup junior business roles broadly in line with junior PM pay (€30-45k gross; an inference from the PM figures above, not a sourced figure). Equity: Carta reports Series A companies average 16 employees (down from about 25) and equity packages about 50% smaller than in 2022 outside AI (Carta compensation trends 2025, via search summary). A claimed 0.05-0.25% equity for chiefs of staff (attributed to Carta 2025) could not be verified. Value the equity near zero when comparing offers.

Career path

Founder's associate / analyst (0-2 years) → Chief of Staff or Head of Strategy & Ops (2-5) → VP/Head of Operations, General Manager or Head of a function (5-10) → COO or founder. Titles are fluid and speed of progression depends on company survival.

Exit opportunities

After 2-3 years: PM, product ops, strategy roles at bigger tech firms, VC associate roles, consulting (MBB lateral moves at some firms), or a second startup. After 7-10 years: COO/GM, operating partner at a VC fund (see Finance branch), or founder.

Tier list of employers

(Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact.)

  • Tier 1: top-quartile venture-backed companies post-Series B (Stripe, Revolut, Ramp-type companies in the US; Revolut, Mistral, ElevenLabs, Bending Spoons in Europe), and high-profile AI labs (no entry-level business roles found at OpenAI or Anthropic on 30 September 2026; roles ask 4-6+ years).
  • Tier 2: seed-to-Series B startups backed by top-tier funds (Index, Atomico, Balderton, Accel, Creandum, Seedcamp), and venture studios such as Antler (investor-brand signals).
  • Tier 3: unbacked or small bootstrapped companies, agencies, corporate innovation units.

How to enter

Backgrounds: management, economics, finance, data analytics, engineering — any, if you can model and write. Routes: warm introductions via investors' talent teams (Index Ventures runs a public portfolio job board); part-time or intern roles at startups while studying; summer internships; Antler- and EF-type programmes. Founders' associate roles are mostly unadvertised (practitioner consensus; unverified); Revolut's Strategy & Operations graduate scheme requires STEM and coding; Bending Spoons hires students year round (company FAQ, 2026). Calendar: year round, tied to funding events, not graduation cycles. Interviews: case or take-home analysis (build a model, size a market), a founder conversation, and a work trial. Certifications: none matter. Skills and portfolio: a financial model of a real business, a dashboard built on public data, a market analysis, shipped side projects, SQL; strong writing. Common mistakes: joining a weak team for the title, overvaluing equity, taking a chief-of-staff title with no real scope, ignoring the runway. Alternative routes: analyst or consultant first; internal transfer; BizOps at a scale-up. Entry difficulty: 3/5.

Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for

Pay is below consulting or banking at entry in Europe; equity is usually worth little; the company may fail and your job with it; the role is vague and depends on one person; you may do a lot of low-prestige work. Not for you if you need job security, structured training or clear boundaries, or if you want a recognised credential rather than a learning-by-doing path.

A reality check on pay, hours and tiers

Every figure here is dated and approximate, copied from the research with its source and year. Pay is in local currency and never converted. Tier lists reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact. Three limits apply to almost every page:

  • Asia and Gulf pay across almost all branches rests on aggregators, small self-reported samples (Levels.fyi has 12-40 submissions in some cities) or recruiter blogs. Tokyo is the thinnest.
  • Hours and stress are researchers' estimates from practitioner accounts, prep sites and a few surveys (mainly M&A). No report found a systematic hours survey by role family.
  • Gated recruiter guides (Hays, Robert Walters, Michael Page tables outside Italy, Management Consulted, CIPD, PMI) could not be read. Many pay figures therefore come via secondary summaries, as the reports note.

The full list of gaps, the rating scales and the researchers’ assumptions are on the sources page.
