What you actually do

You advise governments, regulators, EU institutions and state-owned bodies (section 2.6). The UK's National Audit Office describes the legitimate use: consultants advise on a specific project or programme and supply "expert insight or specialist skills" or an outside view, for a defined period, when government lacks the skill itself (NAO, November 2025). A junior works on one piece of a larger programme: gathering evidence on a service, drafting part of a business case, tracking a funded programme's milestones, preparing papers for a governance board. A graduate advert from Local Partnerships, a consultancy owned by HM Treasury, the Local Government Association and the Welsh Government, lists the duties plainly: research, analysis and data work; reports and presentations for senior stakeholders; attending meetings and recording actions (2026). Typical subjects are adult social care, children's services, health and local-government finances (IMPOWER and Newton sector pages, 2026); in Italy, Intellera Consulting works for central and local administrations and the health service on digital transition and public funds (Intellera site, 2026). Tools: slides, spreadsheets, business-case templates.

A typical day and week

Meetings with officials, drafting a report section or a business case, a workshop with service staff, a governance board. PA Consulting tells graduates to expect at least two days a week in its office or on client site, and up to five on site depending on the assignment (PA 2026 graduate advert). Procurement shapes the calendar: since November 2024, UK central-government consultancy contracts above £600,000 or longer than nine months need a minister's sign-off, and those above £100,000 or longer than three months the permanent secretary's (Cabinet Office, 14 November 2024), so work comes in short, tightly scoped packages. The pace follows the client's budget year and political calendar.

Hours, stress and lifestyle

  • Hours: no reliable data found; no survey covers public-sector practices. Contract and profile figures are low: Local Partnerships advertises a 35-hour week (2026), and the UK National Careers Service gives 37 to 42 hours for management consultants in general (2026). The 45-55 typical and 60-65 peak in the summary table are my reading from the Big 4 row above, not measured.
  • Stress: 3/5. Deadlines are set by budgets, audits and elections; scrutiny is public.
  • Lifestyle: capital cities, ministries, councils and hospitals, with hybrid working common (two office days a week at PA and Local Partnerships); in the Gulf, long placements on site.

Human vs. quantitative profile

People/communication: 4/5. Quantitative/technical: 3/5. The work is persuading officials and writing for non-specialist readers; the analysis is costings, demand data and options appraisal.

Compensation (approximate)

  • US: no firm-published entry figure found; the early-careers pages of Booz Allen and Guidehouse state no pay (2026). Entry follows the analyst scales of 3.2: $78,000-$112,000 base. For comparison, management analysts employed by government itself had a median of $97,300, against $107,330 in professional and technical services firms (BLS, May 2025; all experience levels, not entry pay).
  • UK: PA Consulting advertised "salary potential" of £33,000 plus a £3,500 transport allowance for Autumn 2026 (advert reposted by Stemettes; secondary). Newton, whose sectors include central and local government and health, states £48,000 plus a £2,500 sign-on bonus (2026 intake). Local Partnerships advertised £27,000 for a two-year graduate post starting July 2026. Big 4: £32,000-£37,000, as in 3.2.
  • Italy: no reliable data found; the Intellera site states no pay. Indicative €26,000-€30,000 gross, as in 3.2. Benchmark, not a consulting figure: master's graduates earned €1,488 net a month one year after graduating (AlmaLaurea, 2024 survey).
  • About 5 years: UK £45,000-£65,000 at two to five years (Prospects, September 2026; all consulting, guide only). US and Italy: no reliable data found.

Career path

Analyst (0-3 years) → consultant or manager (3-7) → director or partner; the years are my estimate, as no firm page opened gives them. PA's graduate programme runs 18 months across several rotations (2026). Moves into the civil service or international organisations are common.

Exit opportunities

  • After 2-3 years: government departments and agencies (which employ management analysts directly, as the BLS figure above shows), international organisations, think tanks, in-house strategy at state-owned companies.
  • After 7-10 years: partner, senior public-sector posts, development banks.

Tier list of employers

  • Tier 1: the public-sector practices of McKinsey, BCG and Bain.
  • Tier 2: Big 4 and Accenture, which hold most large public contracts; PA Consulting and Newton in the UK; Booz Allen Hamilton and Guidehouse in the US.
  • Tier 3: national and specialist consultancies: IMPOWER and the publicly owned Local Partnerships in the UK; Intellera Consulting in Italy.
  • Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact.

How to enter

  • Degrees: economics, politics, public policy, management. PA and Local Partnerships accept any discipline; PA sets no grade requirement (adverts, 2026).
  • Schemes and timing: PA's Autumn 2026 programme closed on 7 November 2025 (the advert gives day and month; the year is my inference). Guidehouse was listing "Campus 2027" roles in October 2026; Booz Allen considers interns from late August for the following summer (firm pages, 2026).
  • Constraints: PA requires British citizenship or five years' UK residence, and all its staff need Baseline Personnel Security Standard clearance, with higher vetting for some roles (PA advert, 2026); the government applies that baseline check to "government contractors" (GOV.UK, September 2024). US federal consulting is widely said to need citizenship and a clearance, but neither firm's early-careers page states the rule and individual job adverts were not opened: unverified. In the Gulf, local-hire quotas reduce entry places for expatriates (Saudi Arabia required 35% Saudi staffing in consulting from March 2023 and 40% in the second phase; section 2.6).
  • Italy: public bodies are a small part of the market. Private clients were 89% of the €6.3 billion consulting turnover in 2023 (Il Sole 24 Ore on Assoconsult data, February 2024), which leaves about 11% for the rest (my arithmetic). Demand has been driven by the national recovery plan (section 2.6), which has an end date.
  • Common mistakes: ignoring how procurement shapes the work; assuming policy influence at entry.
  • Non-target routes: a civil-service or EU traineeship first; a policy master's.
  • Entry difficulty: 3/5 at Big 4; 5/5 at the strategy firms.

Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for

Demand depends on political decisions. The UK Treasury estimated central-government spending on consultants at about £1.36 billion in 2022-23; the Chancellor then announced a stop to non-essential consultancy spending and an aim to halve the bill in 2025-26, and the main purchasing framework was cut from £5.7 billion over four years to £1.7 billion over two (NAO, November 2025; Cabinet Office, November 2024). The public sector supplies about a fifth to a quarter of UK consultancies' income (Consultancy.uk on the NAO report, November 2025; secondary), so a cut of that size matters for hiring. Beyond that: slow clients, press scrutiny of fees, recommendations that are not implemented, and nationality and vetting rules that exclude many international students. Poor fit: people who want fast, measurable commercial results.

A reality check on pay, hours and tiers

Every figure here is dated and approximate, copied from the research with its source and year. Pay is in local currency and never converted. Tier lists reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact. Three limits apply to almost every page:

  • Asia and Gulf pay across almost all branches rests on aggregators, small self-reported samples (Levels.fyi has 12-40 submissions in some cities) or recruiter blogs. Tokyo is the thinnest.
  • Hours and stress are researchers' estimates from practitioner accounts, prep sites and a few surveys (mainly M&A). No report found a systematic hours survey by role family.
  • Gated recruiter guides (Hays, Robert Walters, Michael Page tables outside Italy, Management Consulted, CIPD, PMI) could not be read. Many pay figures therefore come via secondary summaries, as the reports note.

The full list of gaps, the rating scales and the researchers’ assumptions are on the sources page.
