What you actually do

Arrange and track shipments by road, rail, air and sea; book carriers; prepare or check documents (bills of lading, invoices, certificates of origin); manage customs declarations and duty; handle exceptions; negotiate rates. In a forwarder you work in teams per mode (ocean, air, road) or per customer; in a shipper you buy transport from forwarders and carriers. Tools: TMS (transport management system), carrier portals (K+N myKN), customs systems, Excel, email. Example: a vessel is rolled (bumped) at Rotterdam; you rebook on another carrier, update the customer's ETA, and check the new Incoterm cost impact.

A typical day and week

Morning: review overnight alerts, vessel and flight schedules, customs holds; midday: quotes and bookings; afternoon: documentation, invoices, calls with agents in other time zones; weekly: customer reviews. Peak weeks (before Chinese New Year, Q4 peak, tariff deadlines) bring double-hours. Month-end closes can pull people into overtime (employee reviews at DSV in Dubai and Singapore report this; self-reported, weak signal).

Hours, stress and lifestyle

Typical 40-45; peaks 50-60. Prospects says logistics managers can work up to 60 hours and may work shifts, evenings and weekends in 24-hour operations. Stress: 4/5 in forwarding and transport operations; 3/5 in a planned shipper-side role. Mostly office-based with desk phones and screens; some roles require port, airport or site visits; remote work possible but limited; on-call duty is common in air freight and customs.

Human vs. quantitative profile

People/communication: 4/5. Quantitative/technical: 3/5. You spend the day talking to carriers, customers and agents; the technical load is rules (customs, Incoterms, dangerous-goods regulations) and cost arithmetic.

Compensation (approximate)

  • US: BLS logisticians median US$82,320 (May 2025), 10th percentile under US$50,890, 90th over US$133,160; transportation and warehousing industry median US$63,530; federal government US$103,790; manufacturing US$83,990 (BLS OOH). 255,100 jobs, growth projected at 18% in 2025-35.
  • UK: entry about GBP 25,000 average (Prospects); real postings: freight forwarding operations coordinator GBP 34,000 (Redditch), import/export coordinator GBP 30,000-32,000 (Coventry), customs specialist GBP 30,000-35,000 (Essex, April 2025), European operations coordinator GBP 30,000-35,000 (Selby) (job postings, weak signal); managers GBP 41,000-65,000 and heads of logistics over GBP 90,000 (Prospects). Kuehne+Nagel UK graduate GBP 28,500 plus GBP 1,000 joining bonus (2026 listing); DHL graduate GBP 30,500.
  • Italy: logistics clerk roles EUR 22,000-28,000 (Randstad listing, Tortona, not graduate); logistics analyst EUR 26,000-32,000 at 0-2 years (Masterin.it, weak). No reliable recruiter salary guide found.
  • Netherlands: average freight forwarder base about EUR 34,206 (Payscale, 2026). Germany: one export specialist posting EUR 50,000-60,000 (posting, upper-range example).
  • Singapore: logistics specialist about S$48,000 and S$62,000 at 5-10 and 10-15 years (Singsaver 2026 blog, source unclear).

Career path

Operations/coordinator trainee (0-2) → senior coordinator or customs specialist (2-4) → team lead / key-account manager (4-7) → branch or operations manager (7-12) → regional/country director. Customs routes lead to compliance manager or director of trade compliance.

Exit opportunities

After 2-3 years: procurement, planning, carrier sales, or customs brokerage. After 7-10 years: head of logistics or supply chain at a shipper, branch/country manager at a forwarder, trade-compliance director, consulting, or founding your own forwarding agency (common among long-experienced forwarders).

Tier list of employers

Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact. Reference: Armstrong & Associates top forwarders by revenue/volume (2025) and the Alphaliner list for carriers.

  • Tier 1 (global forwarders/integrators): Kuehne+Nagel, DSV (with DB Schenker), DHL Global Forwarding, UPS Supply Chain Solutions, Expeditors, CEVA, Sinotrans, Nippon Express. Systematic graduate programmes and training; K+N and DSV are cutting white-collar roles with technology, and employee review sites show more overtime complaints at DSV than at K+N (anecdotal).
  • Tier 2 (large regional/national forwarders and 3PLs): In Italy Savino Del Bene, Fercam, Arcese, Gruber, Geodis, BRT; in the Netherlands and Belgium large regional forwarders; asset-based trucking groups. More personal culture, hands-on responsibility.
  • Tier 3 (small forwarders, customs brokers): low pay, high autonomy, little formal training.

How to enter

Any business, geography, economics, languages or international trade degree; logistics-specific degrees help; a driving licence is often required for UK programmes (Prospects, K+N, DHL listings). Programmes: Kuehne+Nagel graduate programme (UK: two-year rotation with a professional qualification, mentor and buddy; the official page is dated and asks for a 2:2, HR scheme a 2:1 and right to work; no sponsorship), Maersk UK Graduate Programme (pathways including sales, customs, customer experience and finance; video interview, assessment centre at East Midlands Gateway, leadership interview; no visa sponsorship; an Maersk Contract Logistics Management scheme closed May 2026), DHL Supply Chain & Logistics Specialist Graduate Programme (see 3.2). DSV and CEVA run graduate schemes (unverified). Calendar: UK schemes mostly open September-November with rolling closings; forwarders also hire all year round for coordinator roles. Certifications: CILT (Chartered Institute of Logistics & Transport, UK; accredits some degrees, offers diplomas and chartered membership per Prospects); FIATA diploma (unverified); customs qualifications such as the US licensed customs broker exam (unverified, twice yearly) or Italian "spedizioniere doganale" licensing (unverified). They matter for customs roles but not for generalist operations roles. Skills: Incoterms, shipping documents, customs classification, Excel, a TMS, a second language (German, Italian, Spanish, Mandarin are valued in different hubs). Common mistakes: ignoring documentation detail (a single wrong HS code can freeze a container); expecting 9-to-5. Alternative routes: entry-level forwarding clerk/coordinator roles with no degree, then CILT part-time study; customs clerk to customs broker. Entry difficulty: 2/5 for coordinator posts; 3/5 for Tier 1 graduate programmes.

Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for

Reactive, deadline-driven, often poorly paid at entry with variable bonus; forwarders are in cost-cutting mode and automating booking and quoting; you are measured on exceptions. Not a good fit for people who need predictable hours or who dislike phone-heavy, interrupt-driven work.

A reality check on pay, hours and tiers

Every figure here is dated and approximate, copied from the research with its source and year. Pay is in local currency and never converted. Tier lists reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact. Three limits apply to almost every page:

  • Asia and Gulf pay across almost all branches rests on aggregators, small self-reported samples (Levels.fyi has 12-40 submissions in some cities) or recruiter blogs. Tokyo is the thinnest.
  • Hours and stress are researchers' estimates from practitioner accounts, prep sites and a few surveys (mainly M&A). No report found a systematic hours survey by role family.
  • Gated recruiter guides (Hays, Robert Walters, Michael Page tables outside Italy, Management Consulted, CIPD, PMI) could not be read. Many pay figures therefore come via secondary summaries, as the reports note.

The full list of gaps, the rating scales and the researchers’ assumptions are on the sources page.
