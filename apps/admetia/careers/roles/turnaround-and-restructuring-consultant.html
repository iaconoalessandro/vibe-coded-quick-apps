What you actually do

You work for a company in financial trouble, or for its lenders or owners. It is the operational side of the restructuring banker's work in the Finance branch: the banker renegotiates the debt, you fix the business. Two jobs share the label. In turnaround advisory (Alvarez & Marsal, AlixPartners, FTI Consulting) the tasks are those an Alvarez & Marsal advert lists: "building and managing 13-week cash flow forecast", a cash-conservation strategy and controls, a liquidation analysis to test whether a reorganisation plan is feasible, pre-bankruptcy planning and support in running the case (A&M senior associate advert, Chicago, October 2026). Seniors may take an interim seat such as chief restructuring officer. In UK-style restructuring and insolvency teams, much of the work is formal procedures run by a licensed insolvency practitioner; only individuals, not firms, can hold that licence (ICAEW, 2026). Those procedures are mostly small: of 23,938 company insolvencies in England and Wales in 2025, 18,525 were creditors' voluntary liquidations and 1,495 were administrations (Insolvency Service, January 2026). Tools: cash-flow and three-statement models, cost baselines, weekly reporting packs.

A typical day and week

A daily cash call with the client's finance team, checking receipts and payments against the forecast; analysis of a division's costs or a supplier list; a meeting with lenders or the private-equity owner; a weekly report on progress against the plan. The work is done at the client: the A&M advert asks for "willingness to travel 100%", and AlixPartners asks its analysts for the "ability to travel up to 100% Monday through Friday as required" (adverts, 2026). Assignments start at short notice and run on the lenders' timetable.

Hours, stress and lifestyle

  • Hours: no reliable data found; no firm publishes hours and no survey covers the field. A coaching site says turnarounds and restructurings "tend to run hotter than implementation work", without numbers (StrategyCase, July 2026; author's estimate). The 55-65 typical and 70-80 peak in the summary table are an unsourced estimate.
  • Stress: 4/5. A missed payroll or covenant has consequences within days, and creditors test every number.
  • Lifestyle: on site at the client, with little notice and little control over location; quieter between cases.

Human vs. quantitative profile

People/communication: 4/5. Quantitative/technical: 4/5. Cash-flow modelling and accounting detail every day, delivered to managers, lenders and staff who are under strain.

Compensation (approximate)

  • US: the one firm-stated entry figure found is AlixPartners' $110,000 plus a discretionary bonus for performance analysts starting August 2027 in Chicago, a role in its improvement practices, not in turnaround and restructuring (advert reposted on Blind). Otherwise self-reported; weak: Glassdoor totals of $83,000-$115,000 for AlixPartners analysts (17 salaries, June 2024) and $84,000-$112,000 for A&M analysts in Chicago (35 salaries, June 2024), both via Road to Offer (September 2026).
  • UK: no firm-published graduate figure found. Self-reported; weak: A&M analysts £35,000-£49,000 (Glassdoor UK, 83 salaries, via Road to Offer, 2026), consistent with the £35,000 for A&M and £40,000 for FTI in 3.3.
  • Italy: no reliable data found.
  • About 5 years: A&M advertises $160,000-$185,000 plus bonus for senior associates with at least six years' experience (Chicago, October 2026). Self-reported; weak: UK A&M associates £60,000-£90,000 and senior associates £90,000-£128,000 (Glassdoor UK via Road to Offer, 2026).

Career path

At A&M: analyst → associate → senior associate → director → senior director → managing director; at AlixPartners: analyst → associate → vice president → senior vice president → director → partner (titles as listed by Road to Offer, 2026). FTI says most of its consulting roles have six levels (FTI early-careers page, 2026). No reliable data found on years per level; the A&M advert implies about six years to senior associate. On the insolvency track the milestone is the Joint Insolvency Examination Board exam, two papers (corporate and personal) sat once a year, followed by a licence from a recognised professional body, which also requires a minimum number of hours of insolvency experience (ICAEW and JIEB, 2026; the hours are not stated on the pages opened).

Exit opportunities

  • After 2-3 years: private-equity portfolio operations, distressed-debt and special-situations investors, corporate finance and turnaround roles in industry.
  • After 7-10 years: managing director, licensed insolvency practitioner, interim chief restructuring officer or finance chief, operating partner at a fund.

Tier list of employers

  • Tier 1: Alvarez & Marsal, AlixPartners, FTI Consulting.
  • Tier 2: the restructuring practices of the Big 4 and of advisory firms such as Teneo, Interpath and Kroll.
  • Tier 3: small turnaround boutiques, regional insolvency practices and interim-management firms.
  • Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact.

How to enter

  • Degrees: finance, accounting, economics or engineering; accounting fluency is essential. AlixPartners asks analysts for "a quantitative or analytical degree program" (advert, 2026).
  • Graduate routes: they exist, mostly at the insolvency end and in the UK. Search results showed adverts titled "Restructuring and Insolvency Graduate Programme" (A&M, London and Leeds), "2026 Graduate Scheme, Restructuring, London" (FTI), "Graduate Associate, Restructuring" (Interpath) and "Financial Restructuring Graduate Programme, September 2027 intake" (EY), but every one of those pages refused to open, so their terms (reported as a 2:1 degree and study for the ACA accountancy qualification) are unverified. AlixPartners' student page names a Business Analyst programme "for London-based Master's grads", an MBA programme and internships; FTI names internships, an early-identification programme and entry-level posts (firm pages, 2026).
  • Experience: turnaround work proper is an experienced-hire field. The A&M advert wants six years, four of them in restructuring, and prefers the CTP, CIRA, CPA or CFA. The Turnaround Management Association's Certified Turnaround Professional needs at least four years of leadership experience in distressed companies within the past 15; its Certified Turnaround Analyst is knowledge-based and aimed at newer practitioners (TMA, 2026). No reliable data found on the share of hires who come from audit, transaction services or banking.
  • Qualifications: in the UK, the ACA and then the insolvency exam, which non-members can also register for through ICAEW (2026); in the US, the certificates above; the CTP has three 100-question exams (TMA, 2026).
  • Common mistakes: applying without being able to read the three financial statements; confusing the role with restructuring banking.
  • Non-target routes: Big 4 audit or transaction services, then a move across.
  • Entry difficulty: 4/5.

Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for

Job cuts are often part of the plan, and you deliver the news. Travel is close to total and unplanned. The work is busiest when the economy is weakest and thinner otherwise: company insolvencies in England and Wales were 5% lower in 2025 than in 2023, the highest year since 1993 (Insolvency Service, January 2026). Graduate schemes at the insolvency end are closer to accountancy training on liquidations than to rescuing large companies, and pay there is below strategy consulting (section 3.3). Poor fit: people uncomfortable with confrontation or with delivering bad news.

A reality check on pay, hours and tiers

Every figure here is dated and approximate, copied from the research with its source and year. Pay is in local currency and never converted. Tier lists reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact. Three limits apply to almost every page:

  • Asia and Gulf pay across almost all branches rests on aggregators, small self-reported samples (Levels.fyi has 12-40 submissions in some cities) or recruiter blogs. Tokyo is the thinnest.
  • Hours and stress are researchers' estimates from practitioner accounts, prep sites and a few surveys (mainly M&A). No report found a systematic hours survey by role family.
  • Gated recruiter guides (Hays, Robert Walters, Michael Page tables outside Italy, Management Consulted, CIPD, PMI) could not be read. Many pay figures therefore come via secondary summaries, as the reports note.

The full list of gaps, the rating scales and the researchers’ assumptions are on the sources page.
