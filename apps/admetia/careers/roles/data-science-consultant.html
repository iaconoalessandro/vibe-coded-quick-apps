What you actually do

You deliver analytics and AI projects for clients. Typical engagement: a retailer wants to forecast demand, a bank wants a churn model, an energy company wants predictive maintenance. You scope the problem with the client, access and clean their data, prototype a model, build a proof of concept, and present results to executives, ideally with a path to production. Roles range from hands-on data scientist inside a strategy consultancy's AI unit (McKinsey QuantumBlack, BCG X) to analytics consultants in the Big Four and IT integrators (Deloitte, Accenture, Capgemini, Reply, Bip). Tools: Python, SQL, cloud platforms (Azure, AWS, GCP), Power BI/Tableau, PowerPoint.

A typical day and week

Client-site or video calls in the morning, data work and modelling mid-day, slides and weekly steering meeting with the client, plus staffing and proposal activities. Mostly 3-6 month projects, with client changes common. Postings for McKinsey QuantumBlack ask for willingness to travel and to attend meetings outside traditional business hours (McKinsey job listings, 2026).

Hours, stress and lifestyle

45-55 typical, 60+ before steering committees (consensus). A former McKinsey consultant said data scientists there work slightly fewer hours than generalist consultants, unless on site with the client (PrepLounge forum answer, 2022, anecdotal). Stress 4/5: client deadlines, shifting scope, "up or out" or utilisation pressure at strategy firms; IT-consulting body shops run at 3/5 (consensus). Travel: variable; a BCG Hong Kong posting lists travel based on client needs; a McKinsey Lisbon posting lists willingness to travel. Hybrid with client-site days.

Human vs. quantitative profile

People/communication: 4/5. Quantitative/technical: 4/5. You need both: clients buy the story, not the model.

Compensation (approximate)

  • US: BCG data scientist ladder on Levels.fyi US$166k (L1) to US$265k (L5) total compensation (updated 7 June 2025, small sample); no reliable figure for QuantumBlack or Big Four entry-level data scientists found.
  • UK: no reliable data found for data science consultant pay at strategy firms; Big Four and integrators typically pay graduate-level salaries comparable to other consulting entry roles (not verified here).
  • Italy: no firm-specific figures found. Deloitte Italy junior consultants about EUR 30k gross per an aggregator (not data-science-specific, weak); Glassdoor (Feb 2026, 214 salaries) junior data scientist Italy about EUR 29k; NettoCalc Milan junior EUR 42k. Accenture, Deloitte, Reply and BIP post junior data scientist roles in Milan without stating pay.
  • Asia: no reliable data found.

Career path

Analyst/associate data scientist (0-2) → consultant / data scientist II (2-4) → senior consultant / engagement manager (4-8) → principal, associate partner, partner (8-14 at strategy firms; different at IT firms). At QuantumBlack-type units, ladders are Data Scientist I/II, Senior Data Scientist, then Principal/Associate Partner (McKinsey listings use "Data Scientist I", "Data Scientist II", "Senior Data Scientist I").

Exit opportunities

After 2-3 years: in-house data science at a client; product or strategy roles; ML engineering; PhD. After 7-10 years: head of analytics or Chief Data Officer, partner, startup leadership.

Tier list of employers

Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact.

  • Tier 1: McKinsey (QuantumBlack), BCG (BCG X), Bain (advanced analytics). Prestige, very strong colleagues, high pay and the hardest entry; hours are long.
  • Tier 2: Deloitte, PwC, EY, KPMG, Accenture, Capgemini and specialist data and AI firms. Large intakes, more varied quality, more delivery-style work.
  • Tier 3: Italian and European boutiques and integrators such as Reply and Bip (which says it recruits graduates through those with two years' experience), small agencies and outsourcers. Rapid learning, long hours for pay, little prestige.

How to enter

  • Degrees: STEM bachelor's or master's (computer science, statistics, maths, engineering, physics); QuantumBlack postings list a bachelor's, master's or PhD in computer science, machine learning, applied statistics, mathematics, engineering or AI, and ask for statistical sampling and machine-learning fluency. BCG X postings vary: a data science internship exists, and other posts ask for 2-4 years' experience.
  • Programmes and calendar: strategy-firm recruiting is autumn-driven; IT consultancies hire continuously. Accenture Milan's process: online assessment (logic, maths), technical test on SQL and Python, optional case study; Reply Milan: SQL and Python questions then reasoning (Glassdoor reports, anecdotal).
  • Interviews: case interviews (business problem structuring), coding tests, machine learning questions and a "tell us about a project" discussion.
  • Certifications: cloud certificates (Azure, AWS, Google) are viewed positively at Big Four and integrators; not at strategy firms.
  • Skills and portfolio: a portfolio of client-style projects, strong slides, structured problem solving, English fluency.
  • Common mistakes: building models without stakeholder buy-in; ignoring the "so what"; accepting a job at an IT body shop expecting data science work.
  • Alternative routes: analyst-to-consultant moves; PhD graduates enter strategy firms' expert tracks; company switchers from industry.
  • Entry difficulty: 4/5 at Tier 1; 3/5 elsewhere.

Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for

Long hours and travel; the project-based work means shallow involvement in the final product; many projects stop at proofs of concept; pay at Tier 3 firms is low for the workload. Not a good fit if you want depth in one domain or predictable hours.

A reality check on pay, hours and tiers

Every figure here is dated and approximate, copied from the research with its source and year. Pay is in local currency and never converted. Tier lists reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact. Three limits apply to almost every page:

  • Asia and Gulf pay across almost all branches rests on aggregators, small self-reported samples (Levels.fyi has 12-40 submissions in some cities) or recruiter blogs. Tokyo is the thinnest.
  • Hours and stress are researchers' estimates from practitioner accounts, prep sites and a few surveys (mainly M&A). No report found a systematic hours survey by role family.
  • Gated recruiter guides (Hays, Robert Walters, Michael Page tables outside Italy, Management Consulted, CIPD, PMI) could not be read. Many pay figures therefore come via secondary summaries, as the reports note.

The full list of gaps, the rating scales and the researchers’ assumptions are on the sources page.
