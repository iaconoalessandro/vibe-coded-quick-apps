What you actually do

You answer a business question in a few weeks. A typical project: a consumer-goods client asks whether to enter a new country. You build a market-sizing model in Excel, run 10-15 interviews with industry experts and customers, benchmark competitors, pull data from client systems and paid databases, and draft the steering-committee deck (often 20-40 slides) that your manager rewrites with you. Tools: Excel (heavily), PowerPoint (very heavily), Alteryx or Python at some firms, internal knowledge databases, and now firm AI tools (McKinsey's Lilli, BCG's GENE/Deckster) to summarise documents and draft slides. Deliverables: a model, a storyline, a final recommendation, sometimes an implementation roadmap. At MBB you are often "staffed" on your own module within the case, presenting results to the client within weeks.

A typical day and week

Hours 8-9 am: team check-in; morning: refine one analysis; midday: expert call or client working session; afternoon: build slides; late afternoon: problem-solving session with your manager ("what does this say?"); evening: finish the draft for the partner. In the traditional US/UK model the week is Monday to Thursday at the client's site and Friday in the home office; the travel day (usually Thursday evening) is a de facto lost evening. Post-2020 many teams are hybrid and total hours have not fallen much (StrategyCase, updated July 2026; a coaching site). Roland Berger states that consultants usually spend four days on-site and Friday in the office, with over 75% of its work cross-border (Roland Berger FAQ, 2026).

Hours, stress and lifestyle

  • Hours: typical 55-70 per week at MBB; peaks of 75-85 before key client meetings or in due-diligence sprints (StrategyCase, July 2026; PrepLounge coaching forum answers, 2025; no firm discloses hours; an old BCG study of North American staff found 92% worked 50+ hours and the average week fell from 64.5 to 57.7 hours after "predictable time off" rules, HBR/Wharton, 2009-12, stale). UK ONS paid-hours data shows only 38 hours because salaried overtime is unpaid, so ignore it as evidence on hours (earlier project research).
  • Stress: 4/5. Frequent high-stakes client pressure, constant evaluation, and an up-or-out ladder.
  • Lifestyle/flexibility: low control over schedule between projects; partner and client demands dominate; first project assignment is mostly out of your hands. Parental leave and part-time policies exist at MBB but are used by a minority.
  • Travel: high in the US and UK (weekly flights), often cross-border within Europe; Gulf and Asia teams often fly in for the week or are based locally. Hybrid has softened but not removed the four-day on-site norm.
  • Remote/hybrid: partial; most client work involves some on-site days.

Human vs. quantitative profile

People/communication: 4/5. Quantitative/technical: 3/5. You need to be good with spreadsheets and structured problem-solving, but the differentiator is communicating a clear answer to senior people under time pressure; advanced maths is rare outside specialised teams.

Compensation (approximate)

Entry (undergraduate/non-MBA master's):

  • US: MBB base $110-112k (BCG $110k; McKinsey and Bain $112k) for 2025 offers; total cash with maximum bonus and signing about $137-140k (Management Consulted via Poets&Quants for Undergrads, Jan 2026). PrepLounge's US guide gives about $130-134.5k total (Jan 2026).
  • UK: McKinsey £50,000 base (up to ~£60k total), BCG £49,500 (up to £60k), Bain £52,000 (up to £63k) (PrepLounge UK, updated 2026). Levels.fyi self-reports London McKinsey Business Analyst base about £55.3k (30 Sept 2026; self-reported). The two upstream data sets may overlap.
  • Germany/DACH (comparison): McKinsey about €80k base, BCG and Bain about €75k (PrepLounge DACH, Dec 2025); a master's earns a higher entry grade than a bachelor's at McKinsey Germany (e-fellows.net McKinsey FAQ, 2026).
  • Italy: no reliable data found for MBB or tier-2 graduate pay in Milan or Rome. Only scattered points: Oliver Wyman Italy about €43k base for undergraduate/master's level (Management Consulted 2026, single source, as noted in earlier research); a levels.fyi Milan listing showing Bain at an average of about €100k total across all levels (not entry). One PrepLounge forum user says Zurich pays "almost double" Milan (Aug 2025, anecdotal).
  • Middle East: Dubai: McKinsey Business Analyst AED 255k base / AED 276k total, tax-free (levels.fyi, updated Sept 2026; self-reported); a coach estimates AED 20-30k per month for entry (PrepLounge forum, anecdotal). Riyadh: only post-MBA data found (BCG $304k, Bain $266k, McKinsey $242k total; PrepLounge Saudi 2025).
  • Asia: Singapore MBB fresh graduates S$90-120k total (Skillup, 2026, aggregator); Hong Kong: no entry-level data found (Road to Offer gives MBA-level base HKD 1.2-1.8m); Tokyo: a Japanese career site says McKinsey Japan new graduates start at roughly ¥5-7m (unverified, third-party).

~5 years (consultant to engagement manager/project leader):

  • US: total roughly $250-400k for years 2-4 and $350-550k for the manager level (4-6 years) at MBB; Tier 2 about $200-350k and $280-450k (Road to Offer, 2026, a US aggregator; wide ranges).
  • UK: experienced consultant £65-100k and senior consultant/manager £92-125k across firms (PrepLounge UK, 2026, not firm-specific); MBA/PhD hires at MBB £100-110k base, up to £117-125k total.
  • Italy: no reliable data found.

Senior (partner): MBB partners are widely said to earn well over $1 million a year (US coaching sources cite $1-5m+); PrepLounge UK gives £200k to £1m+ across all UK firms. Partner pay depends on firm profit; firms do not publish it, so treat as unverified.

Career path

Entry titles differ: McKinsey Business Analyst, BCG Associate, Bain Associate Consultant; then Associate/Consultant (often after an MBA or 2-3 years) → Engagement Manager/Project Leader/Case Team Leader (4-7 years) → Associate Partner/Principal → Partner/Director. Expect roughly 2 years per step at the junior levels; promotion is competitive at every step. "Up or out" means people not promoted are typically asked to leave within 1-3 years of the expected promotion date. Most undergraduates do not stay to partner (a small minority, often well under 10%, is a common practitioner estimate; no reliable data found). Many firms sponsor an MBA: you leave for 2 years paid in part and return as a post-MBA Associate/Consultant, usually with an obligation to return; McKinsey Germany has historically offered paid leave for the MBA (e-fellows.net, undated, older). Some firms run a "pre-MBA" analyst programme that ends with the MBA.

Exit opportunities

  • After 2-3 years: MBA (top-10 schools; often sponsored), corporate strategy and business-development roles, private equity associate roles (especially after tier-2 PE-diligence work), tech product/strategy and operations, startups and venture-backed scale-ups.
  • After 7-10 years: corporate strategy director/VP or business-unit general manager, chief of staff/CSO/COO roles, private-equity operating partner, start-up COO/CEO, founder roles. A coaching site estimates 15-20% of MBB consultants take corporate strategy jobs between years 3 and 8, and calls PE the largest exit by volume (StrategyCase, May 2026; single coach source, unsourced).

Tier list of employers

Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact.

  • Tier 1 (MBB): McKinsey, BCG, Bain. Highest selectivity, highest pay at entry, broadest alumni network.
  • Tier 1.5/elite specialists: Oliver Wyman (financial services), Roland Berger (Europe/DACH), Kearney (operations), L.E.K. and OC&C (healthcare/consumer, PE diligence), Simon-Kucher (pricing). Sometimes described as "MBB-adjacent"; pay near MBB in some countries.
  • Tier 2: Strategy&, EY-Parthenon, Monitor Deloitte, Arthur D. Little, Alvarez & Marsal, AlixPartners, Altman Solon, Capgemini Invent, Accenture Strategy; Big 4 strategy arms sell on the parent's relationships and are heavily used by PE.
  • Tier 3: Smaller regional strategy boutiques and corporate-strategy arms of IT firms.
  • Differences: rank follows client prestige, selectivity, alumni destinations and pay, not size.

How to enter

  • Degrees/backgrounds: business, economics, STEM, law, humanities; MBB say they hire from many fields. Strongest evidence is academic distinction plus leadership and quantitative aptitude. In Germany/Austria, McKinsey says fluent German is mandatory (e-fellows.net interview with McKinsey recruiter, 1 Aug 2025), and BCG's round 2 includes a case in German (BCG Germany/Austria, 2026). Roland Berger asks for the local language in every office.
  • Internships/programmes: summer/spring internships are the main funnel at MBB. BCG's Visiting Associate (8-12 weeks) leads to a binding "FAST FORWARD" offer without further interviews (BCG Germany/Austria careers page, 2026). McKinsey Germany's Fellow Intern is 8-12 weeks (e-fellows.net, 2026). Bain London has no internship programme (Road to Offer UK guide, 2026).
  • Recruiting calendar (2026 cycle; verify on firm sites):
    • US: McKinsey Business Analyst internship deadline 29 March 2026, full-time (undergrad/master's) 11 August 2026; Bain internship 29 March (spring) and 31 August (fall), full-time Associate Consultant 19 July 2026; BCG full-time around 7-14 July 2026 and internships June-September (sources disagree; Tufts/Management Consulted/Road to Offer 2026). Over 60% of interview slots reportedly fill in the first application wave (one analysis; unverified).
    • UK: McKinsey portal opened 1 Jan 2026 with a first-round deadline of 29 March, expected to reopen autumn 2026; Bain London reopens in September with first-round cases mid-November (Road to Offer UK, 2026). Rolling review: apply in the first weeks.
    • DACH: BCG wants applications at least six months before the start date; McKinsey reviews Fellow Intern applications on a rolling basis (e-fellows.net / BCG, 2026).
    • Italy: I could not verify a published calendar for MBB Milan or Rome; check each firm's Italian careers page and university career services.
    • Gulf/Asia: campus calendars vary; Singapore and Hong Kong recruit mostly from local and global target universities; no reliable calendar found.
  • Interview formats: a 20-30 minute online assessment first (McKinsey Solve, BCG's online case/"Casey", Bain's online test), then two or three rounds of case interviews plus a "fit" interview. McKinsey's Solve is a game-style assessment; in 2026 candidate reports describe Redrock Study plus Sea Wolf (about 65 minutes) as the common version, with a longer variant (Management Consulted, Road to Offer and Leland, 2026; third-party descriptions, check your invitation). McKinsey interviews combine a case with a Personal Experience Interview (PEI: a deep behavioural question on leadership, connection, growth and drive; renamed dimensions, summer 2025, third-party). BCG's global process is application → skills (behavioural) interview → case interview → team interview (BCG careers, 2026). Bain describes a recruiter call and several case-interview rounds with objective scoring (Bain, 2026). McKinsey has piloted AI-assisted case interviews (CFO.com citing the FT, Jan 2026).
  • Certifications: none matter at strategy firms. Language certificates matter in DACH and sometimes Italy.
  • Skills to build: structured problem-solving (case practice, 30-50 cases is a common recommendation; coaching-industry advice), mental maths, clear writing, Excel; the ability to use AI tools and sanity-check what they say.
  • Common mistakes: memorising frameworks instead of reasoning; no concrete leadership examples; applying late; underestimating the German requirement; treating internships as optional in DACH.
  • Alternative routes: non-target schools should use referrals and alumni networking and a master's at a target school; a non-target brand mostly hurts at CV screening, less after that (coaching sources, unverified). Big 4 strategy arms (Monitor Deloitte, Strategy&, EY-Parthenon) are the most common lateral feeders: one coaching firm found over 39% of MBB experienced hires came from other consulting firms (unverified; coaching firm). PhD and "advanced degree" hiring is a separate channel: PhDs typically enter at post-MBA level, up to two years after graduating (coaching sources; check firms). MBA (full-time at a top school) remains the largest route into MBB's higher entry level.
  • Entry difficulty: 5/5

Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for

  • Long hours and travel in the first years; limited control over staffing; constant evaluation; a pyramid that is no longer growing. Graduate pay has been flat three years running and, per a UK trade piece, real Big 4 pay for new hires has fallen 5-13% depending on the firm (eFinancialCareers via Road to Offer, 2026).
  • Much of the junior work is slide-building and data prep; the "strategy" part comes later. AI is shrinking the routine tasks, which can speed up responsibility (good) or reduce hiring (bad).
  • Not a fit if you need predictable evenings, dislike constant travel, hate working with ambiguity, or want deep technical craft (engineering, research). Also poor fit if you are in it only for the brand with no interest in business problems; the work is client-facing and political.
  • In Italy, local MBB offices are small and pay is unverified; choose carefully if you need Italian-market earning.

A reality check on pay, hours and tiers

Every figure here is dated and approximate, copied from the research with its source and year. Pay is in local currency and never converted. Tier lists reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact. Three limits apply to almost every page:

  • Asia and Gulf pay across almost all branches rests on aggregators, small self-reported samples (Levels.fyi has 12-40 submissions in some cities) or recruiter blogs. Tokyo is the thinnest.
  • Hours and stress are researchers' estimates from practitioner accounts, prep sites and a few surveys (mainly M&A). No report found a systematic hours survey by role family.
  • Gated recruiter guides (Hays, Robert Walters, Michael Page tables outside Italy, Management Consulted, CIPD, PMI) could not be read. Many pay figures therefore come via secondary summaries, as the reports note.

The full list of gaps, the rating scales and the researchers’ assumptions are on the sources page.
