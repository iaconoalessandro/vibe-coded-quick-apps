What you actually do

TPM: run large, multi-team engineering programmes: map dependencies, build and track project plans, chase risks, write status reports for executives, run launch readiness reviews and post-incident reviews, and sometimes make architecture trade-off decisions with engineers. Product ops: build the machinery around product teams: planning cycles and OKR tracking, feedback and research repositories, launch checklists, analytics dashboards and tooling, vendor management and cross-functional communication. Tools: Jira, Confluence, Looker or Tableau, SQL, spreadsheets, Notion.

A typical day and week

TPM: stand-ups across teams, a risk review, status updates to leadership, chasing owners, and a weekly programme review; product ops: pulling data for a quarterly planning pack, running a feedback review with support and sales, fixing a broken launch process, and training new PMs on tools.

Hours, stress and lifestyle

45-55 hours typical; peak 60+ during major launches or incidents (TPM). Stress 3/5, 4/5 during outages or deadline crunches. Hybrid is the norm; TPMs in global firms handle frequent cross-time-zone calls and some travel.

Human vs. quantitative profile

People/communication: 4/5. Quantitative/technical: 3/5 for product ops, 3-4/5 for TPM. In practice TPMs need enough technical literacy to understand system dependencies; product ops needs spreadsheet and SQL fluency and process design skills.

Compensation (approximate)

US (TPM): Google TPM median total compensation about US$302k in the US (Levels.fyi, updated 30 June 2026; L3 US$182k, up to US$852k at L8) and Amazon about US$290k (Levels.fyi, 7 August 2025); Google L5 ("TPM III") average about US$338k and Amazon L5 about US$214k (averages, not medians; self-reported). UK (TPM): London all-level median £121,504 (n=62, 25th-75th percentile £92k-166k; Meta £232k, Google £168k, Amazon £122k median; Levels.fyi, 9 October 2026). Italy: no reliable data found (the Levels.fyi TPM sample for Italy is too small). Product ops: no robust survey. Levels.fyi's "Operations Product Manager" median is US$191k (updated 9 October 2025), a PM-track proxy; the vendor LaunchNotes quotes base bands of US$85-105k entry, US$105-140k mid, US$140-180k+ senior (unsourced vendor estimates, May 2025). Treat product ops pay as 10-30% below equivalent PM roles (practitioner estimate; unverified).

Career path

TPM → Senior TPM → Principal TPM / Programme Director (about 8-12 years); product ops associate → manager → head of product operations. Many TPMs move to engineering management, PM or operations leadership.

Exit opportunities

After 2-3 years: PM (more common from product ops), engineering programme management, operations roles. After 7-10 years: Director of Programme/Operations, Chief of Staff to a CTO or CPO, VP Operations, general management.

Tier list of employers

(Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact.)

  • Tier 1 TPM: Google, Amazon, Meta, Microsoft, Apple (the highest pay on Levels.fyi). Tier 2: Uber, Booking.com, Atlassian, Salesforce, Revolut, large scale-ups. Tier 3: consultancies, banks' technology programme offices and IT services firms (programme management titles with delivery focus).

How to enter

TPM: usually engineers, QA/SRE or IT project managers; some come from consulting delivery or from agile coaching. Most TPM hires have 3+ years' experience (practitioner consensus). Product ops: from analysts, bizops, project coordination or support-ops roles; typically hired at companies with 100+ staff. Interviews: programme-management case (plan a complex launch), technical discussion, behavioural. Certifications: PMP/PRINCE2 appear in corporate job ads; at tech firms they carry little weight; agile (Scrum Master, SAFe) badges are common in banking and consulting programme offices (practitioner consensus). Skills: project planning, SQL/analytics, comfort with technical systems, strong written communication. Common mistakes: presenting as a "project coordinator" without technical depth. Alternative routes: engineer → TPM; consultant → TPM/product ops; analyst → product ops. Entry difficulty: 3/5 for product ops and non-tech programme roles; 4/5 for big-tech TPM.

Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for

Credit often goes to the teams you coordinate; roles are among the first to be cut in layoffs because they are "overhead"; career ceilings are lower than PM at some firms; product ops has no standard definition and can turn into administration. Not for you if you want ownership of what is built, need clear measurable output, or dislike chasing others for updates.

A reality check on pay, hours and tiers

Every figure here is dated and approximate, copied from the research with its source and year. Pay is in local currency and never converted. Tier lists reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact. Three limits apply to almost every page:

  • Asia and Gulf pay across almost all branches rests on aggregators, small self-reported samples (Levels.fyi has 12-40 submissions in some cities) or recruiter blogs. Tokyo is the thinnest.
  • Hours and stress are researchers' estimates from practitioner accounts, prep sites and a few surveys (mainly M&A). No report found a systematic hours survey by role family.
  • Gated recruiter guides (Hays, Robert Walters, Michael Page tables outside Italy, Management Consulted, CIPD, PMI) could not be read. Many pay figures therefore come via secondary summaries, as the reports note.

The full list of gaps, the rating scales and the researchers’ assumptions are on the sources page.
