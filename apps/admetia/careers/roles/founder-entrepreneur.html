What you actually do

You find a problem, build and sell a first version, hire and fund the company. Early on, that means talking to 50+ potential customers, building a prototype (often with AI tools), selling to the first users, setting up a legal entity and cap table (list of who owns what), and fundraising. Later: hiring, managing cash and runway, product decisions, board and investor communication. Tools: spreadsheets for cash and metrics, a CRM, analytics, whichever product-building tools your team uses.

A typical day and week

There is no typical day: customer calls, building or reviewing product, recruiting, investor meetings, and fire-fighting. A week at seed stage might include two customer calls a day, a hiring interview, a fundraising pitch, and time to build; investor updates and cash checks come monthly.

Hours, stress and lifestyle

60-80 hours a week is common and peak weeks higher; stress 5/5 when runway is short or a fundraise fails (the scale's own example of a founder close to running out of cash), 3-4/5 in stable periods. Work and life blend; investors and talent cluster in hubs.

Human vs. quantitative profile

People/communication: 5/5. Quantitative/technical: 3/5 (4/5 for technical founders building the product). In practice you sell, recruit and persuade constantly, and also have to understand your numbers and, in software, your technology.

Compensation (approximate)

Founder pay is usually low until a financing. Kruze Consulting's payroll data (reported secondarily, 2026) puts median startup CEO salary at US$147k at seed in 2025 and US$153k in its 2026 benchmark; US$203k at Series A; US$216k at Series B. A much lower Pilot survey of 1,844 founders reported an average of US$75k, down 43% (2025; includes bootstrapped founders). For Europe, Creandum's 2025 data cites a median CEO salary near €115k, though the stage is unclear. US/UK/Italy at the earliest stage: often zero or a living stipend; EF pays £6,000 (London, equity-free) to join; YC invests US$500k per company but does not pay a salary. Honest outcome distribution: most seed-funded companies do not raise a Series A (Carta: about 10% within 12 months, another 15.6% within 12-24 months; Carta's analysis found graduation rates between stages barely improving, February-March 2026, secondary); startup shutdowns are rising (Carta 966 in 2024, +25.6%); returns to founders are extremely concentrated in a handful of companies. Italy: founders raise less and exits are scarcer (Growth Capital observatory, 2026). For a typical student founder, expected cash income in the first three years is below an employed graduate's.

Career path

Idea/prototype (months) → pre-seed/accelerator (6-12 months) → seed (1-2 years) → Series A (about 2-3 years; Carta's median seed-to-Series A interval was 616 days in Q2 2025) → scale or shut down. Most founders' first company does not reach Series A; many go back into employment with strong operator credentials.

Exit opportunities

After 2-3 years (company folded or sold): senior PM or product/strategy roles, chief of staff, founder's associate, VC platform or associate roles, "acqui-hire" employment at a larger firm (the buyer hires the team, not the product), or a second venture. After 7-10 years: continued CEO, operating partner or investor roles, serial founder, or angel investing.

Tier list of employers

(Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact; for founders: accelerators and investors.)

  • Tier 1 accelerators/talent investors: Y Combinator (US$500k for 7% plus MFN SAFE), Entrepreneur First (£6k talent investment then US$125k for 8%; majority-technical cohorts; takes few non-technical people, per EF FAQ, 2025-26).
  • Tier 2: Techstars (US$220k, reported), Antler, Station F-type campus programmes and top regional programmes such as Hub71 (Abu Dhabi) and CDP Venture Capital's Italian accelerator network (17 hubs counted in 2024), plus Seedcamp (Europe; from general knowledge, not verified here).
  • Tier 3: university incubators, corporate accelerators, local/regional programmes and bootstrapped paths.
  • Investors (seed/Series A, perception): in Europe Atomico, Index, Balderton, Accel, Creandum and Speedinvest are widely cited; in Italy CDP Venture Capital, United Ventures and P101 are widely named (not verified for current status).

How to enter

How first-time founders realistically start: (1) work on a problem you know from a job or university; (2) find a co-founder (EF says 80% of participants find one within 8 weeks, employer-stated); (3) build the smallest working version, increasingly with AI tools, and get ten paying or committed users before raising; (4) apply to an accelerator or angels only with traction; (5) keep personal runway for 12-18 months. Backgrounds: computer science, data science and AI graduates have the easiest route (EF explicitly prefers technical founders); business graduates should pair with a technical co-founder. Calendar: YC runs batches throughout the year (Fall 2026 applications closed 27 July); Techstars and EF cohorts have set dates; no gatekeepers for bootstrapping. Interviews: founder pitches and investor interviews, not exam-style. Visas: see 2.6 (UK Innovator Founder endorsement £1,000 and fee £1,357; Italia Startup Visa with €50,000 funds; Singapore EntrePass; Japan Startup Visa; UAE and Saudi programmes). Certifications: none matter. Skills and portfolio: shipped product, customer discovery notes, early revenue, fluent writing and selling. Common mistakes: building without customer contact; raising too early; giving away equity in unequal splits or without vesting; running out of runway; accepting terms without legal advice. Alternative routes: join an early-stage startup first (3.4), a venture studio, or a corporate innovation unit. Entry difficulty: 2/5 to start a company (no gatekeeper) but 5/5 to reach YC-level funding or a funded outcome.

Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for

Most companies fail; you may earn less than a graduate job for years, carry personal financial risk, and lose co-founder friendships; stress can be extreme. Dilution means even successful founders can own a small percentage. Not for you if you need income stability or have large debts, dislike selling, or want work-life balance in the first years.

A reality check on pay, hours and tiers

Every figure here is dated and approximate, copied from the research with its source and year. Pay is in local currency and never converted. Tier lists reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact. Three limits apply to almost every page:

  • Asia and Gulf pay across almost all branches rests on aggregators, small self-reported samples (Levels.fyi has 12-40 submissions in some cities) or recruiter blogs. Tokyo is the thinnest.
  • Hours and stress are researchers' estimates from practitioner accounts, prep sites and a few surveys (mainly M&A). No report found a systematic hours survey by role family.
  • Gated recruiter guides (Hays, Robert Walters, Michael Page tables outside Italy, Management Consulted, CIPD, PMI) could not be read. Many pay figures therefore come via secondary summaries, as the reports note.

The full list of gaps, the rating scales and the researchers’ assumptions are on the sources page.
