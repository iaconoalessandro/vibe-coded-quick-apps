What you actually do

Analysts cover a sector (e.g. European banks, US healthcare) and decide whether stocks or bonds in it are worth owning. You build and update financial models, read filings, listen to earnings calls, visit companies, talk to management and industry experts, write recommendations (often a 2-10 page note or a pitch to the PM) and defend them. Tools: Bloomberg, FactSet, Excel, increasingly Python and alternative data. Fixed-income analysts do credit work (covenants, leverage, recovery) and macro analysts follow rates. Portfolio managers (PMs) decide position sizes, manage risk limits and client mandates, and answer to investment committees.

A typical day and week

7:00 read overnight news and broker notes; morning team meeting; results days mean updating models within hours; afternoons on company meetings or sector deep dives; Friday on a pitch or portfolio review. Quarterly reporting seasons are the busy phase. PMs spend more time on risk, client meetings (as the "investment specialist" in 3.2) and trade sizing.

Hours, stress and lifestyle

45-55 hours typical; 55-60 in earnings season or market selloffs (practitioner estimate; the earlier project file also puts asset management at 50-55). Stress 3/5. Hybrid working (2-3 office days) is common at large firms (unverified). Travel: company visits and conferences, a few trips a quarter. Lifestyle is the best of the investing roles, but performance reviews against benchmarks are public and constant.

Human vs. quantitative profile

People/communication: 3/5. Quantitative/technical: 3/5. You need clear writing and persuasion to convince a PM, and solid modelling, but few firms require advanced maths outside fixed income and quant-driven houses.

Compensation (approximate)

Entry: London graduate analysts in investment banking and investment management £32-52k base, average about £41k (TargetJobs, May 2025); Italy: portfolio/fund managers ("gestori") from about €35k gross annual salary below five years' experience (PageGroup survey via We Wealth, December 2024); US: no reliable entry figure found. ~5 years: US financial and investment analysts at investment-pool and fund firms had a median wage of $146,640 in May 2023 (BLS via search summary; wages only, all experience levels, older than two years); Italy 5-10 years: no AM-specific figure. Senior: Italy portfolio managers from €70k and above (PageGroup 2024); US mutual fund PMs expected $251k base and $1.12 million variable on average in 2018 (Institutional Investor, stale). Incentives: Johnson projected 2025 asset-management incentives +7-12% (equity-focused +9-12%, fixed income +6-9%) (via AI-CIO, 10 November 2025). Hong Kong, Singapore, Tokyo, Gulf: no reliable data found.

Career path

Analyst/associate analyst (0-3 years), analyst/senior analyst (3-7), assistant PM or sector head (6-10), PM (8-15 years, fewer than one in five analysts get there in practice, practitioner view, unverified). At bank-owned managers titles follow bank grades (Analyst, Associate, VP, Director).

Exit opportunities

After 2-3 years: another asset manager, hedge fund analyst (with a strong track record), equity research at a bank, MBA, corporate strategy/IR. After 7-10 years: PM or CIO elsewhere, hedge fund PM, family office or allocator CIO, investor relations at listed companies, start your own fund.

Tier list of employers

Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact. Tier 1 (research-driven, selective, strong training): Capital Group, Fidelity, Wellington, T. Rowe Price, PIMCO (fixed income), BlackRock (active), J.P. Morgan Asset Management, Schroders, Amundi. Tier 2 (large bank- or insurer-owned, solid brand, more bureaucratic): DWS, UBS Asset Management, Allianz Global Investors, Generali Investments, Eurizon, Mediolanum. Tier 3 (boutiques and regional/Italian independents): Anima, Azimut, Algebris, local SGRs, regional UK/Nordic houses; smaller and more varied, can offer earlier responsibility.

How to enter

Degrees: finance, economics, accounting, engineering; strong quantitative skills are rewarded. Graduate programmes: Capital Group's Capital Associates Program (CAP) targets final-year undergraduates or non-MBA master's students with up to one year of experience; the Asia round has a 1 November 2026 deadline with Super Day interviews October to December 2026, the US round opened mid-January and closed early February 2026 (per third-party guides; confirm on the firm site). Fidelity (Equity Research Associate internship, 10 weeks, researches three stocks and pitches to funds; Summer 2027 listings in Tokyo and Hong Kong), T. Rowe Price (Associate Analyst internship) and Wellington (applications expected September to late October per a third-party guide) hire mainly through summer internships, usually with a few places and heavy competition. In Europe, graduate schemes at Amundi, Schroders and Generali exist, and many Italian firms hire through 6-month internships. CFA: widely valued here; Level I pass rates were 39-45% across the four windows from November 2025 to August 2026, and all three levels cost US$3,520-4,570 plus about 300 study hours each (CFA Institute figures in the project career file, 2026; secondary guides quote slightly different fees). The charter needs 4,000 hours of relevant work over at least 36 months (secondary guides). Build: a stock-pitch portfolio, a written equity research sample, modelling skills, and investing club or student fund experience. Common mistakes: generic "I love markets" pitches without a view on a real stock; applying only in the final year. Alternative routes: start in equity research or credit at a bank or rating agency, in performance/risk or operations at an asset manager, or in a boutique, and move to investing after 2-3 years. Entry difficulty: 4/5.

Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for

Fee pressure from passive limits headcount and pay growth in traditional active management; PM seats are scarce; investment style can go out of favour for years. Not for you if you want deal excitement or very high early pay, dislike public performance comparison, or are not curious about reading industry detail. Not a good fit if you want to avoid being measured against an index for a decade.

A reality check on pay, hours and tiers

Every figure here is dated and approximate, copied from the research with its source and year. Pay is in local currency and never converted. Tier lists reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact. Three limits apply to almost every page:

  • Asia and Gulf pay across almost all branches rests on aggregators, small self-reported samples (Levels.fyi has 12-40 submissions in some cities) or recruiter blogs. Tokyo is the thinnest.
  • Hours and stress are researchers' estimates from practitioner accounts, prep sites and a few surveys (mainly M&A). No report found a systematic hours survey by role family.
  • Gated recruiter guides (Hays, Robert Walters, Michael Page tables outside Italy, Management Consulted, CIPD, PMI) could not be read. Many pay figures therefore come via secondary summaries, as the reports note.

The full list of gaps, the rating scales and the researchers’ assumptions are on the sources page.
