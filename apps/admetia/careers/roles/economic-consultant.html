What you actually do

You build the empirical analysis behind an expert witness's report. In competition cases you estimate whether a merger would raise prices (using merger simulation, a model of how rivals react after a merger) or whether a cartel caused overcharges; in litigation you calculate damages; in regulation you analyse costs and returns of utilities. CRA's analyst posting lists researching economic and industry issues, building valuation models, running regressions in Excel, Stata, R, Python or SQL, summarising reports, depositions and academic literature, and quality-checking expert reports, tables and charts (CRA posting, 2026). Juniors do data cleaning and coding; PhD associates design the econometric strategy and draft expert reports.

A typical day and week

Illustrative composite. Monday: clean a dataset of transactions received from the client's lawyers; Tuesday-Wednesday: run regressions and sensitivity checks; Thursday: summarise findings in a slide deck for the case team; Friday: review a draft expert report. The weeks before a filing deadline or hearing are intense, with weekend work.

Hours, stress and lifestyle

Typical 45-55 hours, peak 65-80 before filings or trials (library research reports 45-60 hours with spikes to 75, unverified practitioner figure). Stress 4/5: client and legal deadlines drive crunch. Travel is moderate (client sites, depositions). CRA expects 3-4 office days a week (CRA posting, 2026).

Human vs. quantitative profile

People/communication: 3/5. Quantitative/technical: 4/5. Strong coding and clear writing for lawyers; client interaction grows with seniority.

Compensation (approximate)

US analyst (bachelor's/master's), base: CRA USD 92,500-100,000 (2027 graduates, posted 2026); Analysis Group Analyst USD 95,000-100,000 (Analysis Group pay page via search summary, undated); Cornerstone Research Analyst USD 112,500 base plus starting bonus (University of Florida career hub listing, recruiting from August 2026). US PhD hires: NERA Consultant (new PhD) USD 220,000-235,000 base (Built In posting, undated); Analysis Group Associate USD 180,000-240,000; Cornerstone Associate (Menlo Park) USD 195,000-245,000 (May 2026 posting). Glassdoor-based guide: median total pay for US economic consultants about USD 170,000 (2026, all levels, aggregator). UK/Europe: analyst GBP 48,000-62,000 plus 10-25% bonus; senior analyst GBP 65,000-80,000; PhD associate GBP 85,000-110,000 (library research; secondary and unverified - Frontier, Oxera and RBB do not publish salaries on the pages I found; Frontier lists pay as "competitive"). Italy: no reliable data found. Asia/Gulf: no reliable data found.

Career path

Analyst/research analyst (0-3 years, often a two- to three-year programme) -> Senior Analyst or Associate (PhD entrants start here) -> Manager/Senior Consultant (4-7 years) -> Vice President/Principal -> Partner/Affiliate expert (10+ years, usually a PhD and academic profile). Partner pay is uncapped and tied to origination of cases; data not available.

Exit opportunities

After 2-3 years: top economics PhD programmes (a major route), competition authorities (UK CMA, DG COMP), law, corporate strategy. After 7-10 years: big-tech antitrust and regulatory teams, regulators, hedge funds, academia.

Tier list of employers

Tier 1: the global pure-plays often cited by practitioners: Compass Lexecon, CRA, Cornerstone Research, Analysis Group, NERA, Brattle. Tier 2: European/UK specialists: Oxera, Frontier Economics, RBB Economics, CEPA; big-four and mid-sized practices (economic modelling and valuation). Tier 3: smaller boutiques and in-house economics units at law firms. Differences: tier 1 get the big antitrust and class-action cases and have academic affiliates; European firms lean to regulation and policy. Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact.

How to enter

Degrees: bachelor's or master's for analyst (CRA recruits December 2026 or summer 2027 graduates with a quantitative degree; honours thesis or research apprenticeship highly desired); PhD for associate. In Europe, a master's with strong econometrics (MSc/MPhil in Economics from a research-intensive university) is the standard credential; MBA or MiM without graduate econometrics are screened out (library research, unverified). Calendar: US analyst recruiting runs August-autumn for the following summer (CRA review from 17 August 2026, rolling; Cornerstone began 3 August 2026); PhD hires recruit around the AEA job market in January. Interview formats: resume screen, technical econometrics questions, a case or written test with data, behavioural interviews. Skills: Stata/R/Python/SQL, applied micro and industrial organisation (IO, the study of competition among firms), writing for lawyers. Common mistakes: generic consulting prep instead of econometrics prep; no coding sample or thesis; late applications. Alternative routes: research assistantships with professors, statistical office or regulator internships, master's thesis with data work. Entry difficulty: 4/5 (analyst at tier 1 firms), 4/5 for PhD associate (competitive).

Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for

Billable-hour pressure and crunch before filings; the work is advocacy-adjacent (analysis is tailored to the client's side); junior work can be repetitive data cleaning; pay for analysts is lower than banking. Not a good fit if you dislike deadlines, working for a side, or want to do academic-style open research.

A reality check on pay, hours and tiers

Every figure here is dated and approximate, copied from the research with its source and year. Pay is in local currency and never converted. Tier lists reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact. Three limits apply to almost every page:

  • Asia and Gulf pay across almost all branches rests on aggregators, small self-reported samples (Levels.fyi has 12-40 submissions in some cities) or recruiter blogs. Tokyo is the thinnest.
  • Hours and stress are researchers' estimates from practitioner accounts, prep sites and a few surveys (mainly M&A). No report found a systematic hours survey by role family.
  • Gated recruiter guides (Hays, Robert Walters, Michael Page tables outside Italy, Management Consulted, CIPD, PMI) could not be read. Many pay figures therefore come via secondary summaries, as the reports note.

The full list of gaps, the rating scales and the researchers’ assumptions are on the sources page.
