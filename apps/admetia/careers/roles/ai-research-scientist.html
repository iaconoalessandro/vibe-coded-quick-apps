What you actually do

You pick a research question (for example, how to make a model reason more reliably over long tasks), design experiments, run them on clusters, analyse results, and turn them into methods that go into the next model or into papers. Typical tools: PyTorch or JAX, internal training stacks, experiment trackers, Slurm or Kubernetes clusters. Deliverables: internal write-ups, ablation studies (removing components to see what matters), code that ships into training runs, and for academic and some industry roles, papers at NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR or ACL. Postings differ by subfield: Anthropic's interpretability scientist role is about reverse-engineering algorithms inside models; a Databricks principal scientist role is about scaling; Scale AI's research scientists cover post-training, evaluations and agents; OpenAI's researcher titles span alignment, safety training, reasoning and multimodal work. Academic scientists add grant writing, teaching and supervising PhD students.

A typical day and week

Industry: morning reading experiment results and dashboards from overnight runs; a few hours of coding or analysis; a team research meeting; a call with an engineer about a training bug; writing notes. A week often includes a research review where you present, plus chat-heavy collaboration on shared training runs. Academia: split between supervising students, writing papers and grants, teaching and committees; much less access to compute. Hours below are my estimates.

Hours, stress and lifestyle

Typical 45-55 hours a week at frontier labs, peaks of 60-80 around model releases and conference deadlines (estimate; no survey exists). Stress 3-4/5: no client deadlines but competitive pressure, release timelines and a publish-or-be-scooped dynamic. Academia: 45-60 hours, stress 3/5 (grant and tenure pressure). Most frontier-research postings I read name an office city (San Francisco, London, New York, Seattle, Zurich, Paris, Palo Alto); Anthropic's say staff should be in the office at least 25% of the time. Remote work is rare for frontier research. Travel: conferences.

Human vs. quantitative profile

People/communication: 2/5. Quantitative/technical: 5/5. You mostly work with a small team and communicate through write-ups and code review; the core of the job is mathematics, statistics and programming.

Compensation (approximate)

  • US, industry, total compensation including stock (Levels.fyi, 9 October 2026, self-reported): research scientist median across companies US$305,000 (25th percentile $180,000; 75th $403,000; 90th $520,000). By company: Google median $472,000 (L3 $246,000 to L6 $729,000); Meta median $482,000 (E3 $233,000 to E6 $656,000; 584 submissions); Microsoft median $282,000 (176 submissions); OpenAI median $800,000 (only 16 submissions; mostly stock); Anthropic software engineers $367,000 at entry to $1.13 million at staff level. So entry for a PhD graduate at big tech is roughly $190,000-$430,000 total; about five years in, $430,000-$520,000; senior $650,000+. Equity at private companies is paper value.
  • Employer postings, US base salary ranges (October 2026; levels differ, so do not read them as a ranking): OpenAI Research Scientist/Engineer (San Francisco) $250,000-$445,000 and named researcher roles (interpretability, safety training, multimodal safety) $380,000-$500,000; Anthropic Research Scientist, Interpretability $350,000-$850,000; xAI "Member of Technical Staff" $180,000-$440,000, up to $600,000 for post-training (the pay text names the company SpaceXAI; equity extra); Databricks Principal Research Scientist, Scaling $280,000-$350,000; Scale AI ML Research Scientist $165,600-$207,000, senior/staff agents roles $302,400-$378,000; Google DeepMind Research Scientist (US) $174,000-$252,000 and Frontier AI Research Scientist $147,000-$211,000 (mirrors of 2026 postings); Meta Superintelligence Labs AI Research Scientist $122,000-$181,000, technical-leadership role $219,000-$301,000 (mirrors, May 2026). Career path

Typical (industry): PhD (4-6 years) -> research scientist / member of technical staff (years 0-3) -> senior (years 3-7) -> staff/principal or research lead (years 7-12) -> director of a research area. Lab titles vary ("Member of Technical Staff" at several labs). Academic: PhD -> postdoc (2-4 years; one to three postings) -> assistant professor (tenure track, 5-7 years to tenure) -> associate/full professor. In Italy: dottorato -> assegno di ricerca / RTD-A -> RTD-B -> associate professor through national qualification (abilitazione); the path is long and slow (general knowledge, unverified in detail).

Exit opportunities

After 2-3 years: another lab, a startup (many researchers co-found companies), applied-research roles at big tech, or quantitative finance (hedge funds and trading firms hire ML researchers; general knowledge). After 7-10 years: research director, chief scientist or founder at an AI startup, professor, government advisor (AI safety institutes), venture capital technical partner.

Tier list of employers (alphabetical within each tier; no ranking implied inside a tier)

  • Tier 1 (the frontier): Anthropic, Google DeepMind (offices in London, Bay Area, Zurich, Paris, Tokyo, Toronto, Montreal and others per its careers page), Meta (Superintelligence Labs, including the TBD Lab and FAIR group, reorganised since 2025), OpenAI, plus the strongest academic groups (Stanford, Berkeley, MIT, CMU, Oxford, Cambridge, ETH Zurich/EPFL, Mila/Toronto). Distinguishing feature: access to the largest compute budgets, top pay, and the right to work on the frontier.
  • Tier 2: Amazon's AGI/science groups, Apple machine learning research, Cohere, Databricks, Microsoft Research, Mistral AI (Paris), NVIDIA Research, Scale AI, xAI, newer well-funded labs, and leading Chinese groups (ByteDance, Alibaba, DeepSeek; general knowledge). Strong research and good pay, but either less compute, less publishing freedom, or a narrower mission.
  • Tier 3: corporate research groups at banks, pharma and industrials; national and regional institutes (IIT, FBK, FAIR network spokes, Fraunhofer, INRIA, and in Asia and the Gulf MBZUAI, TII, Sakana AI and similar; I could not verify their standing, so placement is a judgement); mid-ranked universities. Good for applied work, lower pay and compute.
  • Reorganisations (for example the reported cut of about 600 roles at Meta's AI unit in October 2025) show that even Tier 1 job security is volatile.

Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact.

How to enter

A PhD is still the default for research scientist roles; DeepMind's careers page states that research scientists normally hold one. But several lab postings (Anthropic's interpretability and research engineer roles, for example) ask for a bachelor's degree or equivalent experience plus evidence of research ability, so the door has widened. The strongest signals: first-author papers at top venues, strong open-source or replication work, and a mentor's recommendation. Routes: a PhD at a top group (ideally with a lab internship: DeepMind runs a Student Researcher Program); external safety fellowships open to people from outside the field (Anthropic Fellows, MATS, the OpenAI Safety Fellowship and others, compared in 3.3); the ELLIS PhD programme in Europe (I could not verify details). In Italy: a master's in AI/data science/mathematics (Politecnico di Milano, Sapienza, Bocconi, Trento, Pisa, SNS) then a PhD in the FAIR network or abroad. Calendar: European PhD applications close roughly October-January, US ones in December; lab internships recruit mostly September-January (general pattern, unverified per lab). Interviews: at DeepMind, a recruiter call, two or three skills interviews, then leadership interviews (careers page); other labs test coding and research discussion (Anthropic's alignment posting says Python). Certifications do not matter. Common mistakes: applying with only coursework and no research output; chasing every trend instead of going deep; treating Kaggle as a research record. Alternative routes: physics, maths or neuroscience career changers; non-target-school candidates can win with replications, open-source work and fellowships; 80,000 Hours notes some people moved into safety research from other quantitative fields in under a year. Entry difficulty: 5/5.

Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for

The work is concentrated in a few cities and companies, hours can be punishing around launches, and pay is tied to equity in private companies whose value is uncertain. Most experiments fail, and projects get cancelled when strategy changes. Academia pays far less than industry and gives limited compute, and competition for posts is rising. In Europe the pay gap versus San Francisco is large (section 4). Not a fit for people who need predictable hours, want to work mainly with people and clients, will not relocate, or want guaranteed outcomes.

A reality check on pay, hours and tiers

Every figure here is dated and approximate, copied from the research with its source and year. Pay is in local currency and never converted. Tier lists reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact. Three limits apply to almost every page:

  • Asia and Gulf pay across almost all branches rests on aggregators, small self-reported samples (Levels.fyi has 12-40 submissions in some cities) or recruiter blogs. Tokyo is the thinnest.
  • Hours and stress are researchers' estimates from practitioner accounts, prep sites and a few surveys (mainly M&A). No report found a systematic hours survey by role family.
  • Gated recruiter guides (Hays, Robert Walters, Michael Page tables outside Italy, Management Consulted, CIPD, PMI) could not be read. Many pay figures therefore come via secondary summaries, as the reports note.

The full list of gaps, the rating scales and the researchers’ assumptions are on the sources page.
