What you actually do

In pharma or at a CRO (contract research organisation, which runs clinical trials for drug companies), you are the person who makes sure a trial's conclusions are statistically valid. Tasks: write the statistical analysis plan (the pre-agreed recipe for analysing the trial), calculate sample size, specify the randomisation, review the case report forms, analyse efficacy and safety endpoints, handle missing data, produce tables and figures for the clinical study report, and answer regulators' questions. Statistical programmers (a close neighbour role) write the SAS or R code to produce datasets in CDISC standards (SDTM/ADaM) and the outputs. In official statistics (ISTAT, ONS, Eurostat) you design surveys, weight and estimate, check quality and publish national figures such as inflation or unemployment. In public health, you analyse cohort and registry data. Tools: SAS (still dominant in regulated pharma), R, Python growing; survival analysis, mixed models, Bayesian methods.

A typical day and week

Mix of programming and analysis (40%), documentation and review (30%), meetings with clinicians and data managers (20%), and waiting for data (10%). Workload peaks around database lock and submission deadlines; calmer in between (consensus). In national statistics offices the rhythm follows publication calendars.

Hours, stress and lifestyle

38-45 typical; 50+ during database lock or regulatory submission (consensus). Stress 2/5 (occasional crunch around deadlines; accuracy pressure because errors affect regulatory decisions). Remote or hybrid is widespread, particularly at CROs (consensus). Little travel. Public sector hours are 36-40 with strong job security in Italy and the UK (consensus).

Human vs. quantitative profile

People/communication: 3/5. Quantitative/technical: 5/5. Statistical correctness is the job; you also need to explain trade-offs to physicians and regulators.

Compensation (approximate)

  • US: BLS statisticians median US$105,650 (May 2025); P10 US$64,000; P90 US$174,050; highest-paying industry computer systems design (median US$167,180), federal government US$132,620, R&D in life/physical/engineering sciences US$130,590, healthcare US$100,900. Levels.fyi lists Biostatistician as a top-paying data-scientist-adjacent title at US$143,000 median (9 Oct 2026; sample not disclosed). Entry-level biostatistician: no reliable US figure found in this round.
  • UK: Payscale (2026) average biostatistician in London GBP 49,132 (range GBP 33-58k), early-career London GBP 33,971; advertised CRO statistician roles GBP 45-55k (experienced late-phase and senior roles; undated listings); ITJobsWatch biostatistics median GBP 80,000 (6 months to 25 July 2026, 6 salaries; anecdotal). Government: ONS Higher Statistical Officer GBP 35,144 (advert closing 6 January 2026); GBP 30-35k (advert closing September 2025); ONS statistician about GBP 39.4k (2024 advert); senior statistician about GBP 43.0k; Fast Stream Statistician starts around GBP 28-31k (older figures).
  • Italy: no reliable data found for biostatistician pay. ISTAT hires through public competitions (concorsi) with a nationally set pay scale; 2026 competition for 54 researchers and 46 technologists (third level, master's degree required; a doctorate or documented research experience counted favourably) closed 22 January 2026 (ISTAT, accessed Oct 2026); pay in the call text not retrieved. GSK Siena's 2026 statistics/biostatistics graduate programme: pay not stated.
  • Continental Europe elsewhere and Asia: no reliable data found.

Career path

Junior/associate biostatistician or statistical programmer (0-3 years) → biostatistician / senior (3-6) → principal / associate director (7-10 at CROs; IQVIA's associate-director postings ask for a master's with seven years' CRO/pharma experience, or a doctorate with five) → director/VP biostatistics. Academic/public-health track: research statistician → lecturer. Official-statistics track: statistical officer → statistician → senior statistician.

Exit opportunities

After 2-3 years: another CRO or pharma company, regulatory agencies, health-data science, real-world evidence, statistical programming → data engineering, clinical data science. After 7-10 years: head of biostatistics, clinical development leadership, HEOR or market-access modelling (cross-reference Healthcare/Pharma), consultancy, regulator.

Tier list of employers

Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact.

  • Tier 1: large pharma (Roche, Novartis, AstraZeneca, GSK, Novo Nordisk, Sanofi, Pfizer, MSD, J&J, Lilly, Bayer) and top biotech; regulators (FDA, EMA) and elite academic trial units (e.g. the UK's MRC/university units). Better pay and scientific influence, hardest to enter (Roche and AXA are the top Zurich payers on Levels.fyi data scientist pages, 2026).
  • Tier 2: large CROs (IQVIA, Parexel, Syneos, ICON, Thermo Fisher PPD, Fortrea) and mid-size pharma (Italian examples: Chiesi, Menarini, Zambon; names from general knowledge, not verified for biostatistics). Large entry intake, structured training, more repetitive workload and billable-hour pressure.
  • Tier 3: small CROs, hospitals and general statistical consultancies; public-sector offices (ISTAT, ONS) sit apart: lower pay, high stability, good work-life balance, meaningful but less commercial work.

How to enter

  • Degrees: a master's in statistics or biostatistics is the standard ticket (BLS: statisticians typically need a master's, some entry jobs accept a bachelor's; IQVIA's UK graduate postings (2021-22) accepted a BSc or MSc with six months of experience). Italy: Laurea Magistrale in Biostatistica (Milano-Bicocca, taught in Italian, admission interview) or Statistical Methods and Applications (Sapienza, in English), or Statistics and Data Science at Florence with a Biostatistics curriculum (university pages, accessed Oct 2026). GSK Siena's 2026 programme requires an MSc with a strong biostatistics or medical statistics focus, R assessed, and fluent English and Italian. Mathematics, physics, engineering and economics graduates enter with a statistics master's.
  • Programmes and calendar: pharma graduate programmes recruit on a rolling or annual autumn basis (GSK is rolling); CRO graduate roles hire continuously; the UK Government Statistical Service Fast Stream applies a 3.5-year scheme and requires a 2:1 in a numerate subject with taught statistics (GOV.UK/Civil Service). ISTAT hires only through formal concorsi, announced irregularly; applications via the InPA portal, which requires a digital identity (SPID, CIE or CNS).
  • Interviews: statistics theory (confidence intervals, hypothesis tests, survival analysis, regression), SAS or R coding test, case about trial design (how many patients? what endpoint?), behavioural. Public-sector roles use written exams or the concorso format.
  • Certifications: Chartered Statistician (Royal Statistical Society) and the ASA's PStat are valued by employers, particularly in the UK and US, but not required to enter (general knowledge, not verified this round). SAS certifications help marginally.
  • Skills and portfolio: R and SAS, CDISC basics, clinical trial concepts, survival analysis; a project analysing open trial data.
  • Common mistakes: applying with machine learning projects only and no inference background; weak SAS/R; ignoring regulatory context.
  • Alternative routes: a maths or biology bachelor's plus a one-year statistics MSc; statistical programmer → biostatistician; data analysts in healthcare moving via a part-time master's.
  • Entry difficulty: 3/5 (master's required, but CROs hire many people).

Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for

Work is regulated and procedural; innovation is slower; SAS and rigid documentation can feel old-fashioned; CRO workload spikes and billable pressure; pay in Europe is moderate. Not a good fit if you want fast-moving product environments or if you dislike formal documentation and statistics theory.

A reality check on pay, hours and tiers

Every figure here is dated and approximate, copied from the research with its source and year. Pay is in local currency and never converted. Tier lists reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact. Three limits apply to almost every page:

  • Asia and Gulf pay across almost all branches rests on aggregators, small self-reported samples (Levels.fyi has 12-40 submissions in some cities) or recruiter blogs. Tokyo is the thinnest.
  • Hours and stress are researchers' estimates from practitioner accounts, prep sites and a few surveys (mainly M&A). No report found a systematic hours survey by role family.
  • Gated recruiter guides (Hays, Robert Walters, Michael Page tables outside Italy, Management Consulted, CIPD, PMI) could not be read. Many pay figures therefore come via secondary summaries, as the reports note.

The full list of gaps, the rating scales and the researchers’ assumptions are on the sources page.
