What you actually do

Allocators manage large asset pools. Roles include asset allocation and portfolio construction, manager selection and monitoring (due diligence meetings with funds, performance and risk analysis, ESG and governance checks), co-investments and direct deals (at SWFs and large Canadian pensions), liability matching (pensions and insurers) and reporting to boards. Consultants and fund-of-funds analysts do the same for many clients. Deliverables: manager due-diligence reports, investment committee papers, allocation models, reports for trustees.

A typical day and week

Calls and meetings with fund managers, internal committee preparation, risk and performance reviews, writing papers. Cycles follow board and committee calendars. Direct teams at SWFs behave like PE teams on live deals.

Hours, stress and lifestyle

40-50 hours; up to 55 during committee cycles or deals (practitioner estimate). Stress 2/5 (3 on direct investing teams). Hybrid is common (Cambridge Associates requires 12-16 office days a month for analysts, per listings). Travel: manager visits and conferences; larger SWFs have international offices.

Human vs. quantitative profile

People/communication: 3/5. Quantitative/technical: 3/5. You evaluate other people's decisions: you need judgment on people and process, solid statistics and writing.

Compensation (approximate)

Public-sector pay is lower than hedge funds and PE and published only in some places. CalPERS Investment Officer I: US$4,238-8,049 per month (about US$51-97k a year), Investment Officer II US$7,760-9,713 per month (about US$93-117k), a 5% differential for the CFA, incentives of 0-10% of salary, and a 3% pay reduction through the Personal Leave Program from July 2025 (CalPERS postings; annualised by me). CalPERS commissioned a March 2025 McLagan benchmark review (percentile tables not retrieved). Cambridge Associates (consultant) Investment Analyst Program: base US$80,000 (Boston, Dallas, Arlington) or US$88,000 (San Francisco), four-year programme, any major with GPA 3.3+, US work authorisation without sponsorship (listings via TheLadders, Class of January 2027). Canadian pensions publish senior pay in annual reports (not retrieved). GIC, Temasek, NBIM, ADIA, Mubadala, PIF: no reliable data found. UK and Italy: no reliable data found. Johnson Associates reporting does not cover allocators in the public material I found.

Career path

Analyst/associate (0-3 years), senior associate or investment officer (3-6), principal/portfolio manager/director (6-12), head of an asset class or CIO. Consultants: analyst (2-4 years), senior analyst, investment director/consultant.

Exit opportunities

After 2-3 years: PE/VC fund investor-relations or fund-of-funds roles, asset-manager distribution, private markets managers (as the "other side of the table"), MBA. After 7-10 years: CIO roles at endowments, family offices, insurers, or senior roles at managers; moving to a PE firm's fundraising team.

Tier list of employers

Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact. Tier 1 (prestige, scale, direct investing): NBIM, GIC, Temasek, ADIA, Mubadala, CPP Investments, Ontario Teachers', Yale and other large endowments. Tier 2: large public pensions and insurers (CalPERS, APG, PGGM, USS, GPIF, Allianz, AXA, Generali), and top consultants (Cambridge Associates, Mercer, WTW). Tier 3: smaller pension funds, regional foundations (Italy's Cassa Depositi e Prestiti, bank foundations, professional pension funds such as Cassa Forense or Enpam), and regional consultants, which offer stability and a narrower investment scope.

How to enter

SWFs run graduate programmes: NBIM's graduate programme lasts 12 months (nine on projects with the AI team, then three in a business area) for final-year students or graduates with up to two years' experience, in Oslo, London or New York; applications for the 2027 programme ran 1-15 August 2026 (NBIM). Temasek's Associate Programme in Singapore runs 20 months from 3 August 2026 to 31 March 2028; applications opened 2 March 2026 and closed 27 March 2026, with a video interview and a half-day case-study assessment (Temasek listing); summer 2027 internship information was due in July 2026. GIC's Professionals Programme takes final-year students from any discipline with less than a year of work experience on a rolling basis, and its 12-week internship targets penultimate-year students (GIC, via a mirror page; verify on the official site). PIF's Graduate Development Program is for Saudi nationals only (PIF). Japan's GPIF is expanding a small team (187 staff, Bloomberg, 11 August 2025). Gulf SWFs often prioritise nationals for graduate schemes (I found no eligibility page for ADIA or Mubadala graduate programmes; treat as unverified). For consultants, Cambridge Associates and Mercer hire graduates directly. CFA and CAIA are valued here; CFA is explicitly rewarded at CalPERS. Skills: statistics, portfolio theory, private markets knowledge, writing. Common mistakes: expecting the same pay as hedge funds; applying without a view on asset allocation. Alternative routes: asset-manager client roles, performance/risk teams, auditing of funds. Entry difficulty: 4/5 (very high at SWFs, moderate at consultants).

Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for

Slower progression, public-sector pay, committee decision-making and political constraints; at SWFs, nationality and visa rules can restrict access; you rarely own the investment decision. Not for you if you want to be a stockpicker with a big bonus, or want to move fast.

A reality check on pay, hours and tiers

Every figure here is dated and approximate, copied from the research with its source and year. Pay is in local currency and never converted. Tier lists reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact. Three limits apply to almost every page:

  • Asia and Gulf pay across almost all branches rests on aggregators, small self-reported samples (Levels.fyi has 12-40 submissions in some cities) or recruiter blogs. Tokyo is the thinnest.
  • Hours and stress are researchers' estimates from practitioner accounts, prep sites and a few surveys (mainly M&A). No report found a systematic hours survey by role family.
  • Gated recruiter guides (Hays, Robert Walters, Michael Page tables outside Italy, Management Consulted, CIPD, PMI) could not be read. Many pay figures therefore come via secondary summaries, as the reports note.

The full list of gaps, the rating scales and the researchers’ assumptions are on the sources page.
