What you actually do

You sit inside a product team (checkout, search, ads, growth) and own the numbers behind decisions. Concrete tasks: define the success metric and guardrail metrics for a feature; design and size an A/B test (power, sample size, test duration); write SQL against event tables to compute conversion lift and confidence intervals; investigate why a metric dropped 3% on Tuesday; build a causal estimate when you cannot randomise (difference-in-differences, matching, instrumental variables); forecast user growth; present a recommendation ("ship, don't ship, or run it longer") to a product manager and engineers. Tools: SQL (daily), Python or R, a notebook environment, an experimentation platform, dashboards. Deliverables: experiment readouts, metric definitions, analysis memos, occasionally a model.

A typical day and week

Mornings: check experiment dashboards and metric alerts; answer ad-hoc questions in chat. Midday: write SQL and Python for an analysis; a 30-minute sync with the product manager and designer. Afternoon: write up a readout. Weekly: planning meeting, experiment review, a deeper project (e.g. redefining "active user"). Roughly 30-40% of time is meetings and communication (consensus).

Hours, stress and lifestyle

Typical 40-50 hours a week, peaks around launches or quarter planning of 55-60 (consensus; no survey found). Stress 3/5: steady deadlines, pressure when a launch decision hangs on your readout, but rarely overnight crises. Flexible hybrid is common in tech; fully remote less so since 2023 (consensus). No travel beyond offsites. In the US the big employers pay for the flexibility with at-will culture and periodic layoffs (consensus).

Human vs. quantitative profile

People/communication: 4/5. Quantitative/technical: 3/5. Your statistics must be correct, but the scarce skill is persuading a non-technical team to act on a result, so Meta's interview rubric reportedly values product thinking over complicated maths (Glassdoor candidate report, anecdotal).

Compensation (approximate)

  • US (Levels.fyi, 9 Oct 2026, total compensation = base + stock + bonus): Google data scientist L3 US$196k, L4 US$281k, L5 (senior) US$380k; Meta IC3 US$153k, IC4 US$240k, IC5 US$375k, IC6 US$486k (median all levels US$320k); all-employer US median US$180k (n=3,376), 25th percentile US$134k, 75th US$250k, San Francisco Bay Area median US$250k, NYC US$190-200k. Self-reported and skewed to big tech; BLS median for all data scientists is US$120,230 (May 2025), 10th percentile US$67,240, 90th US$199,130.
  • UK: London all-levels median GBP 91,096 (Levels.fyi, 9 Oct 2026, n=326; Meta ~GBP 144.7k and JPMorgan ~GBP 143.8k at the top); 25th percentile GBP 65.7k. No reliable entry-level figure for big-tech product data science found; Morgan McKinley's 2026 guide gives GBP 85-100k as an average London range for data scientists (all levels).
  • Continental Europe: Germany median EUR 77,643 (Levels.fyi, 9 Oct 2026, n=207); Berlin EUR 112,951; Delivery Hero EUR 108k, Wolt EUR 107k, Zalando EUR 102k. Italy: median EUR 46,145 (n=23, all levels), Milan EUR 56,058 (small sample); Glassdoor (Feb 2026, 214 salaries) junior data scientist in Italy about EUR 29,000 gross (EUR 26-33k); Indeed Italy average EUR 34,617 (Aug 2026, 160 salaries). Pay for pure product data science in Italy: no reliable data found (the few such roles are at multinationals' Milan offices or Italian scale-ups).
  • Asia hubs: Singapore median SGD 120,084 (Levels.fyi, n=128), Grab ~SGD 160k; Tokyo median JPY 8.76m (n=16; PayPay ~JPY 18.4m); Hong Kong median HKD 803k (n=12); Dubai median AED 437,560 (n=20). The last three are very thin samples.

Career path

Data scientist (0-3 years; Google L3/Meta IC3) → data scientist II/senior (3-6 years; L4-L5/IC4-IC5) → staff/lead, or manager of data science (7-10 years). Titles drift: "product analyst" or "analytics" at smaller firms.

Exit opportunities

After 2-3 years: ML/AI roles at the same firm, product management, growth/strategy at start-ups, other tech employers. After 7-10 years: head of analytics/data science, product management leadership, chief-of-staff or strategy roles, startup founder, consulting. Meta's Alex Schultz is a recent example of a growth/analytics leader being promoted to chief data officer (July 2026).

Tier list of employers

Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact.

  • Tier 1: Meta, Google, Netflix, Airbnb, Uber, Stripe, DoorDash, Pinterest, Snap. Highest total compensation (Netflix US$500k, DoorDash and Stripe US$450k median on Levels.fyi), most rigorous experimentation cultures and tooling, but the hardest interviews and the thinnest entry intake.
  • Tier 2: Microsoft, Amazon, Spotify, Booking.com, Zalando, Delivery Hero, Wolt, Revolut, Monzo, Grab, Rakuten. Strong analytics, pay 30-50% below Tier 1 in the US, closer in Europe, broader scope.
  • Tier 3: retailers, telecoms, media, marketplaces and fintechs outside the top group; agencies. Smaller teams, less mature experimentation, more dashboard work, lower pay, more generalist learning.

How to enter

  • Degrees and backgrounds: Bachelor's in statistics, mathematics, economics, computer science, engineering or a quantitative social science; a master's is common at the top firms but not required. Economists with causal-inference training are a classic feeder. Bachelor's is BLS's typical entry requirement for data scientists; some employers want a master's.
  • Internships and graduate programmes: US tech internships (summer, applications opened in the preceding autumn). Monzo's Associate Data Scientist internship (London, STEM/CS degree graduating 2028, SQL and Looker; employer listing, 30 Sep 2026) is an example of European early-stage hiring. Recruiting calendars: US autumn (Aug-Oct) for the following summer; UK graduate schemes open Aug-Sep and many close Sept-Dec (consensus, consistent with the banking examples in 3.5).
  • Interviews: for Meta, a recruiter screen, a technical phone screen on SQL and probability, then a 4-5 round virtual onsite covering SQL (window functions, rolling averages), product sense (define metrics and guardrails for a fictional app), experimentation (sample size, power, novelty and network effects), statistics intuition and behavioural questions (Interview Query and Glassdoor candidate reports, 2025-26; anecdotal). Take-homes are common at smaller firms.
  • Certifications: do not matter here; a portfolio does. Skills and portfolio: strong SQL, Python (pandas, statsmodels), hypothesis testing, causal inference basics (Mostly Harmless Econometrics-style methods), a project with a real experiment or natural experiment (e.g. a public dataset where you estimate the effect of a policy or a price change).
  • Common mistakes: overweighting machine learning (product roles test SQL and judgement), ignoring business context, reporting p-values without effect sizes, poor communication.
  • Alternative routes: analyst → data scientist after 1-3 years inside the same company; business or marketing graduates can reach product analytics via strong SQL, a statistics minor and a reference project; non-target schools rely on projects and smaller firms.
  • Entry difficulty: 4/5 at Tier 1 and 2 (hundreds of applicants per opening, consensus; 2025-26 junior freeze), 3/5 elsewhere.

Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for

Entry intake has contracted as AI tools absorb routine analysis; the role at some firms has become a dashboard-and-ad-hoc-queries job with limited influence; experiments often produce flat results and stakeholders may ignore them; US big-tech layoffs happen. Not a good fit if you want to build models all day (that is ML data science or ML engineering) or you dislike negotiating with product managers.

A reality check on pay, hours and tiers

Every figure here is dated and approximate, copied from the research with its source and year. Pay is in local currency and never converted. Tier lists reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact. Three limits apply to almost every page:

  • Asia and Gulf pay across almost all branches rests on aggregators, small self-reported samples (Levels.fyi has 12-40 submissions in some cities) or recruiter blogs. Tokyo is the thinnest.
  • Hours and stress are researchers' estimates from practitioner accounts, prep sites and a few surveys (mainly M&A). No report found a systematic hours survey by role family.
  • Gated recruiter guides (Hays, Robert Walters, Michael Page tables outside Italy, Management Consulted, CIPD, PMI) could not be read. Many pay figures therefore come via secondary summaries, as the reports note.

The full list of gaps, the rating scales and the researchers’ assumptions are on the sources page.
