# Trends

## Bottom line (ranked by how much each should change a decision today)

1. **The entry-level market is the tightest in decades, and it tightened through hiring freezes rather than layoffs.** UK employers received a record 140 applications per graduate vacancy. French grande-école graduates' six-month employment rate fell from 90.5% to 76% in three years. US recent-graduate unemployment is 5.6% against a 4.3% national rate. A master's does not exempt anyone from this. It changes which queue you are in.
2. **AI is cutting junior hiring in exposed occupations, not wages, and not (yet) whole professions.** One in three employers in GMAC's 2026 recruiter survey say they already replace some entry-level roles with AI. The best payroll evidence shows 22–25-year-olds in the most AI-exposed jobs about 19% below trend by mid-2026, while experienced workers in the same jobs are unaffected. The adjustment happens through fewer hires, so the cost falls almost entirely on new entrants.
3. **The squeeze is uneven by employer type.** Big-4 audit and tax cut UK graduate intake by 6–29% between 2023 and 2024–25. McKinsey announced a 12% increase in North American entry hiring for 2026. FMCG and retail are the most over-subscribed (290 applications per vacancy). "Business graduates" is no longer one market.
4. **Post-study work rights are shrinking in the UK just as the market tightens, and US rules are in flux.** The UK Graduate Route falls from 24 to 18 months for applications from 1 January 2027. In the US, OPT and STEM OPT remain in force unchanged; a rule ending "duration of status" is postponed by a court, curricular practical training has been narrowed by SEVP messages that are under legal challenge, and an OPT-fees proposal is unpublished (`visas_immigration/united_states/united_states_visas_immigration_guide.md`, section 3). This mostly matters for non-EU readers, and for EU readers who assume London is still open (it isn't without a visa; see places/visas-and-work-rights.md).
5. **Real starting pay is flat.** French grande-école starting pay rose 0.2% nominal in 2026 (−0.2% real). Top-consulting US starting pay was reported frozen for a third year in 2026. The "master's premium" at entry is not growing.
6. **Internships and apprenticeships are now the main hiring door in continental Europe.** In France, 42.3% of grande-école graduates' first jobs came through the end-of-studies internship or an apprenticeship (CGE 2026). A programme's internship structure matters more than its rank for many readers.
7. **Employers say AI fluency is the skill they will value most in five years, but they still hire on problem-solving and communication now** (GMAC Corporate Recruiters Survey 2025). "AI skills" are a tiebreaker, not a substitute for the core screen.
8. **Demand is shifting from broad MBAs towards specialised pre-experience master's, and ROI is the top decision factor** (GMAC Prospective Students Survey 2025). More competition for the same MiM/MiF seats, and more scrutiny of outcomes, is good for readers who check employment reports.

**How to read this.**
- The squeeze is real, but it shows up as *delay*, not permanent exclusion. French graduates move from 76% employed at six months to ~91–95% within two to three years. The cost of a weak market is mainly a longer search and a worse first match. Both still matter (see decisions/timing-and-sequencing.md on graduating-into-a-recession scarring).
- Headline advert counts overstate the fall. Two UK job-board datasets disagree by a factor of 80 (−40% vs −0.5%), and two-thirds of ISE employers kept or raised hiring. Counts of applications per vacancy are inflated by AI-assisted mass applying (ISE 2025 commentary) [practitioner consensus]. Take the "140" as evidence of *noise at the top of the funnel*, not of 140 equally strong rivals.
- **Non-obvious implication:** when every employer receives hundreds of AI-polished applications, the channels that bypass the open funnel gain value: referrals, internships that convert, school-specific pipelines and apprenticeships. See getting-in/breaking-in.md and getting-in/employer-pipelines.md.

**What this means by function (working view; sector files hold the detail).**
- **Most exposed first jobs:** audit and tax preparation, junior research and reporting, content and copy production in agencies, first-line analytics that is mostly querying and charting, and pitchbook formatting. These are high-volume doors that are narrowing [practitioner consensus; see careers/accounting-and-corporate.md, careers/marketing.md, careers/finance.md].
- **Less exposed / complemented:** client-facing roles, sales and business development, roles whose output is a judgement or a relationship, and roles that *operate* AI tools on proprietary data.
- **The firm-level split matters.** The same wave produced Big-4 cuts and a McKinsey expansion. A plausible reading is that firms whose junior layer is mostly *production* shrink it, while firms whose junior layer is also the *partner pipeline* keep it [practitioner consensus — hypothesis, see evidence/hypotheses.md T-2].

## 3. Visa and mobility tightening (detail in places/visas-and-work-rights.md)

- **UK Graduate Route:** bachelor's and master's graduates applying from **1 January 2027** get 18 months instead of 24; PhDs keep 3 years. The cut-off is the *visa application date*, so most students on courses longer than 12 months who began in September 2025 are affected (Statement of Changes, 14 Oct 2025; ICEF Monitor, 16 Oct 2025: https://monitor.icef.com/2025/10/uk-to-implement-reduced-graduate-route-from-january-2027/) [data].
- The same package **expanded the High Potential Individual visa** for graduates of top-100 global universities, capped at 8,000 applications a year (ICEF, same) [data]. For graduates of HPI-listed schools this partly offsets the Graduate Route cut.
- **Implication for EU readers:** since Brexit, an Italian graduate needs a UK visa exactly as a non-EU graduate does. The 2027 cut therefore applies to them too. A one-year UK MSc started in September 2026 finishes in 2027, so its Graduate Route application falls under the 18-month rule.

## 4. Pay: flat at entry

- CGE 2026: average gross starting salary (excluding bonus) €39,679 overall, €40,825 for management-school graduates. That is +0.2% nominal, −0.2% real. Gender gap 5.8% overall and 7.2% among management graduates (L'Express Éducation, Aug 2026) [data].
- Top consulting starting pay in the US reported frozen for a third consecutive year in 2026 (undergraduate total comp $137–140k; MBA $267–285k, including maximum bonus and signing bonus; Management Consulted's 2026 report as quoted by Poets&Quants for Undergrads, 26 Jan 2026, https://poetsandquantsforundergrads.com/students/consulting-pay-what-business-graduates-earned-in-2025/; the data are Management Consulted's own client offers, not firm-published) [practitioner consensus].
- **Implication:** with flat entry pay and rising tuition, the payback period of an expensive master's is lengthening. See money/salaries-and-roi.md and money/costs-and-funding.md.

## 5. Demand for master's degrees

- GMAC Prospective Students Survey 2025 (4,912 respondents, surveyed during 2024): stated interest in business master's rose; there was a "meaningful uptick" in preference for specialised business master's among pre-experience candidates; ROI was the most-cited decision factor; interest in tech careers dipped (GMAC, 2025: https://www.gmac.com/market-intelligence-and-research/research-library/admissions-and-application-trends/2025-gmac-prospective-students-survey-summary-report) [data].
- Consulting and finance remain the most sought-after post-degree paths (same) [data]. This is exactly where intake is flat or shrinking, so the ratio of aspirants to seats is rising.

## Hypotheses tested

**T-1. "The squeeze is cyclical and will reverse with deal and economic recovery."**
Verdict: *partly supported.* The French data show a clear cyclical profile: fast recovery at 12–30 months, and a fall that tracks the 2023–25 slowdown. The Stanford evidence shows a *structural* component concentrated in AI-exposed jobs, and it widened between Aug 2025 and Aug 2026 rather than narrowing. Holds for client-facing and relationship roles. Fails for production-heavy junior roles (audit, content, basic analysis), where lower intake may persist.

**T-2. "AI shrinks junior intake most where the junior layer is production, not pipeline."**
Verdict: *partly supported (early).* Big-4 audit/tax cuts (6–29%) versus McKinsey's +12% fit the pattern. The Stanford finding that declines concentrate where AI substitutes for tasks is consistent with it. It fails as a general rule, because MBB pay freezes and delayed starts also appeared in 2024–26 (see careers/consulting.md). Evidence is employer-stated and secondary. Treat it as a working hypothesis.

**T-3. "Record applications per vacancy mean a candidate's odds are ~1 in 140."**
Verdict: *not supported.* Application counts are inflated by mass, AI-assisted applying and by one candidate applying to many employers. Two-thirds of ISE employers held or grew hiring, and 97% of graduate roles were filled. The relevant odds depend on the channel (referral, internship conversion, school pipeline) far more than on the open-funnel ratio.

**T-4. "A master's protects against the entry-level squeeze."**
Verdict: *insufficient evidence as stated; likely false for weak programmes.* CGE grande-école graduates are master's-level and still saw employment at six months fall 15 points in three years. What protects is the programme's employer pipeline and internship conversion, not the degree level. See decisions/programme-choice.md and getting-in/employer-pipelines.md.

## Common mistakes and myths

- *"AI means no one will hire juniors."* The best data show a relative decline concentrated in specific occupations, not an end to entry-level hiring. McKinsey and others are expanding. [data + employer-stated]
- *"The job market is fine, it's just headlines."* Also wrong. Employment rates at six months and entry intakes at the largest graduate employers did fall. The accurate framing is "slower and more channel-dependent". [data]
- *"AI skills will get me hired."* Employers still rank problem-solving and communication first *today* (GMAC 2025). AI fluency is a differentiator, and it is also what AI-assisted rivals now all claim. [data]
- *"The UK is still the default for Europeans."* Post-Brexit visa requirements plus the 2027 Graduate Route cut make the UK a visa-dependent choice for EU citizens as well. [data]

## Decision rules

1. **If** your target first job is production-heavy (audit, tax, content, basic reporting), **prefer** a programme with a strong internship-to-offer pipeline, and build a skill that complements AI (client work, data engineering on real data, sales), **because** junior intake in these roles is shrinking through reduced hiring.
2. **If** you are choosing between two programmes of similar rank, **prefer** the one that places more graduates through internships or apprenticeships in your target country, **because** in France 42% of first jobs came through those channels, and open-funnel applications face 100+ applicants per vacancy.
3. **If** you are a non-UK citizen (EU included) planning a one-year UK MSc starting 2026 or later, **plan** for an 18-month Graduate Route, not 24 months, and check whether your school is HPI-listed, **because** the rule depends on the visa application date.
4. **If** you are deciding whether to "wait out" a weak market with a master's, **then** do it only if the master's opens a *specific* new channel (new country, new pipeline, conversion into a new field), **because** a master's alone didn't prevent a 15-point employment fall among grande-école graduates.
5. **If** a programme's employment report is older than 2023, **treat** its placement figures as a ceiling, **because** 2023 was the last strong year before the downturn in most European graduate markets.
6. **If** you are comparing application volumes or "acceptance odds" for employers, **ignore** applications-per-vacancy headlines and **ask** alumni how they got in, **because** the channel mix decides the real odds.
