# Ranking methodologies dissected

## Bottom line

1. **Different rankings crown different winners because they measure different things.** In September 2026 the FT put the University of St. Gallen first in its Masters in Management for the 15th time in 16 years (Poets&Quants, 6 Sept 2026). QS, a year earlier, put HEC Paris first in management, finance and marketing, and did not rank St. Gallen's MiM at all (Poets&Quants, 21 Sept 2025) [data]. Neither is "wrong". They weight different inputs.
2. **A rank is a weighted sum of a few survey answers and many school-submitted numbers.**
   - **FT MiM 2026:** 56% of the weight comes from alumni responses and 44% from data supplied by the schools. Only schools accredited by AACSB or EQUIS can enter, and 132 programmes took part (GMAC summary of the FT method, 9 Sept 2026) [data, secondary].
   - **Weighted salary alone is 15%** (Poets&Quants, 2026). The FT table lists 19 scored criteria. About eight relate to careers and outcomes; the rest cover gender and international mix, faculty and sustainability (FT MiM 2026 table, header read by the lead) [data].
3. **Salary and employment inputs are self-reported, and coverage varies.** Georgetown McDonough entered the 2026 FT table with an 87% "employed at three months" figure that covers 43% of its class. Bocconi's MSc International Management shows 100% on 55% coverage (FT MiM 2026 table) [data].
4. **Scale differs wildly between programmes that are ranked side by side.** In the 2026 table, St. Gallen's ranked MiM programme enrolled 58 students, HEC Paris 684 and LBS 298 (FT MiM 2026 table) [data]. In 2020 Poets&Quants made the same point (46 vs 490 students) and added that the FT "fails to reveal the underlying index numbers", so score gaps can't be judged [practitioner consensus].
5. **Manipulation of ranking data is proven, not hypothetical.**
   - **Temple University's Fox School of Business:** its former dean, Moshe Porat, was convicted of fraud in December 2021 and sentenced in March 2022 to one year and two months in prison and a $250,000 fine. False data on the online and part-time MBA programmes went to U.S. News from at least 2014 to 2018 (US Attorney, Eastern District of Pennsylvania) [data].
   - **Columbia University:** after a mathematician (Michael Thaddeus) showed its U.S. News inputs were inaccurate, Columbia said it "deeply regrets deficiencies in prior reporting". U.S. News removed it from the 2022 ranking, and Columbia settled a student class action for $9 million in 2025 without admitting wrongdoing (Boston Globe, 1 July 2025) [data].
6. **Ranking publishers have commercial incentives.** A peer-reviewed model by Dearden, Grewal and Lilien (Journal of Marketing Research, 2019) shows that a profit-motivated ranking publisher should choose weights that do not match student preferences and change them over time, even when students' preferences haven't changed [data, theoretical].
7. **The honest use of a ranking:** as a coarse tier filter and a way to find schools you haven't heard of, not as a measure of how good a programme will be for you (see decision rules).

## 1. The FT Masters in Management, taken apart

**Source for this section:** the FT MiM 2026 full table (PDF mirror, read by the lead), a GMAC summary of the FT method (9 Sept 2026), and Poets&Quants coverage (6 Sept 2026 and 28 Sept 2020). The FT's own methodology page is paywalled.

| Design choice | What it is | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Entry gate** | Full-time, cohort-based MiM for students with little or no work experience; AACSB or EQUIS accreditation required; 132 programmes took part in 2026 (137 in 2025), 100 are ranked | Schools that don't participate or lack the accreditation are invisible. Not being listed doesn't mean being weak |
| **Alumni vs school data** | 56% from an alumni survey three years after graduation; 44% from the school's own submission | Both are self-reported. School data isn't independently audited (see §5 for what happens when it is false) |
| **Salary** | Weighted salary is 15% of the score; it is converted to US dollars at purchasing power parity and weighted by sector | PPP and sector weights change what the number means (evidence/how-numbers-mislead.md B1) |
| **Criteria count** | 19 scored columns: weighted salary, salary increase, value for money, career progress, aims achieved, careers service, alumni network, employed at three months, female faculty, female students, women on board, international faculty, students and board, international work mobility, international course experience, faculty with doctorates, ESG teaching and carbon footprint. Course length, enrolment and satisfaction are also listed | A programme can rise on diversity, internationalisation or sustainability scores without any change in graduate outcomes. Weights for most criteria aren't published in the sources read |
| **Tiers** | Since 2026 the FT groups the 100 schools into four tiers of 8, 32, 51 and 9 | The FT itself signals that rank differences within a tier are not meaningful. Read the tier |
| **Coverage** | The "employed at three months" column shows the share of the class with data in brackets | Bocconi 55%, HEC Lausanne 46%, TUM 15%, Georgetown 43% (FT MiM 2026) |
| **Size** | Enrolment column (2025–26): St. Gallen 58, LBS 298, HEC Paris 684 | A very small programme can sit at the top on a handful of alumni |
| **Historic concentration** | St. Gallen first 15 times in 16 years; HEC won in 2023 | Stability can reflect a method that favours certain features. The 2020 Poets&Quants critique called such consistency a sign of what is wrong with the list [practitioner consensus] |

**What the FT does well.** It publishes the full table with each criterion and the coverage figure, which lets you do the checks in this library. It dropped TBS Education in 2025 after allegations of inflated salary reporting, and let it return in 2026 at 81st (Poets&Quants, 2026) [data].

## 2. QS Business Masters

**Sources:** Poets&Quants on the QS 2026 ranking (21 Sept 2025); QS's methodology pages (403 on direct fetch, so secondary summaries only).

- **Five "lenses" with discipline-specific weights.** For Management, Marketing and Supply Chain: Employability 35%, Alumni Outcomes 15%, Value for Money 20%, Thought Leadership 20%, Diversity 10%. For Finance and Business Analytics: Employability 30%, Alumni Outcomes 20%, Value for Money 20%, Thought Leadership 20%, Diversity 10% (Poets&Quants, 21 Sept 2025) [practitioner consensus].
- **Reputation surveys and external databases.** Employability draws on an employer reputation survey; alumni outcomes are computed from schools associated with 50,000+ CEOs, executives and board members; thought leadership uses an academic peer survey and research indicators; ROI is calculated over 10 years with a payback-month indicator (same source) [practitioner consensus].
- **Schools may nominate** up to 400 employers for the employer survey (QS search-result summary; unverified, see Claims to verify).
- **What it means for readers.** Reputation surveys reward schools that are well known to the surveyed employers and academics. Alumni-prominence measures favour old, large and Anglo-American schools. A ten-year ROI model depends on assumptions the reader can't see.

**Comparing the two:** the FT puts weighted salary at 15% within a 56% alumni block. QS builds roughly a third of its management score from employability and a fifth from "thought leadership" (research and peer reputation). A programme can rank very differently in each without any change in graduate outcomes.

## 3. Eduniversal Best Masters

**Source:** Eduniversal methodology page (read 2026-10-01).
- Three criteria, each worth 5 points: programme reputation (50% recruiter opinions and 50% the school's Palme of Excellence level), graduates' salary in the first position, and student satisfaction. Programmes get one to four stars by total score (Eduniversal methodology; search summary of the scoring bands) [data].
- **Observed limits:** the page doesn't say how salary is collected, verified or audited, how many programmes are evaluated, or how recruiter opinions are sampled. Palme levels come from a deans' vote and from accreditations and memberships (search summary) [data].
- **Reading it:** half of the reputation score is the school's institutional rating, not the programme's outcomes. Star ratings compress very different programmes into four bands.

## 4. U.S. News, the national press rankings and the law-school exit

- **U.S. News** is the benchmark for US programmes and shaped the global ranking industry. In November 2022 Yale Law School and Harvard Law School withdrew from its law rankings. Yale's dean, Heather Gerken, called the methodology "profoundly flawed" and said it discourages support for public-interest careers, need-based aid and working-class students. She added that graduates in public-interest fellowships were effectively classified as unemployed (Bloomberg Law, 16 Nov 2022) [data].
- **National rankings** (Il Sole 24 Ore and Censis in Italy; Le Figaro, L'Étudiant and Challenges in France; Handelsblatt and WirtschaftsWoche in Germany) use their own mixes of student surveys, employer surveys and data supplied by institutions. Of these, only the Italian ones were looked at here, and only through a search summary: criticism there includes that some parameters (internationalisation, employment success) depend on geography and local economy, and that a "talent" parameter based on school-leaving marks is inflated in some regions (search-result summary; unverified). They need a dedicated review (Claims to verify).

## 5. Documented cases of manipulation and false reporting

| Case | What happened | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Temple University, Fox School of Business (US)** | The dean, a professor and an employee submitted false data on the online MBA and part-time MBA to U.S. News from at least 2014 to 2018. Examples include claiming that 100% of online MBA students took the GMAT when only 20% had. The online MBA was ranked first four years in a row. Enrolment rose from 70 (2013) to 356 (2017) | **Convicted** (jury, Dec 2021); dean sentenced 11 Mar 2022 to 14 months in prison and a $250,000 fine | US Attorney EDPA press releases; Inside Higher Ed |
| **Columbia University (US undergraduate)** | A mathematician showed class-size and faculty figures were inaccurate. U.S. News could not verify Columbia's data and unranked it, dropping it from a three-way tie for second to 18th. Columbia said it "deeply regrets deficiencies in prior reporting" | **Admitted deficiencies**; settled a student class action for $9 million (July 2025), no formal admission of wrongdoing | Columbia statements; Boston Globe, 1 July 2025 |
| **TBS Education (France)** | Accused of coaching alumni to inflate reported salaries; removed from the FT MiM 2025 table; returned at 81st in 2026 | **Allegation only**: reported by Poets&Quants; the FT's absence from one table is documented, but no court, regulator or FT finding was read and the school's response was not read. Do not describe as fraud or misreporting | Poets&Quants, 4 Mar 2025 and 6 Sept 2026 |

**What these cases show.** Rankings that rely on self-submitted data create strong incentives to misreport, and checking is limited. Two of the three cases were found by outsiders or whistleblowers, not by routine audit. Most schools do not do this. The point isn't that every number is false but that **unaudited numbers need a documented reporting rule and a response rate** before they can be trusted.

## 6. The academic critique

- **Dearden, Grewal and Lilien (Journal of Marketing Research, 2019)** model a ranking publisher and prospective students. Rankings carry information about quality and also create prestige. The authors show that a profit-motivated publisher should choose weights that don't match what students value and change them over time. They propose a student-optimal weighting [data, theoretical model, not an audit of any real ranking].
- **John A. Byrne (Poets&Quants, 28 Sept 2020)** on the FT MiM: the FT reveals no underlying index scores; a small programme can top the table next to large ones; the ranking doesn't measure incoming student quality or teaching; no standalone US MiM programme is on the list [practitioner consensus, opinion article].
- **Selection effect.** Rankings that credit a school with its graduates' outcomes ignore that selective schools admit strong students (Dale & Krueger, NBER w7322; evidence/how-numbers-mislead.md C1) [data].

## 7. How to read a ranking claim in 60 seconds (for the site)

1. **Which ranking, which year, which sub-list?** (FT MiM ≠ FT MiF ≠ QS Management ≠ QS Finance.)
2. **Which tier**, not rank? Within the same tier, ignore rank gaps below about 15 places (decisions/school-types-and-accreditation.md).
3. **How big is the programme?** (58 vs 684 students.)
4. **What share of the class is behind the employment and salary figures?** (the bracket in the FT table).
5. **Is the school on the list because it applied?** (Entry gates: accreditation, participation.)
6. **What is the criterion mix?** (How many of the columns are about careers?)
7. **Does the outcome I want appear in the ranking at all?** (E.g. London IB placement is not a column.)

## Hypotheses tested

**R-1. "Rankings agree on which programmes are best."** Verdict: **not supported.** The FT (St. Gallen 1st) and QS (HEC 1st in management, finance and marketing; St. Gallen MiM not ranked) disagree at the top because their inputs and entry rules differ [data].

**R-2. "Ranking data are audited."** Verdict: **not supported.** FT school data and alumni answers are self-reported; the cases above show that false submissions can go undetected for years [data].

**R-3. "Most ranking criteria measure what a student cares about (career outcomes)."** Verdict: **not supported for the FT table.** Eight of its 19 scored criteria relate to careers and outcomes; the rest cover diversity, internationalisation, faculty and sustainability. Weights beyond salary (15%) were not found [data, count not weight].

**R-4. "Ranking publishers set weights in students' interest."** Verdict: **insufficient evidence on real rankings; theory says incentives point the other way** (Dearden et al. 2019).

**R-5. "Large schools are favoured by size-neutral rankings."** Verdict: **insufficient evidence.** Programme size varies by more than ten times in the FT table, but no study of its effect on rank was found.

## Common mistakes and myths

- *"Number one in the FT means best for finance/consulting/marketing."* The MiM ranking is a different list from the MiF ranking, and neither measures sector-specific placement.
- *"QS and FT will say the same thing."* They don't (R-1).
- *"Not in the ranking means not good."* Participation and accreditation gates (FT) and discipline lists (QS) exclude programmes.
- *"A scandal means all rankings are fake."* The cases are specific and were found by outsiders. The practical lesson is to look for coverage figures and reporting rules.
- *"A ranking measures the school's effect."* It mostly measures who got in, plus what they reported.

## Decision rules

1. **If** you compare programmes, **use** the FT tier and the coverage bracket first, and ignore rank gaps under ~15 places outside the top 30.
2. **If** a school's claim cites only a sub-ranking, **ask** for the programme's own employment report and the ranking's tier (evidence/how-numbers-mislead.md B5).
3. **If** a ranking's criteria are mostly non-career, **don't** use it to predict your job outcome. Use employer-specific placement data (getting-in/employer-pipelines.md).
4. **If** two rankings disagree on the top, **read** the entry rules and weights before deciding which to trust.
5. **If** a number comes from a school's own submission, **look** for coverage, a stated reporting standard and a reputational cost for misreporting.
6. **If** a school's marketing leads with a ranking and omits outcome data for your programme, **ask** why.

## What this changes in the site

- **"Ranking X-ray" page** (extends the Report X-ray tool in product/product-map.md): for each FT/QS list, show entry gates, tier, coverage, programme size and the criterion mix. Link to the original publication, with the date.
- **"Documented cases: convictions, admissions and allegations" box** with Temple, Columbia and TBS, worded exactly as in §5 (convicted / admitted deficiencies / accused). Never put an allegation under a heading that says "proven" or "fraud": each case carries its own status label, and TBS carries "allegation, no finding read" only. Publishing an unproven accusation as established fact about a named school risks a defamation or commercial-disparagement claim (for example *dénigrement* in France).
- **Weights table** for FT, QS and Eduniversal, dated, with a "weights change yearly" notice.

## Numbers to treat with care

- FT weights other than 56/44 and 15% weighted salary: not read from the FT (paywalled). The 20% and 10% weights seen in older sources date from 2020.
- QS weights from a Poets&Quants summary of QS (the QS methodology pages blocked direct fetch).
- The Eduniversal scoring bands come from a search-result summary.
- Programme sizes (58, 298, 684) are from the FT table's enrolment column; cohort definitions are the FT's.

## Claims to verify

1. The FT's own methodology page (weights for every criterion, treatment of non-respondents, PPP source, outlier trimming).
2. QS's primary methodology page and employer-survey size, response rate and the 400-employer nomination rule.
3. Eduniversal's scoring bands and how many programmes are rated.
4. Italian national rankings (Censis, Il Sole 24 Ore): primary methodology and criticisms (only a search summary read).
5. French and German press rankings (Le Figaro/L'Étudiant/Challenges; Handelsblatt/WiWo): methodology.
6. The outcome of the TBS allegations (FT statement, any regulator finding).
7. The text of U.S. News' response to the Columbia case and the current status of the Temple dean's appeal.
