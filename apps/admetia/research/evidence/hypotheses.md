## The three assigned hypotheses

### H1. "A public university in Switzerland can lead to higher starting pay than a London business school, but slower long-term career growth."
**Verdict: Partly supported.** The starting-pay half is strongly supported. The slower-growth half is supported only in percentage terms to year three, and is unproven beyond that.

| Evidence | Figure | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Swiss university economics master's, median one year out † | CHF 87,100 (≈ €92k) | [data], BFS 2024 |
| LBS MiM, working in the UK, mean base † | £44,675 (≈ €52k) | [data], LBS 2024 |
| Net / after a central one-bed rent | ≈ €74k vs €42k net; ≈ €3,500 vs €900 a month left | author calculation, money/salaries-and-roi.md §5 |
| FT "salary three years after", PPP † | HSG $150,490 vs LBS $119,990 | [data], FT MiM 2026 |
| Swiss applied-sciences bachelor's vs university master's † | CHF 80,300 vs 80,900 | [data], BFS 2024 |

- **Holds when:** you're an EU/EFTA citizen, willing to work in Switzerland, with German (Zurich) or French (Geneva/Lausanne) or a target of English-speaking global firms, in consulting, banking, insurance, strategy or industry.
- **Fails when:**
  - **(a) Non-EU and no experience.** SEM admits third-country workers essentially with a degree *plus* several years of experience, within quotas.
  - **(b) Target is London IB/PE.** The bonus-driven ladder can beat Swiss corporate pay.
  - **(c) You won't work in Switzerland.** The premium is a *location* effect: a non-Swiss master's graduate who moves to Zurich captures most of it, and a Swiss graduate who leaves loses it.
- **Long-run growth:** London's year-3/start multiple is larger (≈1.8× vs 1.45–1.5×, indicative). Swiss promotions add 13–18% each in real terms against staying put (BFS 2025). Ten-year series now exist on each side (UK LEO business master's median £53,400 at ten years; Swiss BFS LSE age bands) but are not like-for-like, and they show no sign of slower Swiss proportional growth (verification/round-3c.md P1, 2 Oct 2026).
- **The non-obvious insight:** "Study in Switzerland" and "work in Switzerland" are separate decisions. Pay follows the second.

### H2. "Some employers hire mostly from specific schools or regions (e.g. Mercedes and Hochschule Nürtingen) and care little about business-school prestige."
**Verdict: Partly supported.** The mechanism is real, but it's **employer selection of people early** (dual study at 18, apprenticeships, Werkstudent roles, theses, final internships), not a school brand preference. The specific Mercedes–HfWU Nürtingen link is a documented partnership, not a measured hiring concentration: Mercedes-Benz Retail's "Go Innovative" programme with HfWU (2016, up to 40 students) and the Porsche Automotive Campus appear on HfWU's own slides (Nov 2025), while both firms' business dual-study routes run mainly through DHBW. No employer publishes hiring counts by school (verification/round-3e.md P3, 2 Oct 2026).

- **Holds when:** the employer runs a structured internal pipeline near its sites, the role is functional (controlling, purchasing, sales, HR, engineering), and the local language is needed.
  - German auto/industry: Bosch, Mercedes and DHBW Stuttgart; Audi and THI Ingolstadt. About 70% of DHBW dual graduates are taken on by their partner company [data].
  - French corporates via alternance and final internships: 42.3% of grande-école hires † [data, CGE 2026].
  - Swiss banks via HSG events; Dutch Big 4 and Rabobank via RSM [data, school reports].
- **Fails when:** the role is IB, MBB, PE or a global graduate programme with campus targeting. There, school selectivity has a causal effect: attending an Ivy-Plus college roughly triples the chance of working at a prestigious firm (Chetty et al., QJE 2026) [data]. European finance recruiter lists are dominated by the same global firms.
- **Corollary:** the French apprenticeship advantage is weaker for management students than assumed. Only 37.9% of management apprentices are hired by their host, against 47.9% of engineers (CGE 2026) [data].
- **The non-obvious insight:** for German industry, the decision that matters is made at 18–21 (dual study, Werkstudent), not at master's level. For a 22-year-old, the equivalent move is a company thesis or Werkstudent role at the target firm during a German-language master's.

### H3. "Investment banks increasingly hire STEM graduates over finance graduates."
**Verdict: Partly supported.** It is true for the *bank* and for *quant-adjacent desks*. It is not shown for *European IB front office*.

- **Holds when:** "bank" means the whole firm (Goldman's intern class went from 37% STEM in 2017 to ~45% in 2021; engineers are roughly a quarter of the firm) [employer-stated, via eFinancialCareers]. Also holds for quant, strats, e-trading and risk-modelling seats, where STEM screens are the norm.
- **Fails when:** the question is IBD analyst hiring in London and Europe. The only division-level data (London, 2016–17) show economics and finance dominating. The modal European M&A hire combines a quantitative or economics bachelor's with a finance or management master's, which blurs the STEM/finance split [practitioner consensus; stale].
- **Data gap:** no bank publishes IBD-only degree splits after 2017. This is the single most useful dataset to obtain (verification/claims-to-verify.md).
- **The non-obvious insight:** the career-relevant shift isn't "STEM over finance", it's *coding and modelling over credentials*. CFA Institute now requires Practical Skills Modules (modelling, Python) for the charter. McKinsey pilots AI-assisted case interviews. BCG's 2025 hiring growth went mainly to AI engineers and data scientists (careers/finance.md, careers/consulting.md).
