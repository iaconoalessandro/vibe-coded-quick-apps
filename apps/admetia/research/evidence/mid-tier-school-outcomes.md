# Mid-tier and public-university master's outcomes

## Bottom line

1. **A mid-tier or public master's is a reasonable choice if you read the right numbers.** The 52 non-scored programmes in the FT MiM 2026 table have a median weighted salary of US$78,226 against US$114,003 for 23 scored ones (about 46% lower), but a median "employed at three months" of 91% against 98%. Pay separates tiers; the employment headline barely does (FT 2026, author calculation) [data].
2. **Country mix does not explain the gap; the official data suggest it shrinks at home.** The FT gap is 37% to 53% inside France, Italy, Spain and the Netherlands. In InserSup, the six scored French schools earn a median net pay about 21% above 17 other business schools at 18 months (about EUR 3,189 against 2,640 a month, approximate), against about 48% in the FT [data].
3. **Public can beat private per euro, when the public school is selective.** Polimi and LUISS report the same FT salary (US$73,568 and US$73,738) at fees about 4.4 times apart; French public management master's reach 73.7% salaried employment at EUR 255 a year against 64.8% for business schools (EUR 2,405 against EUR 2,749 a month, about 12.5% lower) [data].
4. **Rates mean different things.** InserSup's 64.8% is jobs in France out of all leavers; the FT's 100% at Audencia is respondents. Never compare across definitions or horizons.
5. **EU graduates of English master's mostly leave UK payroll.** Of EU-domiciled business and management graduates, 35.1% are in UK sustained employment at one year and 24.7% at five; UK-domiciled median earnings are £35,100 at one year and £48,300 at five, a modest premium over other master's subjects (LEO 2023-24) [data].
6. **Silence is not proof of poor outcomes, but publishers rarely publish fully.** Frankfurt School, Vlerick and Audencia give rates without response rates; coverage under 75% occurs at 14 of 52 mid-tier programmes in the FT table, and lower coverage goes with lower, not higher, reported employment [data].
7. **Official series have blind spots exactly where this reader looks.** AlmaLaurea omits Bocconi, Polimi, LUISS and Cattolica; LEO covers English providers only; InserSup misses jobs abroad; SIIU has no programme level.
8. **Pipelines are documented only for a few.** Frankfurt School banking (32% of the class of 2025) and Vlerick strategy and consulting (42%) are the clear cases; Maastricht and Tilburg Big 4 pipelines were not verified.

## 1. Why this file exists, and what "mid-tier" means here

The calculator scores about 70 programmes at schools that sit at or near the top of the Financial Times (FT) tables. Most European bachelor's graduates will not attend one of them, for reasons of admission odds or money (see evidence/base-rates-and-failure-modes.md on seats versus supply, and money/costs-and-funding.md). This file asks what the next tier delivers and which numbers about it can be trusted.

**Definition used.** "Mid-tier" means any pre-experience business master's outside the calculator's list: public-university master's (Italian laurea magistrale, French university master, German and Dutch research-university master's), non-elite private schools (the French "PGE" programmes below the top six, Frankfurt School, LUISS, Vlerick), and UK schools outside the main seven. It is a label of convenience, not a quality judgement: Cologne, HHL and Vlerick post FT figures above several scored programmes.

**Method and limits.** One uniform source (the FT MiM 2026 table) gives 52 programmes outside the calculator list on one definition. Four official series give anchors. Own reports were read where they opened; some UK and Dutch pages returned 403. "None found" means not found in this session's searches, not proven absent. Not repeated here: the denominator, response-rate and horizon traps (evidence/how-numbers-mislead.md, Parts A and F), AlmaLaurea by university (places/italy-playbook.md), Nordic stay rates (places/iberia-and-nordics.md), the FT method and public-versus-private (decisions/school-types-and-accreditation.md).

## 2. Official statistics as the anchor

Official statistics link tax or social-security records to graduates, so they cover most of a cohort and are not shaped by who chooses to reply. They rarely show a single programme, but they set the baseline for any school claim.

### 2.1 England: LEO (tax records), business and management master's

The Department for Education's LEO release links graduate records to HMRC and benefits data. The latest release is tax year 2023-24 (published 25 June 2026) (DfE, Graduate labour market outcomes (LEO), https://explore-education-statistics.service.gov.uk/find-statistics/graduate-labour-market-outcomes-leo/2023-24) [data]. It covers graduates of English providers. Figures below are author extractions from the release's underlying CSV (underlying_data.csv, subject "Business and management", level 7, largest row per domicile group); earnings are annualised, rounded to £100 and cover those with a sustained-employment record. The subject group is all level-7 business and management, so it includes part-time, mature and experienced students, not only pre-experience ones.

What this shows (all [data]):
- **A UK-domiciled business and management master's is a middling investment on average.** One-year median £35,100 against £32,200 for all level-7 taught subjects; five-year £48,300 against £39,500. The premium is modest and the spread wide.
- **For EU graduates, most of the cohort is not in UK payroll.** Only 35.1% are in UK sustained employment a year out and 24.7% at five years. For international graduates the denominator is all graduates except those recorded as permanently overseas (DfE data guidance, 2023-24 release) [data], so a low share is partly people who went home, not unemployment. The medians cover only the minority who stayed and are survivor figures. For Italy-domiciled level-7 graduates in all subjects (international file, top-20 countries) the stay rate is higher than for France (26.9% at one year) or Germany (19.7%): 38.2% at one year and 34.1% at five [data].
- **Subjects differ.** The CAH3 file gives one-year medians of £41,000 for finance (1,420 earners) and £28,900 for marketing (1,625). Use the ordering only: these medians do not reconcile with the CAH2 total above [data].
- **Sector mix explains much of it.** Among UK-domiciled business and management level-7 taught graduates in sustained employment, 10.3% worked in financial and insurance activities at one year (median £50,500) against 19.0% in professional, scientific and technical activities (median £34,800); at five years the finance share was 10.2% (median £71,000) and professional services 17.5% (median £53,800) (industry_3digitSIC.csv; one-year n=10,695, five-year n=8,915) [data].

**What LEO cannot tell you.** No school or programme level for master's, no pre-experience filter, English providers only, and no view of people working in Italy, France or Germany. It cannot separate Exeter from Durham.

**HESA Graduate Outcomes (survey).** The 2023/24 survey (published 4 June 2026) had 353,755 responses from 1,002,175 graduates, about 35% (https://www.graduateoutcomes.ac.uk/results/) [data]. Its subject tables returned 403, so no HESA business figure is quoted; the survey that schools cite on course pages has a 35% response rate (Wonkhe, 5 Jun 2026, puts complete responses at 32%), and for international graduates of the class of 2022/23 the response rate was 9% (Jisc, via UKCISA, 16 Sep 2025), so LEO is the stronger anchor. Round 3f again could not open the HESA subject tables or the Bayes MSc Employment Report 2026 (both behind a Cloudflare check).

### 2.2 France: InserSup (social-security records), management master's

InserSup, run by the education ministry's statistics office, matches graduates to payroll (DSN) and self-employment records at 6, 12, 18, 24 and 30 months. It covers public-university master's and the "visé bac+5 grade master" diplomas of business schools on one definition (Ministry of Higher Education, InserSup methodological note, December 2025, https://www.enseignementsup-recherche.gouv.fr/sites/default/files/2026-01/note-m-thodologique-insersup-2025-39129.pdf; open data, https://data.enseignementsup-recherche.gouv.fr/explore/dataset/fr-esr-insersup/) [data]. Four definitional points decide how to read it, all from the note:
- The employment rate is **salaried jobs in France divided by all leavers** (including unemployed, inactive and anyone abroad). It is not "of those seeking work".
- **Jobs abroad are invisible.** So a school whose graduates move to London or Geneva shows a lower rate.
- Salary is **net monthly, full-time-equivalent, including bonuses**, for those in salaried jobs in France; apprenticeship pay is excluded; apprentices who continue studying count as continuing, not as leavers.
- The population is under-30s leaving after their final year; full-time-equivalent pay for public-sector and private-employer jobs is partly estimated by the ministry.

I extracted the file (dataset 2026_S1; business and economics-management domain; all genders, nationalities and regimes; exact promotion 2022 unless stated). Pay is a **weighted average of programme medians, an approximation made by the author** because quartiles cannot be summed.

| Group, class of 2022 at 18 months | Leavers | Salaried employed in France | Of those, stable contracts | Median net monthly pay (approx.) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Public-university master's, "sciences de gestion" (261 programmes) | 13,935 | 73.7% | 86.5% | about EUR 2,405 |
| of which finance (20 programmes) | 1,414 | 67.5% | 90.8% | about EUR 2,720 |
| of which marketing and sales (32) | 2,174 | 70.4% | 84.1% | about EUR 2,227 |
| Business schools and institutes, "visé bac+5 grade master" (48 diplomas; excludes universities, Sciences Po, ENS) | 22,759 | 64.8% | 89.4% | about EUR 2,749 |

The business-school group has a **lower** French employment rate but **higher** pay. That is mainly a denominator effect: graduates abroad are invisible to InserSup (likely part of the gap, not shown directly). School rows are in section 3.

## 4. Patterns

**(a) Like-for-like gap with the elite programmes.** On the FT's own definition, 23 calculator-scored programmes in the table have a median weighted salary of US$114,003 and a median 98% "employed at three months". The 52 other programmes in the table have a median of US$78,226 and 91%. That is a gap of about 46% in pay but only seven points in the employment headline (author calculation from the FT table) [data]. Employment at three months barely separates tiers; pay does.

**(b) How much is country and sector mix?** Country mix explains little of the FT gap, because the gap appears inside each country. Median weighted salary, calculator-scored versus other programmes (author calculation, FT 2026): France US$112,775 against US$76,009 (7 against 16 programmes), Italy US$109,250 (Bocconi) against US$73,653 (LUISS and Polimi), Spain US$114,003 against US$74,665, Netherlands US$113,556 (RSM) against US$82,884, UK US$88,887 against US$70,494, Germany US$117,806 against US$106,836 (only 2 and 5 programmes; the German public schools in the scored set are cheap and strong). But the official French data suggest the FT gap overstates what happens at home. At 18 months, in France only, the six scored French programmes in InserSup have a median net pay of about EUR 3,189 a month against EUR 2,640 for 17 other business schools, a premium of about 21%, against about 48% in the FT (author calculation; approximate programme medians; different horizons) [data]. The likeliest reasons are the share working abroad, which InserSup cannot see, and the sector mix of the jobs; the survey-based FT figures also carry self-selection. LEO shows how large the sector effect is: in England, a UK-domiciled business and management master's graduate in finance earned a median £50,500 a year after graduating against £34,800 in professional services, and finance employed about one in ten (section 2.1). LBS's own reports show the same mechanism (a "finance premium" of 31% that disappears inside finance; evidence/how-numbers-mislead.md, A7).

**(c) Real regional and sector pipelines, where data exist.**
- Frankfurt School: banking 32%, consultancy 17%, fintech 9% and financial services 6% of the class of 2025 (school page) [data]. A banking pipeline is documented, with the caveat that the page gives no response rate.
- Vlerick: 42% in strategy and consultancy (school page, no coverage) [data].
- Maastricht SBE: pay is high (median gross EUR 3,700 a month at 1.5 years), but only 34% of five-year alumni live or work near Maastricht and 54% in that area or the Netherlands, so it is not a purely local feed (ROA 2025) [data]. A Big 4 pipeline for Maastricht and Tilburg was not verified (Claims to verify).
- Politecnico di Milano and MIP: no management-specific employer data found. The all-university report gives 100% of Italian and 88% of international graduates employed within one year, net pay EUR 2,365 against EUR 1,677 (Polimi, 22 Oct 2025) [data]; the nationality gap matters for non-Italians.
- Spain: Navarra, IE Universidad and Ramon Llull show contribution bases of EUR 47,000 to EUR 53,000 at four years, UC3M EUR 38,660; public in-person average EUR 33,124 (SIIU 2026) [data].

**(d) Selection.** Dauphine's, Navarra's or emlyon's pay reflects who is admitted as well as what is taught (Dale and Krueger, evidence/how-numbers-mislead.md C1). What is observable is outcome per student, not value added.

**(e) Public against private.** France: public management master's 73.7% salaried against 64.8% for business schools, pay about 12.5% lower (EUR 2,405 against 2,749). Spain: in-person private master's 73.3% affiliated against 54.4% public, pay 28% to 33% higher, but private programmes enrol more already-working students (online private: 82%), so it is not purely a school effect.

**(f) Self-reported against official numbers.** Audencia reports 100% employed at three months to the FT (92% covered) and 90% within six months on its page; InserSup has 73.5% of its class of 2022 in salaried French jobs at 18 months. Not a contradiction: one is respondents, the other all leavers in France only. The danger is quoting them side by side.

Three readings. ## 6. Weak-outcome signals and what silence means

Signals that justify extra questions (all author tallies from the FT table and InserSup extracts):
- **Low coverage.** 14 of the 52 non-scored FT programmes cover less than 75% of the class, against 3 of 23 scored ones. Examples: HEC Lausanne 46%, NHH 32%, Edinburgh 55%, Jönköping 55%, Esdes 57%, IAE Aix-Marseille 61%, Solvay 65%, Rennes SB 66% (FT 2026) [data]. TUM (15%), which is scored, shows it is not only a mid-tier problem.
- **Low or odd employment headlines.** Cranfield 39% employed at three months, King's 60%, Lund 63%, Trinity 65%, Edinburgh 66%, HEC Lausanne 66%. Note that in this table, lower coverage goes with a lower, not a higher, headline: mean employment is 85.5% where coverage is under 75% (17 programmes) against 93.4% where it is 90% or more (29 programmes), and the correlation across all 75 programmes is about 0.30 (author calculation). That is the opposite of the "only happy graduates reply" story, perhaps because untraceable graduates are often not yet placed. Treat both as warnings.
- **No pay or coverage on the page** (Vlerick, Audencia): half a report.
- **Further study.** LEO: 85.9% of UK-domiciled business master's graduates are employed or studying a year out against 78.9% employed alone, so about 7 points are study. Spain's "not affiliated" mixes study, emigration and unemployment.
- **Thin official outcomes.** In InserSup, Brest Business School shows 51.2% salaried in France and EUR 2,330 a month, ICN 58.2%, Excelia 63.0%; some public "visé grade master" diplomas show only 45% to 55% stable contracts (Toulouse Capitole, Aix-Marseille, Grenoble Alpes), likely reflecting short or apprenticeship-type contracts, though the extract does not say.
- **Small N.** Programmes of 31 to 59 students (Cranfield, UC3M, Henley, TIAS) swing with a few responses.

**What silence means.** No programme-level employment report was found for Cattolica, Padova, Bologna, UC3M, UCD Smurfit, TIAS/Tilburg, Exeter, Durham or Strathclyde, though national data cover some (AlmaLaurea for Padova and Bologna; SIIU for UC3M). Bayes publishes MSc Employment Reports (2023, 2026) that could not be opened. Silence is a reason to ask, not proof of poor results, because publishers also publish incompletely. The test is whether the school answers the section 7 questions.

## 7. How to compare a mid-tier programme with an elite one

Use the 12 questions in evidence/how-numbers-mislead.md (Part F). Four additions for this tier:
1. **Anchor to the official series for the country where you want to work**: LEO in England, InserSup in France, AlmaLaurea in Italy, SIIU in Spain. Ask what the school's number would be on that definition.
2. **Convert to one horizon and one denominator.** FT is three months and survey-based; InserSup is 18 months, all leavers, France only; LEO is annualised pay with a sustained-employment test. Never set a 100% against a 73%.
3. **Compare inside the sector and city you want**, not across the whole class (finance and professional services differ by about 45% at one year in England).
4. **Compare cost per year of premium, not per cent of salary** (section 5), and check whether the premium is observed only for those who stayed in the country.

Schools that publish nothing can be asked for: class size, share responding, definition of employed, median base pay by sector, destination country, and three alumni they did not choose (questions 1, 2, 5, 6, 8 and 12).

## Hypotheses tested

**H1 (assigned). A strong local public-university master's beats a weak private-school master's on outcomes per euro.** Verdict: **supported** where the public school is selective and the private one is not. Holds: Polimi against LUISS (same FT salary, tuition about one fifth); French public management master's against mid-tier PGEs (73.7% against 64.8% salaried in France, pay 12.5% lower, fee EUR 255 against five figures); Dauphine (public) at about EUR 3,050 net a month, level with EDHEC and emlyon. Fails: Italy's Polimi against Bocconi (US$73,568 against US$109,250); public French "visé" diplomas with 45% to 55% stable contracts; any case where the public programme is not in the labour market you want. Evidence: FT 2026, InserSup 2026_S1, school pages [data].

**H2 (assigned). Mid-tier schools that publish no employment report have worse outcomes than those that publish.** Verdict: **insufficient evidence.** Unpublished schools by definition have no programme data, and the FT cannot show them. The indirect signal is mixed: within the FT table, low coverage goes with lower reported employment (85.5% against 93.4%), and publishers often leave out coverage and pay. InserSup lets a non-publisher be checked for French schools only. Fails as a rule: Brest and ICN are in InserSup with weak figures regardless of publication [data].

**H3 (assigned). The elite premium is mostly sector and country mix.** Verdict: **partly supported.** Country mix does not explain the FT gap (inside France, Italy, Spain and the Netherlands it is 37% to 53%). On official French data the within-country premium falls to about 21%, so abroad share, sector and self-selection together account for roughly half of the FT figure. Fails because the official comparison covers only salaried jobs in France and different horizons.

**H4. A UK business and management master's adds only a modest premium for UK-domiciled students.** Verdict: **supported.** One year out the median is £35,100 against £32,200 for all level-7 taught subjects (9%); five years out £48,300 against £39,500 (22%). It fails for finance (about £41,000 at one year in the CAH3 file, ordering only) and for MBA (£70,800) [data].

**H5. For an EU citizen, a UK mid-tier master's rarely leads to UK payroll employment.** Verdict: **supported.** 35.1% in UK sustained employment at one year and 24.7% at five, against 78.9% and 78.0% for UK-domiciled graduates. It fails to say they are unemployed: many return home, and the five-year median of £57,500 is a survivor figure [data].

## Common mistakes and myths

- **"Employment is 95%+ everywhere, so schools are equivalent."** The rate separates tiers by 7 points; pay by about 46% (FT 2026) [data].
- **"The official French rate is 64%, so the school is failing."** InserSup counts only salaried jobs in France out of all leavers; graduates abroad are missing, and the lowest rates belong to the most international schools (HEC 49.9%), consistent with, though not proof of, graduates working abroad [data].
- **"A national average describes my school."** AlmaLaurea omits Bocconi, Polimi, LUISS and Cattolica (places/italy-playbook.md), LEO covers only English providers, SIIU has no programme level.
- **"UK graduate pay applies to me."** For EU students it applies to the 25% to 35% who stay in UK payroll (LEO) [data].

## Decision rules

1. **If** you want to work in France and a selective public master's (Dauphine-type) admits you, **prefer** it over a mid-tier private PGE **because** InserSup shows similar employment and a pay gap of about EUR 340 a month at a fee of EUR 255 a year.
2. **If** you target Milan finance or consulting and cannot get Bocconi, **prefer** Polimi on price and LUISS only with a named employer reason **because** their FT salaries are almost identical (US$73,568 and US$73,738) and the fee ratio is about 4.4 times.
3. **If** a school quotes an employment rate without coverage, **discount** it by assuming the class share is unknown, **because** FT coverage runs from 15% to 100% and low coverage goes with lower headlines.
4. **If** a rate is quoted at three months and another at 18 months, **do not compare** them, **because** definitions and denominators differ (section 4f).
5. **If** you are an EU citizen weighing England, **assume** about one in three in UK payroll after a year and **price the return**, **because** LEO shows 35.1% at one year and 24.7% at five.
6. **If** choosing between a mid-tier private school and a public one at similar pay, **prefer** the cheaper unless the private school names a pipeline you want **because** each EUR 10,000 of fee needs about 2.4 years of the observed French premium.
7. **If** you want finance, **compare** within finance **because** LEO shows finance at about £50,500 against £34,800 in professional services at one year.
8. **If** the school publishes nothing, **ask** questions 1, 2, 5, 6, 8 and 12 **because** silence is not evidence either way.
9. **If** you want Spain, **use** SIIU affiliation and contribution base for the university, **because** private in-person master's show 73% against 54% affiliation but partly through enrolment of working students.

## Numbers to treat with care

- All FT figures are school-submitted, three-year-old alumni data at PPP; coverage varies from 15% to 100% (self-reported).
- InserSup pay is the author's weighted average of programme medians (approximate); the employment shares are exact sums.
- LEO: level 7 includes part-time and experienced students; the CAH3 medians do not reconcile with the CAH2 total (ordering only); international figures are survivor medians. "One year" is the tax year after graduation, about 19 to 24 months after leaving, not 12 months (note added by lead, 2026-10-02; 3a log).
- SIIU contribution base is a capped proxy for gross pay, with small and suppressed cells.
- Maastricht SBE: all SBE master's, UM-wide response 17.2% and 24.9%.
- Frankfurt School, Vlerick, Audencia: single-source school page statements without coverage.
- Polimi: all-discipline report, no response rate seen.
- Fee figures: LUISS, Frankfurt School, Vlerick, Service-Public are from official pages; all others are in Claims to verify.
- Cross-checks against a second statement in the same source: LUISS FT page (93%, 65% salary increase, rank 30) against the FT table; Frankfurt School page (92%) against FT; Maastricht text (EUR 3,700 and EUR 5,000) against its Table 3; LEO industry counts (10,695) against the earnings count (10,700); HEA 86.6% and 25,059 on one page; Service-Public EUR 255 against its stated fee for non-EU (EUR 3,950).
