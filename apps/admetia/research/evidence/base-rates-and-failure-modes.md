# Base rates and failure modes

## Bottom line

1. **No public number gives "seats for business master's graduates".** Published intakes differ in scope: UK investment banks hired 2,069 graduates in 2025 and plan 2,365 for 2026 (all divisions, all degrees, UK only); Goldman takes about 2,500 interns and 2,500 entry hires globally; Big 4 UK intake fell from about 6,500 to about 5,400 between 2023 and 2025; EPSO AD5 sought 1,490 people from 170,000+ applicants. Anything that divides one of these by "all candidates" is not a probability (section 1).
2. **The "90% of Tier-1 students fail" claim is not supported as stated.** At LBS MiM 2025, 14% of accepted offers were in investment banking and 34% in finance, so 86% were not in IB, but 92% had an offer within three months and 88% accepted. Most of the class went elsewhere. No school publishes how many applied to banks, so rejection and choice cannot be separated. The honest number to show is the share of the class that ended up in a destination, with its scope (section 3).
3. **At the application level, conversion is low and concentrated.** In Trackr's 2026 surveys of UK finance applicants, 101 applications produced one summer offer on average, 54% of applicants got none, and the top 10% took 41% of offers. Goldman accepts under 1% of applications. These are self-selected UK undergraduate samples (section 4).
4. **Failure is concentrated in the first year.** Italian second-level graduates: 80.8% employed at one year, 94.4% at five; unemployment 9.3% at one year; net pay EUR 1,495 at one year. EU tertiary graduates 1 to 3 years out: Italy 78.7%, France 82.0%, Germany 92.6%. French grandes-écoles employment at six months fell from 90.5% to 76% in three years (section 5).
5. **Italy's denominator is tens of thousands.** Eurostat counts about 22,700 master's graduates in business and administration in Italy in 2023, and AlmaLaurea counts about 17,000 economics-group biennial master's graduates in its member universities in 2025 (author calculation). Seats at elite employers are in the hundreds (sections 1 and 2).
6. **The standard path has a money gate.** UK working-class graduates are 20 points less likely to do an internship, 40% of unpaid interns rely on parents (Sutton Trust, Jan 2025). Legal floors for post-degree internships are low in Italy (EUR 300 national, EUR 500 Lombardia) and France (EUR 4.50 an hour, about EUR 680 a month) (section 7).
7. **For non-EU students the risk is a chain.** Visa refusal (0.1% to 41% by nationality in the UK), offer, sponsorship, salary threshold and the stay rate (38.5% of non-EEA graduates remain in the Netherlands at five years) each have their own odds. EU students abroad stay far less (20.3%) (section 6).
8. **Gaps remain.** I could not open HESA tables (re-tried 2 Oct 2026; still blocked), so HESA-based UK business-master's supply and outcomes by domicile are unverified (DfE LEO gives a primary substitute for England, section 2.2), and no official UK taught-master's non-completion figure exists in what I found. Several Gemini figures (1,200 to 1,400 seats, 12,000 candidates, 10 to 15% branch A, 52% analyst attrition, USD 10,000 to 20,000 consulting stipends) could not be traced or conflict with its own files (corrections list).

**What the table can support.** UK investment banks alone take about 2,000 to 2,400 graduates a year across all roles, of which front-office analyst seats are only a part. I found no published split. The Gemini lead said London bulge-bracket and elite-boutique summer-analyst seats total "about 1,200 to 1,500". That is not contradicted by 2,069 all-role hires, but it is also not traceable to a source, and the High Fliers sample does not say whether boutiques are included. Use it only as an unverified order of magnitude (see Corrections to the Gemini leads).

**What the table cannot support.** Nobody publishes (a) how many of those seats go to master's graduates rather than bachelor's, (b) how many go to non-UK EU citizens, or (c) how many go to students of a given school. Without those three splits, "seats for MiM students" cannot be computed from public data. Any site figure for it must be labelled an author estimate.

**Non-obvious point.** The seat count is moving in opposite directions by sector. UK investment banks plan 2,365 hires, 26% above their 2021 low, while accounting and professional services firms are about a third below their 2022-23 level (High Fliers) and the Big 4 UK intake fell 6% to 29% by firm between 2023 and 2025. A single "graduate market is shrinking" warning is wrong for finance applicants and right for audit applicants [data via press].

## 2. Supply side: how many graduates

### 2.1 Europe (Eurostat, 2023)

I queried Eurostat's dissemination API for graduates at ISCED level 7 (master's or long first degree) by field, dataset educ_uoe_grad02 (data updated 26 Aug 2026), for 2023. Field F04 is "business, administration and law" and F041 is "business and administration" alone. Economics sits in a different field (F031, social and behavioural sciences, which also holds psychology, sociology and political science), so F041 excludes economics graduates. Germany's F041 count looks low because a large block of its business-and-law graduates is coded at the broad F04 level, so compare countries with care.

| Country | All ISCED 7 graduates, 2023 | F041 Business and administration | F042 Law (incl. long first degrees) | F04 total (the two) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| EU27 | 1,618,561 | 268,106 | 105,142 | 409,061 |
| France | 373,451 | 100,561 | 27,493 | 128,054 |
| Italy | 216,301 | 22,712 | 20,579 | 43,418 |
| Spain | 176,881 | 21,664 | 10,273 | 31,937 |
| Netherlands | 58,403 | 11,480 | 5,736 | 17,216 |
| Germany | 223,999 | 5,961 | 11,713 | 49,208 (the F041 + F042 sum is only 17,674; the other ~31,500 are coded at the broad F04 level, our inference) |

Source: Eurostat, educ_uoe_grad02, https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/api/dissemination/statistics/1.0/data/educ_uoe_grad02 (queried 1 Oct 2026; 2023 values) [data]. F04 totals, and the F041/F042 split, come from two queries on the same dataset; for France, Italy, Spain and the Netherlands F041 + F042 equals F04 exactly. The Netherlands shows an identical F04 value (17,216) in 2022 and 2023, which may be a data-reuse artefact, so treat the Dutch figure with care.

Reading it: at least 268,000 people a year finish a master's in business and administration across the EU27 (F041 only; the EU total is understated by Germany's coding gap, so 270,000 to 300,000 is a fairer range, our inference), and 409,000 including law. France is by far the biggest, plausibly because French "master" status covers grandes écoles and programmes that other countries classify differently (our inference). **Italy produces about 22,700 business and administration master's graduates a year (F041), and a similar number of law graduates**, many of them on single-cycle law degrees. Economics graduates are not included in F041. A second angle: AlmaLaurea's 2026 Percorsi report counts 107,109 biennial master's graduates of 2025 across its member universities, of whom 15.9% (about 17,000, author calculation) are in the "economico" disciplinary group, which covers economics, finance and business degrees (AlmaLaurea, Sintesi del Rapporto 2026 sui Percorsi di laurea, https://www.almalaurea.it/document-download/sintesi-rapporto-almalaurea-2026-sui-percorsi-di-laurea (read in full), 2026) [data]. AlmaLaurea excludes Bocconi, Politecnico di Milano, LUISS and Cattolica, so the true Italian total is higher. For an Italian reader the relevant denominator is therefore **roughly 17,000 business-economics master's graduates a year in public universities, plus several thousand at Bocconi, LUISS and Cattolica, so about 20,000 to 25,000** (author estimate; the two sources use different boundaries and cannot be added). The key point for the site is the order of magnitude: tens of thousands in Italy, not hundreds.

- In business and management, non-EU students were 70% of all postgraduate students in 2021/22 and 92% of that year's increase (Chartered Association of Business Schools analysis of HESA data, 24 Apr 2023, https://charteredabs.org/insights/news/business-student-numbers-increase-by-12-as-enrollments-outstrip-all-other-subjects (fetched)) [data via trade body; stale, pre-2023].

HESA's pages and its CSV downloads (for example the 2024/25 bulletin SB273 figure files) sit behind a Cloudflare bot check that blocked curl and the built-in browser on 2 Oct 2026, and the Wayback Machine holds no capture of the 2023/24 or 2024/25 bulletins (re-tested 2 Oct 2026). Do not put a HESA business-PGT count on the site until a person has downloaded the table.
- **Primary substitute for supply: DfE LEO (England providers).** The LEO 2023-24 release (DfE, published 25 Jun 2026, last updated 14 Sep 2026, https://explore-education-statistics.service.gov.uk/find-statistics/graduate-labour-market-outcomes-leo/2023-24; underlying_data.csv, subject "Business and management", level 7, read 2 Oct 2026) counts the graduating cohort of 2021/22 from English providers as 14,755 UK-domiciled, 3,585 EU-domiciled and 41,915 non-EU-domiciled, 60,255 in all. The count covers all level-7 business and management graduates (part-time and experienced students included, not only pre-experience), so it overstates "MiM-type" supply and is not a HESA qualifiers count [data]. The structural point that survives: in UK business master's, the typical classmate is a non-UK, non-EU student on a one-year programme. That affects who competes for UK graduate-visa-dependent jobs (see places/visas-and-work-rights.md and places/origin-countries-non-eu.md).

### 2.3 Elite-school class sizes

The FT MiM 2026 table lists enrolment of 58 at St Gallen, 298 at LBS and 684 at HEC Paris (evidence/ranking-methodologies.md) [data]. LBS MiM 2025 reported the sector shares: finance 34%, consulting 30%, of which investment banking 14% (on file; used in section 3). ## 3. Funnel arithmetic and the Gemini "90% fail" claim

### 3.1 What the one school report we can read says

London Business School's MiM employment report for the class of 2025 (read in full, 14 pages, https://assets.london.edu/hxo16fanegqh/7oC5DZdSFwJChIaAb4i9AE/e62b2c069b9c86ab5d75c841c006ee0e/mim_employment_report.pdf, published 2026) [data, school-reported, CSEA-compliant]:

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Class | 405 students, 94% international, 52 nationalities, average age 22 |
| Offer within 3 months | 92%; accepted an offer 88%; reporting rate 96% |
| Sector of accepted offers | Finance 34% (investment banking 14%, private equity 8%, investment management 5%, real estate 2%, other 5%); consulting 30%; technology 21%; diversified 15% |
| Job location | UK 54%, Europe excl. UK 21%, Asia 18%, Africa/Middle East 5%, North America 1% |
| Largest named employers | Bain 10, BCG 7, Aspora 5, Simon-Kucher 5, OC&C 5, McKinsey 4, Amazon 4, P&G 4; Goldman 3, JPMorgan 3; Bank of America, PJT, Barclays, Rothschild, Deutsche 2 each |

Author calculation: 14% of about 356 accepted offers is roughly 50 investment-banking entrants; Bain, BCG and McKinsey together took about 21 (5% of the class of 405). These are order-of-magnitude figures because sector percentages are rounded and the denominator of the sector chart is not stated.

What this does and does not show:
- **It supports H-a in its literal form.** At a top European MiM with a strong London finance brand, about 86% of the class did not enter investment banking, and about 66% did not enter finance at all (author calculation from the sector shares). But 92% had an offer within three months. The people who did not enter London IB mostly entered consulting, tech, corporates and other countries. They did not "fail".
- **It cannot say how many tried.** The report does not publish how many students applied to banks. The LBS career centre notes 1,650+ opportunities shared and 3,100 coaching sessions for the cohort, which tells us nothing about bank applications (the same report).
- **A single class is not a population.** LBS students apply for finance because they chose LBS; a Bocconi, HEC or WU class has a different mix. For comparison, HEC's specialised Master in Finance placed 81.4% in financial services and 7.2% in consulting (class of 2025), while the HEC MiM placed 28% in financial services and 24% in consulting (see careers/finance.md; the MiM figure is from an aggregator snippet [anecdotal, secondary]). Concentration depends on the degree label more than on the school.

### 3.2 Why "seats versus all candidates" overstates competition

The red-team claim ran: roughly 1,200 to 1,400 London summer-analyst seats against more than 12,000 competing top-school candidates, so about 90% of Tier-1 students fail to enter London front-office IB, and "branch-A placement probability should be 10 to 15%". I tested each component.

1. **The seat figure** is unsourced. The only on-record UK number is High Fliers' 2,069 UK investment-bank graduate hires in 2025 (2,365 planned in 2026), all divisions, all degree levels. A front-office London figure of 1,200 to 1,400 is plausible as a share of that but no source states it. [data via press for the 2,069; Gemini number unverified]
2. **The candidate figure (12,000)** has no source I could find. Even if true, a count of "top-school candidates" counts everyone at a Tier-1 school who might theoretically apply, including those who never will.
3. **The comparison is of the wrong things.** Seats are for a specific job in a specific city. Candidates are students at specific schools. The match requires the number who actually apply for those seats, and nobody publishes that. Employer-side counts are applications, not people: Goldman had 360,000+ applications for about 2,500 interns in 2025, but one student may send ten to thirty applications across divisions and cities (careers/finance.md; Prospects data in getting-in/recruiting-calendar.md: the share of students making more than 100 applications rose from 8% to 13%). Applications per vacancy therefore overstates the number of distinct competitors and understates how competitive the applicants who do reach the late rounds are.
4. **The conclusion "90% fail" depends on counting non-applicants as failures.** At LBS, 86% did not enter IB, which is close to 90% in size, but 66% of the class ended up outside finance, and the report does not say how many of them ever applied to a bank. Some will have been rejected, some will have chosen consulting or tech first. Without application data, "failed to enter" and "chose not to enter" cannot be separated.
5. **The 10 to 15% probability** is close to LBS's 14% IB share, but it describes an outcome share of a self-selected class at one school, not a probability that a student of a Tier-1 school who applies to banks gets in. These are different quantities and the site should not mix them.

What the real data support: (a) very low conversion at the open front door (Goldman under 1% of applications), (b) a high share of any given class going elsewhere, (c) nothing about the conditional probability for a serious applicant. **The honest probability to show is P(outcome | enrolled at this school), taken from that school's own report, not P(offer | application).**

### 3.3 A funnel template for the site (author reasoning)

For any destination D at school S, show three layers and label each by source:
1. **Seats**: the employer's published intake, with scope (country, all degrees or master's only).
2. **Share of the class**: the school's published sector or employer share (for example LBS MiM IB 14%).
3. **Unknown layer**: "how many applied" is not public; say so instead of inventing a number.

Where the site wants a single odds figure, use the school share (layer 2) and add: "This is the share of the class that ended up here, not the chance that an applicant is accepted."

## 4. Internship-to-offer conversion and acceptance

**What is published and what is not.** I found no bank, Big 4 or MBB firm that publishes its internship-to-full-time conversion rate on a primary page. Figures of 70% to 90% circulate on forums and coaching sites (careers/finance.md Claims to verify) [anecdotal]. The only press-relayed bank figures are from 2023, when eFinancialCareers reported forum posts saying no more than 30% of Rothschild interns and 50% in some Morgan Stanley groups (US classes) received full-time offers, and described "upwards of 75%" as a good year (14 Aug 2023, https://www.efinancialcareers.com/news/2023/08/internship-conversions-banking-2023) [anecdotal, via specialist press]. What does exist is student-survey data from Trackr (a UK finance and tech jobs platform), employer-survey data from the Institute of Student Employers (ISE), and Goldman's one-to-one ratio of interns to entry hires.

### 4.1 Trackr, UK finance applicants, 2025/26 (read in full)

Trackr's Summer Internships Season Report 2026 (296 respondents, UK financial-services applicants; PDF fetched and read, https://the-trackr.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Trackr-Summer-Internships-Season-Report-2026.pdf) and its Spring Weeks Season Insights 2026 (232 respondents from 63 universities, 8,013 applications, 239 offers; https://the-trackr.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Spring-Weeks-Season-Insights-2026.pdf) [data, self-selected survey of platform users; not a random sample]:

| Measure | Summer internships (296 respondents) | Spring weeks (232 respondents) |
|---|---|---|
| Applications per candidate | 74 on average (66 in 2025) | 35 on average |
| Offers per candidate | 0.74 on average | 1.03 on average |
| Candidates with no offer | 54% | 49% (49.4%) |
| Applications per offer | 101 (first time above 100; the PDF's text layer lists 67.9 and 75.0 for the two earlier years but not in a reliable order) | about 34 (author calculation: 8,013 / 239) |
| Share of offers won by the top 10% of candidates | 41% | 40% |
| Interview rate / interview-to-offer | 5.5% of applications reach interview; 18% of interviews become offers | not reported |
| Candidates with 3 or more offers | 6% (17% had 2 or more) | about 12% (chart-read: 6.1 + 2.6 + 0.9 + 1.3 + 1.3) |
| Return offers (spring week to internship) | not applicable | 20% converted, 36% rejected, 44% fast-tracked of convertible programmes (2025: 31% got return offers) (chart-read from the PDF, cross-checked against the 20% headline) |

Key reading for the site:
- **Offers are concentrated.** In both surveys the top 10% of candidates won 40% to 41% of offers, while about half got none. That is what a skewed distribution looks like, and it means the average of 0.74 offers per candidate tells you little about the typical candidate [data, self-selected].
- **Spring weeks predict the summer offer.** Candidates with a past spring week needed 73.3 applications per summer offer against 137.6 without one; spring-week converters needed 39.3 (Trackr). The company itself says this is "correlative" and cannot separate causation from selection [data, caveated by the source]. For a continental European master's student the point is that the first-year spring-week door has closed by the time the master's starts (careers/finance.md).
- **Same-day applicants did better**: 82.1 applications per offer for same-day applications against 119.1 for those applying after 72 hours (Trackr). Association only [data].
- **Reneging intent is high.** 72% of respondents said they would renege on an accepted offer if they got a better one, and 75% would re-recruit after receiving a return offer (Trackr 2026 report). These are stated intentions, not measured behaviour [data, self-reported]. For employers it means offer acceptance is not binding in practice, which feeds the "sticky offer" caution in careers/finance.md.
- **Sample limits.** Trackr's users skew towards UK undergraduates targeting finance. 21% of Trackr's own user base received free school meals, 61% are BAME and 27% study STEM (Trackr "About" page in the same PDF). These respondents are not European master's students.

### 4.2 ISE (employer survey), UK, 2024/25

- Employers hired 4,979 interns and 1,763 undergraduate placement students in 2024/25 against 318,252 internship applications (+34% year on year) and 109,357 placement applications (ISE, "5 internship and placement trends to watch in 2026", https://ise.org.uk/knowledge/insights/508/5_internship_and_placement_trends_to_watch_in_2026/ (fetched)) [data]. Author calculation: the ISE sample took about 1.6% of internship applications and 1.6% of placement applications (4,979 / 318,252; 1,763 / 109,357). This is applications, not people, and covers only ISE members.
- 101 applications per internship vacancy (+16%) and 123 per placement (+27%); 77% of employers agree former interns are more work-ready (ISE Top 10 stats of 2025, https://ise.org.uk/knowledge/insights/513/ise_top_10_stats_of_2025_you_need_to_know/ (fetched)) [data].
- ISE's conversion and acceptance statistics (share of graduate hires who were interns, offer acceptance) sit in the paid Student Recruitment Survey 2025 report, which I could not open. A search summary attributed "around 40% of former interns and placement students are recruited into graduate jobs" to it; that is unverified and sits in Claims to verify. A second secondary page (JobPing blog, Sep 2026) instead cites "50% in 2025, 54% in 2024" for intern-to-graduate-offer conversion across 175 employers; the two figures conflict and neither was read on an ISE page (round 3d).

### 4.3 Banks, Big 4 and CGE

- Goldman Sachs had about 2,500 interns and about 2,500 entry-level hires in 2026, with under 1% of applicants accepted into the internship (Fortune, 8 Jun 2026; careers/finance.md) [employer-stated, via press]. That ratio only implies high conversion if most of the entry class are former interns, which Goldman has not said in terms I could verify.
- In French grandes écoles, 42.3% of hires at the 2026 CGE survey came through the final internship or apprenticeship (CGE, 2026, see evidence/trends.md) [data]. This is the strongest published conversion indicator for a European master's student, but it is a share of hires, not a conversion rate of interns.
- Big 4 graduate schemes: no firm publishes how many placement or vacation students convert (careers/accounting-and-corporate.md). Treat any "90% conversion" as a claim to verify.

## 5. Time to job and failure outcomes

### 5.1 Italy: AlmaLaurea 2026 report, all second-level graduates (read in full)

"Employed" means any paid work or paid training activity. Bocconi, Politecnico di Milano, LUISS and Cattolica are not AlmaLaurea members (places/italy-playbook.md), so the elite Milan pipeline is not in these numbers.

| Second-level graduates (biennial and single-cycle together), national | 1 year after | 3 years after | 5 years after |

### 5.4 What counts as "failure" here

Failure has at least four different meanings and they have different base rates:
1. **No job offer at all after 3 to 12 months.** About 8% at LBS (92% had an offer in 3 months); about 24% at 6 months for CGE schools (76% employed); about 19% at one year for Italian second-level graduates (all fields); recent-graduate employment of 78.7% in Italy and 82.0% in France.
2. **No offer from the target employer type.** Typically the majority: for example 86% of LBS MiM not in IB (section 3). This is not unemployment.
3. **Employment below graduate level.** Roughly one in three Italian employed graduates reports a degree that is not highly effective for the job.
4. **Temporary or non-permanent work.** In Italy only 28.7% of employed second-level graduates have a permanent contract at one year; 22.4% have training contracts.

The site should name which one it is talking about. A single "% employed" figure blends all four.

## 6. Programme-level failure: non-completion, visas, leaving

### 6.1 Non-completion and delay

- **UK taught master's non-continuation: no official statistic found.** HESA's performance indicators and the OfS continuation measures I could find cover undergraduate entrants (a search summary says postgraduate taught completion is above 90%, but I could not trace it to a primary table; see Claims to verify) [unverified]. The honest statement for the site is "no national figure is published for taught-master's drop-out in the UK". Programme-level completion is rarely disclosed by business schools either.
- **Italy: delay is the common failure, not drop-out.** In 2025, 62.7% of Italian biennial master's graduates (AlmaLaurea member universities) finished within the normal two years; for many groups the figure is lower (architecture and civil engineering 40.5%) (AlmaLaurea, Percorsi di laurea 2026 synthesis, read in full) [data]. This counts only people who graduated, so it says nothing about those who never did. Italy's "fuori corso" delay is an economic cost: a graduate who finishes at 26 instead of 24 enters the market later with the same entry salary (author reasoning).
- **LBS reports a 96% reporting rate and excludes sponsored students from its 3-month rate** (LBS MiM 2025) [data], which means its 92% offer rate is for those who completed, reported and were not returning to a sponsor. Completion itself is not disclosed.

### 6.2 Visa-related failure (cite, do not recompute)

For non-EU students the biggest programme-level risk is not the course but the visa. UK study-visa refusal shares by nationality, Q4 2025 to Q2 2026: Pakistan 34% to 41%, Nigeria 17% to 25%, India 7% to 9%, Turkey 1% to 7%, China 0.1% to 1%, United States 0.2% to 0.8% (Home Office datasets, author calculation, as reported in places/origin-countries-non-eu.md) [data]. The tables do not split by sponsor. After the course, the UK Graduate visa shortens from 24 to 18 months for applications from 1 January 2027, and Skilled Worker thresholds are £41,700 (£33,400 new entrant) (see places/visas-and-work-rights.md). Swiss third-country quota 2026 is 8,500, shared by all non-EU skilled hires. EU citizens need no visa to study or work in other EU countries, but do need one for the UK since Brexit, so the same chain applies to them there.

### 6.4 Compounding

Failure modes multiply. A non-EU student targeting UK front-office roles faces (a) a visa refusal chance, (b) a very low conversion rate at the application stage, (c) a time-limited Graduate visa (18 months from 2027), and (d) a Skilled Worker pay threshold above many entry salaries. Each is a conditional probability, and the site should present them as a chain and not collapse them into one score. (author reasoning)

### 7.2 UK evidence: Sutton Trust, January 2025

The Sutton Trust's "Unpaid and underpaid internships" research brief (Erica Holt-White and Carl Cullinane, 23 Jan 2025, https://www.suttontrust.com/our-research/unpaid-and-underpaid-internships/ (fetched)) [data, survey; sample size not stated on the page I read]:
- 51% of recent UK graduates completed at least one internship (+12 points since 2018). Among those who did, 61% were unpaid or underpaid.
- 37% were paid at least the minimum wage (27% in 2018); 23% were paid below it (9% in 2018); about 20% were unpaid. 35% of all graduates did an unpaid or underpaid internship (27% in 2018).
- **Working-class graduates were 20 points less likely than middle-class graduates to do an internship, up from a 12-point gap in 2018.**
- 40% of unpaid interns relied on the "Bank of Mum and Dad" (26% in 2018).
- Only about one in ten internships (11%) were found through open advertisements; family and friends in an organisation were the route twice as often as an advertisement (20%) (the 20% figure comes from a search summary of the same research and was not on the page I read, so treat it as unverified).
- On the employer side, 59% of employers offer internships and 40% support an explicit ban on unpaid ones.

Older but relevant: a 2016 government-commissioned study of investment banking recruitment (Ashley and colleagues, Royal Holloway; published 1 Sep 2016) found, from Sutton Trust 2014 data, that 34% of new entrants to investment banking over the previous three years had attended a fee-paying school and 14% a selective state school, against 7% and 11% of the UK population. It also concluded that outreach programmes focused on work experience rather than mainstream recruitment, and that pre-screening by school grades disadvantaged non-privileged applicants (https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/549994/Socio-economic_diversity_in_life_sciences_and_investment_banking.pdf, read from the downloaded PDF) [data, stale: 2014 and 2016]. Treat it as direction, not as a 2026 share.

Trackr's own users give a counterweight: 21% of its users received free school meals and 61% are BAME (Trackr 2026 report) [data, self-selected]. Platforms that publish roles on day one are a working-class access channel, but the Sutton Trust finding that only about 11% of internships are openly advertised still stands for the economy as a whole.

### 7.3 Italy evidence: social origin of master's graduates

AlmaLaurea's 2025 graduates: 34.7% had at least one parent with a degree (28.5% in 2015). Among biennial master's graduates, 22.4% came from "high social origin" families (entrepreneurs, professionals, managers) and 21.9% from "less favoured" origins (manual or routine clerical parents); in single-cycle degrees 32.3% and 17.2% (AlmaLaurea 2026 Percorsi synthesis, read in full) [data]. The report notes the master's level involves "a further socio-economic selection" relative to the bachelor's. In the employment outcomes model, family background and curricular internship were tested and had negligible explanatory power (AlmaLaurea 2026 Esiti synthesis) [data]. I found no published ISEE (family income indicator) distribution for business-school students, at Bocconi or elsewhere.

### 7.4 What this means for the "standard path"

- The standard path to a London finance seat (first-year spring week, then a summer internship, then the graduate offer) assumes a student can afford a first year with unpaid or poorly paid experience, a London summer, and a master's fee. Where that holds, it selects partly on family resources.
- For a European master's student the money gap appears at a different point: the **post-master's gap period**. In Italy the extracurricular tirocinio floor can be as low as EUR 300 to EUR 500 a month, while in France a post-degree internship at EUR 4.50 an hour is about EUR 680 a month. A student without family support cannot take that as a bridge in Milan or Paris. In Germany and the UK, the legal floor is higher, but the UK's Sutton Trust data show it is often not paid.
- Bridge stipends: the Gemini lead of "consulting deferrals of 6 to 12 months with $10 to 20k bridge stipends" is partly traceable to MBA-class deferrals in 2023 (a snippet says Bain offered some deferred hires $40k to work at a non-profit; see careers/consulting.md) but not to pre-experience master's graduates. I found no MBB or bank policy on deferral stipends for MiM graduates. See the corrections list.

## 8. Variance and luck in hiring

### 8.1 What the evidence says about noise in selection

- **Interview validity is modest and varies widely.** Sackett, Zhang, Berry and Lievens (2022, Journal of Applied Psychology 107, 2040-2068) re-estimated selection validities and put structured interviews at an operational validity of r = .42 and cognitive ability at r = .31 (SIOP summary of the paper, https://www.siop.org/tip-article/is-cognitive-ability-the-best-predictor-of-job-performance (fetched)) [data]. The same paper's unstructured-interview figure (.19) and the 80% credibility interval for structured interviews (.18 to .66) come from search summaries I could not confirm on the page I read, so treat them as unverified. A validity of .42 is high by selection standards and still leaves most variation in later performance unexplained.
- **Interviewers disagree.** Kahneman, Sibony and Sunstein's book "Noise" (2021) is the usual source for the claims that interviewers make widely different assessments of the same people, and that if all you know is that one candidate interviewed better than another, the chance they are the better one is about 56% to 61%. I could only read secondary summaries of these claims (sbam.org, which attributes them to the book without naming the underlying studies) [practitioner consensus, secondary]. Use as direction, not as a measured number for banking or consulting.
- **CV screening is inconsistent in two vendor experiments.** Kickresume (6 Nov 2023) asked recruiters to rate 100 resume-and-job-ad pairs each (over 12,000 pairs in total) and found a Cohen's kappa of 0.49 between recruiters when the same resume reappeared, with recruiters consistent for suitable candidates only 40% of the time (https://www.kickresume.com/en/press/recruiters-seeing-the-same-resume-twice-study/ (fetched)) [anecdotal: vendor study, no stated limitations]. An earlier interviewing.io experiment (10 Nov 2014) had 152 people judge 51 software-engineering resumes; agreement was weak (Fleiss' kappa 0.13) and judges guessed the author's "strong or weak" label right 53% of the time, with no clear gap between recruiters and engineers (https://blog.alinelerner.com/resumes-suck-heres-the-data/ (fetched)) [anecdotal: one author's labels, small sample, engineering not finance]. Neither study covers investment banking or consulting. They show that noise exists in CV screening, not how large it is in your target.
- **Offers are skewed.** In Trackr's 2026 surveys the top 10% of applicants claimed 40% to 41% of offers (section 4). In its spring-week survey, a chart-read agreement scale on "receiving spring week offers is based on luck" put roughly half of respondents at 7 or above out of 10 (chart-read; self-reported attitude, not evidence of luck) [anecdotal].
- **Some of what looks like luck is process.** Trackr found same-day applicants needed 82 applications per offer against 119 for those applying after 72 hours, and past spring-week holders needed 73 against 138 (section 4) [data, associational]. Rolling-process timing and prior brand are not luck, but they are not visible to the applicant until late.

### 8.2 Portfolio-of-applications arithmetic (author reasoning, not a measured result)

Take Trackr's measured rate of about 1 offer per 101 applications (0.99% per application) for UK finance summer internships in 2026. If every application were independent with that same chance p, the probability of at least one offer after n applications is 1 - (1 - p)^n:

| Applications (n) | P(at least one offer), independent model |
|---|---|
| 10 | 9.5% |
| 20 | 18% |
| 30 | 26% |
| 50 | 39% |
| 74 (Trackr average) | 52% |
| 100 | 63% |
| 150 | 78% |

The model compares as follows with what Trackr observed: it predicts about 48% of candidates with no offer after 74 applications, and Trackr saw 54%. For spring weeks (about 3% per application, 8,013 applications for 239 offers; 35 applications on average), the model predicts about 35% with no offer and Trackr saw 49% (Trackr 2026 reports). The model is too optimistic because applications are not independent: some applicants are systematically stronger (the top 10% take 40% of offers), and a weak CV gets rejected everywhere. Three practical readings:

1. **Volume helps, with sharply diminishing returns for a given profile.** Going from 30 to 74 applications raises the modelled chance from 26% to 52%, but each extra application adds only about 0.6 points of probability on average, and less as the total rises. If each application costs hours of tailoring, the marginal hour is better spent on the highest-probability targets.
2. **Quality shifts p, which is worth more than volume.** If tailoring or timing doubled p to 2%, 30 applications would give about 45% rather than 26% (author calculation). Trackr found that extensive tailoring and same-day timing were associated with fewer applications per offer, though these are associations and the sample is self-selected.
3. **Independence is the key assumption.** If the same CV reaches the same weak first-round filter at every bank, the applications are highly correlated and the real benefit of the portfolio is smaller. Apply across different employer types (banks, boutiques, consulting, corporates), because their filters differ.

**What this does not cover:** the figures are UK undergraduate-heavy and finance-focused. They are not rates for a continental European master's student applying to finance or consulting after a master's (see getting-in/recruiting-calendar.md and getting-in/employer-pipelines.md for how those processes differ). Nothing here is a prediction of an individual's chance.

## Hypotheses tested

**H-a (assigned). "Most students at target schools do not enter front-office London IB."**
- Verdict: **supported as an outcome statement; insufficient evidence for the reason.**
- Holds when: the school is a broad MiM with consulting, tech and corporate recruiters. At LBS MiM 2025, 14% of accepted offers were in investment banking and 34% in finance of any kind, so 86% were not in IB (LBS, 2026) [data, school-reported].
- Fails or is unproven when: you want to say they "failed". The same class had a 92% offer rate in three months, and no school publishes how many applied to banks. A specialised finance master's inverts the picture: HEC's MiF placed 81.4% in financial services (careers/finance.md) [data, school-reported].
- Evidence: LBS MiM employment report 2025 (read in full); HEC MiF via careers/finance.md.

**H-b (assigned). "Seats-versus-candidates ratios overstate the failure rate."**
- Verdict: **partly supported.**
- Holds when: the numerator or denominator counts applications or enrolled students instead of serious applicants. Goldman had 360,000+ applications for about 2,500 internships in 2025, but each student sends many applications, and the ISE's 140 applications per vacancy is not 1-in-140 odds (careers/finance.md; evidence/trends.md) [employer-stated; data].
- Fails when: you ask about a specific, rolling, highly sought-after programme. Trackr's applicants still needed 101 applications per summer offer on average, and 54% had no offer in 2026 (Trackr 2026) [data, self-selected]. Competition at the front door is real, but the right measure is conversion per application and per serious applicant, not seats over headcount.
- Evidence: Trackr summer and spring reports (read in full); ISE top-10 stats; High Fliers via eFinancialCareers.

**H-c (assigned). "Internships and gap years are costly to access for lower-income students in a way that changes who enters."**
- Verdict: **supported for the UK; partly supported for France and Italy; insufficient evidence for European business-master's students specifically.**
- Holds when: the route needs unpaid or underpaid experience. The Sutton Trust (Jan 2025) found working-class graduates 20 points less likely to do an internship than middle-class graduates (gap 12 points in 2018), 40% of unpaid interns relying on parents, and about 61% of internships unpaid or underpaid among those who did one [data, survey].
- Fails or weakens when: the system pays interns or embeds them in the degree. French apprenticeship and the CGE finding (42.3% of hires via final internship or apprenticeship) mean pay accompanies the route; German law covers post-degree interns by the minimum wage. In AlmaLaurea's national model, family background and curricular internship did not significantly predict employment at one year (all fields) [data]. That does not test elite finance or consulting entry, where the evidence is older: 34% of new investment-banking entrants had attended fee-paying schools against about 7% of UK children (Sutton Trust 2014 via a 2016 government study) [data, stale].
- Evidence: Sutton Trust 2025; Royal Holloway/BIS 2016; AlmaLaurea 2026; floors in section 7.

**H-d (own). "Failure risk is concentrated in the first year after the master's, and outcomes largely converge by year three to five."**
- Verdict: **supported.**
- Holds when: you measure employment (any job). Italian second-level graduates: 80.8% employed at one year, about 92% at three, 94.4% at five (AlmaLaurea 2026); EU tertiary graduates 1-3 years out 86.9% employed against 90.1% beyond three years; Italy 78.7% against 87.9%, France 82.0% against 89.6% (Eurostat 2025) [data].
- Fails when: you measure job quality or sector. The first job decides where the lateral market sees you, and 30% to 35% of employed Italian graduates say the degree is not highly effective. The CGE six-month employment rate fell from 90.5% to 76% in three years, so a bad cohort year can be sharper than the long-run convergence suggests (CGE 2026) [data].

**H-e (own). "Average outcomes mislead because offers are concentrated in a minority of applicants."**
- Verdict: **supported in the one dataset available, with sampling caveats.**
- Holds when: you use applicant-survey data. The top 10% of applicants claimed 40% to 41% of offers in both Trackr 2026 surveys and about half got none [data, self-selected, n = 232 and 296].
- Fails or is unknown when: you ask about continental master's students. No comparable data exist. An independent-application model predicts 48% with no offer after 74 applications (observed 54%) and 35% for spring weeks (observed 49%), which is consistent with concentration but does not prove it (author reasoning).

**H-f (own). "A single 'graduate market is shrinking' headline is wrong for part of finance."**
- Verdict: **supported.**
- Holds when: you split by sector. UK investment banks plan 2,365 graduate hires for 2026 against 2,069 in 2025, while accounting and professional services plan 3.5% fewer after falling about a third since 2022-23, and Big 4 UK intakes fell 6% to 29% by firm between 2023 and 2025 (High Fliers via eFinancialCareers; careers/accounting-and-corporate.md) [data via press].
- Fails when: you measure applications. Applications to banks are also rising, so the seat gain is not a competition gain.

**H-g (own). "Programme non-completion is a minor risk next to placement risk."**
- Verdict: **insufficient evidence.**
- What exists: no national UK figure for taught-master's non-continuation that I could verify; Italian data show 62.7% finishing biennial master's on time, but do not count non-completers. Schools do not publish completion for pre-experience master's. The assumption is plausible (most business master's are one to two years and fee-paid), but I cannot support it with a number.

**H-h (own). "For non-EU students the sponsorship chain, not the school, sets the odds."**
- Verdict: **partly supported.**
- Holds when: the destination is the UK, the Netherlands or Switzerland, where visa refusals, Graduate-visa length and salary floors act in sequence. UK study-visa refusals run from 0.1% (China) to 41% (Pakistan) by nationality (places/origin-countries-non-eu.md) [data]; the Graduate visa shrinks to 18 months from 2027; the Dutch stay rate of non-EEA graduates at five years is 38.5% (Nuffic) [data].
- Fails or is unproven when: you want a school effect. The Home Office tables do not split by sponsor, so "LSE versus a lower-ranked school" cannot be tested.

## Common mistakes and myths

1. **"140 applications per vacancy means a 1-in-140 chance."** Applications are not people and applicants send many (ISE 2025; Trackr 74 applications per candidate) [data]. The same ratio hides sectors: 290 in retail and FMCG against lower figures elsewhere [data].
2. **"Only 10% of Tier-1 students get in, so 90% fail."** Class-share outcomes are not application outcomes. At LBS 92% had an offer in three months and 86% were not in IB [data, school-reported].
3. **"A 90% placement rate means a 90% chance of a good job."** Rates are for respondents, often exclude sponsored or not-seeking students, count temporary contracts as employment, and can rest on low response (CGE response rates 36.6% to 62.4% by cohort) (evidence/how-numbers-mislead.md) [data].
4. **"A global seat count is a London seat count."** Goldman's 2,500 interns are global; UK investment banks' 2,069 hires are all divisions (careers/finance.md; High Fliers) [employer-stated; data via press].
5. **"If I apply to 100 firms, I will get at least one offer."** Only if applications are independent and your profile is average. Observed no-offer shares (54%, 49%) exceed the independence prediction (48%, 35%) (author reasoning on Trackr data) [anecdotal / data].
6. **"Advert counts show jobs lost."** Adzuna's −45% graduate adverts compare with ISE's −8% for vacancies (ISE, 25 Aug 2026) [data].
7. **"Everyone who studies abroad will stay."** Only 20.3% of EEA graduates remained in the Netherlands five years on (Nuffic 2025) [data].
8. **"Unpaid gap-year internships are a normal first step."** In the UK 35% of graduates did an unpaid or underpaid internship and low-income graduates are less likely to be able to (Sutton Trust 2025) [data]. In Italy the statutory floor can be EUR 300 to 500 a month [data].
9. **"Half of banking analysts quit in two years."** The traceable figure is 44% leaving the industry within three years for one 2010 London cohort of 250 (eFinancialCareers, 2013, via decisions/long-horizon-careers.md) [practitioner consensus, stale]. No source for 52% was found.
10. **"Consulting firms pay deferred graduates $10,000 to $20,000."** The only documented offers were to MBA hires in 2023 (Bain: $40,000 non-profit, $30,000 language or education, $20,000 other, for an April 2024 start; Poets&Quants, 21 Apr 2023) [practitioner consensus]. No evidence for pre-experience master's graduates.

## Decision rules

1. **If** a school shows only a "% employed within 3 months" and a salary, **then** ask for the sector split, the denominator and the response rate, **because** the LBS report shows 92% offers and still 86% outside IB.
2. **If** you target front-office finance or MBB from a broad MiM, **then** treat it as a minority outcome (LBS: 14% IB, 5% MBB-named employers) and keep a second route active in parallel, **because** most of the class lands elsewhere even with strong career services (LBS 2025) [data, school-reported].
3. **If** you quote odds for a tool, **then** show "share of the class that ended up here" and not "chance of acceptance", **because** nobody publishes applicants per seat per school.
4. **If** you rely on volume applications, **then** diversify across employer types and timing, apply on opening day for rolling processes, and put tailoring time into the 10 to 20 best-fit targets, **because** the independent-application model gives a modest gain per extra application (about 0.5 points at 50 to 74 applications) and Trackr found same-day and tailored applications needed fewer applications per offer (association only).
5. **If** you are a non-EU student targeting the UK, **then** model four gates (visa, offer, sponsorship, salary threshold) separately and plan a fallback country, **because** failure at any one ends the route and the Graduate visa shortens to 18 months from 2027.
6. **If** you are an EU student studying abroad without the local language, **then** assume you return or move within five years, **because** only 20.3% of EEA graduates stay in the Netherlands at five years.
7. **If** you cannot fund a low-paid post-degree gap, **then** prefer programmes with paid or embedded work (French alternance, CGE schools, UK placement years) over programmes that depend on unpaid experience, **because** 42.3% of French grande-école hires came via final internship or apprenticeship and UK unpaid internships skew to those with parental support.
8. **If** you plan to work in Italy, **then** budget on EUR 1,495 net a month at one year (EUR 1,903 at five), 80.8% employment at one year across all second-level fields, and only 28.7% on a permanent contract at one year, **because** these are AlmaLaurea's national 2026 figures (excluding Bocconi, Polimi, LUISS, Cattolica).
9. **If** you are targeting UK audit or tax, **then** apply to regional offices and expect fewer seats than in 2023, **because** Deloitte UK started 44% of its 2026 intake outside London and Big 4 UK intake fell by about a sixth.
10. **If** a figure comes from a self-selected survey (Trackr, forums, vendor studies), **then** label it "sample of applicants" on the site, **because** it is not a random sample of students.
11. **If** a statistic has no date, a scope or a denominator, **then** do not show it as a probability, **because** the audit of seat-versus-candidate and attrition claims found most could not be traced.
12. **If** the comparison is between two programmes' employment rates, **then** compare response rate and definition first, **because** a 76% rate at 37% response is not comparable to a 92% rate at 96% response.

## What this changes in the site

- **Show outcome shares, not odds.** On school and programme pages, label sector shares "share of the class that took these jobs (school-reported)" with the year and reporting rate. Do not label them "chance of getting in". LBS MiM 2025 is the model case: 92% offers, 88% accepted, IB 14%, consulting 30%.
- **Add a "what we cannot know" row.** Where applicants per seat or master's share of an intake is not published, say so. Do not fill the gap with an estimate unless it is clearly marked "author estimate".
- **Replace single scores with a chain of gates for non-EU readers** (visa, offer, sponsorship, salary threshold, stay rate), each with its own source and date, rather than one probability.
- **Split "failure" into four types** (no offer, not target employer, below graduate level or non-permanent, left the country) and show the type behind any rate.
- **Add an affordability warning by country**: legal internship floors (France EUR 4.50 an hour, Italy EUR 300 national and EUR 500 Lombardy, Germany minimum wage for post-degree interns, UK NMW with Sutton Trust evidence that it is often not paid) beside the rent and living-cost estimate for the city.
- **If a portfolio-of-applications explainer is added**, show the independence assumption and the observed-versus-modelled gap, and label the table "illustration from UK undergraduate applicant survey, not a prediction for you".

## Corrections to the Gemini leads

1. **"About 1,200 to 1,400 London summer-analyst seats against more than 12,000 competing top-school candidates, so about 90% fail."** The Gemini council audit (council-audit.md) states "about 1,200 to 1,500" seats and "only 400 to 500 seats remain for all European Master's programs combined" with no source; I could not find the 12,000 figure in the Gemini files I could open (the named red-team-audit.md path is empty; v2/audit/ contains no files). The only verified UK seat figure is High Fliers' 2,069 UK investment-bank graduate hires in 2025 (all divisions, all degree levels), with 2,365 planned for 2026. The comparison sets seats against enrolled students, not applicants, so it overstates failure. The "over 90% end up in regional corporates, Big 4 or commercial banking" version is contradicted by LBS MiM 2025 (finance 34%, consulting 30%, technology 21%).
2. **"Branch-A placement probability should be 10 to 15%."** Gemini's own ROI spec (v2/depth/roi-engine-spec.md) gives 20% to 35% for Branch A, so its two files disagree. The one verified school figure is 14% for IB at LBS MiM, but this is a class share, not a probability, and Branch A as Gemini defines it also includes MBB and big tech. No source for either range.
3. **"52% of IBD analysts exit within 24 months."** Not traced (also noted in decisions/long-horizon-careers.md). The nearest traceable figure is 44% leaving the industry within three years, for 250 London M&A analysts who joined six banks in 2010 (PER via eFinancialCareers, 2013), which is stale and measures leaving banking, not one bank. Deloitte UK's firm-wide voluntary attrition was 10.0% in FY25.
4. **"Consulting deferrals of 6 to 12 months with USD 10,000 to 20,000 bridge stipends."** The Gemini council audit gives "6 to 12 months" and USD 2,000 to 5,000 a month, a different figure from the lead. The documented 2023 case is MBA hires only (Bain: USD 40,000 non-profit, USD 30,000 language or education, USD 20,000 other, for an April 2024 start; McKinsey start dates staggered to February 2024 and BCG to January 2024; Poets&Quants, 21 Apr 2023). 5. **"Placing 100 students from Bocconi's 1,500-person cohort is under 7%."** The 1,500 cohort size is unsourced; Bocconi employment reports were not read this session. The arithmetic (100 / 1,500 = 6.7%) is fine, the premise is not verified.

## Numbers to treat with care

- Trackr: self-selected user surveys (n = 296 summer, n = 232 spring), UK-focused, undergraduate-heavy; return-offer split (20/36/44%) and the 3+ offer share for spring weeks are chart-read.
- LBS sector shares: school-reported, CSEA-compliant, one class; the IB headcount (~50) and MBB headcount (~21) are author calculations from rounded percentages.
- Eurostat F041 counts: Germany's count is understated by coding; the Netherlands 2022 and 2023 values are identical; the EU27 sum excludes economics (F031 is a different field).
- "Roughly 20,000 to 25,000 Italian business-economics master's graduates" is an author estimate combining AlmaLaurea member-university counts (about 17,000) with an unmeasured allowance for non-members.
- AlmaLaurea 3- and 5-year unemployment rates and 3-year employment were read from a chart series in the synthesis PDF (chart-read, consistent with the stated 94.4% at five years).
- High Fliers: sector figures reached via press summaries (eFinancialCareers, search summaries); the full report was not opened. The 3.5% fall for accounting and professional services is a search summary.
- Italy's regional internship amounts other than Lombardia come from money.it (21 Jan 2026), a secondary source.
- Milan rent (EUR 1,523) is crowdsourced Numbeo data via another library file.
- Sutton Trust 2025: the sample size and fieldwork details were not on the page read; the 11% "openly advertised" and 20% "family and friends" shares come partly from search summaries.
- Selection-validity numbers (.42 structured interview, .31 cognitive ability) were read on a SIOP summary; .19 and the credibility interval are search summaries only.
- The Royal Holloway/BIS investment-banking socio-economic data are from 2014 to 2016 (stale).
- Vendor studies on CV screening (Kickresume 2023; interviewing.io 2014) have no peer review and cover engineering or generic roles.
