# Careers in commodities trading, energy and shipping

## Bottom line

1. **The Swiss cluster is small, concentrated and cyclical.** The FSO's first official estimate is CHF 19.2bn of value added in 2024 (2.3% of GDP), 58.4% of it in Geneva and 27% in Zug (FSO via blue News, 18 Nov 2025) [data, secondary report]. Core trading-firm employment was about 10,300 in 2021 (federal authorities) against an industry claim of 35,000 for the wider ecosystem; the two numbers measure different things.
2. **The best-documented route for a business master's graduate is operations, risk, finance or trade finance, not a trading seat.** Trafigura's Development programme (operations, deals desk, trade finance) and TotalEnergies' rotations (middle office, trading operations, market analysis) are built that way; "Commercial" programmes lead *towards* junior trading (Trafigura, TotalEnergies pages, 2026) [employer-stated]. No house publishes the share of graduates who become traders.
3. **The University of Geneva MSc is a hiring funnel, not just a degree.** In 2024, 170 applied, 100 were shortlisted, 33 were placed in paid traineeships (four days at work, Friday and Saturday in class); CHF 500 a semester (UNIGE, Commodity Conversations 2024) [employer-stated]. Gemini's "3 days a week" was wrong.
4. **There is no verified graduate pay survey.** Public numbers are a 2018 recruiter table (analyst CHF 70–95k, operator 90–125k, bonus 10–20% of base) and a 2026 guide of unknown method (graduate CHF 90–130k). The "10–20% of desk P&L" bonus rate is not found in any source I could open; only the *mechanism* (traders paid a percentage of their book's profit) is supported (Reuters 2024).
5. **The money is in ownership, not graduate salary.** Vitol paid USD 5.9bn to about 600 employee-owners for 2025 on USD 4.2bn profit; Trafigura has over 1,400 employee shareholders. Averages such as "USD 785,000 per employee" at Vitol in 2022 are distorted by partners and a headcount that includes non-trading staff.
6. **Timing risk is large.** Vitol's 2025 profit roughly halved, Gunvor's fell about 85%, then a 2026 Hormuz-related spike put Trafigura's first-half profit above its whole-year 2025 figure. Intakes are small and annual; Trafigura's graduate windows for 2027 were mostly already closed on 30 September 2026 (China open to 23 October 2026).
7. **Growth is in power, gas, LNG, metals and new businesses, but entry hiring evidence is missing.** Houses are moving into renewables, power trading, carbon credits and shipping; McKinsey (via snippet) projects power, gas and LNG to overtake oil as the biggest value pool by 2030. I found no count of graduate hires in these desks.
8. **Business students face two screens that STEM students do not.** Programmes lean on numeracy and coding tests (Gunvor: 40-minute maths/logic/Python test) and the paid pipelines with Eni are STEM-only (100/110 minimum, EUR 1,500 a month).
9. **Controversies are real and documented.** Trafigura (Swiss conviction, January 2025), Glencore (USD 1.1bn pleas, 2022) and Vitol (USD 135m DPA, 2020). They shape compliance hiring and public attitudes.


## 1. Industry size and concentration in Switzerland

**What the official statistics say.** The Federal Statistical Office (FSO) published its first monetary statistics on commodity traders on 18 November 2025: companies active in commodity trading generated about **CHF 19.2 billion of value added in 2024, or 2.3% of Swiss GDP**. Geneva accounted for 58.4% of that value added, Zug 27%, Vaud 6.5%, Ticino 3.6% and Zurich 2.3%. For comparison, tourism was CHF 23.8 billion (2.9%) and pharmaceuticals about CHF 75 billion (about 10%) (blue News, reporting the FSO release, 18 Nov 2025, https://www.bluewin.ch/en/news/commodities-sector-almost-as-important-for-switzerland-as-tourism-2969272.html) [data, via secondary report; the FSO/Federal Council release itself could not be opened]. The FSO says this was the first time it had calculated the figure for the Federal Council, so there is no trend yet.

**Jobs: three different numbers, three different definitions.**

| Figure | What it counts | Source | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|
| About 10,300 persons, 966 businesses (2021 data) | Businesses whose main activity is commodity trading (fuels, metals, ores) plus some supporting activities | Federal authorities, 25 Aug 2023 (https://www.admin.ch/gov/en/start/documentation/media-releases.msg-id-97472.html) | [data] |
| About 35,000 jobs, about 4% of GDP | Industry-wide estimate including shipping, trade finance, inspection, legal and other service providers | SuisseNégoce annual report, March 2023, as quoted by swissinfo (https://www.swissinfo.ch/eng/swiss-commodities-trading-plummets-at-faster-rate/48706136) | [employer-stated], industry association, methodology not checked |
| 2.3% of GDP | FSO 2024 value added of trading companies | FSO via blue News (above) | [data] |

The gap between 10,300 (core traders) and 35,000 (whole ecosystem) is the main trap. A graduate should read "10,000 core jobs, concentrated in two cantons" as the size of the *trading firms themselves*, and the larger figure as including the banks, inspectors, shipping, insurers and lawyers around them. The industry's 4% of GDP and the FSO's 2.3% also differ because of scope (the FSO counts trading companies only). I did not find a primary FSO table that gives 2024 employment for the new series.

**Market shares.** SuisseNégoce's figures, quoted by swissinfo in 2023, were about 40% of oil trades, 60% of metals, 65% of cotton, 55% of coffee and 35% of cocoa handled by Swiss-based companies (swissinfo, Aug 2023, URL above) [employer-stated, industry association]. These are industry estimates with no public methodology that I could find, and they are older than 2024. Gemini's "50% of oil, 60% of metals, 50% of grains" does not match this source (oil 40% here), and a different set of secondary sources gives 35% for oil, 50% for cereals and 40% for sugar; I could not trace either set to a primary document, so the site should present them as "industry estimates, 35–40% for oil".

**Concentration.** Geneva and Zug together hold about 85% of value added (58.4% + 27%, FSO via blue News). Rough job split from a cantonal source quoted in a search snippet (Geneva 44%, Zug 21.4%, Ticino 9.5% of core jobs) was not verified at the source (unverified, snippet only).

**Firm-level scale (employer-stated).** Trafigura reported FY2025 (to 30 Sep 2025) net profit of USD 2,666 million, about 14,500 employees (the group includes terminals, mining and logistics) and over 1,400 employee-shareholders (Trafigura press release, 9 Dec 2025, https://www.trafigura.com/news-and-insights/press-releases/2025/trafigura-publishes-2025-annual-results/) [employer-stated]. Vitol is owned by roughly 600 of its employees according to Bloomberg, reported in The Star on 27 July 2026 (https://www.thestar.com.my/business/business-news/2026/07/27/vitol-pays-out-us6bil-to-top-senior-staff) [practitioner consensus, press]. Gunvor reported 2025 net income of USD 104 million (adjusted USD 567 million) on gross profit of USD 1.63 billion (Gunvor, 2025 results, https://gunvorgroup.com/financial/gunvor-group-results-2025/) [employer-stated].

**Why this matters for a graduate.** The partners are few (hundreds per house) and the headcount of "commercial" roles is small; the bulk of graduate-accessible jobs are in operations, finance, risk and analytics around them. Hiring is also bursty, because intakes are tiny (see section 3: 33 trainees per year in the UNIGE master).

**What the pages reveal beyond the table (non-obvious).**
- Trafigura describes the *Development* programme as the route into operations, deals desk and trade finance, and the *Commercial* programme as the route "towards junior trading roles". The Commercial programme is therefore not a trading seat on day one, and the page's own sample story of a "former graduate now Traffic Operator" in Montevideo shows that operations is a normal destination (Trafigura early-career page above) [employer-stated].
- TotalEnergies' programme is built from middle office, trading operations and market analysis rotations, with no desk rotation listed (TotalEnergies page above) [employer-stated]. Gunvor's rotations include "operations, risk, analytics" (via snippet).
- Gunvor's eligibility asks for "up to 12 months" of experience, so it is a *pre-experience* scheme, but the page lists trading, engineering, maths, statistics and data science rather than business degrees (Gunvor page above) [employer-stated]. A Management or Marketing master's applicant should expect to compete on numeracy tests (maths/logic/Python) rather than on the degree label.
- Programme numbers and class sizes are not published by Trafigura, Gunvor or TotalEnergies. I found no acceptance rates; any "1% acceptance" figure for these houses would be unsupported.
- Trafigura's career page shows only a handful of open graduate intakes at one time (China open; four others closed on 30 Sep 2026), consistent with an annual, short application window. Check the page each August to October (see getting-in/recruiting-calendar.md for the general calendar).

**Alternative entry route: the trainee model (Geneva).** The University of Geneva master is the only programme I found where the company contract is part of the degree (section 3). Companies in the Geneva cluster also say they take trainees through the programme even where they have graduate programmes (UNIGE executive director, Commodity Conversations, 15 Jan 2024, https://commodityc.substack.com/p/eliane-palivoda-herren) [employer-stated, academic administrator].

**Reading the UNIGE numbers.** About two applicants in three who pass the academic shortlist (100 of 170) then fail to land a traineeship: 33 of 100 shortlisted students were placed, because admission is effectively conditional on a company offer ("only 33 were offered a traineeship by a company"). That makes the programme a *hiring funnel* rather than a purely academic admission. It is also the single best-documented route into Geneva trading with a master's degree. The mix of 33 students (82% Swiss or European) shows that EU candidates are not at a disadvantage in selection, but I did not find the share of graduates who stay with their trainee employer beyond the director's statement that "most stay within the company" (same interview) [employer-stated, no figure]. The UNIGE programme page lists employment prospects in broad terms (banking, consulting, insurance, international trade) and gives no placement statistic.

**Mismatch with Gemini.** Gemini wrote "students work 3 days/week at Vitol, Trafigura, Cargill, Bunge" and that UNIGE gives an "authentic recruitment monopoly". The school says four days a week of work (Monday to Thursday) and names company *types* (trading, shipping, inspection, audit, banks), not those four houses. The programme itself says that Rotterdam and others "offer similar courses, but they are not linked to traineeships" (same interview) [employer-stated], so Rotterdam is not a direct pipeline in the same sense either.

**Eligibility gap for business students.** The best-documented paid pipelines (Eni with Politecnico, TotalEnergies at IFP) are for STEM graduates. A Management or Finance graduate has the UNIGE trainee route, the trading houses' own programmes (with numeracy tests), or the lateral route through trade finance, risk or compliance. Bocconi and the other business schools on the calculator do not publish commodity-trading placement lines that I could find (not verified); see getting-in/employer-pipelines.md for the school-to-employer evidence that does exist.

## 4. Pay and bonus structures

**The honest summary: there is no recent, primary, role-level pay survey for trading-house graduates in the public domain.** What exists is (a) a 2018 recruiter table, (b) a 2026 recruiter guide of unknown method, (c) aggregate payout figures from audited accounts that are dominated by senior partners, and (d) press reports on how traders are paid. Employers do not publish starting salaries or bonus rules.

**(a) Recruiter benchmark, Switzerland, 2018 (stale).** Michael Page's commodities-trading salary factsheet (PDF created April 2018; press release 21 June 2018) lists base salary in CHF and bonus as a share of base (https://www.michaelpage.ch/sites/michaelpage.ch/files/Commodities_Trading_FACTSHEET_EN.pdf) [practitioner consensus, recruiter survey, 2018, possibly stale]. Selected rows:

| Role | Min / average / max base (CHF) | Bonus, % of base |
|---|---|---|
| Commodities Trading Analyst | 70k / 80k / 95k | 10 to 20 |
| Commodities Trading Operator | 90k / 110k / 125k | 10 to 20 |
| Demurrage specialist | 70k / 110k / 120k | 10 to 20 |
| Middle officer (trader's assistant) | 80k / 110k / 160k | 10 to 20 |
| Risk manager | 120k / 150k / 200k | 20 to 50 |
| Supply and demand analyst | 90k / 120k / 170k | 10 to 30 |
| Trade finance officer | 100k / 120k / 130k | 10 to 20 |
| Energy trader, boutique house | 120k / 160k / 250k | 40 to 120 |
| Energy trader, "blue chip" company | 140k / 200k / 350k | 40 to 150 |

The same document says the market was "squeezing" salaries in operations and back-office roles after reorganisations of support functions, and that "oil is still king" in pay. Its own example is a CHF 350,000 base with a 150% bonus (CHF 875,000 total) at the very top (Michael Page, 21 June 2018, https://www.michaelpage.ch/recruitment-expertise/news-and-research-centre/media-releases/energy-traders-top-commodities-trading-salary-tables) [practitioner consensus, 2018]. Eight years old and before the 2021-23 windfall: use for *structure* (junior bonuses of 10–30% of base, trader bonuses a multiple of that), not for levels.

**(b) 2026 recruiter-style guide (unverified method).** A June 2026 Geneva guide gives graduate trader or junior analyst CHF 90,000–130,000 base plus variable; a trader with three to seven years and an established book CHF 150,000–300,000 base plus 100–300% variable; operations/logistics managers CHF 90,000–140,000 (Upreer, June 2026, https://upreer.ch/en/guides/commodities-trading-geneva/) [anecdotal: a single commercial guide that lists GTSA, FSO and company reports as sources without linking figures to them]. Search results also quote the same CHF 90–130k band attributed to graduates; I treat them as one source. The 2018 CHF 70–125k analyst/operator base range and the 2026 CHF 90–130k band are broadly compatible, but neither is a survey of what Trafigura, Gunvor or Vitol pay.

**(c) What the houses disclose is ownership and payouts, not pay scales.**

| Firm | Disclosure | Source and tag |
|---|---|---|
| Vitol 2025 | Profit USD 4.2bn; USD 5.9bn paid to executives and senior staff via buy-backs, more than profit; roughly 600 employee-owners; since-2022 cumulative profit USD 41bn | Bloomberg via The Star, 27 Jul 2026 [practitioner consensus, press] |
| Vitol 2022 | Average salary plus bonus about USD 785,000 across 3,311 employees (USD 394,000 in 2021), excluding distributions to about 450 shareholder-executives | Bloomberg citing audited accounts, via Gestión (https://gestion.pe/economia/empresas/trader-de-energia-vitol-empresa-de-materias-primas-duplico-salarios-tras-ganancia-record-noticia/) [practitioner consensus, press, secondary] |
| Trafigura FY2025 | Net profit USD 2,666m; about 14,500 employees; over 1,400 employee-shareholders | Trafigura press release, 9 Dec 2025 [employer-stated] |
| Gunvor 2025 | Net income USD 104m (adjusted 567m) after USD 462m of Q4 charges taken by new management; Q1 2026 gross profit already equal to full-year 2025 | Gunvor results page [employer-stated] |

Do not read the Vitol "USD 785,000 average" as a graduate or even a typical figure. The headcount (3,311) includes terminals, shipping and other staff, and the mean is pulled up by traders. Likewise, "profit paid to 600 partners" says nothing about what a 24-year-old operator earns.

**(d) How a trader's bonus is calculated: what is and is not supported.** A Reuters investigation drawing on "at least a dozen" trading sources says "most traders at Vitol, Trafigura and others get their pay as a percentage of profit of their book", and that top traders can earn millions in a good year (Reuters via The Globe and Mail, 11 Jul 2024, https://www.theglobeandmail.com/business/article-trading-houses-lure-top-talent-from-bp-shell) [practitioner consensus, press]. The *mechanism* (a share of the book's profit, not a discretionary pool fixed by HR) is therefore well supported. The *rate* ("10% to 20% of desk P&L") that Gemini states is **not** found in that article or in any other source I could open: I found no employer, regulator or survey document that gives a percentage. Gemini presented it as "practitioner consensus" with a citation to the site's own salaries file, which does not contain it. Treat it as unverified, plausible, and highly variable by desk, seniority and house (and the figure of one bank "up to 17%" that surfaced in a search snippet comes from bank trading, not the independent houses, and is also unverified).

Three non-obvious consequences follow from the mechanism:
1. **Junior roles are outside it.** Operators, analysts, risk and finance staff are paid base plus a discretionary bonus, which the 2018 survey puts at 10–30% of base. Only traders with a book get the percentage-of-profit deal.
2. **The real upside is equity.** Houses are employee-owned (Vitol about 600, Trafigura over 1,400, Gunvor 100% employees after the 2025 buyout), and the largest sums go through buy-backs and dividends to partners (USD 5.9bn at Vitol for 2025) [practitioner consensus, employer-stated]. A new graduate does not receive this for many years, if ever, and the payouts are highly cyclical (Vitol's 2025 profit was roughly half that of 2024).
3. **Oil-major pay is the comparator, and it is lower.** Reuters contrasted trading-house payouts with BP and Shell chief executives who each took "just slightly over $10 million" in salary and bonus in 2023 (same Reuters article) [practitioner consensus]. No figure for major-company graduate traders was found.

**Bank commodities desks are not the same thing.** eFinancialCareers reports that commodities professionals at banks saw average compensation fall by 14% in 2025, on a 37% fall in average bonuses (eFinancialCareers, 2026, snippet only: https://www.efinancialcareers.com/news/banking-trading-bonuses) (unverified, snippet only) [practitioner consensus]. This is a survey of bank desks, so not evidence about Vitol or Trafigura.

**Other hubs (snippet-level only).** Payscale-type pages suggest a London "Trader, Commodities" average of about GBP 70,000 with bonuses of up to GBP 35,000, and Singapore about SGD 124,800 (Payscale, 2026, search snippet) (unverified, snippet only) [anecdotal]. I would not use these for graduates.

**Geneva against a London investment bank (H-b), author calculation.** The site's salaries file uses a median Swiss economics master's graduate at CHF 87,100 (BFS) and an anecdotal London IB analyst base of GBP 70–90k with total pay of GBP 100–150k (money/salaries-and-roi.md). Placing the 2026 Geneva guide's CHF 90–130k graduate band (anecdotal) beside that: on *base pay*, Geneva trading roles sit at or above the top of the London IB analyst range, and Swiss income taxes are lower than the UK's, but IB analysts' bonuses are large and Geneva rents are among the highest in Europe. I have no verified Geneva tax-and-rent calculation for a CHF 100k employee and no verified London IB graduate base from an employer, so the claim cannot be confirmed (see Hypotheses).

**Headcount and restructuring signals.**
- Trafigura's average staff count rose from 12,479 (2023) to 13,086 (2024) to 14,476 (2025), per a search snippet of the annual report (unverified, snippet only); the press release states "approximately 14,500" (Trafigura, 9 Dec 2025) [employer-stated]. The figure includes asset-linked staff, not only traders.
- Oliver Wyman's analysis, as summarised in a snippet, said cost per trader had risen more than 25% since 2019 and operating costs by 45%, with houses "beefing up" risk, analytics and finance teams (Oliver Wyman, 2024, snippet only) (unverified, snippet only). That would fit the entry-route finding above: growth in the support and analytical functions that graduate programmes feed.
- Agribusiness is shedding jobs rather than adding: Cargill said in December 2024 it would cut about 8,000 of 164,000 staff (5%) to "cut costs and restructure" (Agri-Pulse, 4 Dec 2024, https://www.agri-pulse.com/articles/22001-cargill-to-reduce-workforce-by-5/amp) [practitioner consensus, trade press]. A report of job cuts at Louis Dreyfus could not be opened (unverified, snippet only).
- Hiring from majors: Reuters reported Vitol, Trafigura and Mercuria poaching named traders from Shell, BP and TotalEnergies in 2024 (Reuters via Globe and Mail, 11 Jul 2024) [practitioner consensus]. For graduates this shows the *lateral* flow runs from majors to houses at senior level, not the other way.
- A Bloomberg report in August 2025 said Trafigura hired a team of precious-metals traders to push into gold and silver (search snippet only) (unverified, snippet only).

**The energy-transition question: where are the jobs growing?**
- McKinsey, in a 2025 analysis summarised by S&P Global (page returned 403), said trading profits fell more than 30% in 2024, with oil and power down about 40% and LNG down 23%, and projected that power, gas and LNG would overtake oil as the largest value pool by 2030, reaching about USD 115bn EBIT (McKinsey via S&P Global, 24 Feb 2025, search snippet only) (unverified, snippet only).
- Houses are moving into assets and new businesses. Trafigura's results cite renewables through MorGen Energy and the Nala Renewables joint venture (Trafigura, 9 Dec 2025) [employer-stated]; its press feed lists "first power transaction in Brazil" (22 Jul 2026), a carbon-removal-credit forestry project (24 Sep 2026) and the launch of "Volare Shipping" (21 Sep 2026) (Trafigura early-career page footer, read 30 Sep 2026) [employer-stated]. Gunvor's graduate rotations include its Nyera renewables division and Clearlake Shipping (Gunvor page above) [employer-stated]. TotalEnergies' trading graduate scheme explicitly covers "oil, natural gas and low-carbon power trading" (TotalEnergies page above) [employer-stated].
- What I could not establish: the number of graduates hired into power, gas or carbon desks. Gemini's claims that "record bonus pools for algorithmic dispatchers" and "S&P Global" data show hiring concentrated in power are not supported by any source I could open. The evidence supports "growing value pool and new businesses", not "more entry jobs than in oil".

**What this means for graduates.** The 2022–23 windfall years were followed by a 2025 slump at some houses (Vitol, Gunvor) and by a 2026 spike. The graduate intakes at the houses I checked are small and annual, so a student's timing (the cohort that applies in the autumn before graduation) is more exposed to whichever year it lands in than the long-term sector trend. Programmes that sit inside majors (TotalEnergies Trading, BP, Shell, Eni) are cushioned by group earnings; the independent houses are not. See also evidence/trends.md for the AI-and-entry-level backdrop, which affects operations and analyst roles in particular (these are the roles most exposed to automation of routine reporting; I found no commodity-specific data on that).

**Two industry-specific points.** First, programmes such as TotalEnergies' ask for "willingness to be internationally mobile" and rotate you through Geneva, Houston and Singapore. Where a rotation falls in a country in which you lack work rights, the employer has to sponsor it, which is a bigger hurdle for non-EU than for EU graduates, though no house states a policy. Second, the UNIGE trainee format needs a Swiss work permit from the first day of the traineeship; the programme advises that "companies invest time and money in their trainees", which includes permit costs, and a non-EU applicant should expect to be assessed on that (author inference, the interview does not mention permits).

## 7. Hours, culture, risk and controversies

**Hours and culture: evidence is thin.** I found no employer-stated or survey figure for hours at the trading houses. Gemini's "60–75 hours a week" for houses and "45–55" for majors have no source and I could not trace them. What is on record:
- The UNIGE executive director describes the trainee format as "intense": 100% at the office Monday to Thursday and 100% at the university on Friday and Saturday, "every weekend for a year", with mandatory attendance and no recordings or online courses (Commodity Conversations, 15 Jan 2024) [employer-stated, academic administrator].
- Michael Page's 2018 factsheet notes that houses want versatile staff, and that boutiques have expanded the scope of support roles (2018) [practitioner consensus, stale].
- Round-the-clock coverage is inherent to the physical business (shipping, time zones), but I have no verified rota or on-call data.

**Diversity.** In the UNIGE master, women were 42% of the 2024 class, against 31% in 2008, with years (2015 and 2020) in which they were 5%; the director says "commodities and shipping remain a male environment" (same interview) [employer-stated].

**Regulatory and reputational controversies (described neutrally; all are matters of public record).** Entry-level staff should know these exist, because compliance, KYC and sanctions work is a growth area in these firms and a large part of what graduate programmes in middle office deal with.
- **Trafigura:** On 31 January 2025 the Swiss Federal Criminal Court convicted Trafigura and a former executive of bribing an Angolan official between 2009 and 2011, fining the company about USD 3.3m (CHF 3m) and setting compensation of about USD 145m; the executive received a 32-month sentence, 12 months to be served. Appeal routes existed (Jurist, 31 Jan 2025, https://www.jurist.org/news/?p=271227) [practitioner consensus, press report of a court ruling]. I did not verify whether an appeal has since been decided. The same search also mentioned a USD 76m Brazilian settlement (snippet only, unverified).
- **Glencore:** On 24 May 2022 Glencore International AG and Glencore Ltd pleaded guilty in the US to bribery and commodity-price manipulation, and agreed to pay more than USD 1.1bn in coordinated US, UK and Brazilian resolutions (US Department of Justice, via search results and reports by Business and Human Rights Resource Centre and OCCRP; the DOJ page itself could not be opened) [practitioner consensus, press].
- **Vitol:** On 3 December 2020 Vitol Inc. entered a deferred prosecution agreement with the DOJ over bribery in Brazil, Ecuador and Mexico, paying USD 135m, plus a CFTC penalty and disgorgement (DOJ release via search results; page not opened) [practitioner consensus, press].
- **Gunvor:** the 2025 buyout and the CEO change are in company disclosures; an earlier Swiss corruption settlement and Russia-related scrutiny are widely reported, but I did not fetch a primary source for them, so they are not stated here (Claims to verify #8).
- Civil-society critics, notably Public Eye, publish regular critiques of the sector (e.g. https://www.publiceye.ch/en/topics/commodities/rohma/when-commodity-traders-get-caught/trafiguras-monopoly-in-angola) [practitioner consensus, advocacy source with a stated view]. Reputational risk for a graduate is a personal-brand and employer-switching issue rather than a barrier, but it also affects how Swiss public opinion and politics treat the sector, which is relevant to the permit and tax environment (see the Swiss votes in places/visas-and-work-rights.md).

## Hypotheses tested

**H-a. "Physical commodity trading hires business graduates mainly via operations/traffic, not trading."** Verdict: **partly supported.** *Holds when:* the employer is an independent house or a major's trading arm and the graduate has no STEM or quant profile. Trafigura splits its programmes into Commercial (towards trading) and Development (operations, deals desk, trade finance), and cites a former graduate who is now a traffic operator; TotalEnergies' rotations are middle office, operations and analysis (employer pages, 2026) [employer-stated]; 2018 recruiter data list far more support-function than trader roles (Michael Page, 2018) [practitioner consensus, stale]. *Fails when:* the house runs a Commercial programme (Trafigura, Glencore, Gunvor) or the graduate is a strong quant, where direct analyst seats exist (Vitol's Commercial Analyst, Gunvor's desk rotations). No house publishes the split, so "mainly" is a reasonable inference, not a measured fact.

**H-b. "Entry base pay in Geneva beats London IB after tax and rent."** Verdict: **insufficient evidence** (author calculation, flagged). *Direction:* on base alone, the Geneva trading band (CHF 90–130k, anecdotal 2026 guide; CHF 87,100 median for Swiss economics master's, BFS) is at or above the London IB analyst base range (GBP 70–90k, anecdotal, in money/salaries-and-roi.md). *Fails or unknown:* IB analysts' bonuses (London first-year averaged about GBP 29k in 2023, in careers/finance.md) narrow the gap, Geneva rents are very high, and I have no verified tax calculation or employer-stated starting salary on either side. Also, the claim applies to the houses' trainee and graduate roles, not to the whole sector.

**H-c. "Trading-house bonuses far exceed IB bonuses at junior levels."** Verdict: **not supported at junior level; supported only at senior and partner level.** The only role-level data (2018) give 10–20% of base for analysts and operators (Michael Page, 2018) [practitioner consensus, stale], which is below the 2023 London IB first-year bonus implied by careers/finance.md (about GBP 29k on a GBP 70–90k base, roughly a third or more). Large pay sits with traders paid on book profit (Reuters 2024) and with employee-owners (Vitol USD 5.9bn buybacks for 2025). *Where it could be true:* a junior hired directly on a desk with a profit share in a windfall year, which I found no source for.

**H-d (own). "The UNIGE master is a hiring funnel; admission is largely decided by companies."** Verdict: **supported.** 33 of the 100 shortlisted were placed; the director says "it's not easy" and that candidates must search actively (Commodity Conversations, 2024) [employer-stated]. *Fails if:* you want the degree without a job (the 33 are placed for traineeships; those unplaced do not appear in the class) or cannot take a Swiss permit. No placement statistics are published.

**H-e (own). "Public pay figures for this sector are dominated by partner payouts and stale recruiter tables, so graduate pay is poorly known."** Verdict: **supported.** The 2018 factsheet is the only role-level table I could open; Vitol's average pay figure covers 3,311 staff and excludes partners; no employer publishes starting pay. *Fails:* nothing in the evidence contradicts it.

**H-f (own). "Business graduates are disadvantaged by STEM- and numeracy-screened entry."** Verdict: **partly supported.** Gunvor's list of degrees (trading, engineering, maths, statistics, data science) and its maths/Python test, and Eni's STEM-only paid master's are on the record (employer pages above) [employer-stated]. *Fails:* Trafigura and TotalEnergies list no degree restriction beyond a bachelor's or master's, and the UNIGE master explicitly accepts finance, management, accounting, law and international relations backgrounds (UNIGE director, 2024) [employer-stated].

**H-g (own). "The energy transition is creating more entry jobs than oil."** Verdict: **insufficient evidence.** Value-pool projections (McKinsey, via snippet) and new businesses (Trafigura power in Brazil, carbon credits; Gunvor Nyera) point to growth, but I found no hiring counts. Oil remained "king" for pay in 2018 and the 2026 results are driven by oil.

**H-h (own). "Joining after a windfall year is riskier than joining in a normal year."** Verdict: **partly supported.** Cost growth (per-trader cost up more than 25% since 2019, a snippet-only Oliver Wyman point), the 2025 profit declines (Vitol, Gunvor), and Cargill's 5% workforce cut in December 2024 suggest cost control after peaks [practitioner consensus]. *Fails:* 2026's spike shows reversals can be quick, and the graduate intakes are tiny relative to total headcount.

## Common mistakes and myths

- **"Commodity trading is a trading job."** For most graduates it begins in operations, middle office, risk or trade finance; Trafigura's Development programme exists for this (Trafigura, 2026) [employer-stated]; the UNIGE director lists "audit, trade finance, legal, inspection, shipping, risk, and compliance" among the professions (Commodity Conversations, 2024) [employer-stated].
- **"You get 10–20% of your desk's profit from year one."** Not found in any source; the verified mechanism applies to traders with books (Reuters 2024).
- **"Trading-house bonuses are bigger than IB at every level."** Average figures include partner payouts; role-level data (2018) show 10–20% of base for juniors [practitioner consensus, stale].
- **"Any business master's will do."** The one programme with embedded employment (UNIGE) has a February deadline, a work-placement stage and a mandatory Friday-Saturday schedule; Rotterdam's MEL and others are "not linked to traineeships" (UNIGE director) [employer-stated].
- **"The Swiss data show a 35,000-job industry."** That is an industry-association number; the federal 2021 count of core trading firms was about 10,300 (federal authorities, 2023) [data].
- **"A paid master's at Eni is open to anyone."** STEM degree with 100/110 and age under 28 (Eni pages) [employer-stated].
- **"Geneva equals Vitol and Trafigura."** Vitol's early-careers search results show London, Geneva, Rotterdam and Singapore roles; Trafigura runs graduate programmes in six locations (Geneva, Singapore, Houston, Calgary, Montevideo, Shanghai); Gunvor, TotalEnergies and Glencore (Baar/Zug) are all in the Swiss cluster.

## Decision rules

1. If you are a business (non-STEM) graduate aiming at Geneva trading, **prefer the UNIGE MSc in Commodity Trading or a house's Development/operations programme over a generic finance route** (because the embedded traineeship is the only documented master's-to-job funnel, and operations is the documented entry door). Apply by the 28 February deadline; in 2024 about 20% of applicants (33 of 170) ended up placed.
2. If you want a trading seat and have a quant or STEM profile, **prefer Commercial programmes and desk-analyst roles** (Gunvor, Trafigura Commercial, Vitol Commercial Analyst) because those screens test maths and code.
3. If you want a trading seat and have no quant profile, **start in operations, risk or trade finance for two to three years before asking for a desk move**, because that is the structure of the programmes; do not expect a promotion timetable, none is published.
4. If you are risk-averse about the cycle, **prefer a major's trading arm** (TotalEnergies, Shell, BP, Eni) because group earnings cushion cuts; for uncapped upside, accept a house, but know the profit swings (Vitol 2025 about half of 2024; Gunvor 2025 down 85%).
5. If you are a non-EU citizen, **prefer Singapore or London programmes where sponsorship thresholds are explicit** or the UNIGE route only after checking quota exposure (Switzerland's third-country quota of 8,500 and priority test), because house sponsorship policies are not published.
6. If your degree is STEM and you are Italian or study at Politecnico, **consider Eni's paid master's** (100/110 minimum, age under 28, 27/30 average for a permanent proposal), because it offers EUR 1,500 a month and fees paid, but verify the current call.
7. If you are weighing pay, **do not compare a Geneva trading base with a London IB total** until you have the employer's offer (because the public data are 2018 or anecdotal; see section 4).
8. If you apply to Trafigura, **check the careers page from August** because 2027 Geneva, Singapore, Houston and Calgary intakes were already closed on 30 September 2026, and sign up for notifications.
9. If an employer's headline is "average pay per employee", **discount it** because it includes partners and non-trading staff.
10. If ethics and reputation matter to you, **read the 2020–2025 enforcement record before applying** (Trafigura, Glencore, Vitol) and prefer compliance, sustainability or transition-related roles if you want to be on the controls side.
