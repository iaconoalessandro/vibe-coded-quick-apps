# Careers in software, data, AI and quant development

## Bottom line

1. **For a generalist software job, a consecutive master's barely changes entry pay and does not pay back.** Entry salaries for master's holders were about 6% above bachelor's holders in Swiss ICT (CHF 86,900 against CHF 82,000, swissICT 2025) and about 11% in Germany (about EUR 51,500 against EUR 46,300, secondary source). Two years of forgone net pay take roughly 22 to 34 years to recover from that premium alone (author calculation, section 3c) [data, secondary].
2. **A master's is the entry ticket for research roles, not for software roles.** Amazon's Berlin applied-scientist ad asks for "PhD, or Master's degree and 4+ years", IMC's London ML researcher role asks for an MSc minimum, while Google, Meta and Amazon software postings ask for a bachelor's (master's preferred) [employer-stated].
3. **The entry door narrowed in the US and the evidence for Europe is thinner.** Big-tech new-grad hiring is about 65% below 2019 (SignalFire, June 2026), and 71% of the rebound in US software postings is senior (Indeed, July 2026). Europe has stock data only: EU ICT employment is still growing (+2.6% in 2025, Eurostat) but Germany's IT vacancy shortage halved from 149,000 (2023) to 79,000 (2026) (Bitkom) [data].
4. **"AI is killing junior software jobs" is not shown for Europe and, within US big tech, non-engineering functions fell more than engineering** (software engineers 55% of hires in 2026 against 46% in 2019; design -48%, marketing -36%, SignalFire). Stanford's 19% decline for 22-25-year-olds covers AI-exposed occupations generally [data, US].
5. **A computing bachelor's in Zurich leaves about as much cash after rent as a Swiss economics master's** (about CHF 2,980 against CHF 3,320 a month; author calculation). Bending Spoons' stated junior engineer pay (EUR 63,965) leaves about EUR 1,760 a month in Milan, above the German average master's in Munich (about EUR 1,270) [employer-stated, author calculation].
6. **Conversion MSc: viable in the UK on paper, evidence thin.** The gates are strict (Imperial first-class; UCL 2:1 and no significant computing), and no conversion-only outcome table was found; old Irish data shows 69-78% employment at 3-6 months for a different population [data, stale].
7. **Quant roles do not all need a PhD.** IMC posts bachelor's/master's quantitative researcher roles at the same USD 250,000 base as its PhD role; Flow Traders asks for "a university degree" for graduate traders. Only the ML research track asks for an MSc or PhD. European quant pay is not published on the employer pages read [employer-stated].
8. **Visa floors differ sharply.** UK Skilled Worker for software developers: going rate GBP 54,700, about GBP 38,300 if the 70% new-entrant rule applies (the GOV.UK going-rate table also shows GBP 40,000 in a second, "lower rate" column for this code; that column applies only to Health and Care jobs and to people on Skilled Worker permission granted before 4 April 2024, not to new graduates; the general floor is GBP 41,700 and the new-entrant minimum GBP 33,400; Appendix Skilled Worker SW 4.2(b), read 2 Oct 2026). Germany: Blue Card reduced threshold EUR 45,934.20 for IT and for anyone who graduated in the last three years [data].
9. **Business graduates can enter analytics more easily than product or engineering.** A junior analyst posting (GoCardless) asks for "a basic understanding of SQL and data / BI tools"; APM programmes found skew to computer-science graduates; AI labs post no junior product roles (careers/tech-business-and-startups.md) [employer-stated].

## 1. Entry market 2023-26

**What is sourced and what is not.** The best published entry-level data is American (SignalFire, Indeed US, Stanford/ADP). European evidence is thinner and mostly about stock (how many ICT specialists exist, how many vacancies employers report), not about new-graduate flow. Treat every US figure below as a signal for what multinational employers do, not as a measurement of the European graduate market.

Two readings pull in opposite directions. First, software hiring is no longer collapsing: Indeed's index has risen about 15% since early 2025. Second, the whole recovery sits at the senior end and in AI-titled roles, while engineering new-grad intake at the largest employers is still about two-thirds below 2019. SignalFire adds that "founder" is now a common first job label among top US CS graduates (twice as likely as for the 2022 class) [data]. For a European reader, the cleanest summary is that **the engineering share of big-tech hiring is rising, but the junior share of engineering hiring is falling**.

Indeed's 2026 pieces say software postings are still 27.5% below pre-pandemic. A 2024 Indeed update put the gap at about 33% (Indeed Hiring Lab Q3 2024) [data]. So the gap has narrowed by roughly five percentage points in two years, but the stock of postings is still far below 2020.

### 1b. Europe: stock still growing, but slowly, and shortage is easing

- **Size and growth.** The EU had 10.45 million ICT specialists in 2025, 5.0% of total employment, up 59.4% since 2015, but annual growth slowed from +4.5% (2023 to 2024) to +2.6% (2024 to 2025). About 68.8% held a tertiary qualification. Sweden (8.9%), Luxembourg (8.7%) and Finland (7.8%) have the highest shares; Italy is 3.8%, close to the bottom (Eurostat Statistics Explained, data extracted April 2026, https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/statistics-explained/index.php?title=ICT_specialists_in_employment ; country shares via Eunews, 27 May 2026) [data]. Germany holds 22.2% of the EU's ICT specialists, France 13.7%, Spain 10.2%, Italy 8.7%.
- **Germany.** Bitkom's survey of 853 firms (published 3 Sept 2026) puts unfilled IT positions at **79,000 in 2026**, against 109,000 in 2025 and 149,000 in 2023, which Bitkom headlines as "the shortage has halved". 97% of firms hiring still report difficulty filling IT posts. Software development and architecture is the second most searched field (17%), after IT administration (26%) (Bitkom Research, "Der Arbeitsmarkt fuer IT-Fachkraefte", slide deck 3 Sept 2026, https://www.bitkom.org/Bitkom/Publikationen/Der-Arbeitsmarkt-fuer-IT-Fachkraefte-0) [data, employer survey]. Press coverage of the same release says conditions for entry-level candidates have tightened, with firms advertising fewer posts and registered unemployed IT specialists rising (secondary summaries only; see Claims to verify).
- **Where Germans expect AI to cut IT jobs.** Among firms expecting or already reporting AI-related IT job cuts (n=107, higher statistical uncertainty), the shares naming each group were about 47% experienced specialists, 30% managers and 25% entry-level, per a chart-read of the deck; the text layer of this chart may be in a different order from the labels, so treat it as [data, chart-read, low confidence]. 10% of firms already using AI in IT say they have cut some IT jobs; 32% of firms with IT vacancies say AI will lead them to cut IT jobs. 35% of firms say AI replaces vacancies they "can no longer find or no longer want to fill" (Bitkom, Sept 2026) [data, employer survey]. If the chart-read is right, German firms do not single out entry-level staff as the main AI casualty, which differs from the US posting data.
- **Where AI-labelled work is going.** In Q1 2026, 4.2% of all job titles in Germany were AI-labelled, 3.3% in France, 2.7% in the UK; in Germany 59% and in the Netherlands 58% of AI-labelled titles sit outside tech occupations (Euronews, 24 Jul 2026, https://www.euronews.com/2026/07/24/the-majority-of-ai-jobs-now-sit-outside-technological-occupations-in-europe) [practitioner consensus, secondary: the underlying dataset was not read].
- **Developer survey.** Stack Overflow's 2025 survey reports 84% of respondents using or planning to use AI tools, with trust in AI accuracy falling (46% distrust against 31% a year earlier) (Stack Overflow press release, 2025, https://stackoverflow.co/company/press/archive/stack-overflow-2025-developer-survey/; figures via secondary summaries) [data, self-selected survey]. Secondary summaries also say the share of 18-24-year-old respondents has fallen since 2022 (unverified, secondary).

### 1c. What this means for an entrant in Europe

- The shortage language you read in German and EU policy documents describes mostly experienced profiles. Bitkom's own headline is that the shortage halved in two years.
- European entry hiring is cyclical but not collapsed: EU ICT employment is still growing about 2.6% a year (Eurostat) [data]. The US-style collapse in new-grad intake is a big-tech and VC-backed-startup phenomenon; many European IT hires are in non-tech firms (banks, industrials, public sector), which the SignalFire sample does not cover.
- No European source found gives a direct, consistent new-graduate software hiring series for 2023-26. That is a gap in the evidence, listed in Claims to verify.

## 2. AI and junior software and data work

### 2a. Stanford "Canaries in the Coal Mine?"

The paper's page (Stanford Digital Economy Lab, revised 12 Aug 2026) reports, from ADP payroll data through June 2026, that employment of workers aged 22 to 25 in AI-exposed occupations is **19% below where it would be had it kept pace with less-exposed peers**. The divergence "persists when excluding technology firms and computer occupations" and when controlling for interest-rate exposure and remote work. Declines are concentrated where AI use substitutes for tasks, while where it complements workers "employment is flat or rising" (https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/publication/canaries-in-the-coal-mine-six-facts-about-the-recent-employment-effects-of-artificial-intelligence/) [data, US]. The page, as fetched, gives no separate figure for software developers aged 22-25. A software-developer-specific decline figure often quoted in press (about 20% from the late-2022 peak, from the original 2025 draft) was not found on the revised page, so it is not used here (Claims to verify).

What the paper supports: AI-exposed US occupations employed fewer 22-25-year-olds than comparable less-exposed ones, and not because of tech-sector or interest-rate shocks. What it does not support: a European effect; a causal claim about any one programme; or the idea that experienced engineers are being displaced (experienced workers show no comparable gap).

### 2b. Productivity evidence: mixed, and weakest where it matters most

| Study | Design | Result | Source | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cui, Demirer, Jaffe, Musolff, Peng, Salz (June 2025), three field experiments at Microsoft, Accenture and a Fortune 100 firm | Randomised access to GitHub Copilot, 4,867 developers, 2-8 months | +26.08% completed tasks (SE 10.3%); less experienced developers adopted more and gained more | https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=1148213 | [data], peer-style working paper |
| METR (10 Jul 2025) | RCT, 16 experienced open-source developers, 246 issues of about two hours | With AI allowed, tasks took 19% longer; developers expected 24% faster and afterwards believed 20% faster. Authors explicitly say it is a snapshot of early-2025 tools in one setting | https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/ | [data], small sample |
| METR update (24 Feb 2026) | Same design, late 2025 | Task-time change of -18% (CI -38% to +9%) for returning developers and -4% (CI -15% to +9%) for new recruits, that is faster, but METR calls it "very weak evidence" because 30-50% of developers refused to submit tasks they would not do without AI | https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/ | [data], selection-biased |

Reading them together: in a corporate setting with routine tasks, assistants raised output, and the gain was larger for less experienced staff. In a mature open-source codebase with experts, early-2025 tools slowed people down, and later results are inconclusive. Neither supports the line that AI makes a junior engineer worthless. Both are consistent with the **Indeed finding that employers now want fewer, more senior engineers per unit of output**, because the benefit of AI accrues to people who can specify and review.

### 2c. Employer statements

- Surveyed employers: one in three says it is replacing some entry-level roles with AI, about 40% among technology employers (GMAC CRS 2026, via careers/tech-business-and-startups.md) [data, chart-read for the tech figure; the bar has a wide 95% error bar and about 66 technology respondents, so read it as 'technology leads', not as a precise share].
- Bitkom's German respondents say AI mostly fills vacancies they could not fill (35%), not existing staff (Bitkom, Sept 2026) [data].
- Press reports quote Anthropic's chief executive warning that AI could remove up to half of entry-level white-collar jobs within five years. This was not re-read on a primary page this round; it is a forecast by an AI vendor, not evidence of what has happened (Claims to verify) [anecdotal, secondary].

### 2d. Verdict on H-b ("AI has reduced junior software hiring more than other junior business roles")

Not supported as stated by the evidence we have, and partly supported in a narrower form. Details in Hypotheses tested.

### 3a.1 Frontier AI Labs: Hiring Realities (OpenAI, Anthropic, DeepMind, Mistral)
The explosion of generative AI has created severe misconceptions among master's students regarding entry barriers at premier AI labs:
- **Research Scientist / Member of Technical Staff (MTS):**
  - *Prerequisites:* **PhD in Computer Science, Machine Learning, or Computational Mathematics** from an elite institution (Stanford, MIT, Berkeley, CMU, Oxford, Cambridge, ETH Zurich, ELLIS network).
  - *Publications:* A mandatory track record of **multiple first-author papers at top-tier peer-reviewed conferences (NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR, CVPR)**. A one-year or two-year pre-experience MSc in Data Science or AI without premier conference publications has an effective **0% direct hiring rate** for Research Scientist roles.
- **Research Engineer:**
  - *Prerequisites:* Deep systems engineering and high-performance computing (HPC) proficiency.
  - *Skills:* Direct experience with **CUDA, Triton, distributed PyTorch, GPU cluster networking (InfiniBand, NCCL), kernel optimization, and memory bandwidth tuning**. Master's graduates can occasionally access these roles if they have built verifiable distributed training systems or contributed to core open-source ML infrastructure (e.g. vLLM, Hugging Face, FlashAttention).
- **Core Software Engineer (SWE) / Infrastructure:**
  - *Prerequisites:* Senior-level distributed systems, high-concurrency microservices, Kubernetes at petabyte scale. Very rarely hires fresh new graduates; prefers engineers with 3–5+ years at Big Tech (Google, Meta, Amazon) or high-scale cloud platforms.

The pattern: **generalist software roles ask for a bachelor's (a master's is "preferred" at best), while research-flavoured roles (applied scientist, ML researcher) ask for a master's or PhD, often with experience on top.** Amazon's Berlin applied-scientist ad is the sharpest example: a master's alone does not qualify, it must be paired with four years of experience. Spotify, SAP and Booking.com were not read in this round (Claims to verify); the sibling file reports that Booking.com's Amsterdam graduate tracks need a Dutch university degree, without stating bachelor's or master's (careers/tech-business-and-startups.md).

### 3c. Does direct bachelor's entry beat a master's financially? (hypothesis H-a, author calculation)

**Method.** Compare two paths from the same bachelor's graduate: path A starts work now; path B studies for two years and then starts with the master's premium. I count only forgone net pay and the entry-pay premium, assume tuition at a public Swiss or German university is close to zero (any fee makes B look worse), assume equal pay growth afterwards, and ignore experience compounding (which favours A further). Net pay uses the method in money/salaries-and-roi.md section 5 (author calculation). All figures flagged as author calculations; the inputs are the swissICT and get-in-it numbers above, so they carry those sources' limits.

| Case | BSc gross | MSc gross | Net premium per year | Forgone net pay (2 years) | Years to recover from premium alone |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Zurich (swissICT university degrees) | CHF 82,000 (net about CHF 65,900) | CHF 86,900 (net about CHF 69,800) | about CHF 3,900 | about CHF 131,700 | about 34 |
| Germany (get-in-it national averages) | EUR 46,300 (net about EUR 30,500) | EUR 51,500 (net about EUR 33,300) | about EUR 2,800 | about EUR 60,900 | about 22 |

(Zurich net uses the 80.3% net ratio implied by money/salaries-and-roi.md for CHF 87,100; at CHF 82,000 the true ratio is slightly lower, which would shrink the premium a little. Germany net uses the 2025 income-tax formula and 2026 social-contribution rates, which reproduces money/salaries-and-roi.md's EUR 60,000 net within 1%.)

**Reading.** For a student who already holds a computing bachelor's and wants a generalist software job, **the master's does not pay back through the entry-pay premium within a working lifetime**. The calculation is deliberately simple, but the gap is large enough (22 to 34 years) that reasonable changes in assumptions do not rescue it. So H-a is **supported for generalist software roles** and for readers whose bachelor's is already in computing. It does not apply where the master's is the entry ticket: applied scientist and ML research roles (Amazon, IMC above), conversion from a non-computing degree (section 5), and non-EU readers who need a degree-linked route to a work permit (section 7). Gemini's version of the claim leaned on "L3 versus L4" levelling and equity vesting numbers that I could not find in any primary source; the directional conclusion survives, the mechanism is unverified.

**Caveat for European readers.** In Italy, Spain and much of continental Europe the "bachelor's" is a three-year degree and the master's is the normal credential for engineering jobs, so the relevant comparison is often a three-year laurea against a five-year path, in a labour market with lower pay. Bending Spoons says it hires students and junior engineers without regard to a master's (careers/tech-business-and-startups.md) [employer-stated].

**What the comparison says.**
- **In Zurich a computing graduate with a bachelor's (CHF 82,000) ends up with about the same after-rent cash as a Swiss economics master's graduate (CHF 87,100).** Computing is not a pay penalty relative to the business degrees the library covers; it is a roughly equal alternative in the highest-pay market.
- **In London, a published-ad median for graduate software engineering (GBP 50,000, very thin data) is above LBS's MiM mean (GBP 44,675), but the typical UK graduate software ad is much lower (GBP 29,000 overall).** The UK evidence cannot support a firm claim either way; Imperial's own undergraduate computing tables show graduates reaching Jane Street, Optiver, DRW, Google, Meta and Goldman Sachs [data], which is where the higher London pay sits.
- **Milan is the surprise.** A Bending Spoons junior engineer salary leaves about EUR 1,760 a month after Milan rent, more than the German average master's in Munich and more than the Paris grandes-ecoles mean in the library (EUR 1,260). This is one employer, the only one in Italy with a published scale, and its intake is extremely selective (about 50,000 Polish applications in nine months, per careers/tech-business-and-startups.md).
- **Against finance and consulting:** a Zurich software bachelor's leaves about the same as a Swiss economics master's; a Munich software master's at national-average pay leaves about EUR 1,270 a month, well below Zurich (see careers/finance.md and careers/consulting.md for the bank and MBB ladders, which were not recomputed here). Quant development is the exception: IMC Chicago's USD 200,000 graduate software engineer base is far above any entry salary in the table (US tax and rent not modelled here).

## 5. Conversion MSc programmes

### 5a. What the programmes require (from the calculator's own model)

The model in data/computing-model.js carries five UK conversion programmes. Their published rules, as recorded there (source codes OFF2 = official document read via a secondary summary, TP = third-party, not read at source), are:

| Programme | Rule recorded in the model | Catch |
|---|---|---|
| Imperial, MSc Computing | First-class degree in any subject outside computing; GCSE maths grade A or equivalent | The highest bar of the five; a computing degree disqualifies |
| UCL, MSc Computer Science | 2:1 in a subject other than computer science or IT; maths to A-level standard must be evidenced | Degrees with significant computer science content are not admitted |
| Bristol, MSc Computer Science | Little or no prior academic computing; 2:1 (TP) | Entry statement not read at source; acceptance rate unpublished |
| Glasgow, MSc Information Technology | 2:1 in a subject with under 50% of credit-bearing modules in computing; a 2:2 sometimes accepted with over eight years of experience | The only one that quantifies the exclusion |
| St Andrews, MSc Computer Science | Listed qualifications are "indicative"; competitive applicants are expected to exceed them | Published gap between stated and real bar |

(data/computing-model.js, read 1 Oct 2026; the model is the author's own reading of programme pages, so the rules should be rechecked at source before a user relies on them.) For a business graduate the practical gates are therefore: **a degree class (first for Imperial, 2:1 elsewhere), evidence of maths, and not having too much computing already.** Italian and other EU graduates need to translate their marks into UK classes; the calculator's Italian converter does this.

### 5b. What outcome evidence exists

I could not find a school-published outcome table for any UK conversion MSc that is separate from the school's other MSc students. What exists:

- **Imperial Computing, all taught postgraduates (not conversion-only).** A search-engine summary of Imperial's careers-service table for 2023 says 81% entered employment, 12% further study, 7% seeking, with 41% of employed graduates in information and communications and 15% in financial services; roles included machine learning engineer, data scientist, software engineer and quantitative analyst (Imperial Careers Service, 2023 PGT table; the PDF itself returned 404 so this is snippet-only) [data, unverified, snippet only]. Imperial's undergraduate computing table, which I did read, shows 84% employment, 5% seeking and 40% in information and communications for 2024 (Imperial Careers Service, https://www.imperial.ac.uk/media/imperial-college/administration-and-support-services/careers-service/public/resources/graduate-outcomes/ug-tables/Computing-tables-2024.pdf) [data, undergraduate].
- **UCL.** UCL's course page, as summarised in search, lists top employers from HESA Graduate Outcomes cohorts as Amazon, American Express, BNP Paribas, IBM and PwC. The page itself returned 403 so this is snippet-only [data, unverified].
- **Ireland (old and not like-for-like).** Ireland's Higher Education Authority evaluated the state-funded ICT skills conversion courses for 2011-2015 cohorts: 69%, 78% and 73% of graduates with reported outcomes (2012, 2013, 2014 cohorts) were in employment 3-6 months after finishing, and the report says three-quarters of employed ICT-sector respondents were new to the sector. Participants were mostly over 30 and often unemployed, courses were one year, and the data is over ten years old (HEA, "An Evaluation of Springboard+ 2011-16", https://hea.ie/assets/uploads/2016/12/an-evaluation-of-springboard-2011-16.pdf) [data, stale, different population]. UCD runs an MSc Computer Science (Conversion) open to non-computing graduates (listing via Europa digital skills portal, snippet only) [unverified].
- **Germany.** No conversion-MSc outcome data and no equivalent category was found; the model's own note says conversion MScs "barely exist outside the UK". Business-informatics (Wirtschaftsinformatik) masters may be a partial substitute but were not researched this round.

### 5c. Do employers accept them?

The employer evidence is indirect but consistent: **big-tech and trading-firm graduate postings screen on the degree in hand and on coding evidence, not on whether the master's was a conversion.** Meta, Google and Amazon postings in section 3 ask for a CS or related technical degree or "equivalent practical experience"; IMC's graduate software postings ask for computer science or a related degree plus proficiency in C++ or Java and algorithms. A conversion MSc in computer science meets those lines on paper; whether a recruiter treats it the same as a three-year computing bachelor's was not testable from public data. Imperial's UG table lists a "Junior Software Engineer" and "Quant trader" among undergraduates' roles, which sets the bar conversion students compete with.

### 5d. Risks (consistent with the model's rules)

- **Time and prerequisites.** One year to learn what a bachelor's teaches in three. The model's own scoring puts weight on mathematics and prior programming evidence even for the conversion track; a candidate with none starts far behind peers in algorithm interviews.
- **Timing against the recruiting cycle.** Large-employer graduate schemes recruit in the autumn before graduation; a September-start one-year MSc student has the same cycle but one term of coursework behind them (see getting-in/recruiting-calendar.md).
- **Selectivity and exclusions.** Imperial wants a first; UCL refuses degrees with significant CS; Glasgow quantifies the 50% rule. A business graduate with a quantitative economics degree can be excluded at UCL, so read the rule.
- **Visa.** A UK conversion MSc gives a Graduate visa (18 months from January 2027), but sponsorship afterwards requires a software-developer salary of at least about GBP 38,300 as a new entrant (section 7).
- **Level ceiling.** Research-flavoured roles (applied scientist, ML researcher) ask for a master's plus experience or a PhD (section 3a). A one-year conversion is a route into software, data engineering and analytics roles, not into ML research.

### 5e. Verdict on H-c ("a conversion MSc is a viable route for non-technical graduates into data and software jobs in Europe")

**Partly supported, with the evidence thin.** It is viable in the UK at selective schools, since Imperial's postgraduate computing cohort shows employment and sector patterns similar to undergraduates (snippet-only) and the programme explicitly targets non-computing graduates. It is not demonstrated for continental Europe, and no conversion-only outcomes were found anywhere. See Hypotheses tested.

## 7. Visas and mobility

General rules, fees and cross-country comparisons are in places/visas-and-work-rights.md; this section adds only the tech-specific thresholds I checked on 1 Oct 2026.

- **Germany, EU Blue Card.** For 2026 the general threshold is EUR 50,700 a year and the reduced threshold is EUR 45,934.20 (EUR 3,827.85 a month) for shortage occupations, which include IT specialists, **and, regardless of occupation, for people whose degree was completed within three years before applying** (ServicePortal Berlin, Blue Card page, read 1 Oct 2026, https://service.berlin.de/dienstleistung/324659/) [data]. The get-in-it average entry salaries (EUR 46,300 bachelor's, EUR 51,500 master's; secondary source, section 3b) mean a bachelor's graduate at the average would just clear the reduced threshold, which makes a master's or an above-average employer relevant for non-EU graduates. Note: an automatic summary of the same page misstated the recent-graduate rule; the German text confirms the reduced figure applies. **Non-degree route:** IT and communication specialists need no degree if they have at least three years of relevant experience in the last seven years (ISCO-08 groups 133 or 25) (same page) [data].
- **UK, Skilled Worker.** The salary requirement is the higher of the general threshold and the occupation's going rate. The standard going rate for SOC 2134 (programmers and software developers) is GBP 54,700; for 2133 (including data engineers and computer analysts) GBP 54,900; for 2433 (including statistical data scientists) GBP 55,100 (GOV.UK, "going rates for eligible occupation codes", first published 22 July 2025 and updated 29 April 2026, https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/skilled-worker-visa-going-rates-for-eligible-occupations) [data]. Applicants who are under 26, current students or recent Graduate-visa holders can be paid 70% of the standard rate if the salary is at least GBP 33,400 (GOV.UK, "When you can be paid less", read 1 Oct 2026) [data]. So for a software developer the new-entrant floor is **about GBP 38,300** (the "70% of going rate (option E)" column of Table 1 in Appendix Skilled Occupations reads GBP 38,300 for SOC 2134; GOV.UK Immigration Rules, updated 3 Aug 2026), with a maximum 4-year stay on that route (SW 12.3). New entrants include people under 26 and people whose last permission was a UK Student or Graduate visa that expired under two years ago (SW 12.2). The page also lists a second, lower going-rate column (GBP 40,000 for 2134, the 25th-percentile rate, Table 2) that applies only to Health and Care jobs and to workers whose Skilled Worker permission was granted before 4 April 2024 with continuous permission since, not to new entrants (SW 4.2(b); GOV.UK "lower rate" wording; verified round 3b, 2 Oct 2026). This is above the typical advertised UK graduate software salary in the ad-based figure of section 4 (median GBP 29,000), so a sponsor must pay a premium to hire a non-UK graduate on this route. Graduate visa fee, 18-month change from 1 January 2027 and ILR rules: see places/visas-and-work-rights.md.
- **US.** STEM OPT, the H-1B weighted lottery and the USD 100,000 charge on out-of-country petitions, including the June 2026 court ruling that vacated the guidance, are covered in places/visas-and-work-rights.md; the point for tech is that the weighted lottery favours higher wage levels, which suits big-tech and quant employers over small ones. Gemini's "27%-35%" H-1B selection range is an overall, pre-weighting rate; the library's figure is about 35% overall in FY2026 (not re-checked here), with Level I about 15% and Level II about 31% under the weighted rule.
- **Switzerland.** Third-country quotas (8,500 for 2026) are in places/visas-and-work-rights.md. swissICT reports a median ICT salary of CHF 130,000, but quota scarcity, not pay, is the barrier for non-EU graduates.
- **EU citizens.** None of the above applies inside the EU/EEA and, for Switzerland, the free-movement agreement covers EU nationals. The relevant question for an Italian reader is therefore the labour market, not the permit.

## Hypotheses tested

**H-a. "Direct entry after a BSc beats a master's financially for software roles."** (assigned; author calculation) Verdict: **supported for generalist software roles when the bachelor's is already in computing.** Holds when: the master's raises entry pay by only 6-11% (swissICT 2025: CHF 82,000 to CHF 86,900; get-in-it 2026: about EUR 46,300 to EUR 51,500) and costs two years of net salary. On those inputs the premium alone takes about 34 years (Zurich) or 22 years (Germany) to recover the forgone pay (author calculation, section 3c). Fails when: the target is applied science or ML research (Amazon Berlin: "PhD, or Master's and 4+ years"; IMC London ML researcher: MSc minimum), the reader is converting from a non-computing degree, the reader needs a degree-linked work permit, or the national bachelor's is a three-year degree with a weak local labour market. Key evidence: swissICT via Netzwoche [data, secondary]; get-in-it [practitioner consensus, secondary]; Amazon and IMC postings [employer-stated]. Gemini's "L3 versus L4" mechanism was not found in any primary source.

**H-b. "AI has reduced junior software hiring more than other junior business roles."** (assigned) Verdict: **not supported as stated; partly supported in a narrower form.** The data that would support it does not exist for Europe and for the US it points the other way inside big tech: in SignalFire's 2026 report software engineers rose to 55% of big-tech hires (from 46% in 2019) while design hiring fell 48% and marketing 36%, so non-engineering functions fell more [data, US]. The narrower form that holds: new-grad hiring overall is about 65% below 2019 at big tech and about 76% below at early-stage startups (SignalFire 2026); software postings are 27.5% below pre-pandemic with 69.3% of them senior, the highest share of any sector Indeed tracks (Indeed, July 2026) [data, US]. Stanford's 19% gap is for AI-exposed occupations in general, not software specifically, on the revised page I read. Limits: US data, top-20 CS graduates only in SignalFire, no European series, and AI is one of several causes (interest rates and over-hiring in 2021-22 also matter).

**H-c. "A conversion MSc is a viable route for non-technical graduates into data and software jobs in Europe."** (assigned) Verdict: **partly supported, evidence thin.** Holds when: the programme is a selective UK one (Imperial asks for a first; UCL a 2:1 and maths evidence), the student can show programming and maths, and the target is software, data engineering or analytics. Imperial's postgraduate computing table (snippet-only) shows 81% employed and 41% in information and communications in 2023, though not for conversion students separately. Fails when: the target is ML research, when the degree already contains computing (UCL refuses it), or in continental Europe where the category barely exists. Old Irish data (2011-15 cohorts) shows 69-78% employment at 3-6 months for state-funded ICT conversion courses, for a different, older population. Key evidence: computing-model.js rules; HEA 2016; Imperial snippet [data, mixed quality].

**H-d (own). "Germany's IT shortage makes entry-level IT jobs easy to get."** Verdict: **not supported.** Bitkom says the shortage "halved" from 149,000 vacancies (2023) to 79,000 (2026), and 97% of firms still report difficulty filling posts, but vacancies are mainly for experienced profiles, and the same release is reported as showing fewer postings and more unemployed IT specialists for starters (secondary). Holds where: the candidate has scarce skills (cloud, security, AI). Key evidence: Bitkom Research, 3 Sep 2026 [data, employer survey]; press summaries [practitioner consensus, secondary].

**H-e (own). "A computing bachelor's in Zurich leaves as much cash after rent as a business master's."** Verdict: **supported (author calculation).** CHF 82,000 gives about CHF 2,980 a month after rent against CHF 3,320 for the Swiss economics master's median. Holds for EU citizens who can work in Switzerland. Fails for non-EU readers (quota) and if employer-specific pay differs from the survey medians. Key evidence: swissICT 2025 [data, secondary]; BFS 2024 [data]; money/salaries-and-roi.md.

**H-f (own). "The UK sponsorship floor makes software a hard route for non-EU graduates."** Verdict: **partly supported.** The new-entrant floor for SOC 2134 is about GBP 38,300 (70% of GBP 54,700), above the median advertised graduate software salary in the ad sample (GBP 29,000) but below the London median ad (GBP 50,000). Holds outside London and outside big employers; fails at banks, big tech and trading firms. Key evidence: GOV.UK going rates and "When you can be paid less" [data]; ITJobsWatch [anecdotal].

**H-g (own). "Top quant firms hire only PhDs and top MFE graduates."** (Gemini's claim reversed.) Verdict: **not supported for IMC and Flow Traders; insufficient evidence for Jane Street, Citadel and Hudson River.** IMC lists bachelor's/master's quantitative researcher roles at USD 250,000 base, the same as its PhD role; Flow Traders asks for "a university degree" for graduate traders. The ML research track does want an MSc or PhD. Key evidence: employer boards, 1 Oct 2026 [employer-stated].

**H-h (own). "AI coding tools make junior developers more productive, so juniors are safe."** Verdict: **not supported as a guarantee.** The three-company field experiments found +26% completed tasks with larger gains for less experienced developers (Cui et al., 2025) [data], while METR found a 19% slowdown for experienced open-source developers in early 2025 and only weak evidence of speedup in late 2025 [data, small and selection-biased]. Productivity gains and hiring demand are different questions: Indeed finds the posting rebound sits at senior level.

## Common mistakes and myths

- **"There is a developer shortage, so I will walk into a job."** Germany's own shortage figure fell by about half in two years, and the rebound in US software postings is 71% senior (Bitkom, Sept 2026; Indeed, July 2026) [data].
- **"A master's in computer science adds a lot of pay."** In the two datasets read, the entry premium over a bachelor's was about 6-11% (swissICT 2025; get-in-it 2026) [data, secondary]. Its value is access to research-track roles and conversion, not pay.
- **"Quant firms only take PhDs."** IMC posts bachelor's/master's quantitative researcher roles at the same base as PhD roles; check each firm [employer-stated].
- **"An 'Early Careers' posting means a new graduate."** IMC's Amsterdam "C++ Software Engineer, Early Careers" asks for 2-3 years' experience [employer-stated].
- **"A UK Skilled Worker visa only needs GBP 41,700."** The occupation going rate can be higher; for software developers it is GBP 54,700 (GBP 38,300 at the 70% new-entrant rate) [data].
- **"Recent graduates need the full EUR 50,700 Blue Card threshold."** In Germany the reduced EUR 45,934.20 applies to anyone who graduated within three years, not only to shortage occupations [data].
- **"A tool summary of a legal page is reliable."** An automatic summary of the Berlin Blue Card page reversed the recent-graduate rule; the German original is clear. Read the primary text.
- **"US graduate-hiring collapse equals European collapse."** SignalFire and Indeed data are US-only; European evidence is stock data, with ICT employment still growing 2.6% a year (Eurostat) [data].
- **"Salary medians on job boards are reliable."** UK graduate software ad medians moved 32% on a change in ad count from 10 to 113 [anecdotal].

## Decision rules

1. **If** your bachelor's is already in computing **and** you want a generalist software job in Switzerland, Germany or the UK, **prefer** entering work over a consecutive master's (because the entry premium is about 6-11% and recovering two years of net pay takes 22-34 years; author calculation).
2. **If** you target applied scientist, ML research or quantitative-research roles that need an MSc or PhD (Amazon Berlin, IMC London), **prefer** a mathematically strong master's, ideally with research output (because the postings state it).
3. **If** your bachelor's is in business or economics **and** you want to write code for a living, **prefer** a UK conversion MSc only if you meet the class and maths gates (first for Imperial, 2:1 and A-level maths evidence for UCL), and plan for the one-year recruiting-cycle squeeze (because outcomes data is thin and degrees with significant CS are excluded).
4. **If** you are a business graduate who wants analytics rather than engineering, **prefer** proving SQL and a BI tool with a public project before spending a year on a master's (because junior analyst postings such as GoCardless's ask for "basic SQL and BI tools" and no degree).
5. **If** you want product management, **prefer** operations, strategy or analytics first and an internal move (because open APM programmes found were aimed at computer-science graduates and AI labs post none).
6. **If** you are a non-EU graduate seeking a UK software job, **prefer** employers that can pay at least about GBP 38,300 and sponsor (banks, big tech, trading firms) over small firms (because 70% of the GBP 54,700 going rate is the floor).
7. **If** you are a non-EU graduate wanting a German IT job, **target** salaries above EUR 45,934.20 within three years of graduating (because the reduced Blue Card threshold applies for graduates and IT shortage occupations).
8. **If** you want a quant software role in Amsterdam, **read the title and experience line** and apply to "Graduate Software Engineer" in your final year, not "Early Careers" posts that ask 2-3 years (because IMC's postings differ).
9. **If** you are an EU citizen choosing a labour market for a software job, **rank Zurich first on after-rent cash and Milan (Bending Spoons) or Munich next**, and treat London as strong only at large banks, big tech and trading firms (author calculation, section 4).
10. **If** you read a headline that AI is ending junior developer jobs, **check** whether the data is US, big-tech, or top-20-CS-only before applying it to your country (because SignalFire and Indeed are US data and European evidence is stock data).
11. **If** you are an Italian student with a three-year laurea in computing, **compare** immediate work at a firm like Bending Spoons with a two-year continental master's, and treat the pay premium of the master's as small (because Bending Spoons states it hires students and juniors without a master's requirement; careers/tech-business-and-startups.md).

## Corrections to the Gemini leads

1. **"UK Skilled Worker threshold GBP 38,700 / new entrant GBP 30,960"**: outdated. GOV.UK now says GBP 41,700 general and GBP 33,400 minimum for the new-entrant and discount routes, with occupation going rates (GOV.UK, "When you can be paid less", 1 Oct 2026).
2. **"Software postings near pre-pandemic baseline"**: contradicted. Indeed (8 Jul 2026) says software postings are about 27.5% below February 2020. The "71% of the increase attributed to senior roles" figure is real but covers the rise since late February 2025, not "May 2025 to May 2026".
3. **"Quant hedge funds hire exclusively STEM PhDs or top-5 MFE programmes"**: not true of IMC (bachelor's/master's quantitative researcher at USD 250,000 base) or Flow Traders ("a university degree"). Not checked for Jane Street, Citadel or Hudson River.

The cited source is a programme page (not read by me), and programme pages do not normally publish pay; swissICT 2025 gives CHF 86,900 for a university master's at entry, and the BFS gives CHF 85,800 for applied-sciences bachelor's in Technik und IT one year out. Higher pay at Google Zurich may exist but no primary source was found.
5. **"Master's students enter at L3 with no premium; BS with two years reaches L4 first; MS premium under 7-10%"**: the premium estimate is roughly consistent with swissICT and get-in-it (6-11%), but the L3/L4 mechanism and its source (Levels.fyi, BLS) were not found or verified.

9. **"German Blue Card EUR 45,934.20 for bottleneck roles and recent graduates"**: correct (ServicePortal Berlin, 1 Oct 2026).

## Numbers to treat with care

- **swissICT 2025 entry salaries (CHF 86,900 / 82,000 / 90,000 / 81,850):** read via a trade-press report, not the study itself.

- **IMC and Flow Traders base pay:** US postings only, base only, bonuses excluded; no European pay was published.

- **HEA Irish conversion outcomes:** 2011-15 cohorts, older and different population.
