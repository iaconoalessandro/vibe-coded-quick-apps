# Careers in pharma, biotech, medtech and healthcare services

## Bottom line

1. **A business master's is enough to get into the large generalist graduate programmes, but not into the technical niches.** Novo Nordisk's Commercial Business Programme lists no science requirement and says pharma experience is not required (Novo Nordisk, accessed 1 Oct 2026, https://www.novonordisk.com/careers/early-career-programmes/graduate/commercial-business.html) [employer-stated]. The specialist life-science consultancies lean the other way: L.E.K.'s Life Sciences Associate and ClearView ask for science backgrounds, IQVIA for life sciences or business plus 2-3 years of industry experience (search snippets only, see section 5) [employer-stated, unverified].
2. **Market access and HEOR are rarely first jobs for a pure business graduate.** The market-access manager adverts I could see ask for a master's in sciences, public health, economics or health economics, and 2 to 4+ years of pharma experience (search snippet of job ads, Sept 2026) [anecdotal, unverified]. The usual route is a graduate programme or a consultancy first. Treat "straight into market access" as unproven.
3. **Sector-wide Swiss pharma pay is high, but the often-quoted "CHF 110-125k entry" is really the median of all pharma employees.** The Swiss Federal Statistical Office (BFS) puts the pharma-industry median at CHF 10,159 a month in 2024 (about CHF 122k a year), against CHF 7,024 for the whole economy and CHF 10,723 for banks (BFS, 25 Nov 2025, https://dam-api.bfs.admin.ch/hub/api/dam/assets/36195847/master) [data]. That covers all ages and functions. No primary source gives entry pay for business roles.
4. **Even so, a Swiss starting salary beats a London one after tax and rent.** At the BFS median for Swiss economics master's graduates (CHF 87,100) the monthly residual after tax and a Zurich-level rent is about CHF 3,320 (about €3,500), against about £785 (about €915) at the LBS MiM UK mean of £44,675 (author calculation, section 3). London wins only for investment banking or MBB-level base plus bonus. The result is robust to the pharma pay level but not to Swiss permit access (see places/visas-and-work-rights.md).
5. **Pharma was not "recession-proof" for jobs in 2023-26.** Novo Nordisk announced 9,000 job cuts on 10 Sept 2025 (about 11.5% of staff, 5,000 in Denmark) (Pharmaceutical Executive, 10 Sept 2025, https://www.pharmexec.com/view/novo-nordisk-cut-9-000-jobs-global-restructuring-effort) [practitioner consensus, trade press]. Bayer's headcount fell from 102,048 (Q2 2023) to 89,556 (Q2 2025) (BioSpace, 2025, search snippet) [practitioner consensus, unverified]. Large pharma cut more than 22,000 jobs in 2025 (Fierce Pharma, 2025, snippet only) [practitioner consensus, unverified]. Medicine demand is stable; headcount at specific companies is not.
6. **Graduate programmes recruit 9 to 12 months before start.** Novo Nordisk opens applications for a 1 Sept 2027 start in Nov-Dec 2026; Chiesi's graduate vacancies run Sept-Nov 2026 for Sept 2027 (Novo Nordisk, https://www.novonordisk.com/careers/early-career-programmes/graduate/application-process.html; Chiesi search result, Sept 2026) [employer-stated]. A student starting a master's in Sept 2026 must apply in the first term.
7. **Pharma is a small destination for business-school graduates.** LBS MiM 2025: healthcare 1% and healthtech 1% of the class (LBS, 2025, https://assets.london.edu/hxo16fanegqh/7oC5DZdSFwJChIaAb4i9AE/e62b2c069b9c86ab5d75c841c006ee0e/mim_employment_report.pdf) [data]. Bocconi's healthcare/pharma share is reported as 6% only by a secondary aggregator (section 6) [anecdotal]. It is a niche you must aim for, not a default outcome.
8. **Italy is a real pharma labour market, and it grew.** Farmindustria counts 72,200 employees in 2025 (+1% on 2024, +10% on 2019), under-35s up 25% since 2019, 85% of under-35s on permanent contracts, and more than 27,000 jobs in Lombardy (Farmindustria, Indicatori farmaceutici, July 2026, https://www.farmindustria.it/app/uploads/2022/03/IF2026_DEF.pdf) [data, trade association]. Most growth is R&D, production and HQ staff, not necessarily business graduates.
9. **The evidence base for pharma commercial careers is thin.** The largest employers' programme pages are partly blocked to automated reading, school reports rarely isolate pharma, and nobody publishes function-level entry pay. Treat the verdicts below as provisional, and see "Claims to verify".

**What the pages say, and don't:**
- **Novo Nordisk's two pages disagree on dates.** The programme page says "Master's from 2026 or 2027", a 1 Sept 2027 start and applications opening in November 2026; the application page lists postings from 14 Nov to 5 Dec 2025 and says positions for 1 Sept 2027 open on 6 Dec 2026. Read the live page before relying on a date. The process has four steps: CV, transcripts and cover letter answering track questions; a first interview with the programme manager; a virtual Graduate Recruitment Centre built on a business case; a final interview (Novo Nordisk application page, accessed 1 Oct 2026) [employer-stated]. The page does not address visas beyond "legal work authorisation".
- **"Years since graduation" differs by employer.** Roche takes graduates within 2 years of graduating; Novo Nordisk allows at most 1 year of full-time experience (Roche and Novo Nordisk pages, 1 Oct 2026) [employer-stated]. A year of consulting before applying can therefore rule you out of Novo's programme.
- **Number of places is not published** by any employer I could read, so conversion rates cannot be given. Do not infer odds from the existence of a programme.
- **Novartis's visible Basel graduate options for business profiles are finance rotations**, not commercial ones. The US market-access programmes are US-student only (Novartis page, 1 Oct 2026) [employer-stated]. That contradicts the idea that Novartis runs a Basel commercial rotational scheme for business master's graduates. See "Corrections to the Gemini leads".

**Employer scale, Basel.** Novartis says its Swiss operations employ about 10,000 associates, about 5,000 of them in R&D in Basel; headcount in Switzerland is 9,800 of 77,050 worldwide; 68% of Swiss-employer staff live in Switzerland, 18% in France and 14% in Germany; there are about 100 nationalities; and the apprenticeship intake is about 80 a year (Novartis in Switzerland 2025, https://prod1.financial.novartis.com/ch-de/sites/novartis_ch/files/novartis-in-switzerland-2025.pdf) [employer-stated]. About one in three Novartis staff in Switzerland therefore commutes from France or Germany, so cross-border living is a normal way to cut rent.

## 2. Do commercial and market-access roles need a science degree?

- **Generalist graduate programmes: no.** Novo Nordisk (no science requirement stated, pharma experience not required), AstraZeneca IBEX (business, economics, finance or management accepted) and GSK finance (business discipline "preferably") all accept business degrees (pages in section 1) [employer-stated; the last two unverified].
- **Specialist consultancies: it depends on the firm.** A French-language L.E.K. Life Sciences Associate advert asks for scientific backgrounds (biosciences, medicine, engineering, pharmacy) "with" interest in life-science economics; ZS's European entry-level adverts accept "any discipline" with strong quantitative work, and list business, economics, marketing and life sciences; IQVIA's associate-consultant adverts ask for life sciences or economics/business plus 2-3 years' healthcare experience, and its recent-graduate adverts ask for a life-science degree and/or industry experience; ClearView's analyst advert asked for life-science degrees (all search snippets, Sept 2026; the pages themselves were not readable) [employer-stated, unverified]. Pattern: science preferred at the most specialist boutiques; analytics-led firms (ZS) more open.
- **Market access, pricing and HEOR: the ads ask for experience, not just a degree.** Snippets of European adverts (Novo Nordisk Spain, GSK, Novartis, Belgium, Netherlands, Germany, Czech Republic) show master's in "Health Economics, Public Health, Life Sciences, Pharmacy, Economics", with 2 years (Spain) to 4+ years (Belgium/Luxembourg) of market-access or pricing experience (search snippet, Sept 2026) [anecdotal, unverified]. So economics is accepted, but it is a mid-level function you reach after a first role.
- **Italy: field sales is a separate, regulated job.** Farmindustria's annex lists regional rules on visits by scientific information officers (informazione scientifica) (Farmindustria, IF2026, July 2026) [data]. I did not verify the degree rules for that role, so the site should not say whether a business degree qualifies (see Claims to verify). Marketing, finance and market-access staff sit in HQ functions instead.
- **Net:** for H-a, the answer is "yes for generalist programmes, finance, supply chain and marketing; mostly no for technical market access, HEOR and BD analyst roles at science-led boutiques". See Hypotheses.

**Caveats that matter.**
- **BFS figures are sector-wide.** The "standardised monthly gross wage" covers every full-time employee, mainly mid-career scientists, engineers and managers. It is a ceiling for an entry-level comparison, not a floor. A graduate economics median of CHF 87,100 is about 71% of the pharma median multiplied by 12 (author calculation). Whether pharma pays business graduates more than other Swiss employers at entry is not shown by any source I found.
- **The German collective agreement is a floor-and-ladder, not an offer.** I could not confirm which group a business master's graduate is placed in, or whether the monthly amounts are paid 12 or 13 times, so the table gives monthly amounts only. Large pharma employers commonly pay above-scale ("außertariflich") salaries to graduates, but I found no source for that, so it stays unverified.
- **The Danish and Dutch data gap.** I found no Statistics Denmark or CBS NL figure for business-graduate pay in pharma. The Gemini file's DKK 500-560k is therefore left out.
- **No pharma-versus-consulting ladder comparison exists in any primary source I found.** Compare only starting pay here, and see careers/consulting.md and careers/finance.md for the consulting and banking ladders.

## 4. Sector resilience, 2023-26

**What happened (all dates as published):**
- **Novo Nordisk.** Announced 10 Sept 2025: 9,000 job cuts worldwide (5,000 in Denmark), about 11.5% of a 78,400 workforce, $1.25bn of annual savings by 2026 and a hiring freeze on non-business-critical roles (Pharmaceutical Executive, 10 Sept 2025, https://www.pharmexec.com/view/novo-nordisk-cut-9-000-jobs-global-restructuring-effort) [practitioner consensus]. Eurofound's restructuring register records the same event with employment effects from 10 Dec 2025 and an end date of 31 Dec 2026 (https://apps.eurofound.europa.eu/restructuring-events/detail/203354) [data, register based on press]. The CEO attributed it to competition in obesity (Pharmaceutical Executive) [employer-stated, via trade press]. Later press says about 7,800 net headcount reduction in 2025 to about 69,500, and about 2,000 hires in 2026 (HRKatha/Techleap, 2026, search snippets) [anecdotal, unverified].
- **Bayer.** Headcount 102,048 at end Q2 2023, 89,556 at end June 2025; more than 12,000 cuts since the "Dynamic Shared Ownership" model began in January 2024 (BioSpace/Fierce Pharma, 2025, search snippets) [practitioner consensus, unverified].
- **Novartis.** Up to 550 jobs at Stein and up to 130 in Basel by end 2027 (swissinfo/bluewin, 2025-26, search snippets) [practitioner consensus, unverified]. Novartis's own Swiss brochure shows 9,800 Swiss headcount and a CHF 4.7bn Swiss R&D spend in 2025 (https://prod1.financial.novartis.com/ch-de/sites/novartis_ch/files/novartis-in-switzerland-2025.pdf) [employer-stated].
- **Whole large-pharma peer group.** Fierce Pharma counted more than 22,000 fewer employees across large pharma in 2025, with a $300bn patent cliff looming (Fierce Pharma, 2025; page returned 403, snippet only) [practitioner consensus, unverified]. Snippets cite Merck about 6,000, Pfizer about 6,000, CSL 15%, Teva about 2,900.
- **US tariffs and pricing.** The US-Swiss framework deal cut US tariffs on Swiss goods from 39% to 15% and a 15% ceiling for Swiss drugmakers under planned section 232 duties, with pledged Swiss investment of $200bn in the US, including about $50bn from Roche and $23bn from Novartis (Al Jazeera, 14 Nov 2025; Pharma Manufacturing, 2025, search snippets) [practitioner consensus, unverified]. This points to US-located investment and jobs, not Basel jobs.
- **Contrast: Italy.** Pharma employment 72,200 in 2025, +1% on 2024; under-35s +25% in five years (Farmindustria, July 2026) [data, trade association with an interest in the sector's image].

**What this implies for entry-level hiring.**
- Reports differ on where Novo's cuts fall: Eurofound's summary says staff functions such as IT, Pharmaceutical Executive says commercial, R&D and manufacturing. Either way, corporate and commercial functions, where business graduates sit, are not protected. A hiring freeze on non-critical roles is the opposite of resilient graduate demand.
- Graduate programmes continue (Novo for Sept 2027; Chiesi for Sept 2027) [employer-stated], but I found no number of places or any entry-level hiring statistic for pharma. The recruiter survey claiming 64% of biopharma organisations were recruiting in late 2025 and 41% expecting more open roles in 2026 (Panda International, 2026) could not be opened and is in Claims to verify.
- **Cause matters.** The 2023-26 cuts came from patent cliffs, obesity competition, restructuring operating models and US pricing and tariff policy, not from a collapse in demand for medicines. That means pharma hiring is exposed to company-specific shocks that a recession-proof label hides.

## 7. Italy angle

- **Size.** Farmindustria reports 74 billion euros of production (69 billion exports) and 72,200 employees in 2025, with employment +1% on 2024 and +10% on 2019; under-35s +25% and women +16% since 2019; 85% of under-35s on permanent contracts; 66% of staff have tertiary education against 26% across the economy; 90% are graduates or diploma holders (Farmindustria, Indicatori farmaceutici, July 2026, https://www.farmindustria.it/app/uploads/2022/03/IF2026_DEF.pdf) [data; trade association]. The strongest growth is in R&D, production and HQ staff (+13%).
- **Geography.** Lombardy has more than 27,000 pharma employees, Lazio about 13,000, Tuscany 7,100, Veneto about 6,000, Emilia-Romagna 5,000; those five regions hold more than 80% of the total, and Milan is the first province by employees in Farmindustria's ranking (IF2026, p. 86 and Table 110) [data]. The ecosystem including suppliers is about 158,000 people, with Lombardy's supplier base the largest (32,000) [data].
- **Employers.** Italian-owned groups (Chiesi in Parma, Menarini in Florence, Recordati in Milan) run graduate or trainee schemes. Chiesi's graduate programme is 2 years, with applications Sept-Nov 2026 for a Sept 2027 start and a month at Parma HQ (search snippets) [employer-stated, unverified]. Recordati advertises curricular internships in Milan for omnichannel and HR (snippet) [employer-stated, unverified]. Roche's in-company training at Bocconi exists (title only).
- **Pay.** I found no Italian pharma graduate pay figure. Farmindustria says pay per employee is "among the highest" in manufacturing (chart index, values not read) [data]. For general Italian graduate pay see money/salaries-and-roi.md and places/italy-playbook.md.
- **Caveat.** Italian growth is mostly in production and R&D; it does not show business-graduate headcount.

## 8. AI and junior commercial or market-access work

I found no primary study of AI replacing junior market-access, HEOR or brand-analytics work. What exists:
- Cross-sector: GMAC 2026 says one in three employers are replacing some entry-level roles with AI, and Stanford's "Canaries" paper shows 22-25-year-olds in AI-exposed occupations about 19% below trend (both cited in the brief) [data].
- Pharma-specific: recruiter commentary says hiring is rising for market access in rare diseases, regulatory, AI and advanced-therapy roles, and that 43% of pharma companies struggle to find digital skills (Panda International and IntuitionLabs, 2026, search snippets) [practitioner consensus, unverified]. The Farmindustria report cites a Politecnico di Milano observatory showing digital-innovation domains in life sciences such as AI for healthcare-professional engagement, RWE and in-silico trials, but I did not read the percentages (IF2026, p. 12) [data].
- **Reading.** The tasks plausibly exposed are the junior ones: literature reviews, dossier drafting, pricing benchmarks, slide production and data pulls. The skill employers say is scarce is combining health-economics logic with data and AI. That is an inference from recruiter commentary, not a measured effect. Verdict: insufficient evidence either way.

## Hypotheses tested

**H-a. "Business master's graduates can enter pharma commercial roles without a science degree."** Verdict: **supported for generalist programmes, partly supported overall.**
- Holds: Novo Nordisk (no science requirement; pharma experience not required), AstraZeneca IBEX and GSK finance (business degrees accepted), ZS (any discipline), finance and supply-chain tracks [employer-stated; some unverified].
- Fails: L.E.K. Life Sciences, ClearView, IQVIA recent-graduate and most life-science VC adverts prefer or require science; market-access adverts ask for 2-4+ years; Italian field sales is regulated [employer-stated, unverified].
- Evidence: section 1, 2 and 5.

**H-b. "Basel entry pay for business roles beats London consulting after tax and rent."** Verdict: **partly supported (direction yes, size uncertain).**
- Holds: at the BFS economics-master's median (CHF 87,100) the residual after Zurich-level rent is about €3,500 a month, against about €915 at the LBS MiM UK mean (author calculation) [data inputs, anecdotal rent]. Cross-border living from France or Germany adds rent savings.
- Fails: no source gives pharma entry pay for business roles; Basel tax and rent are not modelled; the comparison omits London bonuses and MBB/IB base pay; non-EU applicants face Swiss quotas (8,500 third-country permits for 2026, see places/visas-and-work-rights.md).
- Gemini's "CHF 110-125k entry" is closer to the sector-wide median (about CHF 122k) than to a graduate salary.

**H-c. "Pharma is recession-resilient for entry-level hiring."** Verdict: **not supported as stated; insufficient entry-level data.**
- Holds: drug demand is stable; graduate schemes at Novo and Chiesi still recruit for Sept 2027; Italy's pharma under-35 employment grew 25% in 2019-25 [employer-stated; data].
- Fails: Novo cut about 9,000 jobs and froze non-critical hiring, Bayer shed more than 12,000, large pharma cut more than 22,000 in 2025 [practitioner consensus; several unverified]. No entry-level hiring series found. The 2023-26 shocks were sector-specific (patent cliff, obesity competition, US tariffs and pricing), not a recession.

**H-d. "Market access/HEOR is the best-paid non-R&D entry niche."** Verdict: **insufficient evidence.**
- No function-level pay source exists in what I read; the adverts show it is a mid-level function needing 2-4+ years; Gemini's director pay bands (EUR 130-180k, CHF 220k+) have no traceable source. The only pay comparison available is sector-wide (BFS).

**H-e (own). "Pharma graduate programmes recruit earlier than most students expect, so the decision window is the first term of the master's."** Verdict: **supported (for Novo and Chiesi; mixed for Novartis).**
- Novo: applications Nov-Dec 2026 for a Sept 2027 start; Chiesi: Sept-Nov 2026 for Sept 2027; Novartis Finance Development: Oct-Dec; Finance Rotation: Feb-Mar (employer pages) [employer-stated].
- Fails: Novo's own pages conflict on dates; smaller firms may recruit later (not researched).

**H-f (own). "Pharma is a small destination for business-school graduates, so it requires deliberate targeting."** Verdict: **supported.**
- LBS MiM healthcare 1% (2025), 2% (2024); Bocconi 6% only via a secondary page; HEC Paris MiM top five sectors do not include health [data; anecdotal]. Fails where the school sits in a cluster (Basel, Parma, Copenhagen), which I could not measure.

**H-g (own). "The years-since-graduation and work-experience limits mean a gap year or a consulting year can close a programme door."** Verdict: **supported for Novo, not for Roche's recent-graduate track.**
- Novo: at most 1 year of full-time work experience; Roche: graduated within 2 years (employer pages, 1 Oct 2026) [employer-stated]. Fails where employers use experienced-hire programmes (Roche Perspectives need 3-5 years).

**H-h (own). "Italian-owned mid-cap pharma (Chiesi, Menarini, Recordati) offers more entry-level openings for Italian graduates than the Swiss majors."** Verdict: **insufficient evidence.**
- Farmindustria shows growth in under-35 employment and large Milan/Lombardy employment, but not by function or employer, and no programme sizes are published [data].

## Common mistakes and myths

1. **"Pharma pays like consulting from day one."** Sector-wide median CHF 10,159/month (all ages) is not an entry offer; the entry salaries I could source are lower (AstraZeneca £32,500 start, 2025) [data; employer-stated, unverified].
2. **"Market access is a graduate job."** The adverts want 2-4+ years' experience [anecdotal, unverified].
3. **"Pharma doesn't lay people off."** Novo, Bayer, Merck, Pfizer and others did, in 2025 [practitioner consensus].
4. **"Novartis runs a Basel commercial graduate programme for business master's graduates."** Its page lists finance programmes in Basel and US-only market-access programmes [employer-stated].
5. **"A year in consulting first keeps every option open."** Novo's programme excludes more than 1 year of full-time experience [employer-stated].
6. **"Pharma is the same as healthcare services."** Hospital and insurance roles, medtech sales and CDMOs differ in language needs and entry routes; I did not research them in depth (see Claims to verify).

## Decision rules

1. If you want a big-pharma commercial or finance route and are finishing in mid-2027, then plan applications for Nov-Dec 2026 (Novo) and Sept-Nov 2026 (Chiesi), because programmes recruit 9-12 months ahead.
2. If you want Novo Nordisk's Commercial Business Programme, then do not take more than 1 year of full-time work between degree and application, because that is the stated limit.
3. If you hold a pure business degree and want market access or HEOR, then target a graduate programme or a consultancy first and move in after 2-4 years, because ads ask for experience.
4. If you are targeting L.E.K. Life Sciences, ClearView or life-science VC without a science background, then add a visible science or health-economics credential or choose ZS/Simon-Kucher-type firms, because science is the stated gate.
5. If a Swiss pharma offer is available and you are an EU citizen, then compute net minus rent in CHF using Zurich-level rent and subtract health insurance, because the residual is far above London MiM levels (about €3,000 against about €900).
6. If you are non-EU and Switzerland is the plan, then check the quota first (8,500 third-country permits in 2026, see places/visas-and-work-rights.md), because the pay premium is useless without a permit.
7. If you compare sectors on pay, then use sector-wide BFS data only as a ceiling and never as an entry-level figure, because it covers all ages.
8. If you consider pharma a "safe" sector, then check the specific company's restructuring news first, because 2025 cuts were company-specific.
9. If your master's is in Italy and you want pharma, then target the Lombardy/Milan, Parma and Florence clusters and Italian-owned groups' graduate schemes, because that is where employment is concentrated (Lombardy more than 27,000).
10. If you are choosing a master's for pharma, then choose by proximity to a cluster and employer presence, not by school "healthcare placement", because school reports show 1-6% and rarely isolate it.
11. If a programme page gives no number of places or salary, then do not treat it as a likely outcome in a calculator, because no conversion data exists.
