# Careers in accounting, audit, corporate finance and the public sector

## Bottom line

1. **UK Big 4 entry intake has shrunk by roughly a sixth since 2023 and has not bounced back.** The four firms' press-reported graduate-scheme intakes sum to about 6,500 (2023) and about 5,400 (2024, the latest year in the press data); this is our sum of per-firm figures from The Telegraph via Scottish Financial News (23 Jun 2025), not a published total, and definitions differ by firm. KPMG cut hardest (1,399 → 942 graduates, −29%). Deloitte's 2026 UK intake (~1,500 early careers, 1,353 starting on 1 September) is flat on 2025, not a recovery. Plan on fewer seats, not zero seats. [practitioner consensus / employer-stated]
2. **The squeeze is at the bottom of the pyramid, and it is AI plus offshoring plus a weak deals market, not AI alone.** Firm leaders cite all three. KPMG UK reports about a quarter of its audit practice staff are offshore, and the UK regulator flagged in July 2026 that offshore teams now do judgement work too. The tasks a first-year auditor used to learn on are being removed from the UK. [employer-stated / data]
3. **The profession is substituting school leavers for graduates.** ICAEW's May 2026 survey of 35 mid-tier firms expects a 40% fall in graduate trainee hiring offset by a 49% rise in school leavers. For a master's graduate this means the degree buys less in audit than it used to: you compete for the same trainee grade as a bachelor's or A-level entrant. [data, small sample]
4. **Corporate rotational programmes are the stronger master's-specific entry route.** Siemens Graduate Program and ABB's Global Early Talent Program explicitly want master's graduates; Bosch's Junior Managers Program targets master's graduates and PhDs. All three want real international experience (Siemens and Bosch require at least four months abroad). Airbus caps prior experience at three years. These are the programmes where an MiM or MiF is a filter you pass, not a nice-to-have. [employer-stated]
5. **"CFO track" programmes are often post-MBA, not post-master's.** Siemens' Finance Excellence Program asks for an MBA or PhD plus 7–12 years of finance experience; Novartis's US Finance Development Program is aimed at MBA graduates. For a 23-year-old the realistic door is the graduate programme or a finance rotation (e.g. Airbus Finance NextGen, Novartis Finance Rotation Program), not the "CFO of tomorrow" scheme. [employer-stated]
6. **Visa sponsorship is not guaranteed even at big European corporates.** Airbus says it cannot sponsor Skilled Worker visas for UK AGGP roles. Siemens, by contrast, organises visas for its Finance Excellence Program. Non-EU readers must check scheme by scheme. UK new-entrant rules let a sponsor pay 70% of the going rate with a £33,400 floor. [employer-stated / data]
7. **EU public-sector entry is open to graduates but the odds are brutal.** The 2026 EPSO AD5 graduate competition seeks 1,490 people and drew more than 170,000 applicants (under 1% pass). The ECB Graduate Programme takes about 19 people a year (15 generalists, 4 economists). The Blue Book traineeship offers about 1,000 places per session, twice a year, and needs only a three-year degree. Treat the Blue Book and ECB traineeship as the realistic entry points; treat AD5 and the ECB Graduate Programme as lotteries with a skill component. [employer-stated / data]
8. **The World Bank YPP is not a graduate programme.** It now requires a graduate degree plus 2–6 years of relevant full-time experience (no age limit). A fresh MiF graduate is not eligible; revisit it at 26–30. [employer-stated]
9. **The main qualification decision is ACA vs ACCA vs a national title, and it is decided by your employer and country, not by you.** Big 4 UK trainees sit the ACA (or ACCA) on the firm's funding. ACCA exemptions stop at Applied Skills (no exemptions for Strategic Professional); ICAEW credit is capped at the six Certificate modules plus two Professional modules and requires a degree under five years old with 50%+ marks. A business master's saves you exams at the bottom, not the hard ones at the top. [employer-stated]

Other UK signals:
- Combined Big 4 UK graduate-scheme intake fell from about 6,500 in 2023 to about 5,400 in 2024, i.e. roughly 1,100 fewer entry roles. This is our sum of the four per-firm press figures in the table (6,499 and 5,442); neither City AM (23 Jun 2025) nor Accountancy Today (24 Jun 2025), both read in full, prints a combined total, and the end year is 2024 ("last year"), not 2025 (Scottish Financial News, 23 Jun 2025, citing The Telegraph; https://www.scottishfinancialnews.com/articles/big-four-slash-graduate-jobs-as-ai-takes-over-entry-level-tasks) [practitioner consensus, author sum]. Employer-stated cross-checks: PwC UK said about 1,300 graduates and school and college leavers joined in 2025 (PwC UK press release, 22 Sep 2025); a KPMG spokesperson said it recruits "around 1,000 graduates and apprentices a year" (via Scottish Financial News) [employer-stated].
- Indeed UK accountancy graduate adverts in 2025 were 44% below 2023, versus −33% for all graduate adverts and −20% for all jobs (Scottish Financial News, 23 Jun 2025, same URL as above) [practitioner consensus].
- Total UK headcount also fell: in 2024 KPMG −7%, PwC −5%, Deloitte −5%, EY −3%; in 2025 year-to-date KPMG and PwC −4%, Deloitte −2%, EY −1% (same source) [practitioner consensus].
- Deloitte's 2026 intake: 1,353 graduates, apprentices and placement students starting in the week of 1 September, plus 130 progressing from its technical academy, 20 "future academy" starters and 182 BrightStart apprentices within the total; 590 (44%) start outside London across 21 offices (Deloitte UK, 1 Sep 2026, URL above) [employer-stated]. Non-obvious: nearly half of the seats are regional. Applying for Leeds, Manchester or Birmingham rather than London is a real lever.
- Broader graduate market: Adzuna data showed UK graduate adverts down 45% year on year (Aug 2026); the Institute of Student Employers cautions that its own survey showed only an 8% fall in actual graduate vacancies the previous year, and its 2026 survey is due 14 October 2026 (ISE, 25 Aug 2026, https://ise.org.uk/knowledge/insights/594/ise_response_to_adzuna_graduate_vacancy_figures) [data]. Advert counts overstate the fall in hires; don't read −44% as "44% fewer jobs".

### What the firms say
- Marco Amitrano (PwC UK senior partner): "our entry-level numbers are lower this year, reflecting the wider slowdown in investment, hiring and deal-making", and AI "is certainly reshaping roles" (Fortune, 8 Sep 2025, URL above) [employer-stated]. PwC US also plans to cut entry-level hiring by almost a third over three years (same source) [employer-stated, secondary].
- Mohamed Kande (PwC global chair) to the BBC: PwC has dropped its 2021 ambition to hire 100,000 people over five years; with AI "it will be a different set of people", and the network cannot find enough engineers. PwC hired 1,300 graduates in the UK and 3,200 in the US in the year referred to (PQ Magazine, late 2025, https://www.pqmagazine.com/graduate-jobs-threat-confirmed) [employer-stated, secondary].
- Job-posting mix: a Financial Times analysis found nearly 7% of Big 4 postings required AI expertise in 2025 (under 2% in 2022), versus just under 3% of postings for auditing roles (Computerworld, 19 May 2026, https://www.computerworld.com/article/4173296/the-big-four-accounting-firms-are-now-hiring-more-ai-specialists-than-accountants.html) [practitioner consensus]. Caveat: one posting can cover many graduate hires, so this understates audit volume.

### Offshoring
- KPMG UK's transparency report shows about a quarter of its audit practice staff are offshore; the FRC's annual quality report (22 Jul 2026) found the Big 4 increasingly use "extended team" models in which offshore teams, mainly in India, do work involving professional judgement (The Accountant, Jul 2026, https://www.theaccountant-online.com/news/big-fours-offshore-audit-model-draws-scrutiny-from-uk-regulator/) [data, via specialist press].
- ICAEW's mid-tier survey expects offshoring (40% of firms) and outsourcing (29%) to rise (ICAEW, 22 May 2026, https://www.icaew.com/about-icaew/news/2026-news-releases/uk-accountants-still-in-high-demand-despite-ai-jobs-shift-icaew-report-finds) [data].
- I found no primary source this cycle on Poland or other EU nearshore centres taking UK or continental audit work; see Claims to verify.

### Start-date deferrals and layoffs
- EY-Parthenon US deferred graduate start dates for the third year running (to "no sooner than March 2026"), offering $10,000 to defer to late 2026 or letting recruits walk away with their signing bonus; the reason given was "uncertain and evolving market conditions" in deals, plus low attrition (Scottish Financial News, 16 May 2025, https://www.scottishfinancialnews.com/articles/us-graduates-in-limbo-as-ey-delays-start-dates-offers-10k-deferral) [employer-stated, secondary]. Non-obvious: deferrals hit transaction and strategy arms, not audit. Audit has regulatory deadlines and cannot pause.
- PwC cut about 5,600 roles globally in the year before Kande's BBC interview (PQ Magazine, URL above) [employer-stated, secondary].

### Continental Europe (Italy, Germany, France)
I could not verify 2025–26 graduate intake numbers for Big 4 Germany or France from primary sources. Italy, partly: PwC Italia closed its FY26 (year to 30 June 2026) with about 2,000 new entrants, expects about 2,000 in FY27 "in line with the previous year", with turnover stable at 18% and over 70% of FY27 entrants to be recent graduates (ItaliaOggi, 14 Sep 2026, reporting a PwC Italia statement; all new entrants, not only graduates) [employer-stated, secondary]. Deloitte, EY and KPMG Italy are unverified. See Claims to verify. What can be said from UK evidence: the drivers (offshoring, AI in audit sampling and document review, weak M&A) are firm-wide, so continental intakes are unlikely to be immune. Treat any claim of "record hiring" in Italy with caution until checked against a firm press release.

## 2. Pay, qualification support and progression

**Pay.** No employer-published Big 4 salary table was found for any country; Big 4 careers pages (EY, Deloitte, PwC, KPMG UK, checked 2 Oct 2026) say "competitive". The most recent named-outlet UK figure is the Financial Times as reported by eFinancialCareers on 30 Sep 2026: UK Big Four graduates are offered around £36k, mid-tier firms about £30k, and Grant Thornton intends £40k (secondary report of a paywalled FT article) [practitioner consensus]. Aggregator ranges for other countries sit in Claims to verify and must not be used in the calculator until checked. See money/salaries-and-roi.md for cross-sector pay.

**Qualification support.** In the UK the Big 4 audit and tax graduate schemes are built around a funded professional qualification. KPMG UK's audit graduates study the ACA (or ICAS CA in Scotland); KPMG pays "all costs associated with your qualification", including second attempts and the membership fee on qualifying, but a student who exceeds the permitted number of exam attempts "may see their employment contract end" (KPMG UK careers, audit and FAQ pages, 2026, https://www.kpmgcareers.co.uk/graduate/audit/ and https://www.kpmgcareers.co.uk/faq/) [employer-stated]. KPMG also says its graduates have degrees "in a variety of subjects" and that you cannot switch programme after accepting an offer (same pages) [employer-stated]. Non-obvious: exam failure is an employment risk, not just a delay. Fee clawback terms for early leavers were not verified (Claims to verify, item 5).

**Progression.** The typical ladder is associate → senior associate → assistant manager → manager → senior manager → director → partner. Qualification (ACA/ACCA) usually coincides with promotion to senior after about three years [practitioner consensus, not verified from an employer page this cycle; see Claims to verify].

**The "audit exit".** The standard exit is at or shortly after qualification into corporate roles: financial controller, group reporting, FP&A, internal audit, transaction services or PE portfolio finance. The ACA/ACCA is the portable asset; the firm name is second. ICAEW says ICAEW Chartered Accountants work at 98% of the best global brands (ICAEW careers page claim, https://www.icaew.com/about-icaew/news/2026-news-releases/uk-accountants-still-in-high-demand-despite-ai-jobs-shift-icaew-report-finds, page header, 2026) [employer-stated, marketing claim]. Non-obvious: if AI thins the first two years of audit work, the value of the audit route shifts even more onto the qualification itself, and a candidate who leaves before qualifying keeps little.

## 3. AI and junior audit/tax work

- **ICAEW mid-tier survey (Feb–Mar 2026, 35 managing partners/CEOs of firms with 11–249 principals):** 68% think AI will reduce demand for some early-career accountants, but 83% say it will not mean fewer roles overall; firms predict a 40% fall in graduate trainee hiring offset by a 49% rise in school leavers; 74% will hire more specialists (data analytics cited by 100% of those, technology 96%, sustainability 42%, regulation 38%, financial advice 35%); only 17% can assess AI's workforce impact. ICAEW links the school-leaver shift partly to higher employer National Insurance, Level 7 apprenticeship funding changes and employment-rights legislation, not just AI (ICAEW, 22 May 2026, https://www.icaew.com/about-icaew/news/2026-news-releases/uk-accountants-still-in-high-demand-despite-ai-jobs-shift-icaew-report-finds) [data, small sample].
- **ICAEW Insights (10 Jun 2026):** "in the last two years, the Big Four accountancy firms have reduced recruitment for junior roles. However, with offshoring and general cost reductions factored in, it's unclear how much the growth of AI contributed." Juniors interviewed worry less about job loss than about missing foundational skills and reviewing AI output too early (ICAEW, https://www.icaew.com/insights/viewpoints-on-the-news/2026/jun-2026/ai-are-junior-accountants-worried-about-roles) [employer-stated / anecdotal for the individual quotes].
- **"Pyramid to diamond".** The idea that firms will narrow the junior base and widen the middle is widespread in commentary, but I found it only in blogs and newsletters this cycle, not in an on-record Big 4 statement [anecdotal]. What is on record: PwC's Kande says future hires will be "a different set of people" (more engineers), and posting data show AI roles outnumbering audit postings [employer-stated / practitioner consensus].
- **ACCA surveys.** Not verified this cycle (see Claims to verify).

Implication: the risk to a graduate is not that audit disappears; it is that the entry grade becomes (a) smaller, (b) more contested by cheaper school-leaver apprentices, and (c) more tech-heavy. A master's candidate who adds data analytics, Python or audit-analytics tooling to an accounting base is aligned with the one hiring category firms say they are growing.

| Steuerberater (Germany) | German tax advice | Business or law degree; practice time before sitting is **2 years if standard study time is 4+ years, otherwise 3**; bachelor's and master's study times are added together | €200 admission fee + €1,000 exam fee (default rates); exam may be repeated twice | 2–3 years practice, then 3 written papers and a 90-minute oral |

Non-obvious points:
- **Exemption decays.** ICAEW credit requires a degree transcript dated within the last five years (ICAEW CPL page, above) [employer-stated]. A reader who finishes a master's and then spends four years in banking before switching loses the credit.
- **Exemptions are sold, not given.** ACCA charges per exemption (£86–£114 each) and warns candidates with old qualifications that sitting the exam may be better preparation for the Strategic Professional level (ACCA exemptions page, above) [employer-stated].
- **In Germany the master's does shorten the Steuerberater route.** Because bachelor's and master's study times are added, a 3+2 graduate meets the "four years or more" test and needs 2 years of tax practice instead of 3 (BStBK Zulassung page, above) [data, regulator]. This is one of the few places where the master's itself saves a year on a professional title.
- **The hard exams are never exempted.** ACCA's Strategic Professional and the ICAEW Advanced Level are always sat. A master's saves roughly the first year of a three-year qualification at best.
- **National titles are the licence to sign.** In Italy, Germany and France the statutory audit signature and much tax work require the national title (revisore legale, Wirtschaftsprüfer, commissaire aux comptes). ACCA/ACA are respected but do not replace them. For an Italian reader planning to stay in Italian audit long term, the dottore commercialista/revisore route is the one that leads to partnership; ACCA is more useful for shared services and multinational industry roles [practitioner consensus, not verified from a regulator page this cycle].

Patterns that matter:
- **Master's-only filters exist and are explicit.** Siemens SGP ("master graduates") and ABB GETP ("recent master's graduate") are the clearest. Bosch JMP targets master's and PhD holders. Airbus and Roche accept bachelor's degrees. So an MiM/MiF has the most leverage at German-speaking and Swiss industrials.
- **Experience caps, not age caps.** None of the pages read states an age limit. The limits are framed as years since graduation or of experience: Airbus max 3 years; Roche ORDP within 2 years of graduating; ABB "recent" graduate. A reader who works 3+ years before the master's can age out of Airbus-type schemes.
- **International experience is a hard requirement, not a plus.** Siemens and Bosch both require at least four months abroad. An exchange semester or a double-degree year meets this; an Italian student who has never studied or worked abroad is filtered out.
- **German is a real requirement at Bosch Germany.** "Good knowledge of German and English" is listed for the German JMP. Siemens offers German classes on its FEP, which signals headquarters exposure matters.
- **Permanent contract from day one** at Siemens and Airbus. This is a structural difference from Big 4 training contracts and from EU traineeships.
- **Visa sponsorship varies inside one company.** Airbus UK AGGP will not sponsor; Siemens FEP will. Non-EU readers must read the FAQ of each scheme.

Pay: none of these pages publishes a salary. Airbus says salaries are "benchmarked with the local relevant industry standard". See Claims to verify and money/salaries-and-roi.md.

## 6. Corporate finance and controlling careers

- **The in-house ladder.** Graduate schemes feed controlling, FP&A, treasury, internal audit and corporate M&A. Bosch's finance track moves from plant controlling to business-unit controlling and includes transfer pricing, M&A and business cases (Bosch Germany JMP, above) [employer-stated]. Airbus Finance NextGen rotates graduates into project control, budgetary control, treasury or M&A after two years (Airbus graduates page, above) [employer-stated]. ABB lists controlling, planning, accounting, risk control, investor relations and real estate as finance destinations (ABB GETP, above) [employer-stated].
- **Internal audit as a leadership launchpad.** Novartis advertises a Basel internal-auditor role "designed to attract and develop auditors who aspire to move into future leadership roles, using their experience in Internal Audit as a launchpad" (Novartis graduates page, above) [employer-stated]. This is the in-house version of the Big 4 audit exit, and it gives Big 4 audit leavers a natural target.
- **Path to CFO.** Siemens frames its FEP as producing "the CFOs of tomorrow" but only takes people with 7–12 years of finance experience and an MBA or PhD (Siemens FEP, above) [employer-stated]. Novartis's FDP aims to make MBA hires "Senior Finance Leaders" within six years (Novartis, above) [employer-stated]. So the company-designed CFO track starts around age 30–35. The graduate programme is the feeder into the roles that make you eligible.
- **Does an MiF help?** For in-house finance the MiF is valued as the entry filter (Siemens SGP, ABB GETP require a master's) but it is not the qualification that drives promotion. Siemens FEP asks for a "Master's degree in Finance/Accounting, or equivalent" plus an MBA or PhD. Professional qualifications (ACCA/CIMA) and controlling experience matter more later [employer-stated for requirements; the "matters more later" judgement is practitioner consensus, not verified this cycle].
- **Controlling demand in Germany** and in-house pay figures could not be verified from Destatis or the Bundesagentur für Arbeit this cycle (Claims to verify).

Non-obvious points:
- **The Blue Book excludes people with more than six weeks' prior EU-institution experience**, including internships. Do the Blue Book before any agency or EP internship, not after.
- **The Blue Book needs only a bachelor's.** A master's student can apply in the final year of the master's because the bachelor's is already complete. Using the gap between master's and first job for the October session is a known sequencing move (see decisions/timing-and-sequencing.md). **Calendar check (read on the Commission's traineeship calendar page, 2026-10-02):** applications for the March 2027 session ran 22 Jul to 4 Sep 2026 (closed); applications for the October 2027 session run 15 Feb to 12 Mar 2027 (traineeship 1 Oct 2027 to 29 Feb 2028). Plan for the February 2027 window.
- **The ECB traineeship is capped at 12 months' experience since your most recent qualification**, so it is a now-or-never window straight after the master's.
- **EPSO AD5 has no experience requirement but huge volume.** About 114 applicants per place in 2026. Only 10% of applicants were already working for EU institutions, so insiders are not the bulk of the field (EPSO final-number news, above) [data].
- **ECB Graduate Programme needs EU citizenship.** Non-EU readers are excluded; EU readers from any member state compete for about 15 generalist seats a year.

## 8. Stability vs pay

- Big 4 UK headcount fell in 2024 (KPMG −7%, PwC −5%, Deloitte −5%, EY −3%) and again in early 2025 (Scottish Financial News, 23 Jun 2025, above) [practitioner consensus]. The cuts came through lower intake and slower backfill more than mass redundancy of trainees.
- Deal-linked arms carry the most timing risk: EY-Parthenon US deferred start dates three years running (Scottish Financial News, 16 May 2025, above) [employer-stated, secondary]. Audit intake is stickier because statutory deadlines don't move.
- Corporate programmes at Siemens and Airbus give a permanent contract from day one (employer pages, above) [employer-stated]. EU officials recruited via EPSO are permanent civil servants; ECB graduates are on fixed-term contracts with a route to expert level (ECB, above) [employer-stated].
- Layoff data by employer type (corporate finance vs Big 4 vs public sector) from a statistics office could not be found this cycle (Claims to verify).

**Hours and wellbeing (round 3e, 2 Oct 2026).**
- **UK Big 4 audit hours are now published by the regulator, and they are lower than forum folklore.** In the FRC's Firm Metrics (July 2026, voluntary disclosure; audit practice staff; timesheet-based; includes chargeable and non-chargeable time), PwC states an average total working week of 41.3 hours and 44.3 hours in the January-March peak, on a 37.5-hour contract. Against 35-hour contracts at Deloitte, EY and KPMG, busy-period utilisation for unqualified staff was 114%, 121% and 108% (about 40, 42 and 38 hours); for managers 116%, 124% and 115% (about 41, 43 and 40 hours); PwC managers 123% (about 46 hours) (FRC, https://www.frc.org.uk/documents/9774/Firm_Metrics_July_2026_tA0lmUGa.pdf; hours are my conversion of the published percentages) [data: firm-reported to a regulator]. Caveats: these are averages over all staff in the practice, unrecorded time is not counted, definitions differ by firm, and I found no equivalent for Italy, Germany or France. Individual peaks can be higher.
- **Official pay statistics understate the load.** ONS ASHE 2025 (provisional) gives full-time paid hours of 36.4 a week for accounting, bookkeeping, auditing and tax (SIC 6920) against 38.5 for all UK employees, because it counts paid hours and salaried overtime is unpaid (ONS ASHE Table 16.9a) [data]. Do not read this as short hours.
- **Burnout (self-reported, UK chartered accountants).** caba, the ICAEW community's charity, surveyed over 300 UK chartered accountants "from various professional bodies": 74% had felt burnout symptoms sometimes, often or constantly in the past year (a 20% rise over two years); 41% had considered changing career or leaving the profession; 55% of under-30s said burnout had affected their ability to work or caused time off (caba, Ctrl + Alt + Delete Burnout, 2024, https://www.caba.org.uk/static/ab8650fc-1dbc-4627-880c38ed4e87691e/cabaBurnoutWhitepaper2024.pdf) [data: small, self-reported, not Big 4 specific, no comparison group].
- **Germany:** the Federal Labour Court held on 13 Sep 2022 (1 ABR 22/21) that employers must record working time, so recorded hours exist for German Big 4 staff, but no sector figure for audit or consulting was found (BAuA's published splits are too coarse) (BMAS FAQ, 8 Aug 2025) [data]. German forum figures such as "100-150 overtime hours a year" are [anecdotal].

## Hypotheses tested

**H1. "Big 4 audit is the most accessible visa-sponsored entry route for non-EU master's grads in the UK."**
Verdict: partly supported, with low evidence.
Holds when: the candidate is in the UK on the Graduate visa or a Student visa and the firm will sponsor; UK new-entrant rules let employers pay 70% of the going rate if the salary is at least £33,400 and the person is under 26, a recent graduate or on the Graduate visa, with total stay capped at 4 years (GOV.UK, 2026, https://www.gov.uk/skilled-worker-visa/when-you-can-be-paid-less) [data]. The standard general threshold is £41,700 (GOV.UK, https://www.gov.uk/skilled-worker-visa/your-job) [data].
Fails when: intake is shrinking (−6% to −29% by firm) and firms shift to school leavers, who are overwhelmingly UK residents. And some large corporates refuse UK sponsorship outright (Airbus AGGP) [employer-stated]. I did not verify Big 4 sponsorship policies from firm pages. See places/visas-and-work-rights.md.

**H2. "AI cuts audit junior intake more than advisory."**
Verdict: insufficient evidence; the timing evidence points the other way.
Holds when: considered over five or more years. Audit sampling, document review and reconciliations are the most automatable tasks, and KPMG UK already has about 25% of audit staff offshore [data via specialist press].
Fails when: looking at 2024–26. The visible acute cuts and deferrals hit deals and strategy (EY-Parthenon deferrals) and firms blame the deals slowdown (Amitrano) as much as AI [employer-stated]. ICAEW itself says it's "unclear how much the growth of AI contributed" [employer-stated]. Firm-level splits of intake by service line were not published.

**H3. "Corporate rotational programmes pay less at the start but lead to faster general-management progression."**
Verdict: insufficient evidence on pay; partly supported on progression design.
Holds when: the programme is explicitly a leadership pipeline with mentors and senior exposure (Bosch JMP "express route to executive leadership"; Siemens SGP; ABB GETP with Zurich leadership weeks) [employer-stated].
Fails when: judged against the most senior "CFO" tracks, which are post-MBA with 7–12 years' experience (Siemens FEP) [employer-stated]. No employer page published pay, so the "pay less" half cannot be tested.

**H4. "A master's is a weak signal for Big 4 audit, where a bachelor's graduate is hired at the same grade."**
Verdict: partly supported.
Evidence: firms are moving trainee hiring towards school leavers (ICAEW: −40% graduates, +49% school leavers at mid-tier) [data, small sample]. The Deloitte 2026 intake mixes graduates, apprentices and BrightStart school leavers in one early-careers cohort [employer-stated]. A master's earns ICAEW/ACCA credits, but so does an accounting bachelor's, and credits stop before the hard exams [employer-stated]. I found no Big 4 page offering a higher entry grade for a master's (Claims to verify).
Fails when: the master's is in accounting and delivers many exemptions, or the target is advisory/transaction services, where selection is more academic.

**H5. "The ECB graduate programme is a realistic target for MiF grads from top EU schools."**
Verdict: not supported as a primary plan; supported as a stretch.
Evidence: about 15 generalist seats a year for all EU master's graduates, EU citizenship required (ECB page) [employer-stated]. The ECB traineeship (bachelor's, max 12 months' experience, €1,170/month) is the realistic door [employer-stated].
Holds when: the candidate is an EU citizen with strong quantitative and policy skills applying in the winter cycle right after the master's.

**H6 (own). "Corporate graduate programmes with master's-only filters concentrate in German-speaking and Swiss industrials."**
Verdict: supported on the sample read. Siemens SGP (master's), ABB GETP (recent master's), Bosch JMP (master's/PhD) vs Airbus AGGP (degree level) and Roche (bachelor's accepted) [employer-stated]. Fails outside this sample: Nestlé, Unilever and others were not readable this cycle.

**H7 (own). "Rotational programmes impose experience caps rather than age caps."**
Verdict: supported on the sample. No page read stated an age limit; Airbus caps at 3 years' experience after graduating (internships excluded); Roche ORDP requires graduation within 2 years; ABB wants "recent" graduates; the World Bank YPP explicitly has no age limit [employer-stated]. Implication: time spent working before the master's can count against you.

**H8 (own). "The Blue Book is a higher-probability EU entry than EPSO AD5 for a fresh master's graduate."**
Verdict: supported on seat numbers. About 1,000 places per session, twice a year, for the Blue Book vs 1,490 AD5 places against 170,000+ applicants [employer-stated / data]. The Blue Book applicant count per session was not found, so the exact odds are unknown (Claims to verify).

**H9 (own). "A business master's saves at most about a third of ACA/ACCA exams, never the hardest ones."**
Verdict: supported. ICAEW credit covers at most 8 of 15 modules (all 6 Certificate plus 2 Professional), none at Advanced Level; ACCA exempts only Applied Knowledge and Skills (up to 9), never Strategic Professional [employer-stated]. In practice a general MiM earns far fewer than the maximum; the directory decides.

## Common mistakes and myths

1. **"Big 4 is a safe, guaranteed entry."** Intake fell 6–29% by firm between 2023 and 2024 and has not recovered at KPMG or PwC [practitioner consensus / employer-stated].
2. **"−44% adverts means 44% fewer jobs."** The ISE warns advert data overstate the fall in actual hires (its survey showed −8% vacancies the previous year) [data].
3. **"My master's gets me into the CFO programme."** The named CFO-track programmes (Siemens FEP, Novartis FDP) require an MBA and/or 7–12 years' experience [employer-stated].
4. **"Exemptions are free and always worth it."** ACCA charges per exemption and advises sitting the exam if the prior learning is old; ICAEW credit expires after five years [employer-stated].
5. **"Big European corporates always sponsor visas."** Airbus UK AGGP does not [employer-stated].
6. **"An EU internship first will help my Blue Book application."** More than six weeks in any EU institution makes you ineligible [employer-stated].
7. **"The World Bank YPP is for top graduates."** It needs 2–6 years' experience after a graduate degree [employer-stated].
8. **"AI is the whole story."** ICAEW links the graduate-to-school-leaver shift to employer NI rises and apprenticeship funding changes too, and PwC's UK chief blames deal and investment slowdown first [data / employer-stated].

## Decision rules

1. If you want UK audit or tax and you are an EU citizen with no UK right to work, then check Graduate-visa eligibility first (see places/visas-and-work-rights.md), and apply to regional offices as well as London (44% of Deloitte UK's 2026 intake starts outside London), because nearly half the seats are regional and practitioners report fewer applicants per seat there (the "fewer applicants" part is not verified).
2. If you target a German, Swiss or Austrian industrial programme (Siemens SGP, Bosch JMP, ABB GETP), then get at least four months abroad on your CV before applying (exchange, double degree or internship), because Siemens and Bosch list it as a requirement.
3. If you target Bosch Germany, then plan for business-level German, because "good knowledge of German and English" is listed.
4. If you already have more than two years of full-time work since your degree, then drop Airbus AGGP-style and Roche ORDP-style schemes from your shortlist and target direct-entry roles, because experience caps (3 years; 2 years since graduation) will exclude you.
5. If you want a "CFO track", then join a graduate or finance rotation now and plan an MBA or 7+ years of finance experience later, because CFO programmes (Siemens FEP, Novartis FDP) recruit at that stage.
6. If you plan to take ACA or ACCA, then claim exemptions within five years of your degree (ICAEW) and weigh the fee against the preparation value of sitting the exam, because credit expires and exemption fees run £86–£133 each.
7. If you plan to stay in Italian, German or French statutory audit long term, then prioritise the national title (revisore legale/dottore commercialista, WP/StB, DEC/CAC) over ACCA, because only the national title gives signing rights (verify with the relevant regulator).
8. If you are an EU citizen interested in EU institutions and you are finishing a master's, then apply to the Blue Book for the session that starts right after graduation, and do not take any other EU-institution internship over six weeks first, because that would make you ineligible.
9. If you are an EU citizen interested in central banking, then apply to the ECB traineeship within 12 months of graduating, and treat the ECB Graduate Programme (about 15 generalist seats) as a stretch, because the traineeship window closes and the graduate programme is tiny.
10. If you are a non-EU citizen, then remove the ECB Graduate Programme, ECB traineeship and Blue Book from your list, and look at the World Bank Pioneers internship (member-country citizens), because the EU schemes require EU (or candidate-country) nationality.
11. If you want the World Bank YPP, then plan it for 2–6 years after your master's, because fresh graduates are ineligible.
12. If you enter Big 4 audit, then stay until qualification, because the ACA/ACCA is the portable asset behind the corporate "audit exit" and AI is thinning the pre-qualification experience that has value on its own.
13. If you are choosing between two master's for an audit career, then prefer the one listed in the ICAEW CPL directory or ACCA exemption list for your target, because listed degrees convert directly into credits, while unlisted ones need a full syllabus assessment.
