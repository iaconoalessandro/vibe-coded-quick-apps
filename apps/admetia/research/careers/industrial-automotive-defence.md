# Bottom line

1. **German auto employment is in a real, measured slump: 691,500 jobs at end Q2 2026, down 5.8% in a year and the lowest since 2005** (Destatis, 14 Aug 2026) [data]. Suppliers and vacancies are hit hardest: auto vacancies were about two-thirds below the August 2023 peak and supplier vacancies 82% below May 2019 (ifo/Indeed, Sept 2025) [data].
2. **No graduate-cohort size is published by any OEM or supplier I could read**, so the "OEM trainee cohorts cut 40-60%" claim is unverified. What is established is a sector-wide fall in vacancies and entry-level postings, not a graduate-programme number.
3. **The restructuring deals protect people already employed** (VW: more than 35,000 German jobs cut by 2030 with no compulsory redundancies; Porsche, Bosch similar). The adjustment falls on temps, fixed-term staff and external hires. In 2026 an internship, Werkstudent or thesis place at the target firm is worth more than before (inference from deal structure) [employer-stated + inference].
4. **The IG Metall pay table is real and precise, but it is not "the same for everyone".** BW tariff basic pay from 1 April 2026 is EUR 4,796 (EG 11), 5,129 (EG 12), 5,462.50 (EG 13) for a 35-hour week; with add-ons a starter in EG 12 is roughly EUR 70,000-81,000 a year (my calculation). These figures expire on 31 Oct 2026; IG Metall wants +5% and talks start 7 Oct 2026. Who is covered, which grade a business graduate gets, and performance pay (0-30%) are not equal across employers [employer-stated].
5. **Defence demand is real but the graduate share is unknown.** Rheinmetall backlog EUR 63.8bn (+36%) and a plan for about 40,000 staff; Saab bookings +74%; Thales 8,800 hires in 2025 and 9,000+ planned. Hiring mix is mostly engineers, software, industrial roles. Clearance delays average about 8 months in Germany and some roles carry citizenship conditions [employer-stated; clearance delay secondary].
6. **Language is a posting-level filter, not a country-level constant.** BMW's German AcceleratiON page makes German mandatory while its Mexican page does not. No share-of-roles data exist for the cluster cities.
7. **Italy's Motor Valley is small and engineering-led.** Ferrari has 5,718 staff in total and EBITDA of 38.8% (not 50%+); MUNER is an engineering consortium, not a business-school route. Stellantis Italy is cutting through voluntary exits.
8. **For an EU business graduate**: German OEM pay is competitive on an hourly basis and stable, but entry is narrower in 2026 than in 2022 and German is usually required; defence is the growing alternative but needs patience with clearance.

Pattern: the reduction tools are voluntary exits, non-renewal of fixed-term contracts and agency staff, and hiring freezes, backed by employment-security clauses. In other words, jobs are protected for people already on the payroll, and the adjustment falls on those outside it. Graduates, external hires and temps are the outsiders. This is an inference from the structure of the deals, not a measured result. Porsche's 2,000 non-renewed fixed-term contracts and the Stuttgarter Zeitung story of a Porsche graduate whose agency contract ended in February 2025 (reported via a search summary, single case) [anecdotal] are consistent with it.

## Graduate and trainee cohort sizes: verdict on the "40-60% cut" claim

I could not find a published cohort size for any German OEM or supplier trainee programme. BMW's AcceleratiON page states "not explicitly stated; positions vary by departmental demand" (I read no number on it). Mercedes's pages were not readable (403). Bosch's release says nothing on trainees. The Gemini file's headline numbers on this topic carry no traceable source, and the "40-60% cut" figure could not be confirmed or refuted. Verdict: **insufficient evidence for any specific cohort-cut percentage.** What can be said with sources: vacancies at suppliers fell 82% (ifo), German entry-level postings fell 46% and Werkstudent/intern postings 24% against 2019 (Indeed), and VDI reports that "Nachwuchsprogramme" were discontinued at some firms without giving counts (ingenieur.de, 2025) [practitioner consensus, unquantified].

Business-function roles (controlling, purchasing, HR) are probably hit as hard as engineering because Mercedes's severance offer targeted administration, IT and development, and Bosch's cuts include administration. This is an inference; no data split by function was found.

## Tariff round status (as of 1 October 2026)

- The 2024 deal (pilot in Küste and Bayern, 12 Nov 2024) gave EUR 600 once in February 2025, +2.0% on 1 April 2025, +3.1% on 1 April 2026, and raised T-ZUG B from about EUR 630 to EUR 900 from February 2026. It runs to 31 October 2026 and covers about 3.9 million employees. It also has a hardship clause when net sales margin falls below 2.3% (IG Metall press release, 12 Nov 2024, https://www.igmetall.de/presse/pressemitteilungen/tarifergebnis-in-metall-und-elektroindustrie) [employer-stated, by the union].
- The 2026 round: IG Metall's demand is 5% more, protection of jobs, and a profit-sharing element; the decision came on 23 Sep 2026, talks start 7 Oct 2026, the peace obligation ends 31 Oct and warning strikes are possible from 1 Nov. The union says about one-third of firms are in a poor state (mainly auto and machinery), growth sectors include aerospace and defence, and 15% of firms with a positive outlook still cut staff against 5% three years ago (IG Metall, https://www.igmetall.de/tarif/tarifrunden/metall-und-elektro/tarifrunde-metall-und-elektro-2026) [employer-stated, by the union; the 15% vs 5% is the union's survey, not audited]. **The 2026 tables will change after the round; every euro figure here is valid only to 31 October 2026.**
- The 35-hour week is the tariff week in the West German M+E areas (the Südwestmetall documents state "35 Stunden"). The eastern collective areas have a different week; I did not read their documents.

## Who is actually covered (this is the part that breaks the Gemini lead)

1. **Tarifbindung is a membership concept.** Südwestmetall's own brochure says the agreement applies if the employer belongs to the association and the employee to the union, but in practice employers apply it by contract to everybody. Not every car, supplier or industrial firm is in it. Volkswagen AG uses its own in-house tariff (Haustarif) rather than the regional M+E agreement; the December 2024 deal was done under that (VW newsroom).
2. **Pay groups run EG 1 to EG 17.** Above the top group sit "AT" (außertariflich) contracts with individually negotiated pay. For graduates, a union booklet on engineers (Lower Saxony version, search summary) names EG 11-13 as the groups engineers are normally placed in. I could not read a source that says business graduates in controlling, purchasing or HR enter at a given group, so **the claim "graduates start at EG 12/13" is, for business functions, unverified** and probably varies by role and employer. Some large employers may offer entrants AT contracts instead; I found no published split.
3. **Same table does not mean same pay across schools.** The tariff fixes the minimum for a job grade. Grade placement (EG 11 vs 12 vs 13), performance pay (0-30%), and any AT contract are where individual outcomes differ, and employers often place by role requirements, not by school brand. Whether school prestige moves grade placement was not measured (see H-c).
4. **Region matters.** Baden-Württemberg figures above are for one of the three BW wage areas (the Tarifkarte covers BW; Bayern, NRW, Lower Saxony and the East have different tables). Do not quote BW numbers for Munich or Wolfsburg.
5. IG Metall states that engineers in tariff-covered firms earn about 16% more than those in uncovered firms (IG Metall, Ingenieurgehälter 2025 und 2026 page, https://www.igmetall.de/aktive/mitgliederarbeit/personengruppen/angestellte/ingenieurgehaelter-2025-und-2026) [employer-stated, by the union; definition of comparison group not read]. The sentence is promotional: use as a signal, not a premium to apply to your own offer.

## Training and dual-study rules (extends getting-in/employer-pipelines.md)

Südwestmetall's brochure states apprentices (Azubis) normally have a right to be taken on after passing, but **DHBW dual students have no such right**; apprentice pay in BW is EUR 1,303.50 in year 1 to EUR 1,563.00 in year 4 and 35 hours a week. The industry round of 2024 added EUR 140 a month to apprentice pay. The "dual partner takeover" in getting-in/employer-pipelines.md therefore rests on firm practice and works-council deals, not on a tariff right.

**Two things to take from this.** First, the published trainee programmes are small, selective and ask for 6 months of prior work experience (BMW) or 3 months abroad (Mercedes), so they are not an alternative to internships; they come after them. Second, the headline "master's graduates join through trainee programmes" is only part of the story: both BMW and Rheinmetall also offer direct entry, and the 2024 Stuttgarter Zeitung count of vacancy declines at Bosch (-64%), ZF (-55%) and Continental (-25%) for January-July 2024 versus the prior year (https://www.stuttgarter-zeitung.de/wirtschaft/krise-in-der-autobranche-bosch-conti-zf-autozulieferer-vergeben-deutlich-weniger-jobs-79002063.html) [secondary] concerned technical and IT postings, so I cannot say what happened to commercial direct-entry roles.

**Non-EU candidates.** Employers' trainee pages do not mention visa sponsorship. For EU citizens there is no permit issue for any German, French, Italian or Swedish employer. For defence employers the nationality issue is separate (section 4).

## Verdict on H-b ("defence is the fastest-growing industrial graduate employer in Europe 2025-26")

- **Supported for demand growth at German-and-Nordic land systems and defence electronics.** Rheinmetall's backlog rose 36% and it plans about 8,000 additional posts; Saab's bookings rose 74%; Thales recruited 8,800 and plans more than 9,000. IG Metall itself names aerospace and defence among the growth segments while auto and machinery struggle (IG Metall 2026 round page).
- **Not shown for graduate volume.** No defence employer publishes a graduate-cohort size. Rheinmetall's recruiting centre already faces 108,000 applications a year, and its hiring mix is engineers, software developers, cybersecurity experts, mechanics and welders (Deutsche Wirtschafts Nachrichten, March 2025), which is not mostly business graduates. Thales's mix is about 40% engineering and 25% industrial. Business roles (procurement, contracts, programme management, controlling) exist but their share is not published.
- **"Fastest-growing" cannot be ranked from this evidence.** I compared growth in orders and headcount only for the six employers above; AI/software firms and some non-defence segments (rail, shipbuilding, energy equipment) may be growing as fast. Verdict: **partly supported**.

## Security clearance and nationality

- In Germany, security vetting under the Security Vetting Act (SÜG) is handled for private firms mainly by the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Energy with the domestic intelligence service taking part. Companies report about 8 months on average before new staff can take up security-sensitive work; a Bundestag hearing in November 2025 heard 3-6 months for secrecy vetting and 5-8 months for sabotage-protection checks; over 61,000 employees were vetted in 2025 (MS Aktuell, 22 Sep 2026, https://ms-aktuell.de/?p=160448) [secondary, reports hearing and industry claims]. Practical effect: a new hire may be hired before clearance but not usable on classified work, so employers prefer candidates with a clean, traceable residence and work history in the country and in allied states; I did not find a published nationality rule for EU citizens in Germany, and the statute text was not read (see Claims to verify).
- Sweden: Saab's graduate pages say candidates must pass security vetting, with "citizenship obligations" for some roles (snippet only). A non-Swedish EU citizen is likely excluded from some roles, not all.
- France and Italy: I did not retrieve the official rules. The structure (national-security vetting by the state, citizenship or long residence usually required for classified work) is common knowledge but not sourced here, so I do not state it as fact.
- For non-EU readers the practical conclusion is conservative: ask the recruiter in writing whether the posting needs clearance and which nationalities qualify before spending months on interviews. For EU readers, only some roles are restricted, but the vetting delay affects start date.

## Pay in defence

No tariff or salary table was read for Rheinmetall, Airbus, Saab or Thales. Several German defence sites are covered by the M+E tariff, and the IG Metall tariff round names defence as a growth segment, but I did not verify coverage firm by firm. I therefore do not give a defence pay number.

**Ferrari correction to the Gemini lead.** Gemini said Ferrari earns "50%+ EBITDA". Ferrari's own 20-F gives 38.8% for 2025 (EUR 2,772m on EUR 7,146m), 38.3% for 2024 and 38.2% for 2023. **MUNER and the Motor Valley universities.** MUNER links the universities of Bologna, Modena and Reggio Emilia, Ferrara and Parma with Ferrari, Maserati, Lamborghini, Ducati, Dallara, Haas, Magneti Marelli and others. # Hypotheses tested

**H-a (assigned): "German auto and industrial graduate hiring fell sharply in 2024-26."** Verdict: **partly supported; the graduate-specific fall is not quantified.**
- Holds: sector employment fell 5.8% in a year to 691,500 (Destatis, Aug 2026) [data]; vacancies fell about two-thirds from the 2023 peak and 82% at suppliers vs 2019 (ifo, Sept 2025) [data]; engineers and IT professionals unemployed reached 58,392, the highest since 2011 (VDI/IW, Q4 2025) [data]; entry-level postings across Germany are 46% below 2019 and internship/Werkstudent postings 24% below (Indeed, July 2026) [data, secondary].
- Fails or unknown: no published cohort size for any OEM trainee programme; the Indeed series is not auto-specific; the ifo series covers all vacancies. There are still about 1.66 engineering vacancies per unemployed engineer (VDI/IW), so the market is soft, not closed.
- Conditions: the effect is strongest at suppliers (Bosch, ZF, Aumovio) and in engineering/IT; for premium OEMs and for defence the evidence is weaker.

**H-b (assigned): "Defence is the fastest-growing industrial graduate employer in Europe 2025-26."** Verdict: **partly supported** (see section 4). Strong order and headcount growth at Rheinmetall, Saab, Thales, Leonardo; no published graduate numbers; the mix is engineering-heavy; the ranking against non-defence growers was not tested.

**H-c (assigned): "The IG Metall tariff makes entry pay at OEMs the same regardless of school."** Verdict: **not supported as stated; the floor is common, the outcome is not.**
- The tariff fixes the pay per grade (EG 12 = EUR 5,129 basic in BW, April 2026) and the add-on percentages [employer-stated]. So two graduates in the same grade at the same firm and region get the same basic pay.
- It fails because: (1) coverage depends on the employer (VW has its own Haustarif; non-members of the employers' association can apply it or not); (2) the grade depends on the job, and above EG 17 contracts are individual (AT); (3) performance pay ranges 0-30%; (4) regions differ; (5) I found no source showing that school identity changes the grade, and none showing it does not. The honest claim is that school is rarely written into the pay system, while the job you get is shaped by internships and Werkstudent roles.

**H-d (own): "In a freeze, internal pipelines matter more than before, so a Werkstudent or thesis place at the target employer is worth more than a school upgrade."** Verdict: **partly supported (inference).** Supported by deal structure (employment security for insiders; Porsche non-renewal of about 2,000 fixed-term contracts) and by CGE's 42.3% of French grande école hires coming via the final internship or apprenticeship host (library fact, France). Fails for global programmes where the school list still gates the interview. No German causal data on Werkstudent-to-offer conversion was found.

**H-e (own): "Defence jobs are for engineers; business graduates are a small slice."** Verdict: **insufficient evidence, leaning supported.** Thales: about 40% of 2026 hires engineering and about 25% industrial roles; Rheinmetall: engineers, software, cyber, mechanics, welders (CEO via DWN) [secondary]. No firm publishes procurement, contracts or finance hire counts.

**H-f (own): "Clearance, not competition, is the main delay for new defence hires."** Verdict: **partly supported.** Reported average of about 8 months and 3-8 months from the Bundestag hearing in Nov 2025 (MS Aktuell, 22 Sep 2026) [secondary]. Fails as a general statement for non-classified roles; I could not verify nationality rules from the statute.

**H-g (own): "The 35-hour week plus add-ons makes German tariff jobs better on pay per hour than London finance entry roles."** Verdict: **insufficient evidence.** The tariff side is verified (EG 12 basic EUR 33.69/hour); the finance side (hours worked, bonus) was not researched here.

# Common mistakes and myths

1. **"Everyone at Mercedes, BMW or Bosch is on IG Metall EG 12/13."** Coverage and grade depend on employer, region, role and contract; VW is outside the regional agreement. [employer-stated]
2. **"The tariff table is current."** It expires on 31 Oct 2026; the next round could change it, and warning strikes are possible from 1 Nov. [employer-stated]
3. **"Quoting Baden-Württemberg numbers for all of Germany."** Tariff areas differ.
4. **"Trainee programmes are the way in."** BMW needs 6 months of prior work experience and 4 months abroad; Mercedes asks for 3 months abroad; intake sizes are undisclosed. [employer-stated]
5. **"Company = language rule."** BMW's German and Mexican pages differ. [employer-stated]
6. **"Defence = unlimited jobs."** Rheinmetall's recruiting centre got 108,000 applications in 2024 [secondary]; clearance delays slow start dates [secondary].
7. **"Ferrari has 50%+ EBITDA and is hiring wholesale."** Ferrari's EBITDA margin is 38.8% and the company has 5,718 employees [data].
8. **"MUNER = business school route to Ferrari."** MUNER is an engineering consortium [employer-stated, unverified figures].
9. **"The 40-60% cohort cut."** No source found (see Claims to verify).

# Decision rules

1. If you want a German OEM or supplier job and you have no Werkstudent, internship or thesis at it, prefer to secure one in the next 12 months over upgrading school, because restructuring protects insiders and external hiring is soft (Destatis; VW, Porsche deals).
2. If you target BMW's AcceleratiON and have no German at working level, do not apply to the German-side page; check the language rule on the exact page, because it requires fluent German and English.
3. If you quote a German tariff salary, state the pay group, region, date and whether add-ons and performance pay are included, because the figures differ by up to EUR 15,000 for the same grade (EG 12: about EUR 61,500 basic x 12 vs about EUR 81,000 with add-ons).
4. If your offer is "tariff-based", ask the grade (EG number), whether the contract is tariff or AT, and the performance-pay history, because those, not the school, set the euro outcome.
5. If you are a non-EU candidate and target defence, ask in writing about clearance and nationality conditions before the first interview, because roles are conditioned on vetting.
6. If you are EU and flexible on sector, add defence and aerospace (Thales, Saab, Leonardo, Safran, Rheinmetall) to the application list for 2027 starts, because hiring is rising (8,800 Thales hires in 2025) while auto is shrinking, but expect engineering-heavy postings.
7. If you want Italy's Motor Valley as a business graduate, apply for a Ferrari or Lamborghini internship directly rather than a MUNER-type engineering master's, because MUNER is an engineering consortium and Ferrari has about 2,950 white-collar staff in total.
8. If you rely on Stellantis Italy, treat it as a shrinking employer (voluntary exits in 2024-25) and keep an alternative.
9. If you compare auto-industry pay with MBB or IB, compare hours and net pay, not the headline gross, because the tariff week is 35 hours and German employee deductions at EUR 60k gross keep about 62% (money/salaries-and-roi.md).
10. If an advisor says "OEM cohorts are down X%", ask for the company's figure, because none was found.
11. If you read the 2026 collective-bargaining result (expected after 31 Oct), update every pay figure in this file, because the 2026 table is only valid to 31 Oct 2026.
