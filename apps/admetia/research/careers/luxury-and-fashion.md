# Careers in luxury and fashion

## Bottom line

1. **Luxury is not one labour market in 2025-26. Hiring follows brand heat.** Hermès added more than 1,300 people in 2025 (26,494 staff) and Richemont's average headcount rose to 39,601 in FY2026. LVMH's headcount fell 1.9% to 211,552 and its hires fell 11% to 67,937. Kering's headcount fell 6.8% to 43,731 (Hermès 2025 release; Richemont AR 2026; LVMH and Kering 2025 filings) [employer-stated / data]. A graduate who treats "luxury" as a single sector will misread the odds.
2. **The entry level looks hit hardest at Kering.** Permanent employees under 30 fell from 12,426 (2023) to 8,855 (2025), a 29% drop, against 7% for all permanent staff (Kering 2025 URD) [data]. The filing does not give the cause, and store closures in China and the US overlap, so read it as a strong signal rather than proof.
3. **Formal graduate programmes are tiny next to the headcount.** Kering says "more than 60" young people benefit from its graduate programmes. Richemont's early-careers page lists Swiss apprenticeships, a Gulf trainee scheme and a US summer internship, and no European business scheme. LVMH's SPRING pages on lvmh.com publish no eligibility or pay; the Inside LVMH page for SPRING Product Leaders (2025 cycle) states a master's degree and at most 3 years' experience (checked 2 Oct 2026) [employer-stated]. The realistic doors are internships and work-study contracts, retail roles and direct assistant-level hiring. In French grandes écoles, 42.3% of 2026 hires came through a final internship or apprenticeship (CGE 2026) [data].
4. **No verified luxury entry salary exists in this file, so the "glamour discount" cannot be tested.** No luxury group publishes graduate pay, and no applicant-per-vacancy ratio exists for luxury. The only hard numbers are legal internship floors (France EUR 4.50/h; Lombardy EUR 500 gross a month under 2018 rules). Verdict on H-a: insufficient evidence. Do not quote any percentage discount.
5. **Retail is the volume entrance but not a proven fast track.** 45% of LVMH's 2025 recruits were in Selective Retailing, and LVMH, Kering and Moncler each run a retail graduate or trainee path [employer-stated; Moncler snippet only]. No employer publishes promotion timelines or pay for the retail-to-manager route.
6. **There is no evidence that a luxury-specific master's beats a generalist one.** HEC's generalist MiM sends about 5% of its class to retail and luxury (secondary source) yet LVMH and Kering list it as a top recruiter. Bocconi's MAFED, often cited as a luxury feeder, has an average of 3 years' work experience and costs EUR 40,000, so it is not a pre-experience programme [data: school-reported].
7. **Employers say AI will augment the client advisor, not replace the junior analyst's tasks.** LVMH's AI lead says "you still have a client advisor, and that's who we want to empower" (Google Cloud interview, 10 Jun 2025) [employer-stated]. The tasks LVMH names for AI (product descriptions, translations, forecasting, document digestion) are classic junior head-office work. That link is my inference.
8. **The cycle is turning slowly.** Personal luxury goods were about EUR 358bn in 2025 vs EUR 369bn in 2023 (Bain-Altagamma, Nov 2025) and LVMH's H1 2026 organic growth was +2%. A 2026-27 graduate meets a market that is stable but not hiring broadly. Timing and house choice matter more than in 2022-23.

What this adds up to, in plain terms:

- **Luxury "graduate programmes" are small.** Kering's own filing counts "more than 60" people across its graduate programmes. Compare that with Kering's 8,855 permanent employees under 30 (section 2) and LVMH's 67,937 hires in 2025. Programmes such as SPRING are visible, prestigious and numerically marginal. Most juniors enter through internships, work-study contracts, retail jobs or direct hiring into assistant roles. [employer-stated, my comparison]
- **The pipeline is the stage or alternance, not the scheme.** Chanel, Hermès and Moncler all publish internship-led recruitment. Moncler's own figure is that 31% of 2024 internships ended in a contract (snippet only). Section 6 covers legal minimum pay and timing.
- **Languages are mostly unstated.** No luxury page I opened states a language requirement. Kering's China retail ad asks for English only. Do not infer that French or Italian is optional for Paris or Milan headquarters (see Hypothesis H-h, which tests this and finds the evidence thin).
- **Richemont's visible early-career offer is Swiss apprenticeships and Gulf trainees**, not a Geneva business scheme. That differs from the Gemini lead's picture of a Geneva graduate pipeline (see Corrections).

## 2. Sector health 2024-26 and what it means for entry-level hiring

**Demand.** Bain and Altagamma's November 2025 study puts personal luxury goods at about EUR 358bn in 2025, against EUR 364bn in 2024 and EUR 369bn in 2023. The number of luxury consumers fell from 400 million (2022) to about 340 million (2025), and new-customer acquisition fell 5% between 2024 and 2025. The same study projects 4-6% a year growth to 2035 (Bain press release, 20 Nov 2025, https://www.bain.com/about/media-center/press-releases/20252/global-luxury-stays-resilient-despite-economic-headwinds-and-shifting-consumer-trends-that-reshape-marketbain--company-and-altagamma/) [data: consultancy estimate]. 2026 looks like a partial recovery. LVMH reported H1 2026 revenue of EUR 38.6bn, +2% organic, with Q2 at +3%, Fashion & Leather Goods at -1% for the half and Watches & Jewelry at +9% (LVMH, 27 Jul 2026, https://www.lvmh.com/en/publications/accelerating-growth-in-the-second-quarter---solid-first-half-results) [data: company results]. Richemont's FY2026 (to 31 March 2026) operating profit was EUR 4,492m, 20.0% of sales, up 1% (23% at constant rates) (Richemont Annual Report 2026, fetched PDF) [data].

**Headcount, from the groups' own filings (fetched):**

| Group | Headcount, latest vs earlier | Hiring or youth signal | Source, tag |
|---|---|---|---|
| **LVMH** | 211,552 at 31 Dec 2025, from 215,637 at end 2024 (-1.9%) | New joiners 67,937 in 2025 vs 76,604 in 2024 (-11%); Selective Retailing (Sephora, DFS and similar) was 45% of 2025 recruits and 49% of 2024 recruits. 24% of staff are executives and managers; 53% administrative and sales. | LVMH 2025 and 2024 interactive annual reports, https://hosting.fluidbook.com/LVMH/2025interactiveannualreport/en/175-2025-Interactive-Annual-Report-LVMH.html and https://hosting.fluidbook.com/LVMH/2024interactiveannualreport/en/167-2024-Interactive-Annual-Report-LVMH.html [data] |

Leavers 9,171, turnover 21.1%. Gucci average FTE headcount 17,497 vs 20,032 (-13%); Gucci revenue EUR 5,992m, -19% comparable. | Kering 2025 URD, Section 3.1.8 and segment note (fetched PDF) [data] |

| **Burberry** | Plans to cut up to about 1,700 jobs over two years, "mostly head offices, primarily London" | FY2025 loss of GBP 3m (snippets) | theindustry.fashion, Fortune and others, May 2025 (unverified, snippet only; primary Burberry annual report not opened) |
| **Alexander McQueen (Kering)** | 55 jobs at London HQ (Oct 2025) and about a third of its Italian workforce proposed for reduction (Jan 2026) | | modaes.com, fashionunited.com snippets (unverified, snippet only) |

**Reading these numbers.**

1. **The slowdown hit the entry level before it hit total headcount.** At Kering, under-30 permanent staff fell about four times as fast as the whole permanent workforce over 2023-25. That is consistent with fewer juniors being hired, but the filing does not say why. It could also be ageing of the existing workforce and shop closures in China and the US (Mainland China staff fell from 5,740 to 4,410 and the US from 5,517 to 4,768 over the same two years, while Italy stayed at 13,643). Treat it as a strong signal, not proof. [data, my inference]
2. **Winners and losers are separate.** Hermès and Richemont added staff in the same period in which Kering and Burberry cut. A weak "luxury" headline hides a split: houses with strong brand heat hire, repositioning houses cut. [data]
3. **LVMH hires many people but few are graduates in head office.** 45% of 2025 recruits were in Selective Retailing and the group counts 6,283 stores. Fewer total hires in 2025 (-11%) probably means fewer store and seasonal hires. [data, inference]
4. **Cuts concentrate in head offices (Burberry, McQueen) and in the brand being repositioned.** That is where marketing, merchandising and strategy juniors sit. Retail and craft hiring (Hermès, Moncler, Prada's academy) was the more stable side. [snippet-level evidence; flagged]

## 3. Pay at entry and the "glamour discount"

**What exists.** No luxury group in the scope publishes graduate salaries on any page I opened. For contrast, careers/marketing.md records P&G Germany at EUR 67,500 plus bonus and Unilever UK at GBP 35,000 on the employers' own pages. The luxury side offers only these items:

| Evidence | Figure | Quality | Source, tag |
|---|---|---|---|
| French grandes écoles, all sectors, first job within six months | Average gross annual salary excluding bonus EUR 39,679; permanent contract (CDI) share above 80% (83.9% for management graduates); net employment at six months 76% | Official survey, all sectors, no luxury split | CGE 2026 survey (11 Jun 2026) via https://www.mondedesgrandesecoles.fr/emploi-des-jeunes-diplomes-en-2026-les-chiffres-a-retenir/ [data] |
| Junior "chef de produit mode/luxe" (0-2 years), France | EUR 32,000-38,000 gross a year; Ile-de-France retail/merchandising about 15% above provincial rates | Online salary simulator with no stated method | travail-industrie.com (snippet only) [anecdotal] |
| ESCP MSc Marketing & Creativity (Fashion & Luxury track available), class of 2022 | Average starting salary EUR 55,900 | School-reported, 2022, mixed tracks | ESCP via masterstudies (snippet only) [data: school-reported, possibly stale] |
| Hermès employees in France, 2025 | EUR 120 gross monthly rise for all, EUR 3,000 bonus in March 2026, EUR 243m incentive and profit-sharing in France | Employer figures, not entry-specific | Hermès 2025 results release [employer-stated] |
| Internship floors | France: EUR 4.50 an hour once a stage passes two months (see careers/marketing.md). Lombardy: EUR 500 gross a month for extracurricular tirocini, reducible to EUR 400 with meals or EUR 350 for under 4 hours a day. Lazio EUR 800 and a national floor of EUR 300 are snippet-only. | Official or regional rules; Lombardy rules were published in January 2018, so check for later changes | https://www.assolombarda.it/servizi/formazione/informazioni/pubblicati-i-nuovi-indirizzi-regionali-sui-tirocini-della-regione-lombardia-1 [data] |

**Testing the "glamour discount".** The idea needs two numbers per sector: entry pay and applicants per vacancy. For luxury I have neither. The UK's ISE gives 290 applications per vacancy for "retail, FMCG and tourism" (ISE 2025, in the brief), but that category does not isolate luxury and "applications" are not "applicants". The sector evidence points both ways:

- For a discount: luxury houses employ large shares of retail and administrative staff (LVMH: 53% administrative and sales), and the graduate schemes that do exist are small, so a few prestigious roles absorb the applications. [data, inference]
- Against: Hermès pays across-the-board rises and profit-sharing, and the CGE all-sector average (EUR 39,679) overlaps the only luxury range found (EUR 32,000-38,000, anecdotal). A gap of a few thousand euros at junior level would not show up as a "20% to 30%" discount in any data I can open.

**Verdict: insufficient evidence.** What would settle it: sector-split salary tables from HEC, ESSEC or ESCP employment reports (sector by median salary), or published luxury junior pay bands from a recruiter's salary guide (the April 2026 Luxe Talent study mentioned by CB News was behind a paywall). Until then the site should say "luxury pay at entry is not published; compare offers on total package, location and conversion odds."

**Italy and Switzerland.** I did not verify Italian contract minima (CCNL Terziario/Moda) or any luxury-specific Swiss salary. For Geneva, the general Swiss figure in the library is a university economics master's median of CHF 87,100 one year out (BFS, see money/salaries-and-roi.md). Richemont employs about 9,772 people in Switzerland on average (FY2026) [data], but no pay level is published.

## 5. Retail-first career path

**What is documented.**
- 45% of LVMH's 67,937 recruits in 2025 joined Selective Retailing (Sephora, DFS and similar), and LVMH counts 6,283 stores (LVMH 2025 report) [data].
- LVMH SPRING lists "Retail Trainee Manager" among its mission types, and LVMH launched a China Retail Management Trainee programme in 2022 (LVMH graduate pages) [employer-stated]. Kering's Keys retail programme in China (21 months) and its "cross-brand retail management trainee" appear in job-board snippets, and Moncler runs a one-year Global Retail Graduate Program (snippet only).
- A Louis Vuitton newsroom piece on its retail teams and a Hermès boutique-management ad suggest long timelines (a Champs-Elysees store director who spent about six years moving through operations and product roles; a Hermès boutique-management role asking at least six years' experience), but I could only see these through search snippets (unverified, snippet only).

**What is not documented.** No employer publishes a sales-advisor-to-manager timeline, retail trainee pay, or the share of head-office marketers who started in stores. The Gemini lead's "EUR 120,000-180,000+ within 6-8 years" for regional retail directors is unsourced and I could not confirm it.

**Interpretation.** Retail is where the jobs are, so it is the realistic entrance for anyone without a Paris or Milan school network. But a retail trainee path rewards store performance and mobility, and the evidence that it leads to head-office strategy or marketing is anecdotal. It also depends on store growth, and LVMH's 2025 hiring dropped 11% while retail was nearly half of it. Choose it if you want the retail career itself (clienteling, store P&L, regional leadership), not as a back door. See Hypothesis H-b.

## 7. AI and e-commerce effects on junior work

- **Employer stance.** LVMH's AI lead described a central data platform used for sales and supply-chain forecasting, product descriptions for websites, translations, document digestion and "hyper-personalisation", with "more than 40,000 users per month" doing "more than 1.5 million queries". On clienteling: "Crucially, you still have a client advisor, and that's who we want to empower with data and AI." (Google Cloud, interview published 10 Jun 2025) [employer-stated; the page is a vendor case study]. Kering's recruitment now runs partly on AI matching (section 6).
- **What it implies for juniors (inference).** The listed tasks (product copy, translations, summaries, forecasting support) overlap with typical assistant merchandiser, e-commerce and analyst duties. Stanford's "Canaries" paper finds 22-25-year-olds in AI-exposed US occupations about 19% below trend, mainly through reduced hiring (rev. Aug 2026, in the brief) [data, US-wide, not luxury]. No luxury-specific evidence of AI replacing junior roles exists in this file.
- **E-commerce.** I found no luxury group publishing e-commerce headcount or digital graduate numbers. LVMH's Selective Retailing and Kering's youth headcount (section 2) are the closest signals. Treat "digital/CRM is the growth entrance" as a hypothesis, not a finding.

## Hypotheses tested

**H-a (assigned). "Luxury entry pay is lower per applicant than FMCG or consulting."**
- Verdict: **insufficient evidence.**
- Holds when: the comparison is Paris or Milan head-office marketing at a house with heavy applicant demand. Fails when: hard data are needed; none exist for luxury entry pay or applicant counts.
- Evidence: no luxury employer publishes graduate pay (checked LVMH, Kering, Richemont pages); P&G Germany at EUR 67,500 and Unilever UK at GBP 35,000 are published [employer-stated, careers/marketing.md]. The only luxury range, EUR 32,000-38,000, comes from a simulator [anecdotal].

**H-b (assigned). "Retail is the real entry door in luxury."**
- Verdict: **partly supported.**
- Holds when: you count volume. 45% of LVMH's 2025 recruits were in Selective Retailing, and three groups run retail trainee tracks [data; employer-stated].
- Fails when: you mean a route into head-office strategy or marketing. No promotion timelines or conversion rates are published, and anecdotal postings suggest six years or more to boutique management.

**H-c (assigned). "A luxury-specific master's beats a generalist MiM for luxury careers."**
- Verdict: **insufficient evidence.**
- Holds when: the programme sits in Paris or Milan with named house relationships (IFM's board includes Chanel, Hermès and Saint Laurent executives and its master's report 81.8% permanent contracts at six months [employer-stated: school]). Fails when: no school splits luxury offers by programme, HEC's generalist MiM still feeds LVMH and Kering (5% retail and luxury, secondary), and MAFED is a mid-career programme (average 3 years' experience).
- See decisions/programme-choice.md and careers/marketing.md (ESSEC 41% luxury).

**H-d (own). "Formal luxury graduate programmes are a marginal entry route."**
- Verdict: **supported.**
- Evidence: Kering ">60" in graduate programmes vs 8,855 permanent staff under 30; Richemont's page lists no European business scheme; LVMH recruits 67,937 a year [employer-stated; data].
- Fails when: SPRING-type programmes matter for leadership selection later, not volume, which this file cannot test.

**H-e (own). "The luxury slowdown hit juniors before it hit total headcount."**
- Verdict: **partly supported (one group).**
- Evidence: Kering under-30 permanent staff -29% in two years vs -7% overall [data]. Hermès (+1,300) and Richemont (+1.8%) show the opposite. Confounded by store closures and age structure.

**H-f (own). "Where you apply matters more than the sector label: brand heat predicts hiring."**
- Verdict: **supported on headcount.**
- Evidence: 2025 headcount change: Hermès +5.2% (25,185 to 26,494), Richemont average +1.8% (FY26), LVMH -1.9%, Kering -6.8% [data]. Check each group's latest results before applying.

**H-g (own). "Referrals convert far better than cold applications in luxury."**
- Verdict: **partly supported.**
- Evidence: Kering 19% recruitment rate on about 1,100 referred applicants [employer-stated]. No cold-application conversion rate is published, and referred candidates are pre-screened.

**H-h (own). "English alone is enough for luxury head offices."**
- Verdict: **insufficient evidence.**
- Evidence: no luxury page I opened states a language rule; Kering's China retail ad asks only for English [snippet]. Practitioner consensus (Gemini lead, unverified) says French or Italian is needed for Paris and Milan roles. Treat local language as likely but unproven; see careers/marketing.md for the FMCG parallel where German was explicit.

## Common mistakes and myths

1. **"Luxury is booming, so hiring is easy."** Bain shows personal luxury goods at EUR 358bn in 2025 vs EUR 369bn in 2023, with about 60 million fewer luxury consumers than in 2022 [data]. LVMH's hires fell 11% in 2025.
2. **"A luxury master's is the way in."** Published outcomes for luxury programmes are school-stated and rarely split by employer or salary. IFM's 81.8% and MAFED's 90% are real school claims but not comparisons with generalist programmes [employer-stated: school].
3. **"Graduate programmes are how people join."** Kering's graduate programmes cover "more than 60" people; most juniors enter through stage, alternance, retail or direct assistant roles [employer-stated].
4. **"Retail is a guaranteed fast track."** No published timeline or pay supports it (section 5).
5. **"All luxury groups are cutting."** Hermès and Richemont grew headcount in the same period [data].
6. **"MAFED is a pre-experience master's."** Its published class profile is average age 26 and 3 years' experience, with a EUR 40,000 fee [data: school-reported].
7. **"Internships are unpaid or optional."** Floors exist (France EUR 4.50/h; Lombardy EUR 500 a month under the 2018 rules) and internships are the main route into jobs (42.3% of French grande école hires) [data].

## Decision rules

1. **If** you target a luxury group, **check its latest headcount and results first, and prefer** houses with growing staff (Hermès, Richemont) **because** hiring diverged sharply in 2025 (Kering -6.8%, LVMH hires -11%).
2. **If** you are a final-year master's student targeting luxury, **secure a stage or alternance in the final year, because** internships and apprenticeships fed 42.3% of French grandes écoles first jobs and Moncler reports 31% intern conversion (snippet).
3. **If** you rely on graduate programmes (SPRING, Kering's, Moncler's), **also apply for assistant-level roles and internships in parallel, because** programme intakes are small (Kering ">60" across programmes).
4. **If** you have no Paris or Milan network, **consider** a retail trainee route **only if** you want the retail career itself, **because** no promotion path from stores to head office is documented.
5. **If** you are comparing a luxury-specific master's with a generalist MiM at a similar school, **prefer** the one with the better campus location and employer network, not the title, **because** no evidence shows luxury titles lift offers and HEC's generalist class still feeds LVMH and Kering.
6. **If** you consider SDA Bocconi's MAFED, **treat it as a mid-career programme** (EUR 40,000, average 3 years' experience), **because** pre-experience students would be an outlier in the class.
7. **If** you are going to Milan on a tirocinio, **budget on the regional floor** (EUR 500 gross a month in Lombardy under 2018 rules; check the current rules) **because** many house internships pay close to it.
8. **If** you can get an employee referral at Kering or another house with a referral scheme, **use it after a real conversation, because** Kering reports 19% recruitment of referred candidates.
9. **If** you target e-commerce or analytics roles, **build forecasting and data skills rather than copywriting or translation, because** LVMH names those tasks as AI use cases.
10. **If** you are non-EU, **check work rights before chasing a house, because** Paris, Milan and Geneva roles depend on permits (see places/visas-and-work-rights.md); Switzerland's third-country quota for 2026 is 8,500.
11. **If** you are offered luxury pay below an FMCG offer, **compare total package, location and mobility, not a "glamour discount" figure, because** no sector pay gap has been measured.
