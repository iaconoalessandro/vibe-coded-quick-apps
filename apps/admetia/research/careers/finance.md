# Careers in finance for pre-experience master's graduates

## Bottom line

1. **London is still the main door, and the door is an internship.** Front-office analyst jobs in European investment banking are filled mostly through summer and off-cycle internships, not open graduate applications. A one-year master's that starts in September leaves almost no time to land a summer internship for the following year, so the realistic routes are (a) recruiting during the master's for an off-cycle or summer role after it, or (b) arriving with a prior internship. Timing is covered in getting-in/recruiting-calendar.md. [practitioner consensus, see sources in section 1]
2. **The odds at the top are tiny.** Goldman Sachs took about 2,500 interns in 2026 with an acceptance rate below 1% for the third year running; Goldman did not disclose 2026 applicant numbers; the 2025 intake drew more than 360,000 applications, about 0.7% accepted (Fortune, 16 Jun 2025, https://fortune.com/2025/06/16/goldman-sachs-internship-summer-analysts-wall-street-tpg-olaplex-red-lobster; 2026 figures: Fortune, 8 Jun 2026) [employer-stated, via press]. Plan for a portfolio of applications across bank tiers, not a single target.
3. **London analyst pay recovered in 2025 but bonuses are the swing factor.** Global IB analyst pay rose 14% to about $114k in the 2025 cycle, with base down 4% and bonuses up 44% (eFinancialCareers survey, 2026) [practitioner consensus]. London first-year bonuses averaged about £29k in 2023, down 13% on 2022 (Dartmouth Partners / Prospect Rock via eFinancialCareers, 2024) [practitioner consensus]. Since the UK scrapped the bonus cap on 31 October 2023 (FCA PS23/15), UK bonuses can again swing widely year to year [data].
4. **Hours rules exist but do not bind during live deals.** JPMorgan introduced an 80-hour weekly cap in September 2024 with a Friday 6pm–Saturday noon "pencils down" and one full weekend off per quarter, but live deals are exempt (Fortune, 2024) [employer-stated, via press]. Expect 70–90-hour weeks in M&A regardless of policy.
5. **H3 ("banks now hire STEM over finance graduates") is only partly supported.** It holds for the *whole bank* (Goldman's intern class was ~45% STEM in 2021, up from 37% in 2017; about a quarter of Goldman staff are engineers) and for quant and electronic trading. It fails for the *IB division front office in London*, where the best available data (2016–17) show economics and finance degrees dominating analyst and intern classes. A finance master's on top of a quantitative bachelor's is still the modal profile for European M&A. [employer-stated + practitioner consensus; data old]
6. **A specialised pre-experience finance master's works as a placement engine when it is in London and has a strong career office.** LBS MFA 2025: 92% received an offer within three months; 66% of jobs were in the UK and 15% in the rest of Europe (LBS, 2025) [data]. The school's own profiles include an Evercore IB analyst, an HSBC off-cycle analyst and a Deutsche Bank corporate banking analyst.
7. **CFA helps in asset management and equity research, much less in M&A.** CFA Level I pass rates were 39–45% across the four windows from November 2025 to August 2026, and all three levels cost USD 3,520–4,570 plus about 300 study hours per level (CFA Institute, 2026) [data]. For an IB-targeted master's student, Level I competes directly with recruiting preparation for the same hours.
8. **AI will shrink, not abolish, junior classes.** Goldman told Fortune in 2026 that AI will "to some degree reduce the number of people that we start with", but "not dramatically" [employer-stated, via press]. Larger cut figures in circulation (e.g. "two-thirds") could not be verified from a primary source this session (see Claims to verify).
9. **Milan's landscape changed in 2025.** Mediobanca became part of the Monte dei Paschi group on 15 September 2025. In the six months to December 2025 its CIB revenues fell 20%, advisory fees nearly halved, and it launched retention measures for CIB and wealth bankers (Mediobanca results release, 2026) [employer-stated]. Italian readers targeting Mediobanca CIB should treat its junior hiring as uncertain in 2026–27 and apply in parallel to other Milan franchises.

## 1. Entry routes: how pre-experience master's graduates actually get in

### 1.1 The internship funnel
- In both London and New York the analyst class is filled mostly by converting summer interns. Goldman's 2026 intern class (~2,500) was about the same size as its permanent entry-level intake (~2,500 across all divisions) (Fortune, 8 June 2026, https://fortune.com/2026/06/08/exclusive-goldman-sachs-intern-acceptance-rate-falls-below-1-for-third-straight-year/) [employer-stated, via press]. That near one-to-one ratio is why missing the internship cycle is so costly.
- JPMorgan hired 5,000 summer interns globally in 2023, up from 4,604 in 2022, the highest in its recent history (eFinancialCareers, June 2023, https://www.efinancialcareers.com/news/2023/06/banking-summer-interns) [employer-stated, via press]. Note these totals include technology, operations and other divisions, not only IB.
- No bank, Big 4 or school page publishes a summer-to-full-time conversion rate (Barclays, Citi and JPMorgan early-careers pages and the Goldman Fortune story re-read 2 Oct 2026; Citi says only that programmes offer "the opportunity to be offered a full-time analyst role"). Figures of 70–90% circulate on coaching sites and forums without a named dataset [anecdotal]. The only press-relayed numbers are cyclical: in August 2023 eFinancialCareers relayed forum posts saying no more than 30% of Rothschild interns and 50% in some Morgan Stanley groups (US classes) got full-time offers, and wrote that "in good years, upwards of 75% of interns can receive return offers" (eFinancialCareers, 14 Aug 2023, https://www.efinancialcareers.com/news/2023/08/internship-conversions-banking-2023) [anecdotal, via specialist press]. The nearest hard data are for the step before: Trackr's self-selected surveys put spring-week-to-summer direct return offers at 31% (2025) and 20% (2026), with 38% and 44% fast-tracked (Trackr, 2025 and 2026 spring reports, https://the-trackr.com/blog/spring-weeks-season-insights-2026) [data, self-selected]. An all-sector UK intern-to-graduate-offer rate of 50% (2025; 54% in 2024) attributed to ISE appears only in a secondary blog and was not read on an ISE page (unverified). Treat 70–90% as a good-year anecdote, not a measured rate (verification/round-3d.md).

### 1.2 Off-cycle internships (Europe-specific)
- Off-cycle internships (3–6 months, starting outside the June–August window) are a European, and particularly London and Paris, feature. They are the main route for master's graduates whose programme calendar clashes with summer recruiting. LBS's own MFA 2025 profiles include a graduate who went to "Investment Banking, Off-Cycle Analyst, HSBC" (LBS MFA career page, 2025, https://www.london.edu/masters-degrees/masters-in-financial-analysis/career-impact) [data, school-published].
- In France, the *stage de césure* (gap-year internships) is the norm in grande école master's programmes, and it creates a large supply of 6-month interns for Paris banks and boutiques. [practitioner consensus; country detail in places/countries-and-cities.md and getting-in/recruiting-calendar.md]
### 1.2 Off-cycle internships (Europe's primary pipeline)
- Off-cycle internships (3–6 months, starting outside the June–August window) are the **lifeblood of European investment banking recruiting**, particularly in Paris, Frankfurt, Milan, and London. They are the primary route for master's graduates whose academic calendar clashes with UK summer internships:
  - **Paris:** Structured around the grande école *stage de césure* (gap year between M1 and M2) and *stage de fin d'études* (PFE, 6-month final internship). CGE 2026 data shows that 42.3% of French business school hires convert directly from their final stage or apprenticeship host firm. Paris M&A teams at Rothschild, Lazard, BNP Paribas, and US bulge brackets recruit year-round for 6-month cycles starting in January and July.
  - **Frankfurt:** 3–6 month *Praktikum*. German minimum wage legislation (MiLoG §22) makes mandatory internships during enrollment far more advantageous for employers than post-graduation voluntary stages. Top banks (Deutsche Bank, Goldman, Morgan Stanley, Commerzbank) recruit continuously.
  - **Milan:** 6-month *stage curriculare* (enrolled) or *extracurriculare* (recent graduates) at Mediobanca, Intesa Sanpaolo (IMI CIB), UniCredit, Rothschild, Lazard, Vitale, and US coverage teams. This is the dominant gateway into Italian corporate finance.
  - **Zurich:** 6-month *Praktikum* at UBS, Vontobel, private banks, and commodities trading houses.
- In London, off-cycle internships at elite boutiques or middle-market banks (e.g. HSBC, Nomura, Jefferies, Houlihan Lokey) that convert to full-time analyst offers provide the most realistic route for one-year UK MSc students who missed the early summer window.

### 1.3 Analyst vs. Associate: The Master's vs. MBA Divide
- **Analyst (Analyst 1):** The entry grade for **both undergraduates and pre-experience Master's graduates (MSc Finance, MiM)**. London base salary is ~£70k (with total compensation £85k–£130k+); US base is $110k–$120k; Frankfurt and Paris base is €65k–€75k; Milan base is €40k–€45k. Holding an MSc Finance from LSE, HEC, or Bocconi does **not** allow an applicant to skip the analyst ranks. The master's serves as an access ticket, brand signal, and second recruiting cycle, but the title and pay band are identical to undergraduate hires.
- **Associate (Associate 1):** Strictly reserved for **Post-MBA graduates from elite 2-year programmes** (e.g. LBS, INSEAD, Wharton, Columbia, Harvard) or direct internal promotions after 2.5–3 years as an analyst (Analyst-to-Associate / A2A promotion). US Associate 1 base is $175,000–$200,000 (total comp $275k–$350k+); London base is £110k–£125k. Pre-experience master's graduates are screened out of Associate candidate pools.

### 1.4 Bank tiers and what a new analyst actually does

| Tier | Examples (London/Europe) | Analyst pay vs BB | Deal exposure | Typical entry for a master's graduate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bulge bracket (BB) | Goldman, JPMorgan, Morgan Stanley, BofA Securities, Citi, Barclays, Deutsche Bank, UBS | Benchmark (£70k base) | Large-cap; integrated financing, ECM/DCM balance sheet lending | Summer internship converts to offer; some off-cycle |
| Elite boutique (EB) | Centerview Partners, Evercore, Lazard, Rothschild & Co, Moelis, Perella Weinberg, PJT Partners | Equal base, higher bonus (+15–30% at top bucket) | Pure M&A and restructuring advisory; lean deal teams, direct MD exposure | Summer or off-cycle; highly technical modelling screens |
| Middle market / regional | Houlihan Lokey, Jefferies, Stifel, Lincoln, William Blair, Alantra | Slightly lower base/bonus | Mid-cap M&A, debt advisory, restructuring | Off-cycle often converts; wider door |
| National champions | Mediobanca (Milan), BNP Paribas / SocGen / Natixis (Paris), Santander / BBVA (Madrid), Deutsche / Commerzbank (Frankfurt), UBS (Zurich) | Lower base locally; lower cost of living | Domestic champion deals, strong mid-cap flow | Local-language graduate programmes, 6-month off-cycle stages |
| Big 4 corporate finance / TS | Deloitte, EY-Parthenon, KPMG, PwC (Deals) | Lower (£38k–£48k London base) | Mid-market M&A, financial and commercial due diligence | Graduate scheme; lateral to IB analyst after 1–2 years |

**Elite Boutiques vs. Bulge Brackets:**
Elite boutiques operate purely as advisors without balance-sheet lending or commercial banking divisions. As a result:
1. **Leaner deal teams:** An M&A transaction at Centerview or Evercore may run with one Managing Director, one Associate, and one Analyst, requiring the analyst to build complex operating models and merger consequences from day one.
2. **Superior compensation ceiling:** In active M&A cycles, EB bonuses routinely outpace Bulge Brackets (Centerview and Evercore first-year total comp can reach £120k–£130k in London and $170k–$200k in New York).
3. **PE Placement:** Megafund and upper-middle-market PE headhunters target EB analysts heavily due to their intensive modelling exposure.

**Coverage vs product.** Coverage (industry) groups (TMT, Healthcare, Industrials, Consumer, FIG, Energy) own client relationships. Product groups (M&A, ECM, DCM, Leveraged Finance) execute specific transaction types. At European BBs, graduates are often placed in an "IBD pool" and allocated post-training. DCM and LevFin provide direct pipelines into Private Credit; M&A provides the strongest pipeline into Private Equity.

### 1.5 Other front-office and adjacent doors

- **Quantitative Trading, Prop Trading and Market Making:** The highest-paying destination in early-career finance (see deep dive in Section 5.2). Top firms include **Jane Street, Citadel & Citadel Securities, Optiver, Jump Trading, Flow Traders, IMC Trading, Hudson River Trading (HRT), Susquehanna (SIG), DRW, Two Sigma, D.E. Shaw, Point72, and Millennium**. Entry compensation ranges from $200k to $400k+ in the US, £150k to £300k+ in London, and €120k to €250k+ in Amsterdam.
- **Sales & Trading (S&T):** Summer internship route. High-touch voice trading has shrunk while quantitative execution, electronic market making, algorithmic execution, and complex derivatives structuring have grown. Heavily STEM and quant-finance focused.
- **Equity Research:** Smaller intake classes. Focuses on sector modeling, earnings forecasts, and publishing institutional research notes. CFA Level I/II is a powerful signal.
- **Asset Management:** Graduate programmes at major institutional houses (BlackRock, PIMCO, Fidelity, Amundi, Schroders, DWS). Slower-paced than IB, 50–55 hour weeks, strong long-term career stability; CFA widely expected.
- **Private Equity (Direct Entry):** Vanishingly rare directly from a pre-experience Master's (see Section 4 for the PE reality check).
- **Private Credit:** The fastest-growing asset class in alternative investments (Ares, Blue Owl, Blackstone Credit, Oaktree, HPS). Hires heavily from DCM, Leveraged Finance, and Restructuring banking teams after 1–3 years.
- **Corporate Banking & Risk:** Larger graduate intakes, predictable 45–55 hour weeks, solid pay; realistic foundation for non-target candidates.
- **Central Banks:** ECB Graduate Programme (19 places/yr, EU nationals only); national central banks (Banca d'Italia, Banque de France, Bundesbank) require rigorous civil-service examinations (*concorsi*).

Reading the table: a realistic 2025–26 London first-year analyst package at a BB or EB is roughly £70k base plus a bonus that has ranged from about £15k to £65k across 2021–25, depending on bank, bucket and year. Anchors: JPMorgan's London rate rose in January 2022 to £70k for first-year analysts, £80k second-year and £85k third-year, matching Goldman, Perella and PJT (eFinancialCareers, 25 Jan 2022, https://www.efinancialcareers.com/news/2022/01/jpmorgan-analyst-salaries-london) [practitioner consensus]. Goldman's London first-years averaged £130k total in 2021 (£65k salary + £65k bonus) but £106k in 2023, with the average bonus down about 60% to £41k, against a £29k all-bank average (Dartmouth Partners via eFinancialCareers, 16 Oct 2023, https://www.efinancialcareers.com/news/2023/10/goldman-sachs-analyst-pay) [practitioner consensus]. Whether base salaries moved above £70k in 2024–26 could not be confirmed: £70k (Jan 2022) is the last bank-level anchor with a readable date, and the only 2026 figure from a named outlet, PrepLounge's undisclosed-method £48–69k entry-level base (updated 27 Aug 2026), sits below it, so 'about £70k' is not refuted and 'above £70k' is not supported (Claims to verify).

Non-obvious: the bonus is where elite boutiques beat bulge brackets, and it is also where the variance lives. Top-bucket analysts' bonuses were ~30% higher than mid-bucket peers in the 2025 WSO data (eFinancialCareers, 2025) [anecdotal]. Morgan Stanley London analysts lagged after "a very poor bonus round" in 2023 (Pretraga via eFinancialCareers, 2024) [practitioner consensus]. So "EB pays more" is true on average, not bank by bank or year by year (see H5).

### 2.2 Continental Europe
- **No bank publishes Continental analyst pay**, and no specialist-press survey for Paris, Frankfurt or Milan analysts could be opened this session. The best fetched proxy is school data: HEC Paris MIF reports a €103k average starting salary (2025 graduation survey), with 81.4% of graduates going into financial services and 86.6% into finance/accounting functions; its jobs are split between Paris and London, so this is not a pure Paris figure (HEC, 2026, https://www.hec.edu/en/master-s-programs/master-international-finance and /careers) [data, school-reported].
- Aggregator figures seen only as snippets (Paris analyst ~€60k base / ~€85k total; German IB analyst median ~€100k total; Italian junior IB analyst median ~€41k) are in Claims to verify and should not be used in a calculator.
- Senior pay is a weak but real signal of local economics: Rothschild's UK banking material risk takers averaged €486k in 2025, against about €1.5m at bigger investment banks, and average UK Rothschild pay was ~£287k versus $963k at Goldman Sachs International (eFinancialCareers from UK filings, 13 Jul 2026, https://www.efinancialcareers.com/news/rothschild-pay) [data via press]. Advisory-only franchises that are strong in Paris and Milan pay their seniors less than US banks do, which generally shows up in junior bonuses too. [inference]
- Italian readers should see places/italy-playbook.md and money/salaries-and-roi.md for the net-pay and tax comparison.

### 2.3 The UK bonus cap
The FCA and PRA removed the EU-derived limit on the ratio of variable to fixed pay (100%, or 200% with shareholder approval) with policy statement PS23/15 / PS9/23, published 24 October 2023 and effective 31 October 2023, applying to the performance year then under way (FCA, 2023, https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/policy-statements/ps23-15-remuneration-ratio-between-fixed-and-variable-components-total-remuneration) [data]. The cap still applies in the EU. Practical consequence for juniors: the cap hardly bound analysts, whose bonuses sit below 100% of salary. Its removal matters more for senior staff. However, it lets UK banks shift pay back from fixed to variable over time, so future London base rises may slow while bonus volatility increases. [inference from the policy; tag: practitioner consensus]

## 3. Hours and working conditions

- **JPMorgan (Sept 2024):** 80-hour weekly cap for junior bankers "in most cases", the bank's first. Existing "pencils down" from 6pm Friday to noon Saturday, plus one guaranteed full weekend off every three months. Live deals are exempt (Fortune, 12 Sep 2024, https://fortune.com/2024/09/12/jpmorgan-cap-junior-bankers-hours/) [employer-stated, via press].
- **Bank of America (Sept 2024):** already had an 80-hour guideline that juniors said was routinely broken. It introduced daily hours logging for US junior bankers, naming deals, supervising seniors and a 1–4 capacity score (Fortune, 12 Sep 2024, https://fortune.com/2024/09/12/bank-of-america-work-hours-death/) [employer-stated, via press]. The trigger was the May 2024 death of associate Leo Lukenas III, reported to have been working more than 100 hours a week on a deal.
- **Goldman Sachs:** a "Saturday rule" (protecting Friday night to Sunday morning except for live deals) was reaffirmed in 2021 after a junior-analyst survey reported ~100-hour weeks. The primary page could not be fetched this session (Claims to verify).
- **What to expect in practice:** even the eFinancialCareers 2025 bonus piece notes that JPMorgan's cap excludes live-deal work, when analysts typically work longest (eFinancialCareers, 21 Aug 2025, https://www.efinancialcareers.com/news/banking-analyst-bonuses) [practitioner consensus]. Caps are mainly US policies. European offices of US banks generally follow them, and EU working-time rules exist on paper, but the opt-out culture in London means 70–90-hour weeks in M&A remain normal. [practitioner consensus]
- **Trade-off by role:** DCM, corporate banking, risk, AM and research generally run 50–65 hours; M&A and lev fin run the longest. [practitioner consensus]

## 4. Exit options

### The Private Equity Reality Check: Pre-Experience vs. Post-MBA
Private equity remains the most sought-after destination for finance graduates, but candidate expectations are frequently detached from institutional hiring mechanics:

1. **Direct Pre-Experience PE is an Outlier:**
   - Mega-funds (Blackstone, KKR, Carlyle, Apollo, EQT, CVC, Advent, Bain Capital) and upper-middle-market (UMM) buyout funds hire almost zero direct graduates in Europe.
   - The institutionalized path is strictly: **2 years in Investment Banking (M&A, LevFin, or Restructuring) or MBB strategy consulting → Headhunter outreach (Kea, Dartmouth, Blackwood, Walker Hamill) → Associate role at a PE fund**.
   - A minority of firms run tiny analyst classes (e.g. Blackstone, KKR, Point72 Private Investments), but seats are microscopic (1–3 per year across Europe) and target candidates who completed previous summer internships at the firm.

2. **The Post-MBA PE Myth:**
   - **Myth:** "I can work in corporate marketing, tech, or accounting, attend an M7 or European top MBA (INSEAD, LBS), and switch into private equity buyout investment teams."
   - **Reality:** PE investment committees are risk-averse allocators. Post-MBA PE Associate and Vice President hiring is almost exclusively restricted to candidates who possess **pre-MBA private equity or top-tier investment banking M&A experience**. Without prior transaction execution and financial modeling credentials, MBA candidates are routinely screened out of investment team recruiting.

3. **Realistic PE Pathways for Career Changers:**
   - **PE Value Creation & Portfolio Operations:** Operating teams within PE firms (e.g. KKR Capstone, Blackstone Portfolio Operations, EQT Operating Advisors) help acquired portfolio companies execute 100-day plans, digital transformation, EBITDA expansion, and pricing optimization. This track actively recruits former strategy consultants (MBB/Tier 2) and corporate operators post-MBA.
   - **Search Funds & ETA (Entrepreneurship Through Acquisition):** Raising search capital from institutional investors to acquire, lead as CEO, and grow a single cash-flowing SME (€1m–€5m EBITDA). Rapidly growing at INSEAD, LBS, Harvard, and Stanford as a meritocratic path to PE-style equity ownership without prior banking.
   - **Secondaries, Co-investments, and Fund-of-Funds:** Platforms like HarbourVest, StepStone, Ardian, and Neuberger Berman evaluate fund managers and secondaries packages; they feature broader hiring profiles than direct buyout teams.

### Adjacent Exit Routes
- **IB → PE, US:** "On-cycle" recruiting, run by headhunters, starts as early as 6–9 months into an analyst's first year for start dates two years in advance.
- **IB → PE, Europe:** More staggered, ad-hoc, and off-cycle. European headhunters interview analysts between 12 and 24 months into their roles, with language fluency mandatory for domestic country funds (Paris, Frankfurt, Milan, Madrid, Zurich).
- **Private Credit:** Fastest-growing alternative asset class; DCM, leveraged finance, and restructuring backgrounds are aggressively recruited by Ares, Blackstone Credit, Oaktree, Blue Owl, and HPS.
- **Asset Management:** Direct graduate entry is realistic, especially from a finance master's with CFA Level I or II; IB → AM moves are also common.
- **Corporate Development and Strategy:** The most common "lifestyle" exit after 2–4 years, moving into in-house M&A at corporates; see careers/accounting-and-corporate.md.

### 5.2 Interpretation
- **Whole-bank intake has shifted toward STEM.** The Goldman series (37% → 42% in 2019 → 45% in 2021) reflects firm-wide engineering expansion. Technology and engineering make up one of the largest single graduate intakes across global banking.
- **The IB division is different.** In traditional M&A, ECM, and DCM, economics, finance, and accounting graduates continue to dominate junior cohorts. A finance master's on top of a quantitative bachelor's represents the gold-standard profile for European corporate finance.
- **Where STEM dominates: Quant Trading, Electronic Market Making, and Strats.** The highest compensation packages across all early-career finance belong not to investment bankers, but to quantitative specialists.

### 5.4 Quantitative Trading, Prop Trading and Market Making in Depth
The prop trading and quantitative market-making ecosystem represents the pinnacle of compensation and analytical selectivity for STEM, Computing, Mathematics, and Physics graduates.

**Top Firms by Hub:**
- **London / Global:** Jane Street, Citadel & Citadel Securities, Optiver, Jump Trading, Flow Traders, IMC Trading, Hudson River Trading (HRT), Susquehanna (SIG), DRW, Wintermute, XTX Markets.
- **Amsterdam:** Optiver (European HQ), Flow Traders (Global HQ), IMC Trading (Global HQ).
- **Chicago / New York:** Jane Street, Citadel, Jump Trading, HRT, Two Sigma, D.E. Shaw, Point72, Millennium.

**The Three Quant Career Tracks:**
1. **Quantitative Research (QR):**
   - *The Work:* Designing statistical models, mathematical signal extraction, alpha generation, stochastic volatility forecasting, machine learning on tick-level data.
   - *Target Degree:* PhD or elite quantitative Master's (MFE, Mathematics, Theoretical Physics, Machine Learning). École Polytechnique, Cambridge Part III Maths, Imperial, Oxford, ETH Zurich.
   - *Selection:* Heavy probability, statistical estimation, linear algebra, stochastic calculus, coding tests.
2. **Quantitative Trading (QT):**
   - *The Work:* Managing real-time trading books, market making, execution algorithms, pricing derivatives, risk management, game-theoretic decision making under high latency pressure.
   - *Target Degree:* Bachelor's or Master's in STEM, Computer Science, Econometrics, or Quantitative Economics.
   - *Selection:* Fast-paced mental math, expected value calculations, Bayes' theorem under pressure, market-making simulations, decision-making psychology.
3. **Quantitative Development (QD) / Software Engineering:**
   - *The Work:* Building ultra-low latency execution infrastructure, hardware acceleration (FPGA via Verilog/VHDL), Linux kernel bypass, network socket optimization, lock-free concurrent data structures in modern C++ (C++20/23).
   - *Target Degree:* Computer Science, Computer Engineering, Electrical Engineering.
   - *Selection:* Advanced algorithms, systems architecture, memory management, operating system internals, concurrency.

**Early-Career Compensation:**
- **New York / Chicago:** Base $175k–$250k; guaranteed or target first-year bonus $100k–$200k; total entry package **$300,000–$450,000+**.
- **London:** Base £120k–£175k; total first-year package **£180,000–£320,000+**.
- **Amsterdam:** Base €100k–€140k; total package **€150,000–€250,000+** (performance-linked bonuses at Flow Traders and Optiver can multiply during market volatility).

### 5.5 Verdict on H3
**Partly supported.** It holds completely for markets, trading, quant research, and strats desks. It fails for classic investment banking advisory (M&A), where finance, economics, and business school degrees dominate.

## 6. AI and the analyst job

- Goldman (2026): AI will bring "nuanced changes that probably to some degree reduce the number of people that we start with", but "not what you and I would call dramatically" (Fortune, 2026) [employer-stated, via press].
- The work most exposed is the analyst's first-year core: pitchbook formatting, comps, first-draft models, CIM drafting and research summaries. Banks have publicly described internal generative-AI assistants for bankers since 2024, but the specific Goldman, JPMorgan and Morgan Stanley statements on pitchbooks and modelling could not be opened this session (Claims to verify).
- Evercore, an elite boutique, saw London headcount rise from 386 to 428 in 2025, but its hiring "slowed down substantially" in 2026 even as H1 revenue rose 56%; eFinancialCareers links this to Evercore's partnership with OpenAI on a "ChatGPT for financial services" (eFinancialCareers from UK filings, 16 Sep 2026, https://www.efinancialcareers.com/news/evercore-london-pay) [data via press; the causal link is the journalist's inference]. Revenue growing while junior hiring slows is the pattern to watch.
- Context: Wall Street firms collectively shed about 10,000 jobs in 2025 even though it was a strong year for banks (eFinancialCareers, 8 Jul 2026, https://www.efinancialcareers.com/news/salaries-and-bonuses-goldman-sachs-jpmorgan-citi-baml-morgan-stanley) [practitioner consensus].
- Reports that banks might cut junior hiring by as much as two-thirds circulate widely but were not traced to an on-record primary source (Claims to verify). The on-record Goldman statement above points to a modest reduction.
- Wider labour-market signal: Stanford Digital Economy Lab found employment of 22–25-year-olds in the most AI-exposed occupations fell about 13% relative to others (Brynjolfsson, Chandar, Chen, Aug 2025) [data; cited from product/landscape-map.md, not re-fetched]. See evidence/trends.md.
- Practical implication: the analyst who can check, structure and explain AI-drafted work, and who shows judgment in client-facing settings, is the one banks describe wanting. Skills that were once the entry ticket (fast Excel formatting) are losing signalling value. [employer-stated (Goldman on "judgment, adaptability") + practitioner consensus]

## 8. Which master's feed finance

- **MiF (pre-experience Master in Finance / MSc Finance):** the default for IB, S&T, AM and research. The strongest London feeders are LSE (MSc Finance, MSc Finance & Economics), LBS MFA and MiF, Imperial MSc Finance, Oxford MFE and Cambridge MFin (the latter post-experience). On the Continent: HEC MIF, ESSEC, ESCP, EDHEC, Bocconi MSc Finance, St. Gallen MBF, Mannheim, RSM, Esade and IE. Rankings and fit are in decisions/programme-choice.md and decisions/school-types-and-accreditation.md.
- **MiM:** a broader door (consulting, corporate, tech) that still places into finance at HEC, LBS, St. Gallen, Bocconi and ESCP. It is the better hedge when finance is only one of several targets. [practitioner consensus]
- **MFE / quantitative finance (e.g. Berkeley MFE, Oxford MSc Mathematical and Computational Finance, Imperial MSc Risk Management & Financial Engineering):** for quant, strats and risk modelling. Admission requires real maths. [practitioner consensus]
- **Fetched outcome data for specialised MiFs:**

| Programme | Offers / employment | Where they go | Pay | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| LBS MFA (2025 cohort) | 92% received an offer within 3 months | UK 66%, Europe excl. UK 15%, Asia 14% | Highest reported £119,315 | LBS, 2025 [data] |
| HEC Paris MIF (class of 2025) | 100% employed (FT 2024 ranking data) | 81.4% financial services, 7.2% consulting; 86.6% in finance/accounting functions | €103k average starting salary; €169k three years out (FT 2024) | HEC, 2026 [data, school-reported] |

- What these say: a top specialised MiF sends 80–90% of graduates into finance, whereas top MiMs spread across consulting, tech and corporate roles. The trade-off is optionality versus concentration. LSE and Bocconi employment reports were not read in full this session; see getting-in/employer-pipelines.md and decisions/programme-choice.md.
- Warning on UK MiMs as a finance route: FT-derived three-month employment for UK MiMs fell from 96% (2019) to 79% (2026). LBS MiM fell from 94% to 88% and Warwick from 98% to 82% over the same period (eFinancialCareers analysis of FT rankings, 9 Sep 2026, https://www.efinancialcareers.com/news/masters-in-management) [data via press]. For a finance-only target, a MiF has the stronger placement record in the UK.

## 9. Europe-specific notes

### 9.1 Paris
- Paris is the strongest Continental hub for two pre-experience routes: grande-école-fed M&A/ECM (with *césure* internships), and quant/e-trading, where French engineering and maths schools dominate (eFinancialCareers, Sep 2026, see §5.2) [practitioner consensus]. ### 9.2 Milan
- **Mediobanca is now part of the Monte dei Paschi group.** Mediobanca's own 6M results release confirms that after the BMPS exchange offer results (11 September 2025) confirmed a change of control, Mediobanca was removed from the register of banking groups on 15 September 2025 and became subject to MPS's direction and co-ordination, with a new board from 28 October 2025. CIB revenues fell 20% year on year in the six months to December 2025 and CIB fees fell 34%, with advisory fees down to €42.3m from €78.3m. The bank launched "incentivization and retention measures in both WM and CIB", paid €18m in severance to departing senior management, and still completed 32 advisory deals in the half (Mediobanca, 6M results to 31 Dec 2025, https://www.mediobanca.com/static/upload_new/pre/press-release_results-end-dec--2025-final.pdf) [employer-stated].

A Teleborsa headline on the same page refers to a 29 October shareholders' meeting on Mediobanca's merger into MPS (Claims to verify).
- Reading for students: Mediobanca remains Italy's "advisor of choice" on its own account, but a CIB under retention spending and integration is a less predictable graduate employer in 2026–27. Apply in parallel elsewhere.
- Other Milan doors: Equita (independent broker/advisor), Rothschild, Lazard, Vitale, Lincoln, Houlihan Lokey, BNP Paribas, UniCredit CIB, Intesa Sanpaolo IMI CIB, and the Italian offices of US bulge brackets (mostly coverage). Italian is required for domestic coverage. [practitioner consensus]

## Hypotheses tested

### H3 (assigned). "Investment banks increasingly hire STEM graduates over finance graduates."
- **Verdict:** partly supported.
- **Holds when:** "bank" means the whole firm (engineering is roughly a quarter of Goldman headcount; the intern class went from 37% to ~45% STEM between 2017 and 2021), or the role is quant, strats, e-trading or risk modelling.
- **Fails when:** the question is IBD front-office analyst hiring in London and Europe, where economics and finance degrees made up most interns and analysts in the only division-level data (2016–17). It also blurs because many European hires combine a STEM bachelor's with a finance master's.
- **Key evidence:** eFinancialCareers 2017 and 2021 reporting of Goldman data [employer-stated]; eFinancialCareers 2016 and 2017 analyses of London classes [practitioner consensus; stale]. No bank has published IBD-only major splits since.

### H4 (own). "A pre-experience MiF in London is now the main entry door to London IB for non-UK Europeans."
- **Verdict:** partly supported (insufficient data on the "main" part).
- **Holds when:** the candidate has a three-year Continental bachelor's and no UK internship, and the programme runs structured off-cycle and summer recruiting (LBS MFA: 92% offers within three months, 66% of jobs in the UK, and named IB/off-cycle outcomes).
- **Fails when:** the candidate can already recruit as a UK undergraduate, or targets Paris or Milan, where home grande-école and Bocconi pipelines (with *césure* internships) compete directly. No bank discloses the share of analysts with a master's.
- **Evidence:** LBS MFA career page, 2025 [data]; eFinancialCareers 2016 analysis of LSE dominance [practitioner consensus; stale].

### H5 (own). "Elite boutiques pay the same as bulge brackets at analyst level."
- **Verdict:** partly supported; base is similar, bonus is higher on average at EBs.
- **Holds when:** comparing base salaries, which converged in 2021–22.
- **Fails when:** comparing total pay: self-reported 2025 bonuses averaged ~$80k at boutiques vs ~$75k at bulge brackets and ~$65k at middle market (eFinancialCareers/WSO, 2025) [anecdotal]; London surveys put US banks and boutiques (Lazard, Perella) at the top in 2024 [practitioner consensus]. The aggregator figures (Centerview ~£125k vs Goldman ~£92k) point the same way but are method-opaque [anecdotal]. The size of the EB premium also varies by year and bank.

### H6 (own). "CFA Level I has no measurable effect on IB offers."
- **Verdict:** insufficient evidence (no outcome study found), but directionally supported by practitioner consensus for M&A; not supported for AM and research.
- **Holds when:** the target is M&A/ECM analyst hiring, where internships, school and technical interviews dominate.
- **Fails when:** the target is AM, equity research, credit or wealth management, or the candidate lacks any finance coursework and needs a signal.
- **Evidence:** CFA cost and time burden (CFA Institute, 2026) [data]; practitioner consensus on role fit. No controlled or survey data on offers.

### H7 (own). "Bank hours caps have materially reduced junior hours."
- **Verdict:** not supported as a general claim.
- **Holds when:** there is no live deal, and in US offices where logging is enforced (BofA daily logging).
- **Fails when:** a live deal is running, which is exempt at JPMorgan, and in any week where the cap is "in most cases" rather than hard. Juniors reported BofA's earlier 80-hour guideline being routinely ignored (Fortune, 2024) [employer-stated + reported].

### H8 (own). "AI will cut junior banking classes by around two-thirds."
- **Verdict:** not supported by on-record evidence found.
- **Holds when:** read as a long-run scenario for pure production work.
- **Fails when:** tested against on-record employer statements: Goldman expects a reduction "not … dramatically" (Fortune, 2026) [employer-stated]. Goldman's entry-level intake was still ~2,500 in 2026.

## Common mistakes and myths

1. **"I'll do the master's first, then look for an internship."** For one-year UK MSc programmes, bank recruiting for the post-master's summer opens around the programme's start; waiting until after exams usually means missing the cycle. See getting-in/recruiting-calendar.md. [practitioner consensus]
2. **"The CFA will make up for no internship."** Not in IB. Internships and deal-relevant experience dominate IB screens; CFA matters in AM and research. [practitioner consensus]
3. **"Hours caps mean 80-hour weeks are the ceiling."** Live deals are exempt at JPMorgan, and BofA's earlier cap was widely ignored (Fortune, 2024). [employer-stated, via press]
4. **"STEM beats finance for IB."** True for the bank as a whole, not clearly for IBD (see H3). A strong quantitative bachelor's plus a finance master's is the most common European profile. [practitioner consensus]
5. **"PE hires straight out of a master's."** Rare in Europe; plan 2–3 years of IB or TS first. [practitioner consensus]
6. **"Bonus = a fixed share of salary."** London analyst bonuses fell by double digits in 2023 and rose sharply in 2025; buckets and bank results drive them (eFinancialCareers, 2024 and 2026). [practitioner consensus]
7. **"Mediobanca is the safe Italian IB default."** Since September 2025 it is part of the MPS group, with CIB revenues down 20% in its first half under new control and retention spending under way (Mediobanca, 2026). Its 2026–27 junior plans are uncertain. [employer-stated]
8. **"Any finance master's is equal for London."** Location, career-office strength and employer presence on campus matter; London-based programmes have London employers physically recruiting. [practitioner consensus; see getting-in/employer-pipelines.md]

## Decision rules

1. **If** the target is London IB M&A **and** the candidate has no finance internship yet, **prefer** a London pre-experience MiF (LBS MFA/MiF, LSE, Imperial) that starts recruiting from week one, over a two-year Continental master's (because off-cycle and summer cycles run during the one-year programme, and LBS MFA shows 66% of jobs landing in the UK).
2. **If** the target is Paris or Milan IB **and** the candidate speaks the local language, **prefer** the local top programme (HEC/ESSEC/ESCP for Paris; Bocconi for Milan) with an internship or *césure* year (because home pipelines and local-language coverage roles dominate).
3. **If** the target is quant, strats or e-trading, **prefer** an MFE or maths-heavy finance programme over a generalist MiF (because these seats are STEM-selected; H3 holds here).
4. **If** the target is asset management or equity research, **then** sit CFA Level I before or early in the master's (because it is a valued signal there and costs ~300 hours).
5. **If** the target is IB **and** the recruiting season overlaps the CFA exam window, **then** defer CFA (because the hours compete directly with recruiting).
6. **If** a bulge-bracket summer is missed, **prefer** an off-cycle at an elite boutique or middle-market bank over a delayed application to the next BB cycle (because off-cycle roles often convert and EB pay is at least comparable).
7. **If** work-life balance or visa stability is a priority, **prefer** corporate banking, DCM, risk or AM graduate programmes over M&A (because they run shorter hours, with larger intakes).
8. **If** the long-run target is PE in continental Europe, **then** pick IB M&A or TS in the target country and learn the local language (because European PE recruiting is off-cycle and network-driven).
9. **If** the candidate is non-EU **and** targets Zurich, **then** check Swiss permit quotas before choosing St. Gallen or a Swiss offer (see places/visas-and-work-rights.md).
10. **If** comparing a BB and an EB analyst offer on pay alone, **then** treat them as roughly equal and decide on deal exposure and exit record (because base pay has converged and EB bonus premia vary by year).
11. **If** targeting Mediobanca in 2026–27, **then** apply in parallel to other Milan franchises (Equita, US and French banks' Milan offices, boutiques) (because of the post-takeover uncertainty).
