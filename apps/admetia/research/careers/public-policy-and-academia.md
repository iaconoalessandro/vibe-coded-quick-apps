# Public-sector, international-organisation and academic careers

## Bottom line

1. **EU institutions: use the traineeship and contract-agent routes, treat AD5 as a lottery with a good prize.** EPSO/AD/427/26 drew 174,727 applicants for 1,490 places (about 117 applicants per place; 90% from outside the institutions), with reasoning tests only starting in November 2026 and no recruitment guarantee even for reserve-list names (EPSO notice and news, 2026) [employer-stated / data]. The Blue Book (about 1,000 places a session) takes a three-year degree, pays 25% of AD5 step 1 (EUR 1,538.16 a month in Brussels, author arithmetic), and is the realistic first step.
2. **Italians are 45% of the AD5 applicant pool** (79,343 of 174,727), against Spain 13,789, Germany 11,699 and France 10,939 (EPSO, 8 May 2026) [data]. Being Italian does not change the rules but it means the field you rank against is Italian-heavy.
3. **AD5 pay is high against private-sector first jobs.** Basic salary EUR 6,152.64 a month from 1 July 2025 [data]; an EU agency's worked example gives net EUR 4,859 without allowances and EUR 5,870 with the expatriation allowance [employer-stated]. That is roughly 1.4 to 1.7 times the net pay of a top-programme first job in the library (author calculation). It explains the applicant volume.
4. **Banca d'Italia is the most concrete national central-bank door and has a hard grade floor.** The December 2025 concorso offered 45 Esperti places in economics (laurea magistrale at 105/110 or above in LM-77, LM-56, LM-16, LM-52 or LM-62) and drew 8,307 applications (about 185 per place); preselection takes the top 500, then 400 go to the oral (Banca d'Italia, 2025-26) [employer-stated].
5. **International organisations: Italy is shut out of the UN Secretariat YPP route and has its own state-funded door.** Italy is not on the 2026 YPP country list on careers.un.org (47 countries, read 2 Oct 2026), and the Secretariat's own reports show Italy overrepresented (31 Dec 2022, 31 Dec 2023 and 31 Oct 2024); the YPP is open only to nationals of un- or under-represented states. The MAECI JPO programme offers 45 places for Italians under about 31 (2026 edition) [data / employer-stated]. The World Bank YPP needs 2-6 years' experience. The OECD Young Associates Programme (EUR 3,886 a month, two years in Paris) takes only recent bachelor's graduates: applications from anyone who already holds or is enrolled in a master's are not considered, so it is for undergraduates, not for a master's student [employer-stated, OECD page captured 1 Sep 2026].
6. **A funded business PhD pays less than a typical first job in every country where I could compare.** Stipends are about 60% (UKRI, Swiss SNSF floor), 70% (France), about 90% (LBS, Italy) and about 100% or 70% (Germany at 100% or 65% contracts) of first-job net pay (author calculation from official scales, section 5c). The PhD makes financial sense mainly for the US-style academic market, central-bank and IMF research jobs (PhD required), or for readers who value the work.
7. **The UK Fast Stream is effectively closed to EU graduates who arrived after 2020** unless they hold settled or pre-settled status; sponsorship is not available at the GBP 31,554 starting salary (Civil Service Careers, 2 Oct 2026) [employer-stated].
8. **Evidence gaps matter.** I could not read the EBRD and Bundesbank pages (the IMF, OECD and UN YPP pages were read in verification round 3a), found no published conversion rate from traineeship to staff, and found no school-published data on non-academic outcomes for business PhDs.

Reading: the reasoning tests have modest pass marks (10/20 verbal, 10/20 combined numerical and abstract), so ranking on the later tests is likely to decide the list among tens of thousands of people, but the notice does not publish pass rates, so that is an inference. **Italians are 45% of the applicant pool.** That matters for a reader of this site, because ranking is against the whole pool, not against other nationalities (the notice sets no national quotas; the Commission does monitor geographical balance across its staff in recruitment, which can shape who is hired from the list, but that is a separate rule and I did not read it in full). Time from application to a possible job offer is at least 12 to 18 months (March 2026 application, tests from November 2026, list later); this is an author reading of the published timetable, not a published figure.

The earlier library figure of "170,000+ applicants" is consistent with 174,727. The Gemini lead said 174,922 and a "0.85%" rate; the first number does not match EPSO's own page (see Corrections).

### 1b. Pay: AD5 step 1

- **Basic monthly salary, AD5 step 1: EUR 6,152.64**, applicable from 1 July 2025 (Council document 16220/25 ADD 1, 1 Dec 2025, which carries the Article 66 table; the same table lists steps 2 to 5 at 6,411.17 / 6,680.58 / 6,866.46 / 6,961.29) [data]. Pay is updated each year; the 2026 update was not yet published when I checked (1 Oct 2026).
- **Official worked example (EU agency AMLA, rates as of 1 July 2025):** AD5 step 1 with under three years' experience, net **EUR 4,858.96 a month with no allowances** and **EUR 5,869.95 with the expatriation allowance**. Deductions listed: pension 13.10%, health insurance 1.70%, accident cover 0.10%, unemployment insurance 0.81%, EU tax progressive from 8% to 45%, solidarity levy 6% (AMLA careers page, accessed 2 Oct 2026) [employer-stated]. Caveats: this is a temporary-agent example at a Frankfurt weighting of 102.7%, "for information only and not legally binding". Brussels has a coefficient of 100, so the real Brussels figure for an official should be within a few per cent, but I did not find a Commission page with a Brussels official example. Treat EUR 4,800 to 5,900 a month net as the range (about EUR 58k to 70k a year at 12 payments), with the upper figure only for a non-Belgian national.
- EU salaries are exempt from national income tax and instead bear the EU tax and levy above (Protocol 7 on privileges and immunities; I did not re-read the Protocol, the deductions list on AMLA's page is the evidence read) [employer-stated].
- Steps rise roughly every two years in the same grade, and promotion to AD6 and AD7 is the main lever; I did not read promotion statistics, so I make no claim about speed.

**Takeaway for H-a.** Seat arithmetic: Blue Book about 1,000 places a session (about 2,000 a year) against, per a Commission news line I could not open in full, about 36,000 applications a year (unverified, snippet only; implies about 5-6% selection); AD5 about 1,490 places against 174,727 applicants (0.85%). Blue Book is roughly six to seven times easier by this rough measure, and it is a five-month, paid, pre-job step rather than a career. The real conversion route is: Blue Book or another traineeship, then contract-agent roles or the next AD5/AD7 competition with contacts and an EU knowledge base already built. No source I found publishes the share of trainees who later become staff, so that step is unquantified.

## 2. Central banks and supervisors

### 2a. Banca d'Italia: the concorso is the clearest national central-bank door, and it has a grade floor

Source: Banca d'Italia, "160 assunzioni per l'Amministrazione Centrale e la Rete territoriale", bando of 10 December 2025 (Gazzetta Ufficiale 4th special series no. 98, 16 Dec 2025), plus the convocation notice for the preselection tests (March 2026) [employer-stated / data].

Reading: **the 105/110 floor is a hard filter for the profile that matches a master's graduate.** A reader finishing an Italian LM-77 or LM-56 with 103 is ineligible for the Esperto profile in this notice; a reader with a foreign master's must obtain recognition and convert the grade (see places/italy-playbook.md on grade conversion). The notice does not state starting pay. A third-party search summary gave EUR 50,126 gross a year for newly hired Esperti (secondary, unverified; see Claims to verify). The approximate odds per place (about 0.5% of applicants for an Esperto place) are an author calculation from two official numbers. The bank has also held earlier economics-and-statistics concorsi (60 posts in 2022, 61 in 2024 per URLs on its site; counts not read), so this is a recurring, roughly annual door, not a one-off.

### 2b. ECB and the other EU supervisors

- **ECB Graduate Programme:** master's or PhD (PhD mandatory for the economist track), EU national, **19 graduates a year (15 generalists, 4 economists)**, two-year fixed term at band E/F from 1 September, optional third year at expert level (F/G); generalist vacancies published in winter (recent window closed 13 February), economist vacancies in autumn (ECB graduate page, accessed 2 Oct 2026) [employer-stated]. Salary figures are not on the page I could read (the ECB salary pages returned errors). The Gemini lead gave "EUR 4,982 net" and a grade; I could not confirm either and do not use them.
- **ECB traineeship:** see careers/accounting-and-corporate.md (EUR 1,170 a month; max 12 months since last degree).
- **EBA and ESMA traineeships:** EBA traineeships appear on an EBA decision as a monthly grant of EUR 2,096.82 (from a search snippet of an EBA document; year not established, treat as unverified); eligibility reported as EU/EEA/candidate-country nationals with a degree completed within five years of the deadline. ESMA's 2026 deadline was reported as 15 July 2026 (snippet only). I did not read primary pages for either, so treat these as pointers.
- **Not covered, deliberately:** Bundesbank, Banque de France, Banco de España, DNB, CONSOB, BaFin, FCA graduate schemes. I did not read their notices this cycle. The structural point holds only for Italy, where the concorso (not a graduate programme) is the route, and where Italian is a requirement.

### 3b. UN nationality rules: what the Secretariat's own data show

Source: Report of the Secretary-General A/80/508, "Composition of the Secretariat: staff demographics" (28 October 2025, data to 31 December 2024) [data].

- The Secretariat had **35,231 staff from 191 Member States**. Representation status at 31 Dec 2024: **17 unrepresented, 31 underrepresented, 123 within range, 22 overrepresented** (chart-read from the report's five-year figure; 17 and 31 are also stated in the text).
- The report's tables of underrepresented and unrepresented states **do not list Italy**. Table 5 lists the 31 underrepresented states (including Germany, Japan, the United States, Singapore, Republic of Korea, Saudi Arabia and Malta) and the text lists the unrepresented ones. So Italy was **neither unrepresented nor underrepresented** at the end of 2024. A/80/508 no longer prints a per-country table (it points to the Secretariat Workforce Portal, which is for Member States), but the two previous reports and an Advisory Committee note do: Italy was **overrepresented** at 31 Dec 2022 (A/78/569: Italy 43 above the range, second after Kenya in the report's text), at 31 Dec 2023 (A/79/584, annex table: 124 geographical staff against a desirable range of 65-87, 37 above) and at 31 Oct 2024 (A/79/747, ACABQ para. 18, 5 Feb 2025: 161 current staff against a range of 87-117, exceeding it by 44) [data]. Italy's status at 31 Dec 2024 is not stated in A/80/508 itself.
- Young professionals programme recruitment is the Secretariat's tool for unrepresented and underrepresented states. External recruitment to geographical posts was only **104 in 2024** (128 in 2023, 176 in 2022) and the YPP share fell from 27.3% (35 of 128) to **15.4% (16 of 104)** because of hiring restrictions on regular-budget posts (paras 93-96).

Verdict for H-c below: Italy is excluded from the Secretariat YPP route (not on the 2026 country list on careers.un.org, and overrepresented in the Secretariat's own tables), so for Italians the **nationality rule is binding for that programme before qualifications are considered**. The state-funded JPO programme works the other way, in Italians' favour.

## 5. Academia: the business and economics PhD in Europe

### 5a. How it works

- **Structure.** In continental Europe a business PhD is normally a full-time, salaried or stipend-funded research job of 3 to 5 years, with 1 to 2 years of coursework and 2 to 3 years of thesis work. Bocconi's PhD in Business Administration and Management (management and marketing tracks) is **4 years** with nine compulsory courses in year 1 (Bocconi PhD page, read 2 Oct 2026) [employer-stated]. HEC Paris describes a **5-year** programme (two years of courses, three of research) funded for up to five years with EUR 26,000 a year, per a search summary of its page (unverified, snippet only; the HEC page I opened gave no stipend). LBS: all students get a fee waiver and a tax-free stipend (GBP 32,154 from Sept 2026) for five years, usually finishing in 5-6 years (see decisions/timing-and-sequencing.md) [employer-stated].
- **Entry.** Bocconi's 2026/27 call (applications 17 Apr to 18 May 2026): a completed second-level degree (Italian laurea magistrale or a non-Italian level-7 degree), B2 English, and GMAT or GRE **"required for most programs"** on the admissions page; the short call for the management programme says scores are "not indispensable" and are worth up to 15 of 100 points, against 50 for CV and academic record and 35 for the statement of purpose and references, with a 60-point minimum (Bocconi admissions pages and short call, read 2 Oct 2026) [employer-stated]. The two statements are not fully consistent; check the current call. A taught MiM or MSc Finance is accepted as a degree level, but whether it is *competitive* for a top research PhD depends on quantitative coursework and a research record, which no source I read quantifies.
- **Self-funding is not the norm but exists.** Bocconi's published fees for a student without a fellowship are EUR 15,000 a year in years 1-2 and EUR 5,000 in years 3-4 (Bocconi financial-matters page) [employer-stated]. Treat any unfunded offer as a rejection.
- **Placement.** HEC Paris lists 2024-26 graduates mostly going to assistant-professor or postdoc posts (Warwick Business School, LSE, Chinese University of Hong Kong, Imperial postdoc, Texas A&M postdoc and others) with few non-academic exits (a Bank of England research economist in 2023 and a fintech role in 2024 are the exceptions listed). The page states that "90%" continue to academic jobs per a search summary (unverified, snippet; page says the cohort is mostly academic) [employer-stated, selective]. Bocconi lists recent placements at IESE, St. Gallen, INSEAD, Wharton, Tsinghua and Dartmouth [employer-stated]. School placement pages list successes; they do not report attrition.
- **Public-sector payoff of a PhD.** The ECB's economist graduate track requires a PhD (ECB graduate page) and the IMF Economist Program is aimed at recent PhDs (section 3). For central-bank and IMF research jobs the PhD is a ticket; for the ECB generalist track a master's is enough.

**Result.** In none of the cases with a sourced first-job benchmark does the stipend exceed typical first-job net pay. The gap ranges from about 10% (LBS, Italy) to about 40% (UKRI, SNSF floor). Over four years, the UKRI gap is about GBP 56,000 and the SNSF-floor gap about CHF 114,000 before lost pay progression and pension accrual (author arithmetic). Better PhD pay shows up only where a stipend is well above the legal floor (LBS) or where first-job pay is very low (Italy).

**Lifetime pay.** The US academic market pays far more than Europe: AACSB 2026 reports new business-school doctorates earning a mean of USD 139,659, finance assistant professors about USD 180,000 and accounting USD 170,000, but EMEA new doctorates a median of USD 77,664 from only 23 hires (decisions/timing-and-sequencing.md) [data]. I did not compute lifetime comparisons; a fair one needs the tenure rate (see the Royal Society figure in Claims to verify) and a country-specific professor pay scale, which I did not read. The Gemini lead's "break-even" tables rely on Levels.fyi-type tech and quant pay that do not apply to business PhDs, and its "STEM/Business Master's out-earns Humanities PhD" point is about a different degree field.

### 5d. Non-academic exits (what the sources show and do not show)

HEC's own list shows a small number of non-academic exits among about 30 recent graduates (a Bank of England economist, a fintech behavioural scientist) [employer-stated, selective]. The central-bank and IMF economist tracks accept PhDs (section 3 and 2). I found no school-published share of business PhDs working in consulting, central banks or industry, and no study I could read of earnings after non-academic exit. This is the biggest gap in the evidence: a reader choosing a PhD for optionality has no primary data for what the options pay.

## Hypotheses tested

**H-a (assigned). "EU institution entry is realistic for a 25-year-old master's graduate via traineeships and contract-agent routes, not via AD5 lottery odds."**
Verdict: **supported on access, unquantified on conversion.** Evidence: AD5 had 174,727 applicants for 1,490 places and gives no recruitment guarantee (EPSO) [data]. The Blue Book has about 1,000 places a session, twice a year, needs only a three-year degree, and the March 2027 round opened 22 Jul to 4 Sep 2026 and the next opens 15 Feb 2027 (Commission traineeships site, 2026) [employer-stated]. EPSO's CAST database is always open and FG IV needs three years of university study [employer-stated]. Holds if you are an EU national, have C1 plus B2 languages, and have not done more than 42 days in any EU body before. Fails where: no source shows how many trainees or CAs become officials, and CA contracts are fixed-term (up to six years in the institutions). The Gemini hint of a roughly 5-6% Blue Book acceptance (36,000 applications a year against about 2,000 places) is a snippet only.

**H-b (assigned). "A funded business PhD pays better than a typical first job for the first five years in some countries and worse in others."**
Verdict: **not supported in the "better" direction on the data I could source; the gap varies from about 10% to 40%.** Evidence: section 5c (author calculation from UKRI, SNSF, TV-L, CAO NU, French and Italian scales, and library first-job benchmarks) [data inputs]. Closest to parity: LBS (GBP 32,154 tax-free), Italy (low first-job pay), and Germany at 100% E 13. Fails where: Germany and the Netherlands have no sourced first-job benchmark in the library, so those rows are illustrative; Dutch net is overstated.

**H-c (assigned). "International-organisation entry for Italians is constrained by nationality quotas more than by qualifications."**
Verdict: **partly supported.** For the **UN Secretariat YPP** it is supported: Italy is absent from the 2026 participating-country list on careers.un.org (read 2 Oct 2026), is not among the unrepresented or underrepresented Member States listed in A/80/508 (data to 31 Dec 2024), and was overrepresented in the Secretariat tables for 2022, 2023 and October 2024 (A/78/569, A/79/584, A/79/747) [data / employer-stated]. The Secretariat also recruited only 104 people to geographical posts in 2024 [data]. It is **not** the main constraint elsewhere: the World Bank YPP is limited by experience (2-6 years), the IMF Economist Program by a PhD, the OECD Young Associates Programme requires OECD-member nationality (Italy qualifies) and a bachelor's, and excludes anyone already holding or enrolled in a master's [employer-stated]. And Italians have a quota working in their favour: 45 of 47 places in the 2026 JPO programme [employer-stated]. Italy's status was "overrepresented" in the 2022, 2023 and October 2024 tables (see section 3b); A/80/508 does not print it for December 2024.

**H-d (own). "The Banca d'Italia Esperto concorso is harder per place than the EU AD5 competition but filters on grades, not on lottery."**
Verdict: **supported on the numbers.** 8,307 applications for 45 places (about 185 per place) against about 117 per place for AD5 (author arithmetic from two official counts) [employer-stated / data]. The 105/110 floor and the 60-question preselection (top 500) mean rank is earned by grades then by tests. Fails where: the applicant pools are different (Banca d'Italia applicants already hold a master's at 105+), so the per-place ratios are not like for like.

**H-e (own). "Italy is over-represented among EPSO AD5 applicants relative to its population."**
Verdict: **supported.** 79,343 of 174,727 applications (45.4%) came from Italy (EPSO) [data]. Italy has roughly 13% of EU citizens (author approximation, not sourced here). Meaning: the competition is not "26 nationalities equally". The notice contains no national quotas; geographical balance is monitored elsewhere (Commission rules on staff balance, not read).

**H-f (own). "A paid EU traineeship leaves little cash in Brussels."**
Verdict: **supported (author arithmetic).** Grant EUR 1,538.16 minus the Blue Book FAQ's EUR 700-1,000 for accommodation leaves about EUR 540-840 a month before food, transport and travel. The Commission rules add optional meal/transport allowances of 5% of the grant and a Luxembourg accommodation allowance of 20%, subject to funding [employer-stated]. Fails where: trainees with savings, family support or scholarships; no source reports how many do the traineeship unaided.

**H-g (own). "The UK Fast Stream is not an option for an EU graduate on a Graduate visa."**
Verdict: **supported on the published rules.** The scheme needs settled/pre-settled status (or equivalent), Irish/Commonwealth/UK nationality, or sponsorship, which is unavailable at the starting salary except in transitional cases; the right to work must last for the whole programme (Civil Service Careers nationality page) [employer-stated]. The Graduate visa is 18-24 months (places/visas-and-work-rights.md), shorter than a typical scheme (author inference).

## Common mistakes and myths

1. **"Being at a top business school gets you into the EU institutions."** The AD5 route is decided by multiple-choice reasoning tests, an EU knowledge test, a digital test and an essay, with 174,727 applicants (EPSO notice and news, 2026) [employer-stated / data]. School brand is not an input.
2. **"A traineeship before the Blue Book will help."** More than 42 calendar days at any EU institution, body or agency (including parliamentary assistant, interim, intra-muros consultant) excludes you (Commission Decision C(2026) 4902) [employer-stated].
3. **"UN YPP is open to anyone aged 32 or under."** It is open only to the nationals of countries on that year's list (47 for 2026); Italy is not on the 2026 list [employer-stated, careers.un.org, read 2 Oct 2026]. The age rule is "32 years or younger" in the exam year, and a bachelor's degree suffices. Check the current exam call.
4. **"The World Bank YPP is a graduate programme."** It needs 2-6 years' experience (careers/accounting-and-corporate.md) [employer-stated].
5. **"The EU salary is tax-free, so AD5 pay is EUR 6,152 net."** The basic salary is gross; EU tax, solidarity levy and pension reduce it to about EUR 4,860 net (no allowances) in an EU agency's worked example [employer-stated].
6. **"Any good master's makes me eligible for Banca d'Italia."** The Esperto profile requires 105/110 and specified classes; foreign degrees need recognition (Banca d'Italia bando, 10 Dec 2025) [employer-stated].
7. **"A PhD is a free master's plus a salary."** Funded business PhDs in the sources pay about 60-100% of a typical first job's net pay, and tuition-free is not the same as costless (section 5c) [data / author calculation]. Unfunded places (Bocconi's published EUR 15,000 a year in years 1-2 for non-fellows) are a different decision.
8. **"The UK Fast Stream is open to EU graduates."** Only with settled or pre-settled status (Civil Service Careers) [employer-stated].

## Decision rules

1. **If** you are an EU national finishing a master's within about 6 months and want an EU career, **then** apply to the Blue Book for the session that starts after you graduate, and avoid any other EU-body work over 42 days first (because that excludes you).
2. **If** you also apply to AD5 (no experience needed), **then** treat it as a free lottery ticket that needs only test preparation, and do not plan finances around it (because 1,490 places and 174,727 applicants gives about 0.85% by seats, and no recruitment guarantee).
3. **If** you are Italian and apply to EPSO, **then** prepare for an Italian-heavy field (45% of applicants) and use the reserve-list wait productively (because the reasoning tests alone started 8 months after the March 2026 deadline).
4. **If** you are Italian and want an international organisation before 31, **then** apply to the MAECI JPO programme (45 Italian places in 2026, deadline early May) rather than the UN YPP (because Italy is not an eligible nationality on the 2026 YPP list).
5. **If** you want the World Bank YPP, **then** plan it for 2-6 years after your master's, not now (because fresh graduates are ineligible).
6. **If** you want a central bank role in Italy, **then** check the 105/110 floor and the degree class before choosing a master's, and watch the bank's site year-round (because bandi have appeared at different times: the page URLs show November 2022, August 2024 and December 2025), and the Esperto profile requires the grade and the preselection takes the top 500 of about 8,300.
7. **If** you are a non-EU citizen, **then** drop the Blue Book, ECB, EPSO and Banca d'Italia profiles that require EU nationality, and look at OECD member-country routes only if your country is a member (because those programmes require specific nationalities).
8. **If** you are an EU graduate without UK settled status, **then** do not plan on the UK Fast Stream (because sponsorship is not available at its starting salary).
9. **If** you want an economics research job at the IMF or the ECB economist track, **then** a PhD is the entry route and a master's is not enough (because the IMF programme is aimed at recent PhDs and the ECB economist track requires one).
10. **If** you are considering a business PhD, **then** accept it only with full funding and compare the stipend with your realistic first-job net pay using section 5c (because stipends are about 60-100% of typical first-job net pay, and no source shows non-academic outcome pay).
11. **If** you want a US-style academic salary, **then** target a top school's PhD with US placement (because AACSB reports finance assistant professors at about USD 180,000 and EMEA new doctorates at a median of USD 77,664 from 23 hires).
12. **If** a PhD website says the stipend is "tax-free" or "competitive", **then** find the official scale (TV-L, UKRI, SNSF, CAO NU) and the contract percentage (because 65% contracts cut German gross from EUR 57,112 to EUR 37,123).
