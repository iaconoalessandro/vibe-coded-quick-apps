# Marketing careers for pre-experience master's graduates

## Bottom line

1. **FMCG brand management is the most competitive graduate market in the UK data, not the least.** UK ISE members in retail, FMCG and tourism received 290 applications per graduate vacancy in 2025, roughly double the all-sector record of 140 (ISE, Oct 2025, https://ise.org.uk/knowledge/insights/513/ise_top_10_stats_of_2025_you_need_to_know/) [data]. Treat a P&G, Unilever or L'Oréal offer as being as hard to get as a strategy-consulting offer, and apply broadly.
2. **The big FMCG graduate schemes do not require a marketing degree.** Unilever UK accepts "degree level qualifications across all disciplines" for its marketing track, P&G Germany asks for a "Bachelor's or Master's degree with strong academic results", and L'Oréal UK recruits "graduates from diverse academic backgrounds" [employer-stated, sources in §1]. A specialised MSc Marketing is optional for brand management. What gates entry is the assessment process, the local language and the graduation window.
3. **Local language is the binding constraint on the continent.** P&G's German graduate brand role requires "fluent English" plus "solid German" (P&G via iagora, June 2026) [employer-stated]. An Italian with English only is effectively limited to UK, Geneva/regional-HQ, Amsterdam/Rotterdam and Italian roles. Adding French or German at B2+ opens more FMCG doors than any choice of master's.
4. **Pay spread at entry is wide and mostly set by employer type, not degree.** Verified 2026 figures: P&G Germany graduate brand role €67,500 plus bonus [employer-stated]; Unilever UK UFLP £35,000 plus a 25% benefits envelope and bonus [employer-stated]; L'Oréal UK marketing trainee £35,000 (advert text read on a job-board mirror, 2 Oct 2026; careers.loreal.com page not readable); UK agency graduate trainees £20,000–£25,000 (Prospects, Jan 2026) [practitioner consensus]. In the UK, corporate brand schemes pay roughly 40–75% more at entry than agency graduate roles.
5. **AI and consolidation are shrinking the classic agency holding companies.** WPP's headcount fell from 111,268 to 104,083 in the year to June 2026 (−6.4%), and IPG cut 3,200 jobs before Omnicom completed its takeover on 26 November 2025 (City AM, Aug 2026; Wikipedia citing Adweek) [practitioner consensus]. Publicis, which is built around data (Epsilon, and LiveRamp from 2026), reported 114,000 staff in 2025 and kept expanding. The agency entry door is narrowing unevenly. Data-heavy agencies are the safer bet.
6. **Early-career exposure to AI is real, but it hits tasks that AI substitutes rather than whole functions.** US payroll data show a 19% relative employment decline for 22–25-year-olds in AI-exposed occupations, driven by fewer hires, and concentrated where AI substitutes for tasks rather than complements them (Brynjolfsson, Chandar & Chen, rev. Aug 2026) [data]. Junior copy, content production, reporting and media trafficking are the substitutable tasks. Brand P&L ownership, retail/customer negotiation and experiment design are not.
7. **Within the same school, a generalist management MSc beat marketing MSc outcomes.** At Warwick, the MSc Management reported 91% employed within six months at a $67,035 PPP-adjusted average salary, against 72% at about $60,000 for the MSc Marketing & Strategy (Warwick/QS, Apr 2026) [data]. This is one school and carries selection effects, but it runs against the idea that specialisation buys a marketing job.
8. **Luxury entry is concentrated in rotational programmes and in Paris/Milan headquarters, and it is heavily marketing-flavoured.** ESSEC's English-taught MSc Marketing Management & Digital places 41% of graduates in luxury, and LVMH's SPRING Future Leaders programme puts two of its three 10-month rotations in marketing/communications, retail/sales or operations [employer-stated / data]. Verified luxury salary data were not found; see Claims to verify.
9. **Brandstorm is a signal, not a hiring lottery.** L'Oréal says "nearly 500 participants" get their first professional role through Brandstorm each year, out of 240,000 participants in 2025 (loreal.com, 2025) [employer-stated]. That is about 0.2% of participants. It is worth doing if it forces you into a real consumer-insight project and a L'Oréal conversation, not as a primary route.

**What this means in practice**

- **The graduation window is tight and non-negotiable.** Unilever caps post-graduation experience at 24 months, L'Oréal UK at two years since graduating and LVMH at three years. P&G diverts anyone with 2+ years into a different hiring route. A pre-experience master's lands you in the sweet spot. A gap year of unrelated work after the master's uses up the window. Timing detail is in decisions/timing-and-sequencing.md.
- **P&G is the only major FMCG employer here that hires straight into a brand-owning role rather than a rotational scheme.** The German ad describes the entry job as a permanent Brand Management role with reassignments every 2–3 years. Unilever, L'Oréal and LVMH run rotational programmes (18–36 months). Rotations buy optionality. Direct hire buys ownership of a brand line earlier.
- **Internships are the real feeder on the continent.** P&G France and Italy recruit brand interns (Dijon internship at €1,600/month, snippet only), and Ferrero's ABM internships are "curricular" (they need a university agreement, so they must happen during the master's). An Italian student who does not secure a curricular FMCG internship during the master's is at a structural disadvantage for Ferrero-type roles. [employer-stated, snippet only]
- **Assessment is standardised and heavily screened.** Unilever UK runs four stages: an online application, a personality and cognitive "profile assessment", a digital interview with a business case (explicitly "reviewed by a real person"), and a full-day discovery centre (Unilever, 2026) [employer-stated]. LVMH runs a similar four-stage funnel. Practise game-based and cognitive tests before the autumn cycle opens.

**For non-EU readers:** UK schemes need a right to work in the UK (Graduate route or sponsorship). Unilever's Dutch marketing role explicitly required eligibility to work in the Netherlands before the start date (snippet only). Check places/visas-and-work-rights.md before targeting country-specific schemes.

## 2. How competitive is it?

**UK benchmark (the only sector-level ratio found).** The ISE 2025 Student Recruitment Survey, published 22 October 2025, reports [data]:

| Metric | 2025 value |
|---|---|
| Average applications per graduate vacancy, all sectors | 140 (highest since records began in 1991; 86 in 2022/23, 38 in 2002/03) |
| Retail, FMCG and tourism | **290 per graduate vacancy** (highest of any sector) |
| Graduate vacancies, year on year | −8% |
| Internship applications per place | 101 (+16% y/y) |
| Median graduate starting salary (all ISE members) | £33,000 |
| Employers redesigning recruitment in response to AI | 79% |

Source: https://ise.org.uk/knowledge/insights/498/5_trends_you_need_to_know_from_ises_recruitment_survey_2025/ and https://ise.org.uk/knowledge/insights/513/ise_top_10_stats_of_2025_you_need_to_know/.

Two caveats. First, "applications" are not "applicants". One candidate sends many applications, and AI tools have made mass applying cheaper, which inflates the ratio. Second, the category mixes FMCG with retail and tourism, so the FMCG-only figure is unknown. Even so, the direction is clear: consumer-facing brands attract far more applicants than finance or engineering employers.

**L'Oréal Brandstorm as a pipeline.**
- 2025 (33rd edition): 240,000 participants from 64 countries; "nearly 500 participants secure their first professional role" through it each year; the winning team gets a 3-month mission in Paris and permanent Management Trainee contracts after graduating (https://www.loreal.com/en/brandstorm/, 2025) [employer-stated].
- 2026: over 350,000 participants from more than 60 countries, with 45 global finalists in Paris (ProPakistani, Aug 2026, https://propakistani.pk/2026/08/14/this-lums-trio-just-claimed-the-global-stage-at-loreal-brandstorm-2026/) [anecdotal: secondary press repeating L'Oréal figures].
- **Implied conversion: about 500 / 240,000 ≈ 0.2% (2025); on 2026 participation, about 0.14%.** Non-obvious point: the value is not the win. Brandstorm gives you a structured consumer-insight and innovation case you can talk through in any FMCG interview, and a named L'Oréal contact at national-final stage. The finals are also where L'Oréal recruiters are physically present.

**Does the master's type matter for competitiveness?** Employers say no in writing (see §4). Recruiters decide through cognitive tests, a business case and, for continental roles, language. A marketing MSc mainly helps if it comes with (a) a curricular internship slot, (b) corporate projects with FMCG or luxury companies, or (c) a campus in Paris or Milan.

**Reading the table**

- **The agency discount is real and large in the UK.** An agency graduate trainee on £20,000–£25,000 earns 30–45% less than a Unilever or L'Oréal trainee on £35,000 before Unilever's 25% benefits envelope. - **P&G Germany is a top-of-market outlier.** €67,500 plus bonus for a graduate is far above the UK scheme level. The analytics premium shows up later and in specific employers (tech, retail media, pricing consulting), not in generic analyst jobs. See Hypothesis 4.

## 4. Does a specialised MSc Marketing help?

**Employer statements.** Unilever UK: degrees "across all disciplines" for marketing [employer-stated]. P&G Germany: "Bachelor's or Master's degree with strong academic results", no subject named [employer-stated]. L'Oréal UK: "graduates from diverse academic backgrounds" (advert text read on a mirror, round 3e). LVMH SPRING Product Leaders: a master's degree, no subject, no luxury or product experience needed (LVMH Inside page, 2025 cycle, round 3e) [employer-stated]; Future Leaders eligibility not stated on its page. **Exception:** Ferrero's curricular ABM internships ask for a master's in Marketing, Economics or Communication (snippet only). That is a field preference, but it is broad enough to include a MiM.

**Non-obvious reading.** Imperial's marketing cohort is more likely to go into tech (18%) or finance (16%) than into FMCG & retail (16%), and only 15% go into agencies and media. A top "marketing" master's in London is effectively a general business master's with a commercial tilt. At ESSEC, a marketing master's is a luxury feeder, which reflects the school's Paris network and its APAC campus more than the curriculum. In other words, **the school's location and employer network matter more than the programme title.**

**When the specialised degree does help:**
1. You have a non-business bachelor's (e.g. languages, engineering) and need marketing vocabulary and case practice. A MiM does the same job, though.
2. The programme has curricular-internship mechanics (important in Italy and France) or corporate projects with named FMCG or luxury partners.
3. You want luxury specifically and the programme sits in Paris or Milan with a luxury alumni base (see §7).

**When it hurts:** if you might switch to consulting, finance or general management. A MiM keeps those doors open at no cost to FMCG chances, because FMCG employers don't ask for the marketing title. See decisions/programme-choice.md.

## 5. AI's impact on entry-level marketing work

**Agency holding companies are shrinking or consolidating.**
- **WPP:** headcount fell from 111,268 to 104,083 in the twelve months to June 2026 (−6.4%), including 1,267 cuts in H1 2026. Revenue less pass-through costs fell nearly 5% to £4.7bn. A turnaround plan ("Elevate28") targets £500m of savings by the end of 2028 (City AM, 6 Aug 2026, https://www.cityam.com/wpp-slashes-jobs-as-revenue-continues-to-fall/) [practitioner consensus: specialist business press reporting company results]. The CEO change in 2025 was reported by the FT under the headline "WPP chief steps down as advertising group struggles with rise of AI" (9 Jun 2025, cited via Wikipedia) [practitioner consensus]. A former Microsoft executive, Cindy Rose, took over on 1 September 2025.
- **Omnicom–IPG:** announced December 2024, cleared by the FTC in June 2025 and completed on 26 November 2025, valued at about $13.5bn. IPG, with 53,300 staff in 2024, cut 3,200 jobs before the takeover (Adweek, 12 Nov 2025, cited via https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interpublic_Group_of_Companies and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omnicom_Group) [practitioner consensus; primary article not opened]. Mergers of this size usually remove duplicated junior layers first. Post-merger cut figures are in Claims to verify.
- **Publicis** is the counter-example: 114,000 staff and €17.39bn revenue in 2025, won Mars's advertising mandate in June 2025 and agreed to buy LiveRamp for $2.2bn in May 2026 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Publicis_Groupe, citing Adweek) [practitioner consensus]. Its growth sits in data (Epsilon since 2019) and technology services (Publicis Sapient).

**In-house FMCG is cutting office roles too, but more slowly.** P&G announced in June 2025 that it would cut 7,000 jobs over two years (CNN/Reuters, 5 Jun 2025, cited via https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Procter_%26_Gamble), and its headcount went from 108,000 (June 2024) to 104,000 (June 2026) according to its 10-K filings, as summarised on the same page [data, via secondary summary]. That is about −3.7% over two years, against WPP's −6.4% in one year. Unilever announced 7,500 job cuts in March 2024 alongside the ice-cream demerger, which completed in November 2025 as The Magnum Ice Cream Company (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unilever) [practitioner consensus, via secondary summary]. Both companies still ran 2026 graduate intakes with published pay (§1). Graduate schemes are the last thing large FMCG firms cut, because they feed the management pipeline.

**Early-career evidence.** Stanford Digital Economy Lab researchers, using ADP payroll data to June 2026, find a **19% relative employment decline for 22–25-year-olds in AI-exposed occupations**, mainly through reduced hiring. Declines concentrate "in occupations where AI usage primarily substitutes for human tasks", while complementary uses are flat or rising (https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/publications/canaries-in-the-coal-mine/, rev. 12 Aug 2026) [data, US only]. The authors call these descriptive early indicators, not causal estimates.

**Which marketing tasks are substitutable (inference from the above, not measured directly):**

| Entry task | Exposure | Where it sits |
|---|---|---|
| First-draft copy, social posts, product descriptions, image variants | High substitution | Agencies, content studios, e-commerce teams |
| Weekly performance reports, dashboard commentary | High substitution | Media agencies, digital teams |
| Campaign set-up and trafficking in ad platforms | High (platform automation plus AI) | Media agencies |
| Desk research, competitor summaries | High | Research agencies, strategy teams |
| Owning a brand P&L, negotiating with retailers, forecasting with sales | Low; AI complements | FMCG brand management |
| Designing experiments, building mix models, pricing analysis | Complementary; demand likely rising | In-house analytics, pricing consultancies, retail media |

**Recruiting is changing too.** 79% of UK ISE employers are redesigning selection because of AI, and 61% reported candidates using AI undisclosed in interviews (ISE, Oct 2025) [data]. Expect more in-person assessment centres and live cases. Unilever explicitly states its digital interviews are reviewed by a human.

## 6. Skill shift: from creative to quantitative

**What is verifiable:** Prospects' UK career profiles now list "data analytics" and Google Analytics 4 alongside creative tools for marketing executives and media planners, and they note that digital marketing roles "typically command higher salaries" (Prospects, Nov 2025 and Mar 2026) [practitioner consensus]. Imperial's marketing graduates' job titles include "Digital Analyst" and "Product Manager", and 18% of the cohort enter tech [data]. Simon-Kucher, the largest pricing consultancy (2,200+ staff in 30+ countries), screens graduates on "technical, problem-solving, and analytical skills, usually with case studies" (https://www.simon-kucher.com/en/careers) [employer-stated].

**What matters for a 2026–27 graduate** (my synthesis of the verified screening criteria above and the AI-substitution evidence in §5. The market claims behind it are in Claims to verify #22):
- **SQL and a spreadsheet model** are now baseline for analytics, CRM, retail media and e-commerce entry roles. They are also a differentiator in FMCG, where brand teams increasingly read sell-out and retail-media data themselves.
- **Marketing mix modelling (MMM) and incrementality testing** have moved from specialist agencies into in-house teams as privacy changes weakened user-level attribution. A graduate who can explain a geo-holdout test or read an MMM output has a rare, interview-proof skill.
- **Certifications:** the vendor certificates (Google Ads, Google Analytics, HubSpot, Salesforce Marketing Cloud) are free or cheap and are useful as proof of hands-on familiarity for digital, performance and CRM roles. No FMCG or luxury employer page reviewed lists them as a requirement. In the UK, the IPA Foundation Certificate is the agency-world signal (Prospects) and the MRS Foundation course is the research-world one [practitioner consensus]. Don't expect any of them to move a brand-management application.

## 7. Luxury specifics

- **Concentration.** Group headquarters and most Maison marketing teams sit in Paris (LVMH, Kering, Hermès, Chanel, Richemont's French Maisons) and Milan (Prada, Armani, and Gucci and Bottega Veneta design and marketing), with Geneva for Richemont and watchmaking. ESSEC's marketing master's sends 41% of graduates into luxury [data], and Imperial's 10% in luxury shows London is a secondary hub [data].
- **The entry door is often retail.** LVMH's SPRING Future Leaders programme lists "Retail Trainee Manager" and "Retail Customer Experience Coordinator" alongside Junior Product Manager as typical missions, and LVMH runs a separate China Retail Management Trainee programme [employer-stated]. Non-obvious: in luxury, store and client-experience roles are a respected route into merchandising and marketing, not a consolation prize. They are also the part of the business where rotational programmes put most graduates.
- **Languages.** LVMH SPRING asks for English at business fluency, with French "valuable" (snippet only). ESSEC's luxury-heavy programme requires no French [employer-stated]. In practice, local-language fluency matters more for headquarters roles in Paris and Milan and for retail roles in Europe. For an Italian reader, Italian is an asset in Milan and French is the upgrade.
- **Pay and internships.** No luxury salary figures could be verified in this session. In France, the legal floor for an internship is €4.50 an hour. It becomes mandatory once a stage runs longer than two consecutive months (or 309 hours) in an academic year (service-public.gouv.fr, verified 1 Jan 2026, https://www.service-public.gouv.fr/particuliers/vosdroits/F32131) [data]. At 35 hours a week, that is about €650–700 a month (my calculation). This floor applies to Paris luxury internships, which are often the realistic first step. What houses actually pay above it was not verified. Italian extracurricular *tirocinio* minimums are set by each region and were not verified (Claims to verify).
- **Inside LVMH** is a free 30-hour online certificate that LVMH says more than 125,000 learners have completed since 2021 (snippet only). Given that volume, it is not a differentiator.

## 9. Career progression in FMCG

**Verified:** P&G describes "new job assignments every 2–3 years" from graduate entry, with Geneva HQ as a destination (P&G, 2026) [employer-stated]. Unilever's UFLP lasts 2–3 years (36 months in France) and explicitly includes sales/go-to-market rotations and international exposure "to accelerate … readiness to take on business leadership roles" (snippet only). L'Oréal UK: 18 months with three rotations. LVMH: 30 months of rotations inside a 3-year programme.

**Promotion timelines** (ABM → BM → Marketing Director) could not be confirmed from a primary page. The commonly quoted pattern is in Claims to verify #20. The 2–3-year reassignment rhythm P&G states is the best verified proxy for how often a role change, and so a promotion opportunity, comes. The general-management track at P&G and Unilever runs through brand roles plus at least one sales/customer rotation and at least one international move.

**Mobility is a condition of the track, not an option.** The Unilever marketing ads ask for willingness "to travel for geographic mobility" [employer-stated, snippet only], and P&G's reassignment model assumes you move. For an Italian graduate, the most common high-growth path is: entry in Italy or at a regional hub, then Geneva, London or Amsterdam, then a country-lead role. Refusing moves in years 3–8 usually caps progression at Brand Manager level.

## Hypotheses tested

**H1. A generic MiM gets more FMCG brand offers than an MSc Marketing.**
- Verdict: **partly supported.**
- Holds when: the target is a large FMCG graduate scheme (P&G, Unilever, L'Oréal), where subject is not screened and cognitive and case tests decide.
- Fails when: a curricular internship is the main entry route and the employer names marketing, economics or communication (Ferrero, snippet only), or when the MiM school lacks FMCG recruiting ties that the marketing programme has.
- Evidence: subject-agnostic eligibility at Unilever, P&G and L'Oréal [employer-stated]. At Warwick, MSc Management had 91% employed at $67,035 PPP vs 72% at ~$60,000 for MSc Marketing & Strategy [data]. This is one school, with selection effects and sector mix unknown. No school reports FMCG offers split by programme.

**H2. Luxury marketing is the lowest pay-per-applicant career in business.**
- Verdict: **insufficient evidence.**
- Why: no verified luxury entry salary or applicant ratio was found. ESSEC's luxury-heavy cohort reports $74,000 on average, but that cohort works mainly in Asia and is not comparable to Paris or Milan headquarters pay.
- What would settle it: Paris/Milan luxury graduate salary data (school reports split by sector, e.g. HEC or ESSEC sector tables) and any applicant ratio for SPRING.

**H3. AI hits agency junior roles harder than in-house brand roles.**
- Verdict: **partly supported.**
- Holds when: comparing traditional holding companies with FMCG graduate schemes. WPP −6.4% headcount in a year and IPG −3,200 before merger, while Unilever, L'Oréal and P&G all ran 2026 graduate intakes with published pay [practitioner consensus; employer-stated]. The substitutable tasks (copy, reporting, trafficking) cluster in agencies [data: Stanford, by inference].
- Fails when: the agency is data- and technology-led (Publicis grew to 114,000 staff), or when FMCG companies cut their own office roles. P&G announced 7,000 cuts over two years in June 2025 and Unilever 7,500 in March 2024 (via Wikipedia summaries of CNN/Reuters and press coverage), although P&G's headcount fell only about 3.7% over two years, compared with WPP's 6.4% in one.
- Caveat: agency decline is also caused by client consolidation and in-housing, not only AI.

**H4. Marketing analytics skills produce a larger salary premium than a marketing master's.**
- Verdict: **partly supported, but only after entry.**
- Holds when: the comparison is marketing MSc vs general MSc at the same school (Warwick: the marketing title did not earn a premium) and for roles like pricing consulting and tech, which screen for analytics.
- Fails when: comparing generic entry analytics jobs (£23,000–£30,000 UK) against FMCG marketing schemes (£35,000+). At entry, the employer tier sets pay, not the skill.
- Evidence: Prospects UK pay bands [practitioner consensus]; Warwick/QS [data].

**H5 (own). For continental FMCG, language is the binding constraint, not degree.**
- Verdict: **supported.**
- Evidence: P&G Germany requires "solid German" alongside English [employer-stated]; Ferrero ABM internships require Italian and English (snippet only). UK schemes are English-only, which is why the UK sees such high applicant ratios.
- Fails when: the role is at a regional or global HQ that works in English (Geneva, Amsterdam, London).

**H6 (own). The UK corporate–agency entry pay gap is at least 40%.**
- Verdict: **supported for the UK.**
- Evidence: Unilever and L'Oréal £35,000 (Unilever plus a 25% benefits envelope) vs agency graduate trainee £20,000–£25,000 [employer-stated; practitioner consensus]. £35,000 is 40–75% above that agency range, and more once the envelope is included.
- Fails when: the comparison is with a top media agency's analytics track or a London tech firm (not verified).

**H7 (own). Brandstorm converts well enough to be a primary route into L'Oréal.**
- Verdict: **not supported.**
- Evidence: about 500 first roles a year out of 240,000 participants (≈0.2%) [employer-stated]. Only the winning team gets guaranteed Management Trainee contracts.
- Where it still helps: as interview material and a recruiter touchpoint at national finals.

**H8 (own). A top marketing MSc is mostly a marketing-industry feeder.**
- Verdict: **not supported for Imperial; supported for ESSEC's luxury pipeline.**
- Evidence: Imperial MSc Strategic Marketing: only 15% go into marketing/advertising/media/PR and 16% into FMCG & retail, while 34% go to tech or finance [data]. ESSEC: 41% luxury [data].

## Common mistakes and myths

1. **"FMCG is the easy option after a business master's."** In the UK, retail/FMCG/tourism attracts 290 applications per graduate vacancy, the most of any sector (ISE, 2025) [data]. Apply to 10+ programmes, not two.
2. **"I need an MSc Marketing to get into brand management."** None of the large-scheme eligibility statements reviewed names a subject [employer-stated]. A marketing MSc title does not show up as a premium in the one same-school comparison found [data].
3. **"I'll do a year of something else first, then apply."** Unilever caps experience at 24 months after graduation, L'Oréal at two years, LVMH at three, and P&G reroutes 2+ years to another track [employer-stated; partly snippet only]. Unrelated work after the master's eats the window.
4. **"English is enough in Europe."** It is enough for London and some HQ roles. It is not enough for P&G Germany (German required) or Italian Ferrero internships (Italian required) [employer-stated; snippet only].
5. **"Agency work is a good stepping stone at similar pay."** UK agency graduate pay is £20,000–£25,000 against £35,000 at corporate schemes [practitioner consensus], and holding-company headcount is falling (WPP −6.4% in a year) [practitioner consensus]. Agency work is still valid for media, data or creative careers, but not as a low-cost detour into brand management.
6. **"Certificates will get me a marketing job."** Google, HubSpot or Salesforce certificates help show hands-on familiarity for digital and CRM roles, but no FMCG or luxury page reviewed asks for them. The IPA and MRS certificates are the recognised UK sector signals for agencies and research [practitioner consensus].
7. **"Luxury means Paris HQ from day one."** LVMH's own programme puts graduates in retail and client-experience roles as a standard mission [employer-stated]. Store roles are a normal part of the path.
8. **"Brandstorm is my way in."** About 0.2% of participants get a first role through it [employer-stated]. Use it as preparation, not a plan.

## Decision rules

1. **If** you target FMCG brand management **and** your only fluent languages are Italian and English, **prefer** applying to UK schemes (Unilever, L'Oréal), English-speaking regional HQs (Geneva, Amsterdam) and Italian roles, **because** German- and French-market roles list the local language as a requirement.
2. **If** you are choosing between a MiM and an MSc Marketing at a similar school **and** you are not certain about marketing, **prefer** the MiM, **because** FMCG employers don't screen on subject and the MiM keeps consulting and finance options open.
3. **If** you are set on luxury **and** can choose a campus, **prefer** a programme in Paris or Milan with a documented luxury placement share (e.g. ESSEC's 41%), **because** luxury hiring concentrates in those HQs and programme networks.
4. **If** your target employer recruits through curricular internships (Ferrero, many Italian and French FMCG employers), **prefer** a master's whose calendar includes an internship slot with a university agreement, **because** curricular internships can only happen while you are enrolled.
5. **If** you finish the master's without an offer, **do not** take more than 12–18 months of unrelated work before reapplying, **because** Unilever caps experience at 24 months after graduation and P&G reroutes anyone with 2+ years.
6. **If** you are weighing an agency offer against a corporate scheme offer in the UK, **expect** the agency to pay 30–45% less at entry and have a lower headcount outlook; **prefer** the agency only if you want media, data or creative work, not brand management.
7. **If** you want to go into an agency, **prefer** data- or technology-led groups and media/analytics tracks over pure creative graduate roles, **because** Publicis-style data businesses kept growing while WPP and IPG shrank.
8. **If** you have 3–6 months before recruiting opens, **invest** in SQL, a spreadsheet P&L model and one experiment-design or MMM case you can explain, **because** substitutable junior tasks are declining and analytical cases are how pricing consultancies and in-house analytics teams screen.
9. **If** a luxury rotational programme offers a retail mission, **accept** it, **because** at LVMH retail and client experience are standard missions, not a demotion.
10. **If** you are non-EU, **check** the work-authorisation line in each ad before investing in the assessment funnel, **because** country schemes (e.g. Unilever NL) can require local work eligibility before the start date (see places/visas-and-work-rights.md).
11. **If** you enter Brandstorm, **treat** it as interview preparation and a recruiter touchpoint, **because** only about 0.2% of participants get a role through it.
12. **If** you want a general-management track at P&G or Unilever, **plan** for at least one sales/customer rotation and one international move within the first 5–8 years, **because** both employers build those into their programmes.
