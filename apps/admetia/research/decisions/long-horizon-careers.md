# Long-horizon careers, years 3-15

> **Not legal, tax or immigration advice.** This file summarises official sources as of the dates given. Rules change and individual cases differ: confirm with the competent authority or a qualified adviser before acting.

## Bottom line

1. **Nobody publishes cohort-level retention for banking, consulting or the Big 4, and the viral numbers have no primary source.** "52% of analysts exit in 24 months" and "85% exit before Director" could not be traced. The best traceable figures are a 2010-cohort London count (44% left the industry within three years, published 2013), and Deloitte UK's firm-wide voluntary attrition of 10.0% in FY25 (9.4% in FY24, 11.1% in FY23). H-a is therefore "insufficient evidence as stated", with a plausible direction.
2. **Attrition follows the market, not only the culture.** Deloitte UK entry hires fell from 2,767 (FY23) to 1,908 (FY25) and partner and director promotions from 501 to 341, while headcount fell about 5%. A first job is a bet on the lateral market you will meet at year 2 to 4, not a fixed ladder.
3. **The "95-hour" Goldman survey was 13 first-year analysts in one week of February 2021** (mean 98 hours since January, 105 that week, 5 hours of sleep). Banks have since set 80-hour guidelines with live-deal exemptions, but one survey cited in 2026 reports that bulge-bracket juniors still averaged 81 hours in 2025, the same as 2022.
4. **For non-EU graduates the settlement clock can matter more than the pay scale, and rules are in flux.** Germany grants settlement after 2 years (German degree) to 3 years, with the Blue Card at 21 to 27 months. The UK is at 5 years today but has a 10-year baseline proposal on the table with a pay-based discount (GBP 50,270 or GBP 125,140 for 3 years), a policy the Home Secretary told MPs on 15 September 2026 would be set out "later this year" (HC 662, Q56-Q57). The Netherlands is 5 years with a pending 10-year citizenship proposal. Switzerland is 10 years or 5 early. EU citizens in the EU are not affected.
5. **Exits are broad, not a PE funnel.** In a LinkedIn analysis of 1,644 ex-MBB staff, only 5.1% went to VC and PE; tech, financial services and "business consulting" each took 13% to 17%. At HBS 14% of the class of 2025 goes into PE, so the buy-side route mostly runs through the MBA.
6. **AI evidence so far is at the entry rung (age 22 to 25), not at the 5 to 15-year rung.** Stanford finds about 19% below trend for 22 to 25-year-olds in exposed occupations; PwC UK cut entry-level hiring from 1,500 to 1,300 and cited productivity and AI. No verified study yet shows effects on managers.
7. **Big Tech is not an up-or-out-free zone.** Meta's CEO wrote in January 2025 that it would "move out low-performers faster" (about 5% of staff). I found no verified source for "terminal level" stability, so H-b is not supported on current evidence.
8. **Childcare costs differ by country more than by job.** OECD net costs for a two-earner couple: below 10% of average earnings in Germany and Italy, about 35% in Switzerland. England's 2025 funded-hours expansion and the Dutch 2029 reform are changing the picture faster than the OECD figures.

## 1. Attrition and tenure

**What is actually published is thin, old, or sample-limited.** No bank, MBB firm or Big 4 firm publishes "% of a graduate intake still here at year 5". The numbers that circulate (for example "52% of IBD analysts exit within 24 months", "85% exit before Director") have no primary source that I could find. Treat them as unsourced.

**What this supports.** Voluntary attrition is cyclical. In 2021 to 2022 it was high, and by FY24 to FY25 Big 4 UK firms were running about 9 to 11% a year and then cutting graduate intakes (Deloitte hires fell 31% from FY23 to FY25). In a closed lateral market, people stay longer than the "2 years and out" folklore suggests. Equity partners are under 3% of Deloitte UK's people, and 341 partner and director promotions in FY25 was a firm-wide count, not a graduate-intake conversion rate.

**Hypothesis H-a ("most IB/consulting entrants exit within 5 years").** See the hypotheses section: insufficient evidence as a number, directionally plausible for exit from the *first firm*, but "exit" mostly means a move to the buy side or a corporate role, not leaving a career.

**Why the survivorship maths matters.** Under up-or-out, tenure is a policy outcome, not a preference. Of the employers I checked, only Deloitte UK publishes a voluntary attrition rate, and it covers all grades together.

## 2. Hours and wellbeing

### The Goldman 2021 survey, read in the primary document
The widely quoted "95 hours" comes from an internal slide deck made by first-year analysts in February 2021. I read the deck (Goldman Sachs analyst "Working Conditions Survey", Feb 2021, https://jbc.joshbersin.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/GS-Survey.pdf, a copy hosted by a third party) [anecdotal]:
- **Sample: 13 respondents**, self-selected, first-year analysts.
- Mean hours in the week ending 13 Feb 2021: 105. Mean since January: 98. Sleep about 5 hours a night. The deck headline says "over 95 hours".
- Mental health 8.8 before to 2.8 after (1 to 10 scale); physical health 9.0 to 2.3.
- 100% said hours hurt relationships with family and friends; 77% felt they had been victims of workplace abuse; 75% had sought or considered seeking counselling or therapy.
- The analysts asked for **80 hours a week as a maximum**, "pencils down" 12 hours before client meetings, and no non-urgent work after midnight.

It is a vivid signal about a busy SPAC-era quarter, not a measurement of the average analyst. The deck is not an official Goldman document, so Gemini's tag of "primary-official" and its claim that "100% had considered therapy" are both wrong (the figure is 75% sought or considered).

### What banks did afterwards
| Bank | Policy as published or reported | Source | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|
| JPMorgan | Junior bankers' hours limited to 80 a week "in most cases", live deals exempt; "pencils down" 6 pm Friday to noon Saturday; one full weekend off every three months; electronic tracking piloted March 2026 | Fortune, 12 Sep 2024, https://fortune.com/2024/09/12/jpmorgan-cap-junior-bankers-hours ; AOL/Business Insider, 3 Apr 2026, https://www.aol.com/articles/wall-street-tries-keep-junior-092201530.html | [practitioner consensus] |
| Bank of America | Tracking tool from 2024 flags hours above 80; protected weekends | same AOL article | [practitioner consensus] |
| Goldman Sachs | "Saturday rule": juniors not expected to work from Friday 9 pm to Sunday 9 am | same | [practitioner consensus] |
| Odyssey Search Partners survey data cited in the same article: bulge-bracket juniors averaged **81 hours a week in 2025, the same as 2022** | same | [practitioner consensus]; I could not open the Odyssey report itself |

Reading: caps exist and are a floor against the worst weeks, but the one published survey statistic shows no fall in average hours. The "80 hours" cap is itself about 2 times a normal 40-hour week.

### Rules that apply in Europe
- **UK**: 48 hours a week averaged over 17 weeks unless the worker opts out in writing; employers must keep records (GOV.UK, https://www.gov.uk/maximum-weekly-working-hours, no update date shown) [data]. Most banks and consultancies ask staff to sign the opt-out, so the law is a weak constraint in practice.
- **Germany**: 8 hours a day, extendable to 10 if the average over 6 months or 24 weeks stays at 8 (ArbZG section 3, https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/arbzg/__3.html) [data]. Stricter than the UK in form, but German banking and consulting use trust-based time and "leitende Angestellte" exemptions (not verified here).
- France: executives often work under the "forfait jours" annual-days regime; I did not verify the 218-day figure on a primary page, so it is in Claims to verify.

### Health evidence, handled with care
- **Long hours and cardiovascular disease**: WHO/ILO joint estimate, Environment International, 17 May 2021: working 55 or more hours a week is associated with a 35% higher risk of stroke and a 17% higher risk of dying from ischaemic heart disease than 35 to 40 hours; 745,000 deaths in 2016 attributed to it (https://www.who.int/news/item/17-05-2021-long-working-hours-increasing-deaths-from-heart-disease-and-stroke-who-ilo) [data]. These are population averages across all workers, not estimates for bankers aged 23.
- **Burnout in a French financial company**, 1,300 workers: burnout 10.2% (1.2% severe), insomnia 16.8%, 97% non-restorative sleep; burnout was about 15 times more likely with insomnia (OR 14.7, 95% CI 9.8 to 21.9); cross-sectional, self-reported sleep (BMJ Open 2017;7:e012816, https://bmjopen.bmj.com/content/7/1/e012816) [data]. It is not a junior-banker sample, so it shows the sleep-burnout link, not the prevalence among analysts.
- Trade surveys of "39% of younger finance professionals took time off for stress" (AccountsIQ, vendor survey) are marketing research with no method I could read, so I do not use the figure [anecdotal].
- Care point: none of this shows that any given job will harm any given person. It shows (a) the worst-hours weeks are documented at 95 to 105 hours in one survey, (b) sleep loss is the strongest measured burnout correlate, and (c) the employers that changed policy did so after a death (BofA, 2024, per press) and a leaked deck.

**Firm-level promotion volumes that are published (Deloitte UK):** 7,631 promotions across the business in FY25 (8,200 in FY24; 7,377 in FY23), against 341 partner and director promotions in FY25 (485 in FY24; 501 in FY23). In a firm of about 27,000 average employees, roughly 28% of staff are promoted each year, and about 1.3% of staff are promoted to partner or director grade. These are annual flows, not a cohort conversion rate, and partner and director are counted together (Deloitte UK, 2025, https://www.deloitte.com/content/dam/assets-zone2/uk/en/docs/about/2025/deloitte-uk-annual-review-2025-business-and-esg-performance-metrics.pdf) [employer-stated]. The 341 figure fell 32% between FY23 and FY25 while headcount fell about 5%, which is evidence that the top of the ladder narrows faster than the base in a weak market.

**Partner economics, as published.** Average profit per equity partner: Deloitte UK GBP 1.051m in FY25 (FY24 GBP 1.012m, FY23 GBP 1.06m; same Deloitte source) [employer-stated]; PwC UK GBP 865,000 in FY25 (GBP 862,000 in FY24; peak GBP 920,000 in FY22) (Scottish Financial News, 17 Sep 2025) [practitioner consensus]. Average effective tax and NIC for Deloitte members is 49% (Deloitte source). The FY26 figures that Gemini quoted (Deloitte GBP 1.125m, PwC GBP 935k) could not be found: Deloitte's FY26 results were scheduled for 30 Sep 2026 and were not indexed when I searched, so they are not used.

**Goldman as a bank-side marker.** Nearly 45% of Goldman partners were campus hires, and the average employee tenure is about 6 years while the Management Committee averages about 23 years (Goldman Sachs 10-K FY2025) [data]. A campus hire can reach partner, but the tenure gap shows the senior group is a long-tenure minority.

**Plateau versus up-or-out.** Corporate and tech ladders are mostly "up-or-stay" and consulting and banking ladders are "up-or-out". Evidence for Big Tech terminal levels is tested under H-b below. For pay compression: in up-or-out firms pay per hour is highest at partner level only if you get there; for the typical leaver the main economic gain is the first-exit step-up, not the climb.

**What is solid.** (1) Ex-MBB moves are spread widely: tech (about a quarter), financial services (12 to 14%) and business consulting and services (about 17%) lead, and VC and PE is only about 5% of moves, much smaller than prep sites imply. (2) An MBA class is a big exit channel: at HBS, finance plus consulting is 55% of seekers, and 14% of the class goes to PE, which is the main post-analyst step for former bankers (the pre-experience route to this is covered in decisions/mba-and-career-switchers.md). (3) The ex-MBB data skew towards seniority: close to half hold VP or director titles, but this is a selected group with a public LinkedIn profile.

**What is not available.** A published, representative flow from "IB analyst class of year X" to destinations at 2, 4 and 7 years. Gemini's exit matrix with shares like "25% to 30% of target-school analysts go to PE" and "20% of consultants pursue sponsored MBAs" has no source I could trace. I treat the direction (buy side, corporate development, tech strategy, MBA) as practitioner consensus and the shares as unknown.

**Option value in practice.** The 2013 and 2025 to 2026 datasets agree on one thing: exits are lateral moves into roles with a wider employer base, not a retreat. A first job in consulting or banking buys breadth of exits (tech, finance, founder, corporate) rather than a particular next job. This argues for choosing a first job by what it teaches and who you meet, since the "exit to PE" funnel is narrow.

## 5. AI and long-run career risk

**Verified in the library:** Stanford's "Canaries" work finds 22 to 25-year-olds in AI-exposed occupations about 19% below trend (revised August 2026); the original paper reported a 13% relative decline in employment for those workers after late 2022, with older workers and less-exposed jobs stable (Brynjolfsson, Chandar and Chen, Stanford Digital Economy Lab, 2025, https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/news/2025-year-in-review) [data]. GMAC's Corporate Recruiters Survey 2026: one in three employers is replacing some entry-level roles with AI [data].

**Firm statements.**
- PwC UK's chief said the firm was "hiring 200 fewer entry-level talent this year (1,300 versus 1,500)" and that AI is "reshaping roles", while naming struggling productivity as the biggest cause (Fortune, 8 Sep 2025, https://fortune.com/2025/09/08/pwc-uk-chief-cutting-entry-level-junior-gen-z-jobs-ai-economic-headwinds-like-amazon-salesforce) [employer-stated, on the record, via press].
- Deloitte UK entry-level hires fell from 2,767 in FY23 to 1,908 in FY25 and PwC UK headcount fell from about 36,000 to about 33,700 in a year (see section 1). These show shrinking pyramids, but cyclical demand rather than AI is the stated cause in the PwC quote above [employer-stated].
- Search results also report that McKinsey targets a 10% cut in non-client-facing headcount and that Big 4 graduate postings fell 44% in 2025; I could not open those sources, so they are in Claims to verify.

**What the evidence says about mid-level careers.** The measured effect so far is at the entry rung (ages 22 to 25), not at 5 to 15 years. Nothing I found measures effects on managers or VPs. The structural argument is that firms whose profit model rests on a wide base of junior hours (banking analysts, audit associates, consulting analysts) may promote from a smaller base, which would make the later ladder steeper. This is an inference, not a finding. Roles that rely on client relationships, sector judgement or accountability for decisions are less exposed on the evidence available, but I have no verified study that says so.

**Practical reading for a first-job choice.** The risk is concentrated on the entry route you are now choosing, so the decision is between jobs that train judgement early (smaller teams, client contact, ownership) and those that train throughput on tasks tools now do (formatting, first-draft modelling, routine testing). That is a claim about training content, which you can check in an interview by asking what a second-year in the role does that a tool cannot.

## 6. Settlement and citizenship

**Who this matters for.** EU/EEA citizens (most Admetia readers) have free movement inside the EU and need none of this in Germany, France, NL, Italy or Spain. It matters for (a) **non-EU readers anywhere**, (b) **EU citizens going to the UK**, where post-Brexit arrivals face rules close to those for third-country nationals (see places/visas-and-work-rights.md for entry rules; Switzerland has a separate free-movement agreement for EU citizens, not covered here). The rules below start from the case of a graduate who arrives on a student visa.

### UK: the settlement clock is being rewritten, and the rewrite is unfinished
- **Rule in force today (checked 1 Oct 2026):** the standard Skilled Worker route to indefinite leave to remain (ILR) is 5 years ("After 5 years, you may be able to apply to settle", GOV.UK Skilled Worker visa page, https://www.gov.uk/skilled-worker-visa/settle-in-the-uk, no date shown) [data]. **Student and Graduate-route time does not count**: the Home Office's own consultation paper says time on the Graduate route does not count towards five-year settlement, though study and Graduate time do count towards the separate 10-year long-residence route (A Fairer Pathway to Settlement, Home Office Command Paper, consultation 20 Nov 2025 to 12 Feb 2026, https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/691edda450b16caf978153d8/Command_Paper_final_-_reviewed7.pdf) [data]. So the clock starts at the Skilled Worker visa, not at the master's.
- **180-day absence rule:** an applicant "must not have been outside the UK for more than 180 days in any 12-month period" (Immigration Rules, Appendix Continuous Residence CR 3.1; page last updated 3 Aug 2026, https://www.gov.uk/guidance/immigration-rules/immigration-rules-appendix-continuous-residence) [data]. Exceptions include compelling and compassionate circumstances such as life-threatening illness of a close family member, and travel disruption from disasters or pandemics. Work travel is not an exception for Skilled Workers. A consultant on a long overseas assignment can fail it.
- **The reform, status as of 30 Sep 2026:** the government's "earned settlement" model would make **10 years the baseline** and let people shorten it. The consultation paper proposes: income of at least GBP 50,270 for the 3 years before applying cuts 5 years (so 5 years); at least GBP 125,140 cuts 7 years (so 3 years); English at C1 cuts 1 year; public-service work for 5 years cuts 5 years; volunteering cuts 3 to 5 years; Global Talent and Innovator Founder keep roughly 3 years; sponsored roles skilled below RQF level 6 could face 15 years; receipt of public funds adds 5 to 10 years; required English rises to B2; routes that "currently lead to settlement after 5 years" including Skilled Worker "will default to a qualifying period of 10 years" unless a reduction applies; and the changes would apply to people already in the country who have not yet received ILR [data: the Command Paper is a consultation, not law].
- **What is not decided (read on primary documents, 2 Oct 2026):** the Home Secretary's oral evidence to the Home Affairs Committee on 15 Sep 2026 (HC 662, https://committees.parliament.uk/oralevidence/18096/html/, Q55-Q59) says "Later this year we will set out the new policy on settlement", that decisions will come "in good time before you get the first round of people qualifying who arrived in 2022" (who, absent change, "would qualify in February 2027"), that "the rules that apply to you are the rules in force at the point of your application, not when you arrived in the country", and that transitional protection is "policy to be settled", ranging "from passporting certain cohorts through - so no change, and they still qualify at five years - to different options for what you might do with other cohorts over a longer period" [data]. The consultation closed on 12 Feb 2026 and GOV.UK lists no government response as of 2 Oct 2026 (https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/earned-settlement) [data]. The autumn Statement of Changes HC 584 (3 Sep 2026) contains no earned-settlement provisions (accessible text searched; changes take effect 8 Oct, 29 Oct and 30 Nov 2026) (https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/statement-of-changes-to-the-immigration-rules-hc-584-3-september-2026) [data]. A report of English rising from B1 to B2 on 26 March 2027 (law-firm summary) is not in HC 584 and I found it in no primary text; the B2 rise that is in a primary text is for Skilled Worker, HPI and Scale-up initial grants from 8 Jan 2026 (HC 1333) [data]. The consultation's "more than 200,000 responses" figure is from the law-firm report only.
- **What it means for a graduate:** for someone who starts a Skilled Worker job at about GBP 45,000 to 50,000 in 2027, a 10-year path is the planning case unless pay passes the GBP 50,270 line for three years. A London finance or consulting job passes that line quickly (see money/salaries-and-roi.md); a corporate rotational programme or a Big 4 audit grade may not for several years. Pay, not the employer, is the lever in the proposal.

### Germany: fast and rule-based for degree holders
- **Permanent settlement (section 18c AufenthG):** a skilled worker gets a settlement permit after **3 years** of holding a skilled-worker-type permit, **shortened to 2 years if they completed a German degree or training**, with 24 months (36 months otherwise) of pension contributions and "sufficient" German. **Blue Card holders: 27 months with basic German, 21 months with sufficient (B1-level) German** (https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/aufenthg_2004/__18c.html, read 1 Oct 2026) [data]. Gemini cited section 18g for this: the 21-month rule is in section 18c(2).
- **Citizenship (section 10 StAG):** 5 years' lawful habitual residence and B1 German. The 3-year "turbo" route was **repealed**: the Bundestag voted on 8 Oct 2025 and the Bundesrat on 17 Oct 2025 (https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stag/__10.html shows subsection 3 as "weggefallen"; vote dates from Fragomen/EIG Law alerts, https://www.fragomen.com/insights/germany-fast-track-naturalization-pathway-abolished.html, https://eiglaw.com/germany-ends-fast-track-citizenship-option-reinstates-five-year-residency-requirement/) [data for the statute; practitioner consensus for dates]. Gemini's "3-year citizenship" was out of date. Dual citizenship is allowed since the 2024 reform (same sources).
- Whether time as a student counts towards the 5 years of "lawful residence" was not confirmed on a page I could open (Claims to verify).

### Netherlands: 5 years, low language bar, a pending 10-year proposal
- **Permanent residence permit:** a valid residence permit for **at least 5 consecutive years**, civic integration exam at **A2**, fee EUR 254 (IND, last update 20 May 2026, https://ind.nl/en/replace-extend-renew-and-change/permanent-residency/permanent-residence-permit) [data]. A law firm's guide (Law & More, 31 Aug 2026) says study permits are generally treated as temporary and do not count at application [practitioner consensus]; confirm on the IND exceptions page.
- **Citizenship:** 5 years still applies. The outgoing cabinet approved on 30 Sep 2025 (consulted to December 2025) a draft to double it to 10 years; no bill was enacted as of the Law & More piece (https://www.iamexpat.nl/expat-info/dutch-news/cabinet-pushes-ahead-plan-double-residence-requirement-dutch-citizenship ; https://lawandmore.eu/naturalisation-netherlands/) [practitioner consensus].

### Switzerland: slowest, with conditions
- Cantonal migration offices describe the C permit as **10 years** of residence (with the last 5 on a residence permit), or **5 years early** with proven integration (A2 oral / B1 on the fast track); education years are counted only if followed by two years of continuous residence, and temporary stays such as students are not counted in the last 5 years (Canton Solothurn and Canton Lucerne guidance; Canton Zurich page https://www.zh.ch/de/migration-integration/niederlassungsbewilligung.html, found by search, summarised secondhand) [practitioner consensus]. The federal quota on third-country permits (8,500 in 2026, verified in the library) sits on top of this. A non-EU graduate faces a permit lottery first and a long clock second.

### France: B2 and a 2-year shortcut for French degree holders
- **Naturalisation by decree:** 5 years' residence, **reduced to 2 years if you completed at least two years of higher education in France**; French language level raised to **B2** in 2026 (previously B1); cost EUR 255 (service-public.gouv.fr F2213, verified 16 July 2026, https://www.service-public.gouv.fr/particuliers/vosdroits/F2213) [data]. For a non-EU HEC, ESSEC or ESCP graduate, this is the shortest citizenship route in the sample, but **two years of study plus the language bar** and a stable job and income are required.
- **Residence permits:** the 10-year "carte de résident" page (verified 1 July 2026) is built around family-link and humanitarian categories and demands French at B1 (https://www.service-public.gouv.fr/particuliers/vosdroits/F2208) [data]; I did not confirm the multi-year card to resident-card steps for worker routes, so I give no numbers for them.

### Italy and Spain
- **Spain** (Civil Code article 22, BOE consolidated text, read 1 Oct 2026, https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1889-4763): citizenship by residence requires **10 years** of legal, continuous residence; 5 for refugees; **2 years for nationals of Ibero-American countries, Andorra, the Philippines, Equatorial Guinea or Portugal, and Sephardic Jews**; "good civic conduct and sufficient degree of integration" [data]. For Latin American graduates at IE, Esade or IESE, the 2-year route makes Spain unusually attractive.
- **Italy:** a legal-news search result states 10 years for non-EU residents and 4 for EU citizens under Law 91/1992 article 9, with B1 language; Normattiva's article 9 text did not load for me, so this is **(unverified, snippet only)** (Claims to verify).

## 7. Childcare and dual-career feasibility

**Scope note.** I could not find a London-specific or city-by-city current fee table from a government source, and the Gemini figure of "GBP 1,500 to 2,200 a month in London" has no traceable source. The comparison below uses the OECD model and each country's own policy page. Treat city-level fees as a gap.

**OECD model (couple, two children aged 2 and 3, both in full-time centre care, earnings at 100% and 67% of the average wage, 2021 data except Switzerland 2020):** gross fees are 1% of average earnings in Germany, about 60% in the Netherlands and 64% in Switzerland; net of subsidies, the cost to a two-earner couple is below 10% of average earnings in Germany, Italy, Spain and Portugal (grouped with other high-subsidy countries), at least 18% in the English-speaking countries, Czechia, the Netherlands and Switzerland, and about 35% in Switzerland (OECD Family Database PF3.4, April 2022, https://webfs.oecd.org/els-com/Family_Database/PF3-4-Childcare-support.pdf) [data]. This is **stale** for three of the countries below, because the UK (2025), the Netherlands (2026 to 2029) and Switzerland (2027) have changed policy since.

**Reading for dual-career feasibility.** (1) Childcare is not the main cost difference *between jobs*; it is a cost difference *between cities and countries*, and in 2026 that gap is moving: England and the Netherlands are subsidising more each year, Switzerland remains costly, Germany, France and Italy are the cheapest on the OECD model. (2) For couples both on finance or consulting salaries, the fee is smaller than the hours problem: 80-hour weeks in the first jobs and 55-plus-hour weeks later (section 2) are what make two such careers hard to combine, and no childcare subsidy fixes that. (3) The 2025 to 2026 UK expansion makes London more feasible than the pre-2025 OECD figure suggests, but only for children from 9 months and only for eligible working parents.

## Hypotheses tested

**H-a. "Most IB and consulting entrants exit within 5 years" (assigned).** Verdict: **insufficient evidence as a number; directionally plausible for leaving the first firm.** Holds when: you count any move (buy side, corporate, MBA) as an exit; tenure at the elite firms is short by design (up-or-out at McKinsey: about 2.5 years per manager-level promotion in the March 2024 US memo). Fails when: you ask how many leave the *career* (ex-MBB data show many stay in strategy, tech or finance roles) or measure in a closed lateral market (Deloitte UK voluntary attrition 10.0% in FY25, with PwC UK cutting roles after attrition fell). Evidence: PER 2013 (44% left banking within 3 years, n = 250, 2010 cohort, stale) [practitioner consensus]; Deloitte UK FY25 [employer-stated]; Goldman 10-K (average tenure about 6 years across all employees) [data]. No cohort-level source exists for 5-year exits.

**H-b. "Big Tech terminal-level plateaus offer more stable long-run careers than MBB up-or-out" (assigned).** Verdict: **not supported on verified evidence (insufficient for the terminal-level claim).** The claim has two parts. I found no primary or reputable source on terminal levels (Google L5, Meta E5) and Gemini's table is unsourced. On the opposing part, Meta's CEO wrote that the firm would "raise the bar on performance management and move out low-performers faster", cutting roughly 5% of staff (internal memo, reported by Bloomberg, 14 Jan 2025, https://bnnbloomberg.ca/business/company-news/2025/01/14/meta-to-cut-5-of-staff-with-eye-on-lowest-performers-memo-says) [practitioner consensus]. Holds when: you are an engineer or product person with pay above corporate scales, and the firm is growing. Fails when: the firm runs performance cuts, and for business-school graduates who typically enter strategy, operations or finance roles in tech, which are not the engineering ladder. See careers/tech-data-and-ai.md and careers/tech-business-and-startups.md.

**H-c. "Settlement rules, not salary, decide where migrants build long-term careers" (assigned).** Verdict: **partly supported, and conditional on nationality and pay level.** For non-EU graduates the clock differs a lot (Germany 2 to 3 years, Netherlands 5, UK 5 now and possibly 10, Switzerland 10 or 5 early), and in the UK the proposal itself ties the clock to pay (GBP 50,270 for 3 years cuts the baseline from 10 to 5; GBP 125,140 cuts it to 3), so salary and settlement are coupled rather than rivals. Holds when: you are non-EU, expect to stay beyond 5 years and your pay would sit below the UK thresholds. Fails when: you are EU (free movement inside the EU), or your pay is high enough to buy the short route, or you plan to leave in 3 to 5 years. Evidence: Home Office Command Paper, Nov 2025 [data]; AufenthG section 18c [data]; IND 2026 [data]; Breytenbachs 30 Sep 2026 on timing [practitioner consensus].

**H-d (mine). "Tenure at big employers is set by the lateral market, so the first job should be chosen for how portable its experience is when the market closes."** Verdict: **supported (moderately).** Deloitte UK voluntary attrition ran 11.1% (FY23), 9.4% (FY24), 10.0% (FY25) with 4,109 experienced hires in FY23 and about 1,250 in FY24 and FY25; PwC UK cut roles after attrition dropped. Holds when: demand for lateral hires is weak (2024 to 2026). Fails when: the lateral market is strong (2021 to 2022 attrition was higher). Single employer for the hard numbers [employer-stated].

**H-e (mine). "Bank hour caps reduced the hours junior bankers work."** Verdict: **not supported on current evidence (single-source).** JPMorgan's 80-hour guideline came in September 2024 with live deals exempt; Odyssey Search Partners data cited in April 2026 puts 2025 bulge-bracket juniors at 81 hours, the same as 2022 (AOL/Business Insider, 3 Apr 2026) [practitioner consensus]. Holds when: caps are tracked electronically (JPMorgan piloted a tool in March 2026, so that may change). I could not open the Odyssey report.

**H-f (mine). "For a non-EU graduate, the clock shortened by a local degree can outweigh school prestige elsewhere."** Verdict: **partly supported, and untested on outcomes.** Germany shortens section 18c settlement to 2 years after a German degree; France cuts naturalisation from 5 to 2 years after two years of French higher education but raises French to B2; the UK counts neither the student nor the Graduate route towards the 5-year clock. Holds when: you want to stay long term, will work in the local language and expect to meet the job requirement. Fails when: your target employers sit elsewhere; the rules are about status, not job access (see places/visas-and-work-rights.md and money/salaries-and-roi.md).

**H-g (mine). "Childcare fees, not pay, separate dual-career feasibility across countries."** Verdict: **partly supported.** OECD net childcare for a two-earner couple: below 10% of average earnings in Germany and Italy against about 35% in Switzerland (2020 to 2021 data); but pay in finance and consulting is high enough that hours matter at least as much as fees (section 2). Stale for the UK, Netherlands and Switzerland, whose policies changed after the OECD year.

## Common mistakes and myths

- **"Half the analyst class is gone in two years, so analyst roles are disposable."** The traceable numbers are a 2010 London cohort (44% left banking in three years) and small-sample profile checks; recent firm-level attrition is about 10% a year in the Big 4 [practitioner consensus; employer-stated].
- **"95 hours is the standard analyst week."** It is a mean from 13 self-selected analysts in 2021; 81 hours is the 2025 figure cited from a recruiter survey [anecdotal; practitioner consensus].
- **"Time on the UK Graduate visa counts towards ILR."** It does not count towards the 5-year route (Home Office Command Paper, 2025) [data].
- **"Germany still has a 3-year citizenship fast track."** It was repealed in October 2025; citizenship is 5 years (section 10 StAG) [data].
- **"Settlement is a formality once you have a job."** The UK rules are being rewritten, applicants are assessed under the rules at the time of application, and transitional protection is undecided [practitioner consensus].
- **"Consulting leads to PE."** In the LinkedIn data, 5.1% of ex-MBB moves went to VC or PE [anecdotal].
- **"Equity partner is the default goal."** Deloitte UK has 751 equity partner FTE in about 27,000 people [employer-stated].
- **"Childcare is a pay problem."** It is a country-policy problem and the policies are moving [data].

## Decision rules

1. **If you are non-EU, want to settle, and the UK is your target:** plan on a 10-year settlement horizon unless you expect to earn above GBP 50,270 for three years in a row (5 years) or above GBP 125,140 (3 years); re-check the Home Office announcement due "later this year" and before the first 2022 arrivals qualify in February 2027 (because the reform is a proposal and the transitional rules are undecided).
2. **If you are non-EU and want the shortest legal route to permanent status in Europe:** compare Germany (2 years after a German degree, B1, Blue Card 21 months) with the Netherlands (5 years, A2) before choosing a country by salary (because the clock, not the salary, differs most).
3. **If you plan to take overseas assignments as a UK Skilled Worker:** keep absences under 180 days in any rolling 12 months, because the rule bites on travel-heavy roles such as consulting.
4. **If you are weighing a junior banking or consulting offer:** ask the team for last year's average and peak weekly hours and whether the bank's cap is tracked, and compare with the 80-hour guideline (because caps exist but one survey shows no fall in average hours).
5. **If you want the buy side after banking or consulting:** assume the route is an MBA or a specific recruiting round, not a default; 14% of HBS's class of 2025 went to PE while only about 5% of ex-MBB moves reached VC or PE (because the funnel is narrow).
6. **If you prefer option value to a specific next job:** prefer first employers whose alumni land across tech, finance, corporate strategy and founding roles, as MBB alumni do in the 2025 to 2026 LinkedIn data (because breadth of exits is the measurable benefit).
7. **If you are considering Big 4 audit or advisory:** treat years 1 to 3 as training and qualification, and expect the real filter at manager to senior-manager level, with fewer partner promotions in weak markets (341 in FY25 against 501 in FY23 at Deloitte UK).
8. **If the first job is in a function exposed to AI-automatable tasks (formatting, first-draft analysis, routine testing):** ask what a second-year does that a tool cannot, and choose the role that gives client contact or decision ownership (because the measured AI effect is concentrated at ages 22 to 25).
9. **If you and a partner both work in finance or consulting and want children within 5 to 8 years:** model hours first, then childcare; in England use the 30-hour entitlement, in Switzerland budget for the highest net costs in the OECD sample, in the Netherlands expect the benefit rate to rise to 96% by 2029 (because policy changes are already scheduled).
10. **If a source quotes a retention or exit percentage without a cohort, a year and a sample size:** do not use it in a decision tool (because none of the viral numbers has a traceable primary source).
11. **If you are an EU citizen studying inside the EU:** ignore settlement rules for intra-EU moves and focus on pay, hours and exits, but still model the UK as a third-country-style system (because free movement does not apply there).
