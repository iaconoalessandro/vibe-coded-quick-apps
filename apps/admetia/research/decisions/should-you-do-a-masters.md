# Should you do a master's at all?

## Bottom line

1. **The value of a master's depends mostly on your country's degree norm and your target career, not on the master's itself.**
   - **On the Continent** (Italy, France, Germany, Austria, Switzerland, Spain), a master's is the *default entry credential* for graduate business roles. Italian graduate schemes and Bocconi/CLEAM-type paths assume a laurea magistrale or equivalent. French cadre-level hiring is built around the Bac+5 grandes écoles. McKinsey Germany starts master's graduates a level higher than bachelor's graduates (Fellow vs Junior Fellow).
   - **In the UK and the US**, graduate schemes are designed for bachelor's graduates, and a master's often doesn't change the entry grade.

   Sources: careers/consulting.md; places/italy-playbook.md; decisions/school-types-and-accreditation.md.
2. **Where entry grade and pay are the same with or without a master's, it buys three things: access, timing and visa.** In UK/US MBB, undergraduates and master's graduates share one pay band ($112k US base). UK Big 4 audit hires master's graduates at the same trainee grade as bachelor's graduates and, increasingly, school leavers. What the master's buys there is:
   - **access** to a recruiting school and its pipelines;
   - **a second recruiting cycle**;
   - for non-UK students, **a UK student visa and then a Graduate visa**.

   If you already have all three, the master's case is weak (careers/consulting.md; careers/accounting-and-corporate.md).
3. **The labour market is shifting towards non-degree and earlier entry at the bottom of the professional-services pyramid.**
   - UK graduate vacancies fell 8% while apprentice vacancies rose 8% in 2024/25. Employers hired 1.8 graduates per apprentice, down from 2.3 in 2024, with 1.6 forecast (ISE, 8 Dec 2025) [data].
   - ICAEW's survey of 35 mid-tier firms expects a 40% fall in graduate trainee hiring and a 49% rise in school leavers (ICAEW, May 2026, small sample) [data].
4. **Much of the measured "master's premium" is selection.** Strong students choose and get into strong programmes. The best-known test found that students attending more selective colleges did not earn more than equally able students admitted to comparable schools who went elsewhere (Dale & Krueger, NBER w7322) [data]. Treat any school's "graduates earn X% more" claim as an upper bound (evidence/how-numbers-mislead.md C1).
5. **The most common good reasons to do a master's:**
   - you are on the Continent and your target employers expect one;
   - you need to **change field** (e.g. a humanities graduate into finance) or **change country** (study where you want to work);
   - you need **quant credentials** for finance or quant roles;
   - you want a **deferred-MBA option** (HBS 2+2, Stanford, Wharton Moelis), which needs a direct transition with no full-time work in between.
6. **The most common bad reasons:**
   - "the job market is bad, so I'll wait it out";
   - "a second master's will make me more employable";
   - "a specialised MSc is required for that job";
   - "prestige pays for any debt".

   The evidence on each is in §3.
7. **Working first is not a free option either.** Pre-experience windows close on *graduation date*: LBS MiM within 2 years; Unilever within 24 months; L'Oréal UK within 2 years; LVMH within 3 years. Any full-time job between degrees ends deferred-MBA eligibility (decisions/timing-and-sequencing.md; careers/marketing.md).

## 1. Country norms: is a master's the entry credential where you'll work?

| Where you'll work | Norm for graduate business roles | What it means | Source file |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Italy** | Laurea magistrale (LM-77/LM-56/LM-16) is the standard; a triennale is seen as incomplete for many graduate roles | Do the LM or a foreign equivalent. A "Master di I livello" isn't a degree (DM 270/2004 art. 3 c. 9) | places/italy-playbook.md |
| **France** | Bac+5 (Programme Grande École / master's) for cadre roles; the end-of-studies internship is the hiring channel (42.3% of grande-école hires via internship or apprenticeship host) | A master's plus a final *stage* is how you enter | getting-in/employer-pipelines.md, getting-in/recruiting-calendar.md |
| **Germany / Austria** | Master's common for corporate and consulting; dual-study bachelor's is a strong alternative for industry (≈70% of DHBW graduates taken on by their partner) | Master's raises the MBB entry level; dual study can beat both for industry | careers/consulting.md, getting-in/employer-pipelines.md |
| **Switzerland** | Pay follows location more than degree: applied-sciences bachelor's graduates earn CHF 80,300 vs CHF 80,900 for university master's graduates, one year out (BFS 2024) | For Swiss pay, *where you work* matters more than an extra degree | money/salaries-and-roi.md |
| **UK** | Graduate schemes are built for bachelor's graduates; master's often enter at the same grade | A master's buys access, timing and visa, not grade | careers/consulting.md, careers/accounting-and-corporate.md |
| **US** | Same band for undergraduate and master's at MBB; STEM-designated master's add 24 months of work authorisation for internationals | For internationals, the visa value can exceed the education value | places/visas-and-work-rights.md |
| **EU institutions** | Blue Book traineeship and EPSO AD5 require only a 3-year degree | A master's is not required to enter | careers/accounting-and-corporate.md |

## 3. Testing the common reasons

| Reason given | Evidence | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| "The market is bad, so I'll wait it out in a master's" | Graduating into a recession costs ~9% of initial earnings, halving in 5 years and gone by ~10, mostly via a weaker first employer. Graduate enrolment barely rises in downturns (decisions/timing-and-sequencing.md) | **Weak** unless the master's opens a new channel (country, field, pipeline) |
| "A second master's makes me more employable" | No recruiter evidence found. It adds about a year of age and fees. Useful only for a named pipeline (London banking, UK Graduate visa, quant track) | **Usually weak** |
| "This job requires a specialised MSc" | FMCG accepts all disciplines. Only 15% of Imperial Strategic Marketing graduates went into marketing roles. Business analytics graduates go to tech and finance (decisions/programme-choice.md) | **Usually false** |
| "Prestige pays for any debt" | Payback depends on country of work and sector. A finance-master's pay premium disappears outside finance (LBS MFA non-finance £44,972 ≈ MiM consultants £44,571) | **False as a rule** |
| "I need it to change field" | Admission gates (quant ECTS) and employer filters (Revolut STEM 2:1) are real. A conversion-style master's is often the only door | **Often true** |
| "I need it to work in another country" | Graduates mostly work where they studied, except mobile finance tracks. Visa routes depend on a local degree (Germany 18-month search permit; UK Graduate visa) | **Often true** |

## 4. The alternatives, briefly

- **Work straight after the bachelor's.** Strongest where the degree norm is a bachelor's (UK, US) and your target career doesn't raise the grade for a master's. Weakest in Italy, France and DACH business roles.
- **Apprenticeships and dual study.**
  - UK apprentice vacancies +8% in 2024/25 (ISE).
  - French apprenticeship pays tuition plus a salary, but only 37.9% of management apprentices are hired by their host (CGE 2026).
  - German dual study: ≈70% retention.

  All three are strongest when you'd want to stay at the host firm.
- **Professional qualifications** (ACA/ACCA via an employer, CFA). Usually funded by the employer in audit. The CFA costs USD 3,520–4,570 across three levels plus about 300 hours a level (careers/finance.md). These complement a master's or replace it in audit; they don't replace it for IB access.
- **MBA later.** Deferred-MBA routes favour going *straight through* (no full-time work between degrees). A regular MBA after 3–6 years is a different decision (out of scope; see product/landscape-map.md).

## 5. A fair way to compute the value (for the ROI tool)

**Value of master's ≈ (post-master's net pay − pay you'd have had without it) over N years − tuition − living costs − forgone salary during the programme − loan interest.** Then:
- discount the post-master's pay for selection (Dale & Krueger);
- use the *median within your target sector and country*;
- use the whole-cohort outcome where available (evidence/how-numbers-mislead.md).

**Inputs we have verified:**
- tuition and deposits (money/costs-and-funding.md);
- net-minus-rent by city (money/salaries-and-roi.md);
- the ISE UK average graduate starting salary of £33,000 as a UK bachelor's counterfactual (ISE, Dec 2025);
- AlmaLaurea's Italian outcomes (places/italy-playbook.md).

**Missing:** a clean bachelor's-only counterfactual salary by country and sector. Listed in verification/claims-to-verify.md.

**Affordability caveat.** Unpaid or low-paid internships and gap years are only feasible with family support or savings. Italian internships often pay €600–1,000 a month (places/italy-playbook.md). Any plan that relies on them should include a liquidity check (product/product-map.md).

## Hypotheses tested

**SM-1. "On the Continent, a master's is the default credential; in the UK and US it's an option."** Verdict: **supported.** DACH MBB entry titles differ by degree. Italian and French graduate norms are built around second-cycle degrees. UK/US MBB and Big 4 hire undergraduates at the same grade.

**SM-2. "Most of the published master's premium is selection."** Verdict: **partly supported.** The direct US evidence (Dale & Krueger) supports it for typical earnings. Elite-firm access shows a real school effect (Chetty et al. 2026). No European estimate was found.

**SM-3. "Apprenticeships are displacing graduate entry in professional services."** Verdict: **partly supported (UK).** ISE apprentice vacancies +8% vs graduate −8%. ICAEW mid-tier firms plan −40% graduate trainees, +49% school leavers (small sample). Continental evidence is mixed: French management apprentices' host-hire rate is only 37.9%.

## Common mistakes and myths

- *"Everyone does a master's, so I must."* True in Italy and France for most business roles; not true for UK schemes, tech business roles or EU traineeships.
- *"A master's will get me a higher starting salary everywhere."* Not at UK/US MBB or UK Big 4 audit, where grades are the same.
- *"I'll decide after a gap year of any work."* Unrelated full-time work can close pre-experience and deferred-MBA windows.

## Decision rules

1. **If** you'll work in Italy, France or DACH business roles, **then** plan on a master's (laurea magistrale, PGE or equivalent), **because** it's the entry norm and in DACH MBB it raises the entry title.
2. **If** you'll work in the UK and already have UK work rights and a target-school bachelor's, **then** apply to graduate schemes first and treat a master's as optional, **because** many UK schemes don't change grade for a master's.
3. **If** you lack access to your target city's recruiting (school, visa or timing), **then** choose a master's in that city with a built-in recruiting cycle, **because** access is what it buys.
4. **If** your only reason is a weak job market, **then** don't start a master's you wouldn't otherwise do, **because** recession losses fade and enrolment barely helps.
5. **If** you're changing field into finance or quant, **then** a conversion or quant master's is usually necessary, **because** admission and employer filters screen on quant credentials.
6. **If** you might want a top US MBA later, **then** go straight from bachelor's to master's and apply to deferred MBAs in the master's final year, **because** full-time work in between ends eligibility.
7. **If** a plan depends on unpaid or low-paid internships, **then** check your liquidity first, **because** these are only feasible with savings or family support.
