# MBA and career switchers

## Bottom line

1. **The "dead zone" is real at the pre-experience programmes that have caps (LBS MiM about two years, HEC under three) but is not a ban from MBAs.** HBS's middle 80% is 3 to 7 years (mean 4.9); LBS's MBA2025 range is 2 to 13 years (average 6); Bocconi reports 30% of its class at 3 to 4 years. Class averages sit at 5 to 6 years everywhere (class profiles, 2026) [data].
2. **MBA placement figures are not comparable until you know the base.** HBS and Stanford only have 63-65% of the class seeking work; INSEAD 97%; Oxford 75% of seekers; LBS publishes both "received" (88%) and "accepted" (86%) under CSEA rules; IE's 95% is for "master's graduates" (school reports, 2025) [data].
3. **Payback depends more on your starting salary than on the school.** On my arithmetic (assumptions in section 3), an INSEAD MBA pays back in about 6 years from a €50,000 job and about 17 years from €80,000; LBS about 11 and 28 years from £55,000 and £75,000. Published tuition: LBS £123,950, INSEAD €109,860, Oxford £88,800 [data + own calculation].
4. **The sector shift is real but smaller than it looks.** LBS "diversified" destinations fall from 33% to 11% of graduates while consulting, finance and technology gain; at Cambridge about half of post-MBA consultants and financiers came from the same top-level sector. No school splits pay change by switchers and non-switchers, so H-b ("most of the uplift is the switch") is untested [data].
5. **"Part-time means no recruiting" is wrong in the strong form.** Booth Evening and Weekend students can use campus recruiting for full-time roles; internship recruiting is full-time only; Stern part-time has a limited cohort route; the EMBA is outside recruiting (school pages, 2026) [data].
6. **UK Graduate visa: 24 months for applications by 31 December 2026, 18 months from 1 January 2027.** The HPI list (4 November 2025) includes Harvard, Stanford, Columbia, MIT, Northwestern, NYU, Chicago, Berkeley and Penn, and not INSEAD, HEC, IESE, IE, ESADE or Bocconi; UK schools are outside it by design [data]. US OPT/H-1B fee proposals were moving in August-September 2026 and STEM status depends on the programme's CIP code.
7. **Italy: Bocconi's MBA costs €86,000 against €109,860 at INSEAD, with a 29-year average age and 19% Italians.** The largest LUISS scholarship (up to 100%) is reserved for non-Italians. Italian pay at five years is about €1,800 against about €2,900 abroad (AlmaLaurea 2025, library), which is the real ROI lever [data].
8. **Non-degree routes have provider-defined evidence only.** CIRR bootcamps report 71% in qualifying work at 180 days; Google reports 70%+ "improvement in career trajectory"; CFA Level I passed 43% in August 2025 and needs 4,000 hours of experience; 51.3% of 2024/25 UK apprenticeship starts were people aged 25 or over. No study compares them with an MBA [data / employer-stated].

Reading the table:

- **The class average is a narrow 5 to 6 years across every school**, US and European. Only HBS publishes a spread, and its middle 80% is 3 to 7 years (HBS, 2026) [data]. That means roughly one in ten HBS students arrives with under three years and one in ten with more than seven.
- **The experience a recruiter-friendly applicant needs at the top US schools is not a hard minimum.** Stanford's range starts at 0 (unverified, snippet only); LBS says it considers under three years for outstanding cases (LBS, 2026) [data]. So "3-4 years is too junior" is a statistical statement, not a rule.
- **Consulting plus finance takes roughly 40% to 55% of a top class.** At Booth 53% (consulting, financial services, PE/VC) and at HBS 45% (consulting 19, PE/VC 16, financial services 10), using each school's own categories (Booth, HBS, 2026) [data]. Categories are not identical (Stanford groups "investment management/PE/VC" separately; IESE labels "finance"), so do not compare cross-school to the point.
- **Europe's classes are a year older than the US figure on average (IESE, HEC, Oxford and Cambridge about 6 years, ages 29-30)**, and 85-96% international (IESE 86% non-Spanish; HEC 95% international; Oxford 97% international; LBS 93%).

### Dead-zone test (hypothesis H-a)

The dead zone claim says someone with 3-4 years is too senior for a MiM and too junior for a top MBA. The class data say:

1. On the MiM side, the wall is real but programme-specific. LBS MiM and MFA stop at about two years, HEC under three, Imperial case by case (decisions/timing-and-sequencing.md). Plenty of other MiMs publish no cap (ESCP, ESSEC), and post-experience specialised master's exist: LBS Masters in Finance asks for a minimum of two years, which I could not re-verify directly today (see Claims to verify).
2. On the MBA side, 3 years is inside HBS's middle 80% and is the lower edge of INSEAD's published typical band. At 3-4 years you are not an outlier at the top US schools; you are below the median by 1-2 years. The difficulty is competitive (a 4.9-year class has many people with stronger track records), not eligibility.
3. So the dead zone is **at 2-3 years of work, and mainly at LBS and a few French/UK MiM programmes**. For 3-4 years the better description is "below median for MBA, above cap for MiM; options are an MBA at a school with a younger profile, a post-experience specialised master's, or waiting one or two years".

4. **LBS's own employment report gives the cleanest spread of any European school.** The MBA2025 class (488 students) averaged 6 years of work experience, range 2 to 13 years, average age 28 (LBS MBA employment report 2025, https://assets.london.edu/hxo16fanegqh/3AUIB9h2HJvzgh89cXjwdt/73abce036f2fe9511567750fb15755b3/mba_employment_report.pdf, read in full 1 Oct 2026) [data]. The next class (MBA2026, 437 students) averages 5.4 years, same 2 to 13 range (same report). So a 2-year candidate sits at the very bottom of the LBS MBA class, but the class does contain people with two years. The same page shows pre-MBA background of the class: consulting about 27% and finance/accounting about 21% (chart-read from the text layer, labels listed in reverse order; cross-checked against the "pre-LBS" sector split in the switchers chart on page 6, consulting 29% and finance 22% for the graduates with both records) (same report) [data].

Verdict on H-a: partly supported (see Hypotheses section).

### What the table shows against the evidence/how-numbers-mislead.md checks

1. **Denominators differ by a factor of three.** At HBS and Stanford only 63-65% of the class is in the market, so "90% with offers" describes about 58% of the class. INSEAD's 81% is on 97% of the class. Oxford says 75% of those seeking, and its own report notes that 22.7% of reporters were not seeking. LBS follows the CSEA convention (sponsored returners excluded) and publishes both "received" and "accepted". Treat "75% (Oxford, HEC) vs 88% (LBS)" as not strictly comparable until the base is known.
2. **Received versus accepted.** Only LBS and HBS publish both: LBS 88/86, HBS 90/84. Schools quoting a single number usually quote whichever is higher; where the label is "secured employment" (IESE, Cambridge "employed") it is the stricter measure. Cambridge's gap between "employed 73%" and "job offers 80%" is the same thing.
3. **Reporting rate.** LBS 98%, INSEAD 98%, HBS 100% (P&Q) and Oxford 87% are published; HEC, IESE, ESADE, IE, Bocconi pages read today do not state one. Non-response in an MBA survey is typically not random, so a missing reporting rate lowers the weight to put on the salary.
4. **Salary is not comparable across currencies and methods.** INSEAD reports median in euros, LBS mean in pounds with dollars and PPP-dollars, Cambridge average base with a PPP dollar figure, HBS median in dollars with bonus separate. A mean (LBS £91,928) is pulled up by a small number of private-equity and North American salaries: LBS's North America mean is £143,262 against £79,344 for Europe ex UK and £88,516 for the UK (LBS report, page 8) [data]. The Bocconi page's "North America €247,585" is a similar outlier of a handful of people; do not read it as a destination premium.
5. **The school's own "consulting share" includes sponsored returners.** INSEAD's 50% consulting share is not all new hires: Clear Admit reports 23% new hires and 27% returners (unverified, secondary; see Claims to verify). LBS says its sector figures include sponsored students "to best represent the choices" while its employment rate and salary exclude them (LBS report, p. 4 and 7) [data].

### Where the sector shift actually happens

LBS publishes the clearest before-and-after of any school. For graduates with both pre- and post-LBS records (page 6 of its report): consulting 29% to 39%, finance 22% to 26%, technology 16% to 24%, and "diversified" (industry, FMCG, energy, healthcare and others) 33% to 11%. The MBA is therefore an engine that moves people **out of industry and into consulting, finance and technology**. Cambridge gives the same picture by function: of its graduates now in consulting, 95% switched from another sector or sub-sector, 86% from another function; for finance 63% and 56%; for industry 70% and 62% (Cambridge report 2026) [data]. Cambridge's pre-MBA sector for those now in consulting: 57% consulting (including sub-sector moves), 24% finance, 14% industry; for those now in finance: 55% finance, 30% industry, 11% consulting. So **about half of the post-MBA consultants and financiers were already in the same top-level sector**; the "switch" is often a move within the sector to a better firm or function.

Oxford's report shows the same: financial services 28% pre-MBA to 38.6% post (chart-read from a switchers panel; matches the sector panel on page 4), consulting 28.7% to 22.2%, impact 8.3% to 4.4%, technology 10.2% to 13.3% (Oxford report 2024-25, page 6) [data].

- Other schools (IESE, HEC, Bocconi, Cambridge): see Claims to verify; I could not open a primary fee page today.

### Illustrative payback arithmetic (my own, with assumptions stated)

I set out cost as tuition + the school's living budget (or my assumption where none is published) + forgone pre-MBA gross salary for the programme months, and uplift as post-MBA base minus pre-MBA base, taxed at an assumed 40% marginal rate on the difference. Bonuses are ignored on both sides, which understates the post-MBA side for LBS (mean other compensation £41,000) and INSEAD (median sign-on €28,900) and shortens the real payback somewhat.

| Case | Cost (tuition + living + forgone pay) | Post-MBA base used | Net annual uplift | Simple payback |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| INSEAD, pre-MBA salary €50,000 | €181,500 (10 months) | €100,000 (median) | €30,000 | about 6 years |
| INSEAD, pre-MBA salary €70,000 | €198,200 | €100,000 | €18,000 | about 11 years |
| INSEAD, pre-MBA salary €80,000 | €206,500 | €100,000 | €12,000 | about 17 years |
| LBS, pre-MBA £55,000 | £251,500 (18 months, living assumed £45,000) | £91,928 (mean) | £22,200 | about 11 years |
| LBS, pre-MBA £75,000 | £281,500 | £91,928 | £10,200 | about 28 years |
| Oxford, pre-MBA £45,000 | £163,800 (12 months, living assumed £30,000) | £81,466 (average) | £21,900 | about 7.5 years |
| Oxford, pre-MBA £60,000 | £178,800 | £81,466 | £12,900 | about 14 years |

Post-MBA figures from the school reports in section 2 (INSEAD median €100,000; LBS mean £91,928; Oxford average £81,466) [data]; costs from the prices above [data]; the rest is arithmetic and assumption.

What follows from this:

- **The payback depends more on your starting salary than on the school.** For INSEAD, moving the starting salary from €50,000 to €80,000 moves payback from 6 to 17 years. Someone earning €80,000 and staying in the same sector gets little from an MBA on pay alone.
- **LBS's published average salary increase is 34% (PPP dollars)** (LBS report 2025, p. 8) [data]. That is an average over a class that includes consulting and finance entrants who were already well paid. At 34%, a person on £55,000 lands on about £74,000, not the £92,000 average; the mean is lifted by people who started higher and by sector.
- **Cheaper, shorter, regional.** Oxford and Cambridge cost less in tuition and forgone pay for 12 months than LBS (18 months), but their average base is £10,000 to £20,000 lower than LBS's (Oxford £81,466, Cambridge £71,216 vs LBS £91,928 mean) [data]. Whether the cost saving pays off depends on whether the geography (UK, often London) is where you want to work, not on the brand.
- **The FT ranking's "weighted salary" is not the same as these numbers.** The FT surveys alumni three years after graduation (2026, 2025 and 2024 responses in the 2026 table), corrects pay for PPP, and gives weighted salary 16% and salary increase 16% of the score (menlocoaching.com summary of FT methodology, 2026, https://menlocoaching.com/top-mba-programs/financial-times-mba-ranking/; secondary) [practitioner consensus]. A search summary of the 2026 table gives HBS weighted salary $259,874 and a top five of MIT, INSEAD, Wharton, IESE, LBS (GMAC page, https://www.gmac.com/resources/learners/business-programs/explore-programs/financial-times-mba-ranking) [practitioner consensus]. I could not open the full FT table (paywalled); evidence/ranking-methodologies.md covers the FT method in depth. The school-reported first-job figures above and the FT three-year alumni figures differ in timing and method, so do not mix them in one comparison.

### What GMAC says about motivation and cost

GMAC's 2025 Prospective Students Survey had 4,912 respondents across 147 countries; 47% put ROI in their top three decision factors (up from 42%), nearly 70% cited financial obstacles (tuition, living costs, opportunity cost), and about one in three favoured a specialised master's over an MBA (Poets&Quants, 16 Apr 2025, https://poetsandquants.com/2025/04/16/forget-the-rankings-new-gmac-survey-shows-what-drives-mba-decisions-today/) [practitioner consensus, secondary to GMAC]. A search summary of the same survey says the share wanting to change industry or function fell from 58% in 2022 to 42% in 2025 (unverified, snippet only; see Claims to verify). I could not find a published 2025 GMAC Alumni Perspectives Survey with pre/post pay; the latest items surfaced were from 2020-21, so the library does not rely on GMAC for pay uplift.

### H-b: how much of the uplift is sector switching?

School reports do not split salary uplift by switchers and non-switchers. What exists:

- LBS: 45% switched sector and the class average increase is 34% (LBS 2025) [data]. No split.
- Cambridge: 75% switched sector; average base £71,216 (Cambridge 2026) [data]. No split.
- Older US survey summaries (Bloomberg 2014: switchers +$55,000 or 85%, same-industry +$47,000; GMAC 2011-12: +71% switchers vs +67% non-switchers) via an Accepted blog summary (https://blog.accepted.com/how-much-more-could-you-make-with-an-mba-career-switching-compensation-increase-and-more) [anecdotal because secondary and older than 2023]. Even in those, **non-switchers gained nearly as much as switchers in percentage terms**, which points the other way from H-b.

Verdict: insufficient evidence; the little there is suggests the pay jump comes from the combination of seniority reset (the MBA re-enters you at associate level at a higher pay scale) and sector, not sector alone (see Hypotheses).

### US: which MBAs are STEM-designated

- Designation is by the **degree's CIP code**, so schools use a "STEM-designated MBA" for either the whole programme (a management-science-coded MBA) or a track of electives. A February 2026 article (updated May 2026) lists Columbia, Berkeley, CMU Tepper, Booth, Duke Fuqua, NYU Stern, Michigan Ross and Vanderbilt, among others, as having designation for the whole degree or substantial tracks, and Wharton, Michigan Ross tracks, Kellogg's Management Science major and WashU Olin (extra 15 STEM credit hours) as track-only (CrackTheMBA, https://www.crackthemba.com/?p=3750; Vanderbilt news, 2 Jan 2025, https://business.vanderbilt.edu/news/2025/01/02/vanderbilt-mba-program-earns-stem-designation/ in search results) [practitioner consensus]. The lists disagree between sources (one lists Harvard and Stanford as fully STEM; I could not confirm that on school pages), so **read the designation and the CIP code on the school's own page and on your I-20** before relying on 36 months.
- **OPT and H-1B policy is moving.** Reports in August-September 2026 say DHS cleared a proposed H-1B fee of $103,265 per cap-subject petition (comment period closed 24 September 2026; no final rule found on 2 October 2026) and sent a separate OPT fee rule through White House review, with no amount published for OPT (search summaries of trackmyopt.com, bgov and Fragomen, Aug-Sep 2026) [practitioner consensus, proposals not final]. Treat any US-based ROI case for a non-EU MBA student as carrying visa-policy risk; places/visas-and-work-rights.md tracks the live position.

### What this means for an EU-citizen MBA candidate

- In Europe the legal asymmetry is large. An Italian at INSEAD, IESE, HEC or Bocconi has full EU work rights; the same MBA for a non-EU student depends on a national permit.
- The HPI list is irrelevant to the main European MBA schools (INSEAD, HEC, IESE, IE, ESADE, Bocconi are not on it, and UK schools are outside its scope), but it can help a non-UK graduate of Harvard, Stanford, Columbia, Chicago, Penn or MIT working in the UK.
- For the UK, 18 months from 1 January 2027 versus 24 months now is a real change; an 18-month MBA with a spring 2027 finish falls on the new, shorter rule.

So the real restriction is **internship recruiting**. That matters because consulting and banking (MBB, bulge brackets) hire most MBAs through summer internships, which part-time students cannot enter at these schools. Part-time access to full-time job recruiting exists but is thin (Stern: a cohort with a limited number of companies and roles). The Gemini lead's phrase that Booth part-time is "barred" from full-time recruiting is **wrong** on the page read today; its Stern statement ("isolated from full-time OCR pipelines") is closer but overstates it (see Corrections). Outside the US the question is largely moot: European one-year MBAs (INSEAD, IESE, Bocconi) do not have a part-time equivalent that recruits like the full-time class; ask each school for its "who can access recruiters" rule in writing.

### Executive and part-time MBAs: what they are for

- **They are mostly a promote-in-place degree.** The FT's 2025 Executive MBA ranking (129 programmes) reports average base pay three years after graduation of nearly $230,000 for EMBA alumni, up 4.4%, against $180,000 for online MBAs and $150,000 for traditional MBAs (down 3.2%) (Human Resources Online summary of the FT 2025 EMBA ranking, https://www.humanresourcesonline.net/executive-mba-graduates-see-steady-salary-growth-in-ft-2025-ranking) [practitioner consensus, secondary]. The groups are not comparable: EMBA students are older and already senior when they start.
- **EMBA pay rise at graduation is modest.** Executive MBA Council exit surveys reported an average 17.7% rise in salary plus bonus by graduation (2022 survey) and 14.7% (2021 graduates), with about 40% promoted during the programme (2019) (Poets&Quants for Execs, 2022 and 2020 coverage, https://poetsandquantsforexecs.com/news/2022-student-embac-survey-higher-pay-more-promotions-after-graduation/ and via search summary) [practitioner consensus, secondary]. These are 2019-2022 figures and may be stale; they refer to people who stay with their employer, which is the opposite of a switch.

### Who pays

- **Employer sponsorship is now the minority case in EMBAs.** The Executive MBA Council's head said that only 19% of EMBA students have their course fully funded externally and that the self-funded share rose from 39% to 54% over ten years (quoted in the FT-based article above, 2025) [practitioner consensus, secondary]. This is for EMBAs, not full-time MBAs. For full-time MBAs, the main sponsored cases are consulting firms and large corporates, which count in the school reports as "sponsored" students and are excluded from CSEA placement rates (LBS report; Oxford report: 22.7% of reporters were not seeking, incl. sponsored) [data].
- **Sponsorship comes with repayment terms.** Most sponsorship agreements require repayment of some or all of the funding if you leave before a set period, and many cover only part of the tuition (AccessMBA guide, https://www.accessmba.com/articles/view/how-to-ask-for-employer-mba-sponsorship) [anecdotal]. I could not find a primary source for MBB's sponsorship terms; the Gemini lead's "two-year return commitment, 100% year-one and 50% year-two clawback" is not verified (see Claims to verify).
- **Law changes the clawback in some places.** California's AB 692 prohibits most repayment and exit-fee provisions in agreements entered into on or after 1 January 2026, but still allows tuition reimbursement for transferable credentials such as a degree, with a maximum stay period of two years, proration of the repayment and no interest (Mayer Brown, 9 March 2026, https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/03/a-deeper-dive-into-californias-new-limitations-on-stay-or-pay-clauses-as-of-january-1-2026) [practitioner consensus]. For Italy and other EU states I did not find a primary source on how courts treat such clauses; read the agreement with an employment lawyer.
- **Tax on sponsorship (US).** The Gemini lead cites the $5,250 tax-free educational assistance limit under IRC section 127; I did not re-open IRS Publication 15-B today, so I only mention it as a lead (see Claims to verify).

Where the non-degree routes fit, as a judgement from the evidence above rather than a measured result:

1. **They work for a narrow kind of switch**: a function whose entry gate is a skill test (data analytics, software, accounting qualifications, CFA for people already in finance) and where an employer will hire on demonstrated skill. They do not offer the employer access, brand and recruiting calendar that MBA classes publish: INSEAD, LBS and others list 250 to 520 hiring employers a year (INSEAD hired by 257 companies; LBS 524 unique hirers) [data].
2. **They do not substitute for the MBA in consulting and finance leadership tracks**: recruiters for those targeted tracks recruit through school pipelines (see careers/consulting.md and careers/finance.md).
3. **A degree apprenticeship is the cheapest credible route for a switcher in the UK**, because the employer pays and you earn, but you need an employer willing to hire a career changer into the apprenticeship role; the statistics show adult starts rising but no outcome for switchers. Apprentices are typically paid in the role and cannot use it to switch into banking or consulting front-office roles without a matching vacancy (an inference, not a published finding).
4. **Cost comparison** is simple: an MBA at INSEAD, LBS or Oxford costs €90,000 to €125,000 in tuition alone (section 3); a bootcamp or certificate costs a small fraction, and a CFA programme costs a few thousand euros plus years of study alongside work (CFA fees not verified today).

## 8. MiM first, MBA later, and when the pre-experience route closes

Caps, deferred-MBA schemes (HBS 2+2, Stanford, Wharton Moelis, MIT, Booth, Darden, Yale Silver Scholars) and the rule that full-time work before the master's ends deferred-MBA eligibility are in decisions/timing-and-sequencing.md, sections 1 and 4. They are not repeated. This section adds only what the MBA side of the data says about the sequence.

**European deferred option missing from the list above: IESE Young Talent Path (YTP).** Open to final-year students and to graduates with up to two years of full-time work; admitted candidates have an MBA seat reserved for up to four years and, according to programme summaries, a €22,000 scholarship when they join if they meet the GMAT/GRE criteria, after an assessment boot camp in Barcelona (IESE undergraduate pages and third-party summaries, read 4 Oct 2026) [practitioner consensus; the scholarship amount was not read on an IESE page]. Programme lengths also differ more than "one-year vs two-year" suggests: IESE runs about 19 months and LBS 15 to 21 months, against 10 months at INSEAD and 12 at Oxford and Cambridge; only the longer formats include a summer internship, which is how most MBB and banking offers are made (section 5) [practitioner consensus].

### The timeline in numbers

| Stage | Typical position | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-experience master's (MiM, MFA) | Window closes at about 2 years after graduation (LBS), under 3 years' work (HEC), case by case beyond 2 (Imperial) | decisions/timing-and-sequencing.md (school pages, 2026) [data] |
| Gap of 2 to 4 years | No cap-free pre-experience route at those three schools; MBA possible but below class average | section 1 above |
| Full-time MBA | Class averages: 4.9 (HBS), 5.1 (Kellogg, snippet), 5.3 (Stanford, snippet), 5.5 (LBS MBA2027 page) to 6 years (IESE, Cambridge, INSEAD 5.9, Oxford 6, Bocconi 6); LBS MBA2025 range 2 to 13 years; Bocconi: 30% of the class at 3 to 4 years | section 1 [data] |
| Post-experience specialised master's | LBS Masters in Finance full-time: "5 years' work experience on average", 16 months with a 10-month exit option, requires relevant finance experience; 83% employed within three months (the school's figure) | LBS MiF page, https://www.london.edu/masters-degrees/masters-in-finance-full-time (read 1 Oct 2026) [data] |
| Executive or part-time MBA | Built for people who keep their job; typically older and more senior (EMBA ranking comparisons are not like-for-like) | section 5 |

### What follows

1. **MiM then MBA is a coherent path, but it is a two-step plan with a work gap in the middle**, because the MBA classes above average 5 to 6 years. A MiM graduate who starts work at 23 arrives at the average experience at 28 to 29, the age of the INSEAD, IESE and Bocconi classes (29 to 29.5) [data]. There is no published evidence in the school reports read here that a prior MiM weakens an MBA application, nor that it helps; see decisions/timing-and-sequencing.md for the advisor view.
2. **The most useful thing a MiM can do for a later MBA is to put you in the sector you then want to leave less abruptly.** In the Cambridge and LBS data, about half of post-MBA consultants and financiers were already in those sectors beforehand (section 2). A MiM that lands a consulting or finance start therefore does not obstruct a later MBA; it supplies the pre-MBA sector that most of the class has.
3. **If you are at 2 to 3 years and a MiM is no longer open, the practical alternatives are the three in the table**: wait to 4 to 6 years and apply to a full-time MBA, move into a post-experience specialised master's if your target is finance (LBS MiF), or take a part-time/executive route if you want to keep your job. Which is right depends on the starting salary (section 3 payback table) and whether the target employers recruit part-time students (section 5).
4. **The LBS MBA class is getting slightly younger** on the data given: MBA2025 averaged 6 years and MBA2026 5.4 years, with the same 2 to 13 range (LBS report, pages 3 and 10) [data]. That is one year-on-year change in one school; do not extrapolate it.
## Hypotheses tested

**H-a (assigned). "The experience dead zone (3-4 years of work) leaves candidates too senior for MiM and too junior for a top MBA."**
- Verdict: **partly supported.** The MiM half is true at LBS (two years), HEC (under three) and, less strictly, Imperial; it is not true at every MiM (ESCP and ESSEC publish no cap; decisions/timing-and-sequencing.md). The MBA half is not true as an eligibility statement.
- Holds when: you target a school with a MiM cap and want a top US MBA with a 5-year average class, where 3 years is the lower edge of HBS's middle 80% and 4.9 is the mean.
- Fails when: you apply to European one-year MBAs: LBS MBA2025 had a 2 to 13 year range (average 6) and Bocconi reports 30% of its class at 3 to 4 years; or you choose a post-experience specialised master's.
- Evidence: HBS class profile 2026 [data]; LBS employment report 2025 [data]; Bocconi class profile 2025-26 [data]; Stanford 0 to 16 range (snippet only).

**H-b (assigned). "Most of the MBA salary uplift comes from switching sector or function, not from the degree."**
- Verdict: **insufficient evidence.** No school report splits pay change by switchers and non-switchers. Older US survey summaries show non-switchers gaining nearly as much in percentage terms (+67% vs +71%, GMAC 2011-12, via a blog) [anecdotal, stale].
- Holds when: a person moves from a low-paying sector (public sector, non-profit, many industry roles) to consulting, finance or technology. LBS shows "diversified" destinations falling from 33% to 11% of graduates [data], a sign of that flow.
- Fails when: the person was already in consulting or finance: about half of post-MBA consultants and financiers at Cambridge came from the same top-level sector (55% to 57%) and still got the pay step. For them the step comes from the level reset and the new firm, not sector change.
- Test the site could run: ask school career offices for pre/post salary of "same sector, same function" graduates.

**H-c (assigned). "A part-time MBA locks you out of on-campus recruiting."**
- Verdict: **not supported as stated; supported for internships.** Booth says Full-Time, Evening and Weekend students can use campus recruiting for full-time roles; only Full-Time students recruit for internships; the EMBA is outside both. NYU Stern part-time students have no access to summer internship recruiting but can apply for permanent roles through the Langone Recruiting Cohort, with limited companies and roles.
- Holds when: the target employer hires MBAs through summer internships (consulting, banking), the programme is an executive MBA, or the school restricts you in the way Stern does.
- Evidence: Booth recruiting page 2026 and 2020 admissions chat; Stern part-time career page 2026 [data].

**H-d (own). "School placement headlines are not comparable until you know the denominator and the received-versus-accepted definition."**
- Verdict: **supported.** HBS and Stanford only have 63 to 65% of the class seeking work; INSEAD 97%; Oxford 75% of seekers with 22.7% of reporters not seeking; LBS 88% received, 86% accepted; IE's 95% is stated for "master's graduates", not the MBA. See section 2 [data].
- Holds when: you compare across schools without the base. Fails when comparing a single school year to year with the same definition.

**H-e (own). "Your starting salary matters more to payback than the school you attend."**
- Verdict: **supported (arithmetic, assumptions stated).** For INSEAD, moving a pre-MBA salary from €50,000 to €80,000 moves payback from about 6 to about 17 years; LBS payback runs from about 11 to about 28 years between £55,000 and £75,000 (section 3). Based on school-published post-MBA pay and an assumed 40% marginal tax on the increment; bonuses ignored.
- Fails when: sponsorship pays tuition or the person receives a large sign-on (INSEAD median €28,900) or when the MBA is needed to enter a track otherwise closed.

**H-f (own). "For EU-citizen readers, the HPI list and the UK Graduate visa are second-order for the main European MBAs."**
- Verdict: **partly supported.** An EU citizen at INSEAD, IESE, HEC or Bocconi needs no permit in the EU, and none of INSEAD, HEC, IESE, IE, ESADE or Bocconi is on the HPI list (updated 4 November 2025). But UK schools (LBS, Oxford, Cambridge) are outside the HPI list by design, and an EU citizen there depends on the Graduate visa: 24 months for applications on or before 31 December 2026 and 18 months from 1 January 2027 [data].
- Fails when the reader is a non-EU citizen (national permits matter).

**H-g (own). "A bootcamp, certificate or apprenticeship is a substitute for an MBA for a career switcher."**
- Verdict: **insufficient evidence.** Provider outcomes differ in definition (CIRR 71% within 180 days; Google's 70%+ "improvement in career trajectory"). No comparative study was found. Likely to hold for skill-gated roles (data, software, accounting), unlikely for consulting or finance leadership tracks that recruit through schools.

## Common mistakes and myths

1. **"The employment rate is the share of the class with a job."** It is usually the share of those seeking, and 35% to 37% of HBS and Stanford classes were not seeking (HBS 2025; Stanford snippet) [data].
2. **"An MBA with 3-4 years is not possible."** Not a published rule at LBS, Bocconi or HBS; the LBS range starts at 2 and Bocconi has 30% at 3 to 4 [data]. The competitive disadvantage is real, the ban is not.
3. **"I can do a part-time MBA and then recruit like the full-time class."** At Booth you can recruit for full-time roles, not internships; at Stern the access is limited to a cohort [data].
4. **"The salary figure is what I will earn."** It is a mean or median of responders, in a different currency each time, often excluding sponsored students and including a few outliers (LBS North America mean £143,262 vs UK £88,516) [data].
5. **"A one-year MBA gives the same UK/US post-study rights as a two-year MBA."** In the UK the Graduate visa is the same length for MBAs and other master's, and on 1 January 2027 it drops from 24 to 18 months; US STEM OPT depends on the CIP code, not the MBA name [data].
6. **"Employer sponsorship is free."** Repayment clauses commonly apply and some jurisdictions now limit them (California AB 692 from 1 January 2026) [practitioner consensus].
7. **"Switching sector is the point of the MBA."** In a 2025 GMAC prospective-students survey, the share wanting to change industry or function fell from 58% to 42% (search summary; unverified); and in school data a large part of "switching" is a country change (LBS 44% location, INSEAD 34% country) [data].

## Decision rules

1. **If you have 0 to 2 years since graduation and want a business master's, prefer a pre-experience master's (LBS MiM/MFA, HEC) (because the cap closes at about two years since graduation at LBS and under three years' work at HEC; see decisions/timing-and-sequencing.md).**
2. **If you have 2 to 3 years' experience and target an MBA, prefer European one-year schools that publish 3-to-4-year classes (Bocconi 30%, LBS range from 2 years) over HBS/Stanford (because the class mean is 5 to 6 years and 3 years is the lower edge of HBS's middle 80%).**
3. **If you have 3 to 4 years and want finance specifically, prefer a post-experience specialised master's (LBS MiF averages 5 years) or wait one to two years for an MBA (because the MBA applicant pool average is 5 to 6 years).**
4. **If you are on a salary above about €70,000 and are staying in the same sector, prefer to avoid a full-time MBA on pay grounds alone (because simple payback runs 11 to 17 years at INSEAD and 28 at LBS in section 3).** Re-run the payback with your numbers.
5. **If your pre-MBA salary is below about €50,000 and you target consulting, finance or technology at a school with published placement in those, prefer a one-year MBA (INSEAD, Oxford, Cambridge, Bocconi) over a 15-to-21-month MBA (because payback was 6 to 7.5 years in the examples and tuition is lower).**
6. **If a school quotes a placement percentage, require the denominator, reporting rate and received-versus-accepted definition before comparing (because HBS/Stanford report on 63 to 65% of the class, INSEAD on 97%, IE on "master's graduates").**
7. **If you want to switch into consulting or banking and consider part-time, prefer full-time (because summer internship recruiting is closed to part-time students at Booth and Stern).**
8. **If you want to keep your job and get promoted, prefer an executive or part-time MBA and ask the school which recruiting routes are open (because the EMBA is not in Booth's recruiting databases; EMBA pay uplift data are for people who stay).**
9. **If you are an EU citizen and want to work in the EU after the MBA, prefer to treat visa rules as non-binding and optimise on pay and tuition; if you are non-EU, price the post-study route first (because EU citizens need no permit; non-EU readers depend on UK Graduate visa, French permit or Singapore EP rules).**
10. **If you complete an LBS MBA with an exit in or after January 2027, prefer to assume an 18-month Graduate visa, not 24 (GOV.UK: 18 months for applications on or after 1 January 2027).**
11. **If you want US work rights after a US MBA, prefer to check the school's CIP code and I-20 for STEM designation and monitor OPT/H-1B fee rules before accepting (because designation is per programme or track and fee rules were proposed in 2026).**
12. **If you are considering sponsorship, prefer to read the repayment term, the prorating rule and the jurisdiction's law before signing (because terms vary and California limited them from 2026).**
13. **If your target is a skills-gated role (data, software, accounting), prefer a bootcamp, certificate or degree apprenticeship first (because it costs a fraction of an MBA and employers hire on demonstrated skill), and keep the MBA for leadership tracks.**
14. **If you are an Italian candidate, prefer to compare Bocconi (€86,000, 12 months, 19% Italians) with INSEAD or LBS using your expected salary abroad and in Italy, not on prestige (because AlmaLaurea pay at five years is about €2,900 abroad against €1,800 in Italy).**
