# Programme choice: which kind of master's, and which format

## Bottom line
1. **A specialist finance master's pays more at the same school, but only because it sends people into investment banking.** At LBS in 2025, MFA graduates earned a mean base of £53,866 and MiM graduates £41,229, a gap of 31%. But 50% of the MFA class went into investment banking, against 14% of the MiM class. MFA graduates who took non-finance jobs averaged £44,972, about the same as MiM consultants (£44,571) (LBS MFA and MiM Employment Reports 2025, published 2026) [data]. Choose the finance degree for the pipeline, not for a general pay premium.
2. **A generalist MiM leaves the most doors open, and employers hire it most widely.** The LBS MiM splits roughly one-third each between finance, consulting and technology or other sectors. In GMAC's 2026 survey, 73% of employers had hired MiM graduates in 2025. That is the highest of any pre-experience specialised master's (Accounting 72%, Data Analytics 70%, Finance 68%, Business Analytics 68%, Marketing 66%), though below the MBA (88%) and bachelor's (84%) (GMAC Corporate Recruiters Survey 2026, Figure 17, read by the lead; see method note in §8) [data].
3. **Business analytics master's lead to tech and finance jobs, not consulting.** The LBS MAM class of 2025 went 41% into technology, 34% into finance and 18% into consulting. Imperial's MSc Business Analytics class of 2024 sent 38% into finance (LBS MAM report 2025; Imperial career-impact page) [data]. For MBB consulting, the MiM is the stronger route.
4. **A marketing master's often does not lead to a marketing job.** Only 15% of Imperial's MSc Strategic Marketing class of 2024 went into marketing, advertising, media or PR (Imperial, 2025) [data]. At HEC, the Master in Marketing reports an average starting salary of €63K, against €103K for the Master in International Finance (HEC programme pages, 2025 graduation survey) [data]. A specialist marketing or luxury degree narrows your options without guaranteeing entry to the specialism.
5. **Quant programmes (MFE, MFin) are a different market.** Berkeley MFE's class of 2025 had a median base salary of $150,000, and Princeton's MFin admits about 40 students from around 1,000 applicants. Entry needs multivariable calculus, linear algebra, differential equations and programming (Berkeley MFE; Princeton BCF, 2026) [data]. Most business bachelor's graduates are not eligible without extra maths.
6. **The continental two-year format builds in the work experience that one-year UK programmes assume you already have.** ESSEC's one-year Intensive track is only open to applicants with at least 6 months of professional experience. The Flexible 2–3-year track requires 8 months of experience during the programme (ESSEC, 2026) [data]. HEC's optional gap year is waived only for students who already have 8–16 weeks of work experience (HEC, 2026) [data]. If you arrive with no internships, a one-year programme gives you very little time to get them.
7. **The CEMS MIM is an add-on to a home-school master's, not a separate school.** It adds a term abroad, an international internship of at least 8 weeks, a business project and a three-language requirement (CEMS, 2026) [data]. Its graduates tilt towards consulting (34–37%) and consumer goods, and its 73 corporate partners include no US bulge-bracket bank (CEMS facts and partner list, 2026) [data]. It is worth adding if you want an international consulting, FMCG or industrial career. It adds little for London investment banking.
8. **No employer data shows that a double degree beats a single degree plus exchange.** Double degrees usually add a year and a second set of fees. Few students take them: at SSE, 11 of 336 MSc graduates in 2025 did one, against 60 who did CEMS and 164 who went on exchange (SSE MSc Employment Report 2026) [data]. Treat a double degree as a way to get a second country's labour market and network, not as a CV multiplier.
9. **Discount both employer projections and school headline rates.** GMAC's own data shows that business master's hiring projections overshoot reality: 80% of employers predicted hiring data analytics graduates for 2025, but only 70% did (GMAC 2026) [data]. School employment rates differ by denominator (3 vs 6 months, "seeking employment and reporting", or "one year after graduation"), so rates such as 92%, 94% and 98% are not directly comparable.

What this shows:
- **The MFA premium comes from where graduates work.** MFA graduates who left finance earned about the same as MiM consultants (£44,972 vs £44,571). The MiM's finance graduates (£45,676) earned less than the MFA's (£55,674), because MFA finance means mostly front-office investment banking and private equity.
- **MiM graduates in technology earn the least** (£36,231 mean base). Tech bonuses are high but volatile: "other compensation" in MiM tech ranged up to £144,174 [data].
- **The LBS MiM is an international product.** Only 6% of the MiM class came from the UK and 37% from continental Europe. 94% were international students (MiM report 2025) [data]. It is effectively a one-year programme for international graduates, most of whom want to stay in London.
- The LBS MiM's top employers were Bain (10 hires), BCG (7), Aspora (5), Simon-Kucher (5), OC&C (5), McKinsey (4), Amazon (4) and P&G (4) (MiM report 2025) [data].

## 2. One-year (UK) vs two-year (continental) formats

### What the formats actually are
| Programme | Length | Built-in work experience | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| LBS MiM | 12 months (reports say 12–16 months) | None required | Tuition £52,950 for 2026 (https://www.london.edu/masters-in-management) [data] |
| Imperial MSc Management | 12 months | None required | Careers support "before you start" (Imperial career-impact page) [data] |
| HEC MiM (Grande École) | 2 years (M1 + M2), plus an optional gap year | Gap year between M1 and M2. Exempt only with 8 weeks (direct M1 entry) or 16 weeks (L3 entry) of experience by the start of M2 | M2 is about 40 ECTS of specialisation plus a 20 ECTS research paper; over 20 specialisations in 7 degree groups (https://www.hec.edu/en/master-s-programs/master-management/course-content/optional-gap-year; …/m2-specialization-phase) [data] |
| ESSEC MiM | Flexible 2–3 years, or Intensive 1 year | Flexible track needs at least 8 months of experience (internships, apprenticeship or start-up). Intensive track needs at least 6 months of prior experience and allows no apprenticeship | Apprenticeship makes tuition free and pays a monthly salary (ESSEC, 2026) [data] |
| Bocconi MSc | 2 years | Internship earns credits | Abroad for one semester (exchange) up to one year (double degree) [data] |
| CEMS MIM (added to a home master's) | Two consecutive academic terms (August–July), plus an internship term | International internship of at least 8 consecutive full-time weeks | At least 2 of the 3 terms abroad (https://cems.org/cems-mim/programme-description/curriculum) [data] |

### Trade-offs
- **Internship access.** A one-year UK MSc has no summer internship slot between years. Graduate-scheme applications at banks and consulting firms open in the late summer and autumn before the start date, so students apply within weeks of arriving. See getting-in/recruiting-calendar.md for firm-by-firm windows. The continental formats solve this in their structure. ESSEC requires 8 months of experience and HEC effectively requires a gap year unless you already have 8–16 weeks, so students graduate with CV lines they did not have on entry [data for the programme rules; the timing claim is practitioner consensus, see getting-in/recruiting-calendar.md].
- **The schools themselves set experience thresholds.** ESSEC bars applicants without 6 months of experience from its one-year track. That is a school-stated signal that a compressed format suits people who have already done internships (ESSEC, 2026) [data].
- **Outcomes at the top UK one-year programmes are still strong on average.** LBS MiM had 92% of graduates with an offer within 3 months, and Imperial MSc Management 94% within 6 months [data]. The disadvantage is conditional: it hits the student who arrives with no internships and an unclear target. The average student is not affected.
- **Cost and opportunity cost.** One year means one year's fees and living costs, and an earlier salary. For numbers, see money/costs-and-funding.md and money/salaries-and-roi.md.
- **Visa length (non-EU and, since Brexit, EU citizens in the UK).** A one-year UK course leads to the Graduate Route: 24 months if you apply on or before 31 December 2026, 18 months from 1 January 2027, so any one-year MSc starting in September 2026 or later gets 18 months (GOV.UK, 2026) [data]. Continental two-year programmes give longer student residence plus local job-search permits. See places/visas-and-work-rights.md.
- **Depth and specialisation.** HEC's M2 gives about 40 ECTS of specialisation plus a thesis. A UK one-year MiM is mostly core courses plus a few electives [data for HEC; UK comparison inferred from programme structures].
- **Employer perception.** I found no primary source (employer statement or survey) showing that employers prefer two-year over one-year master's graduates of equal quality (see Claims to verify). Keep this as an open question, not a fact.

## 3. CEMS MIM: structure, outcomes, and whether it is worth it

**Structure.** CEMS is a joint degree of 33 member schools. You apply through your home school and must be enrolled in its master's programme (https://cems.org/cems-mim/how-to-apply) [data]. The requirements are:
- 66 ECTS in total, with at least 24 ECTS completed in each term;
- a one-week Block Seminar, and the Global Strategy and Global Leadership courses;
- a two-day Global Citizenship Seminar;
- a consultancy-style Business Project with a corporate or social partner;
- skill seminars;
- from 2026/27, at least 5 ECTS of data analytics electives, replacing the old "hard skills" requirement, plus a mandatory Self-Leadership seminar;
- an international internship of at least 8 consecutive full-time weeks in one company, which you find yourself (it can be at a partner firm, but doesn't have to be). Alternatively, prior work experience, the first 8 weeks of a full-time job, or a start-up can count;
- proficiency in three languages by graduation, with English required on entry.

At least two of the three terms must be spent abroad. Admission needs a GMAT of 600 (Focus 555) where the school asks for one, and TOEFL 100 or equivalent (cems.org curriculum, internship, languages and how-to-apply pages, 2026) [data].

**Scale.** CEMS has 33 schools, 73 corporate partners and 8 social partners. The 2025-26 class has 1,296 students of 90 nationalities, and there are more than 22,850 alumni (https://cems.org/about-cems/facts-figures; https://cems.org/corporate-social-partners/cems-corporate-partners, 2026) [data]. Calculator schools on the CEMS school list are HEC Paris, Bocconi, St. Gallen, RSM, SSE, CBS, WU, Esade, Nova and LSE. ESSEC, ESCP, LBS, Imperial, Mannheim and IE are not on it (https://cems.org/academic-members/school-list, fetched 30 September 2026) [data]. Note that HEC is the only French member.

**Outcomes.** Among recent graduates, 98% were employed or continuing their studies and 95% had worked outside their home country (March 2026 survey). 51% of alumni have worked for a CEMS corporate partner (cems.org) [data]. The FT 2025 MiM ranking reported CEMS alumni average earnings of $123,488 three years after graduation, above the average for all ranked schools. CEMS itself is not ranked separately because of its multi-school delivery (https://cems.org/news-events/news/financial-times-2025-ranking-cems-alumni, 9 August 2025) [data, as reported by CEMS].

**Who recruits from CEMS.** The 73 corporate partners are heavily weighted towards:
- consulting: Bain, BCG, McKinsey, Kearney, Oliver Wyman, Simon-Kucher, Accenture, AlphaSights, zeb;
- consumer goods and luxury: L'Oréal, LVMH, Richemont, P&G, Henkel, Beiersdorf, Barilla, H&M, Inditex;
- European industrials: ABB, Siemens Healthineers, Bosch, Hilti, KONE, Airbus, Mercedes-Benz.

The banks on the list are European (BNP Paribas, Société Générale, HSBC, Raiffeisen, Erste). Goldman Sachs, JPMorgan and Morgan Stanley are not on it (partner list, fetched 30 September 2026) [data]. The pipeline therefore matches the sector split: consulting and FMCG, not front-office investment banking.

**Is it worth the complexity?**
- *Selection effect:* the most mobile and multilingual students at each home school pick CEMS, so the $123K figure mixes the effect of the programme with the effect of who takes it. At SSE, 60 of 336 MSc graduates in 2025 also earned CEMS, and 54 of those came from the MSc International Business (SSE MSc Employment Report 2026) [data]. No source I found compares CEMS and non-CEMS graduates within the same school on salary.
- *Worth it if:* you want a multinational rotational or graduate programme, or a European consulting career across two or three countries; you already speak, or can certify, a third language; and your home school's MiM has room for a CEMS year without adding much time.
- *Less useful if:* you are targeting London investment banking or US jobs, or you would have to extend your degree by a year only to fit CEMS.

## 4. Double degrees
- **What is on offer.**
  - HEC: over 30 double degrees, including MIT, Yale (M2M), HKUST, Tsinghua, TUM and Polytechnique. M2-phase double degrees with Bocconi, Esade, FGV-EAESP, IIM Ahmedabad, Keio, NUS, St. Gallen and Tsinghua. Double degrees are priced separately from the standard two-year MiM fee (HEC MiM and fees pages, 2026) [data].
  - ESSEC: 11 international double degrees, including Bocconi, Mannheim, Queen's, Peking Guanghua, IIM Ahmedabad, Keio, NTU and SNU. Dual degrees with MIT (MSMS), Yale SOM, CMU (MSBA), Imperial and HKUST carry extra fees (ESSEC, 2026) [data].
  - Bocconi MSc International Management: double degrees with ESSEC (Paris/Singapore, plus a Luxury Management concentration) and with Fudan (Bocconi, 2026) [data].
  - Mannheim MMM: eight double degrees, with half the time spent at each school; partners include Bocconi, CBS and ESSEC (https://www.bwl.uni-mannheim.de/en/international/outgoing/mmm/double-degree/, 2026) [data].
  - SSE: double degrees with St. Gallen, Bocconi and Sciences Po (SSE report 2026) [data].
  - **A double degree can carry legal value that a single foreign degree does not.** ESCP runs a Master in Management double degree with Politecnico di Torino for engineering students: one year at Politecnico, then two years across ESCP campuses, leading to the Italian *laurea magistrale* from Politecnico and ESCP's *Grade de Master* (ESCP event and programme pages, read 4 Oct 2026) [data]. An Italian laurea magistrale is a *titolo* usable for concorsi and professional registers without the equivalence step in decisions/school-types-and-accreditation.md. The route is for engineering graduates, not a general feature of the ESCP MiM; a plain ESCP MiM student is in the "foreign master's" row of the legal table. Confirm with the school which of your two diplomas the Italian ministry recognises before relying on it for a concorso or an *albo*.
- **Value to employers vs cost.** No school or employer report I found gives separate outcomes for double-degree graduates. SSE's report says double-degree graduates gain an edge in Europe's integrated labour market, but that is the school's own marketing, not outcome data (SSE report 2026) [anecdotal]. The concrete benefits are: (a) a local degree and alumni network in a second country, which matters where local employers screen on domestic brands (for example French or German recruiters); (b) legal residence and an internship period in that country; (c) for US partners (MIT, Yale), a US brand and potentially US job-search status (see places/visas-and-work-rights.md). The costs are usually an extra year and a second tuition fee.
- **Take-up is small.** At SSE, 11 of 336 graduates took a double degree, against 164 who went on exchange (SSE 2026) [data]. That suggests most students get their international exposure more cheaply through exchange.

## 5. Business analytics master's
- **Demand.** GMAC 2026: 68% of employers hired Master of Business Analytics graduates in 2025 and 75% planned to in 2026. For Master of Data Analytics, 70% hired and 77% planned to (GMAC CRS 2026, Figure 17, read from chart) [data]. GMAC notes that data analytics hiring came in 10 points below the previous year's projection (80% predicted vs 70% actual). Technology, AI and data analysis skills grew the most in importance for employers (GMAC 2026) [data].
- **Pay and destinations vs MiM.** LBS MAM: mean base £45,741, above the MiM's £41,229. Its consulting hires averaged £52,451, but that is a small group (18% of 80 students) (LBS 2025) [data]. Imperial MSc Business Analytics: 95% employed within 6 months, 38% in finance (Imperial, class of 2024) [data].
- **For consulting**, the MiM places more people in MBB-type firms (LBS MiM: 30% consulting; Bain, BCG and McKinsey among the top hirers). **For tech and data roles in finance**, analytics is at least as good and slightly better paid at LBS. See careers/consulting.md and careers/tech-business-and-startups.md.

## 6. MFE / quantitative finance vs MiF
| | Berkeley MFE | Princeton MFin | WU Vienna QFin | Typical MiF/MFA (LBS MFA) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Prerequisites | Grade B or higher in calculus, multivariable calculus, linear algebra and differential equations; programming (C++/Python); ML libraries; data tooling | Highly selective, with a maths camp before the programme | Exams in mathematics, statistics or computing; R training; 2 years with Science or Industry tracks | GMAT (LBS MFA average 703); no advanced maths |
| Size / selectivity | 67 graduates with offers (2025) | About 40 students from about 1,000 applicants | 81% international students | 216 students |
| Outcomes | 2025: mean base $148,318, median $150,000, mean sign-on $34,267. Industries: investment banking 31%, asset management 28%, hedge funds 19%, trading 12%. 55% in quant research or portfolio roles. 2023: 96% of job-seekers placed within 3 months | 100% placement for full-time jobs and internships, 2017–2023. Employers include Citadel, Jane Street, Two Sigma, AQR, Millennium and the bulge brackets | School says graduates "generally find jobs within one month or less" (no report found) | Mean base £53,866; 50% investment banking |
| Source | https://mfe.haas.berkeley.edu/careers/mfe-placements; …/admissions/prerequisites (2026) [data] | https://bcf.princeton.edu/academic-programs/master-in-finance/recruiting-career-development-and-job-placements/ (2026) [data] | https://www.wu.ac.at/en/programs/masters-programs/quantitative-finance/overview (2026) [data, school claim] | LBS MFA report 2025 [data] |

Takeaways:
- US MFE pay is in a different bracket, but it is US pay for US jobs. 60–62% of Berkeley MFE hires were on the US East Coast, and only 10–13% were international [data].
- For an EU reader, the realistic quant routes are ETH/UZH MQF, WU QFin, Imperial or LSE quant MScs, or a US MFE if a US visa is viable. Given the US sponsorship retreat in GMAC 2026 (under one-third of US employers open to international hires), weigh that last route carefully.
- A MiF or MFA is for banking, private equity and asset management generalist roles. An MFE or quant MSc is for strats, trading and quant research, and those desks screen on maths and coding.

## 7. Specialised degrees (luxury, sustainability, sports, digital marketing)
- **Evidence of weaker or narrower outcomes.**
  - HEC Marketing starts on about €63K vs €103K for the MIF (2025 graduation survey) [data].
  - Warwick MSc Marketing & Strategy had 72% employment at $60,000, against 91% at $67,035 for MSc Management (via careers/marketing.md, wbs.ac.uk, April 2026) [data, via sibling].
  - Imperial MSc Strategic Marketing sent only 15% of graduates into marketing, advertising, media or PR (2024) [data]. That suggests a large share of specialist marketing graduates end up competing for general business roles anyway.
  - Bocconi Marketing Management graduates are the least likely of the three MScs to work abroad (40.9%) [data].
- **Luxury, sports and sustainability.** Top schools increasingly deliver these as tracks inside a MiM or double degree rather than as stand-alone degrees. Examples are HEC's "New Luxury" and sustainability specialisations, the Bocconi–ESSEC Luxury Management double degree, and ESSEC's 30% of graduates in health, beauty, luxury, sports and similar sectors [data]. This lets a student signal interest while keeping a generalist MiM title. I found no published employment report for a stand-alone luxury or sports master's at a calculator school (see Claims to verify).
- **Employer side.** The luxury employers in the data (LVMH, Richemont, Cartier, L'Oréal) hire from generalist MiMs: they appear in the LBS MiM employer list and in the CEMS partner list [data]. Nothing I found shows that they require a luxury-specific degree.

## 8. GMAC Corporate Recruiters Survey 2026: employer demand by degree
The survey had 621 respondents from 39 countries, 53% of them from Global Fortune 500 companies. It was fielded January–May 2026 with EFMD and CSEA, and global results are weighted by regional GDP (https://www.gmac.com/-/media/files/gmac/research/employment-outlook/2026-corporate-recruiters-survey/report.pdf) [data].

Other findings from the survey [data]:
- One-third of employers have replaced at least some entry-level roles with AI.
- Four in five Western European employers are willing to hire graduates who need work authorisation, up 17 points on 2025. Just under one-third of US employers are.

- Projections for business master's hiring have historically been more optimistic than the actual outcomes.

**Implication.** The MiM is the second most widely hired graduate business degree after the MBA. Specialist master's are hired by roughly 15 fewer employers in every 100, which is consistent with a narrower but sometimes better-paid market.

## 9. Programme features that matter to employers
- **Internship built into the programme.** ESSEC (89% had offers before graduation), SSE (80% found positions before completing their studies; 91% within 3 months) and Bocconi Finance (82.7% employed on graduation day) all combine a built-in or credit-bearing work period with high "job before graduation" rates [data]. This is consistent with conversion from internships but does not prove it. No school publishes the share of offers that came from the student's own internship (see Claims to verify).
- **Corporate projects.** CEMS Business Projects, ESSEC Singapore's two six-week Junior Consultant missions, and Berkeley's Applied Finance Project are the usual forms [data]. These are CV material rather than hiring channels.
- **Career centre intensity.** LBS reports that 86% of MiM students used the Career Centre, with 230+ workshops, 3,100+ one-to-one coaching sessions and 1,650+ job posts in 2024-25. It has sector-specific heads for consulting, finance, tech and diversified careers (LBS MiM report 2025) [data]. GMAC 2026 notes that employer participation in on-campus events has fallen over five years while digital channels held steady [data]. So in-person recruiting events are now less central than school marketing suggests.
- **Class size and nationality mix.** Classes range from about 40 (Princeton MFin) to 405 (LBS MiM) and 1,296 across CEMS. A large class with 94% international students (LBS MiM) means most classmates compete for the same London roles. A mid-size class with a strong home-country base (Bocconi, ESSEC, HEC) builds a domestic alumni network that local employers recruit from. This is my own inference from class profiles, not a measured effect; getting-in/employer-pipelines.md covers how feeder schools work.

## Hypotheses tested

**H1. A generalist MiM leaves more doors open than a specialised MSc at the same school.** *Supported.*
- Holds when the target sector is undecided, or split between consulting, finance and tech.
- Fails for committed investment-banking candidates. The LBS MFA puts 50% of its class into investment banking, against 14% for the MiM, and pays 31% more at the mean.
- Evidence: LBS MiM vs MFA sector splits [data]; GMAC 2026: 73% of employers hired MiM graduates in 2025, the highest among pre-experience specialised master's (MFin 68%, business analytics 68%, marketing 66%) [data].

**H2. The one-year UK MSc is a recruiting disadvantage for students who aren't already prepared on arrival.** *Partly supported.*
- Evidence for: the format has no built-in internship; ESSEC gates its one-year track behind 6 months of experience; HEC exempts from the gap year only students with 8–16 weeks of experience [data].
- Where it fails: average outcomes at LBS (92% offers) and Imperial (94%) are strong [data], so prepared students clearly succeed.
- No source splits outcomes by prior internships.

**H3. A mandatory internship semester is worth more than a higher ranking.** *Insufficient evidence.*
- High pre-graduation offer rates at ESSEC (89%), SSE (80%) and Bocconi (78–83%) are consistent with the idea [data].
- But no dataset compares a lower-ranked school with a mandatory internship against a higher-ranked school without one, holding the student constant.
- Treat it as plausible for students who have no internships yet.

**H4. CEMS adds measurable salary or employer access over the home school's MiM.** *Insufficient evidence on salary; partly supported on access.*
- CEMS gives structured access to 73 partners, and 51% of alumni have worked for one [data].
- No within-school comparison of CEMS and non-CEMS graduates exists in the sources, and selection bias is strong: the most internationally minded students opt in.
- The access benefit is sector-specific (consulting and FMCG, not US investment banks).

**H5. Niche specialised master's (luxury, sports) have weaker employment outcomes than generalist or marketing degrees.** *Partly supported for marketing-type specialisms; insufficient evidence for luxury and sports.*
- Evidence: Warwick Marketing & Strategy at 72% vs 91% for Management [data, via sibling]; HEC Marketing at €63K vs MIF at €103K [data]; Imperial Strategic Marketing with only 15% in marketing [data].
- No stand-alone luxury or sports master's report was found.

**H6 (own). Business analytics master's are finance and tech degrees in disguise, not a route to consulting.** *Supported.*
- LBS MAM: 41% tech, 34% finance, 18% consulting. Imperial Business Analytics: 38% finance [data].
- Fails for analytics-heavy consulting (e.g. QuantumBlack-type roles): the MAM's consulting hires earned the programme's highest mean base (£52,451).

**H7 (own). The pay premium of a finance master's disappears outside finance.** *Supported.*
- LBS MFA graduates in non-finance roles averaged £44,972, against £44,571 for MiM consultants and £41,229 for the MiM overall [data].
- Holds at LBS in 2025; untested elsewhere.

**H8 (own). Employer hiring projections overstate real demand for specialist business master's.** *Supported.*
- GMAC reports that data analytics hiring was projected at 80% and came in at 70%, and says business master's projections are "particularly optimistic" [data].

**H9 (own). The quant premium needs a maths and coding background that most business bachelor's graduates lack.** *Supported.*
- Berkeley requires multivariable calculus, linear algebra and differential equations at grade B or higher, plus C++/Python and ML experience. It pays a $150K median base [data].
- Fails for engineering, maths or physics graduates, for whom it is the highest-paid pre-experience route in the dataset.

**H10 (own). Top UK one-year MiMs are mostly bought by non-UK students who want to stay in the UK.** *Supported.*
- LBS MiM: 94% international students, 6% UK-origin, but 54% took UK jobs [data].
- For EU readers since Brexit, this makes the Graduate Route central to the degree's value (see places/visas-and-work-rights.md).

## Common mistakes and myths
- **"A marketing (or luxury) master's is the way into marketing."** Imperial's Strategic Marketing class sent 15% into marketing roles [data]. Luxury houses in the data also hire from generalist MiMs (LVMH, Richemont and Cartier appear in the LBS MiM employer list) [data].
- **"A finance master's pays more, full stop."** The premium applies only to people who land finance roles (H7) [data].
- **"Business analytics is the modern consulting degree."** Its graduates go mainly into tech and finance (H6) [data].
- **"CEMS is a school you apply to."** You must first be admitted to a CEMS member school's master's and then apply to CEMS through it (cems.org) [data].
- **"A double degree doubles my employability."** No outcome data supports this, and it usually costs a year and a second fee. Few students choose it (SSE: 11 of 336) [data].
- **"Headline employment rates are comparable across schools."** They are measured at 3 months (LBS, CSEA), 6 months (Imperial, ESSEC), 7 months (HEC Marketing), on graduation day or one year after (Bocconi), and they exclude non-respondents and people not seeking work [data from each methodology note].
- **"Employer surveys show strong demand for my specialist degree."** GMAC's own data shows that projections for specialist master's run 5–10 points ahead of actual hiring [data].
- **"The gap year is a delay."** At HEC it is the main internship mechanism and is effectively compulsory without prior experience [data].

## Decision rules
1. **If** your target is front-office investment banking in London **and** you are admitted to both a MiM and a finance-specialist master's at the same school, **prefer** the finance master's. LBS MFA placed 50% in investment banking vs 14% for the MiM, with a 31% higher mean base.
2. **If** you are undecided between consulting, finance and tech, **prefer** the generalist MiM. It has the widest sector spread and the widest employer hiring among pre-experience master's (73% of GMAC employers hired MiM graduates in 2025, against 66–72% for the specialised degrees).
3. **If** you have fewer than 2 relevant internships **and** you are choosing between a one-year UK MSc and a two-year continental MiM with a built-in internship or gap year, **prefer** the continental format, unless you can start recruiting in the first 6 weeks of the UK course.
4. **If** you already have 6 or more months of relevant experience, **prefer** the one-year format (LBS, Imperial, ESSEC Intensive). You save a year of cost, and the schools' own entry rules show the format is designed for you.
5. **If** you target MBB or strategy consulting, **prefer** a MiM (or CEMS on top of one) over business analytics or marketing. LBS MiM: 30% consulting, vs 18% for MAM and a low share for marketing degrees.
6. **If** you want a data or tech role, including in fintech or bank data teams, **consider** a business analytics master's. It is at least as well paid as the MiM (LBS MAM £45,741 vs MiM £41,229 mean base).
7. **If** you have a maths, engineering or physics bachelor's and can code, **consider** an MFE or quant MSc over a MiF. For EU citizens, prefer European quant programmes unless a US visa pathway is realistic (Berkeley MFE median $150K, but 87–90% of hires stay in the US).
8. **If** your home school is a CEMS member **and** you want an international consulting, FMCG or industrial career in Europe or Asia **and** you have, or can certify, a third language, **add** CEMS. **If** you target US banks or US jobs, skip it.
9. **If** a double degree adds a full year and a second tuition fee, **take it only** when you want to work in the partner's country, or need its local brand or residence status. Otherwise take an exchange semester.
10. **If** you want luxury, sports or sustainability, **prefer** a specialisation track inside a top generalist MiM (e.g. HEC M2 specialisations or the Bocconi–ESSEC luxury double degree) over a stand-alone niche master's at a weaker school.
11. **If** you are comparing schools' employment rates, **normalise** them first: use the same horizon (3 vs 6 months) and check the denominator, then compare salary by sector, not overall means.
12. **If** you are a French-resident or EU student admitted to ESSEC's Flexible track, **check** the apprenticeship option. It makes tuition free and pays a salary, but it is not available on the Intensive track.
