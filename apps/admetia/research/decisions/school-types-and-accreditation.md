# School types, rankings and accreditation

## Bottom line

1. **The FT Masters in Management (MiM) ranking is fairly stable at the top and noisy below about 30th.** In the 2026 table, programmes ranked in the top 30 moved a median of 4 places from 2025, and only 3 of them moved 10 or more. Across the whole table, 34 of the 85 programmes I could match in both years moved 10+ places, and the median move was 7 (my count from the FT 2026 table, reproduced by IIM Ahmedabad, 2026) [data]. The FT itself now groups the 100 schools into four tiers (8, 32, 51 and 9 schools). Read the tier, not the rank.
2. **The FT salary figure is converted at purchasing power parity (PPP), which inflates pay in cheap countries and shrinks it in expensive ones.** IIM Ahmedabad shows a "weighted salary" of $199,706 in the 2026 MiM table (FT 2026) [data]; Poets&Quants set the 2025 IIMA figure of $181,239 against actual starting salaries under $45,000 (Poets&Quants, 2025) [practitioner consensus]. For a European reader, the consequence runs the other way: St Gallen's $150,490 is shrunk, not inflated, by PPP, so the Swiss pay advantage over Paris or Milan is larger in cash than the FT column suggests.
3. **Rankings can be gamed through the alumni survey, and the FT does not publish its enforcement.** A 2022 TBS Education graduate alleged in 2025 that the school asked him to raise his reported salary by 35% and to net internship pay off his tuition. TBS then vanished from the 2025 MiM ranking after 19 consecutive years, and returned at 81st in 2026 (Poets&Quants, March 2025, September 2025, September 2026) [practitioner consensus]. At an EFMD deans' conference, deans voted "too easily influenced or gamed by schools" the biggest flaw of rankings (Poets&Quants, 2025) [practitioner consensus].
4. **The FT ranks the schools that choose to take part, not the market.** Only two US schools appear in the 2026 MiM top 100, France supplies 23 programmes, and LSE, Oxford and Cambridge do not appear in the MiM table at all (FT 2026; Poets&Quants, 2026) [data]. A school's absence says nothing about its quality.
5. **Low-tuition public schools compete with private ones on FT outcomes, but not everywhere.** In Germany, public Cologne ($122,131), Mannheim ($118,678) and TUM ($116,933) match or beat private Frankfurt School ($106,836) and EBS ($102,555); private WHU ($140,877) and HHL ($139,768) lead. In Italy, private Bocconi ($109,250) is far ahead of public Politecnico di Milano ($73,568), but private Luiss ($73,738) is level with Polimi (FT 2026) [data]. Selectivity and recruiter access explain more than legal status.
6. **Check the coverage of every employment and salary figure, not just the number.** Bocconi's 2026 "employed at three months" figure of 100% covers only 55% of the class; TUM's 70% covers 15%; HEC Lausanne's 66% covers 46% (FT 2026, bracketed coverage figures) [data]. A 100% rate on half a class is marketing, not evidence.
7. **Accreditation is a floor, not a differentiator, among the schools on the Admetia list.** AACSB counts 1,000+ accredited schools in 100+ countries (AACSB, 2026) [data], and 149 schools held the "triple crown" (AACSB + EQUIS + AMBA) in February 2026 (Wikipedia compiling MBA Today, 2026) [data]. LSE, Oxford and Cambridge are not on the triple-crown list and recruit very well. Accreditation mainly matters as a gate to rankings and as a screen for unknown schools.
8. **In France, check that a programme carries the state "grade de master", not just a school "MSc" label.** The July 2026 ministerial list shows HEC's Programme Grande École and most of its specialised diplomas with the grade de master, but its "Diplôme en innovation et entrepreneuriat" is only "visé Bac+5" (Bulletin officiel n°28, 9 July 2026) [data]. The CGE "MSc" label is a private association's quality mark on a school's own diploma (CGE, 2026) [data]. The body that advised the ministry, the CEFDG, was abolished by decree on 26 June 2026 (CEFDG, 2026) [data].
9. **For Italians who might ever sit a public competition (concorso), a foreign master's has no automatic legal value.** Using it for admission to a concorso needs an equivalence decision from the Dipartimento della Funzione Pubblica, with an opinion from the university ministry (MUR), under art. 38 of D.Lgs. 165/2001 (CIMEA, 2026) [data]. Private employers need no procedure.
10. **Within one country, the students' own preference ranking beats press rankings.** In France, the SIGEM ranking (derived from where double-admitted prépa students actually enrol) is described as more authoritative than any newspaper league table (Wikipédia "Sigem", citing Midena 2021 and Vuibert 2022) [practitioner consensus].

## 1. How the FT MiM and MiF rankings are built

**Structure.** Both FT pre-experience rankings combine an alumni survey (about 56% of the weight) and school-supplied data (about 44%), across 19 criteria. Alumni are surveyed three years after graduation: the 2026 editions surveyed the class of 2023 (Poets&Quants, 2026; Master Grad Schools, 2026) [practitioner consensus]. The FT's own methodology page is paywalled, so the weights below come from reliable secondary summaries.

| Element | FT MiM 2026 | FT MiF pre-experience 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Weighted salary (3 years out, PPP) | 15% | 16% |
| Salary increase | 9% | 10% |
| Other alumni "career" criteria (value for money, career progress, aims achieved, careers service, alumni network, employment at 3 months) | ~31% together | career progress 6%, value for money 6%, others smaller |
| School data (diversity, international mix, faculty doctorates, ESG teaching, carbon footprint) | ~44% | ~44% |
| Programmes ranked | 100 | 70 of 88 that entered |
| Eligibility | 20% alumni response and at least 20 responses | same; 2,700 graduates surveyed, 35% response |

Sources: Poets&Quants MiM 2026; Master Grad Schools MiF 2026 [practitioner consensus]. The 20% and 20-response rule has been stable since at least 2013, when the FT also stated that salaries are "converted to US dollars using purchasing power parity rates supplied by the International Monetary Fund", with non-profit, public-sector and student respondents removed and extreme values trimmed (FT methodology reproduced by Euro2day, 2013) [data, possibly stale on details].

**What that means for the reader.**
- **Most of the weight is not about careers.** Only about half the score measures pay and career outcomes. Diversity, international mix, ESG teaching and carbon footprint make up much of the rest. A school can rise by recruiting more international students or women faculty with no change in graduate outcomes.
- **PPP distorts cross-country comparison.** PPP divides local pay by local price levels. Switzerland has among the highest price levels in Europe, so a CHF salary becomes fewer PPP dollars than at market rates. India and Morocco have low price levels, so their salaries become many more PPP dollars. This is why IIM programmes post $136,000–$199,706 weighted salaries and why Rabat Business School ranks 10th with an 81.84% salary increase (FT 2026) [data]. **For a Swiss vs French comparison:** HSG's $150,490 against HEC's $137,760 understates the cash gap, because PPP shrinks Swiss pay more than French pay. This is my inference from how PPP works, not an FT statement; see money/salaries-and-roi.md for nominal pay.
- **"Salary increase" rewards low starting pay.** Luiss (65.01%) and Kozminski (78.72%) post bigger increases than Mannheim (27.86%) or WHU (32.18%), largely because they start from lower bases (FT 2026) [data].

## 2. Volatility and gaming

**Measured volatility, 2025 to 2026 (FT MiM)** (my calculations from the FT 2026 table) [data]:
- 85 programmes ranked in both years could be matched. 34 moved 10+ places, 27 moved 15+, and only 31 moved 5 places or fewer.
- Top 30: median move 4 places; only 3 moved 10+ (Vlerick 14→6, IESE 16→8, Paris School of Business 43→17).
- Biggest falls: Trinity College Dublin 38→68, EM Strasbourg 45→75, Cranfield 69→99, Burgundy 49→78, TUM 54→80, EBS 52→78. Biggest rises: Católica Porto 95→66, Antwerp 91→64, PSB 43→17, UCLouvain 74→48.
- 13 programmes had no 2025 rank: new entrants or returners such as RSM, Audencia, TBS, CBS, Aalto, King's and Georgetown.
- Longer run: EDHEC went 4th (2024) → 14th (2025) → 17th (2026). HEC Paris went 2nd → 2nd → 5th, while its programme barely changed.

Poets&Quants made the same point for 2025: 39 of 100 programmes rose or fell by double digits although the programmes were "essentially the same" as a year earlier (Poets&Quants, September 2025) [practitioner consensus].

**Why it is noisy.** Three mechanisms can be seen in the data:
1. **Small, self-selected samples.** Eligibility needs just 20% of alumni and 20 responses (FT/Master Grad Schools) [practitioner consensus]. For small programmes (HSG 58 students, WHU 57, Cologne 48), a few high earners move the mean.
2. **Participation churn.** When schools enter or leave, everyone below them shifts. Georgetown entered at 92nd with only 43% of its class surveyed (Poets&Quants, 2026) [practitioner consensus].
3. **Survey management by schools.** The TBS case (above) is the documented example. The FT's education editor said its policy is "not to comment on the status of individual schools" (Poets&Quants, September 2025) [practitioner consensus]. Poets&Quants reports that the FT has previously dropped schools over "suspicious replies" without announcing it (Poets&Quants, March 2025) [practitioner consensus].

**Non-obvious reading rule:** the FT's own 2026 tiering (Tier I = 8 schools; Tier II = ranks 9–40; Tier III = 41–91; Tier IV = 92–100) is a tacit admission that ranks within a tier are not meaningfully different.

## 3. Do employers use rankings?

The evidence is thinner than the ranking industry implies.
- **GMAC's Corporate Recruiters Survey 2026** (621 recruiters in 39 countries, fieldwork January–May 2026, run with EFMD and CSEA) does not ask how employers pick schools. It does show that employers' planned hiring of business master's graduates has repeatedly run ahead of what they actually hired. Twelve months earlier, 80% of employers had forecast hiring Master of Data Analytics graduates, but only 70% then did (GMAC CRS 2026 report) [data]. Treat school-quoted "recruiter demand" as an upper bound.
- **What drives campus recruiting is history and relationships.** This is practitioner consensus across careers offices and recruiters rather than published data: firms recruit where past hires succeeded and where they have a presence. A school's FT rank shifting 10 places does not change a bank's target list. See getting-in/employer-pipelines.md for named pipelines. [practitioner consensus, not verified in this session; see Claims to verify]
- **The one ranking family that directly asks employers is national.** Germany's WirtschaftsWoche university ranking for BWL ("Das sind die besten Unis und FHs für BWL-Studenten", 2024) is reportedly built from a survey of HR decision-makers (unverified; see Claims to verify). It ranks universities and Fachhochschulen separately, which suggests that German employers treat HAW graduates as a distinct hiring pool (WiWo, 2024; article paywalled, methodology in Claims to verify).
- **Rankings matter most to applicants and to school funding**, not directly to recruiters. They also matter to employers and governments who don't know the schools, for example sponsors of international scholarships and some non-EU employers comparing unfamiliar European schools [practitioner consensus; see Claims to verify].

**Implication.** For a European reader, "Is this school on the target list of the employers I want?" beats "Where is it in the FT?" The FT is useful mainly to rule out weak schools and to compare unfamiliar ones in the same tier.

## 5. The French grandes écoles system

- **The Programme Grande École (PGE) is the MiM.** The FT marks with ** the French MiMs that are PGEs, and they are large: ESCP 1,238 enrolled, Kedge 1,192, emlyon 1,087, EDHEC 1,048, ESSEC 897, HEC 684 (FT 2026) [data]. Compare HSG 58 or WHU 57. A French PGE is a mass programme with many tracks, entry points and campuses, so an average across it hides a lot.
- **Two main entry routes.** (a) After two years of classes préparatoires économiques et commerciales (ECG) through the BCE or Ecricome written exams. The BCE serves 18 grandes écoles de management and 3 associated schools (Concours BCE, 2026) [data]. Places after the exams are allocated by SIGEM, which is compulsory: "aucune inscription ne peut se faire directement dans une école" (Wikipédia "Sigem", 2026) [data]. (b) Admission parallèle / admission sur titre (AST) for students with a bachelor's (Bac+3) or earlier degrees, via banks such as Passerelle and Tremplin or school-specific processes. This is the route for almost all Italian and other international applicants (Wikipédia CPGE ECG, 2026) [data]. Application detail: getting-in/admissions.md.
- **SIGEM as a ranking.** Because double-admitted students reveal their preference, the "classement SIGEM" orders schools by where students actually go. The top five in 2021 were HEC, ESSEC, ESCP, EDHEC and emlyon (Wikipédia "Sigem", citing Le Monde 2023 and Midena 2021) [practitioner consensus]. Le Monde reported in 2023 that nearly half of business schools lacked enough prépa candidates (cited in the same article) [practitioner consensus; possibly stale].
- **How employers see AST vs prépa intake (non-obvious).** French alumni and recruiters often describe a status gap: prépa entrants are the "core" cohort, and AST/international entrants are sometimes seen as having taken an easier road. For internationals targeting jobs in France, fluent French and French internships matter far more than the entry route. I could not find a primary source quantifying this [anecdotal; see Claims to verify].
- **Press rankings.** Le Figaro Étudiant, L'Étudiant and Challenges publish annual business-school rankings; the SIGEM order is generally treated as more authoritative (Wikipédia "Sigem") [practitioner consensus]. Methodologies are in Claims to verify.

## 6. Accreditation

| Accreditor | What it certifies | Scale | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| AACSB (US) | A whole business school, against its Global Standards: "strategic management, learner success, thought leadership, and societal impact", via self-assessment plus peer review, "mission-driven" rather than a single template | 1,000+ accredited schools, 100+ countries | AACSB, 2026 [data] |
| EQUIS (EFMD, Belgium) | A whole school, by peer review with a senior corporate practitioner; requires e.g. a minimum core faculty of 25 FTE | EFMD itself says the label "is often a prerequisite for entry to rankings" | EFMD, 2026 [data] |
| AMBA (UK) | Specific programmes (MBA, DBA, Master's in Management), stressing employability and learning outcomes; claims to accredit "the top 2%" of postgraduate business programmes; has removed its former cap of 300 accredited schools | n/a on page | AMBA, 2026 [data] |
| Triple crown | Holding all three | 149 schools, 7 Feb 2026 | Wikipedia, citing MBA Today [data] |

**What this means in practice:**
- **Accreditation checks process and resources, not graduate outcomes.** None of the three certifies salaries or placement quality.
- **Most European schools the reader will consider already hold it.** The triple-crown list includes St Gallen, Mannheim Business School, TUM, SDA Bocconi, Luiss Business School, POLIMI Graduate School of Management, HEC Paris, ESSEC, EDHEC, emlyon, Nova, Católica Lisbon, Esade, IE, RSM, CBS, WU, LBS, Imperial, Warwick and Manchester (Wikipedia, 2026) [data]. **Absent from it:** LSE, Oxford Saïd and Cambridge Judge. So "triple crown = top 1%" (the marketing line used in school press releases) is not a quality ranking.
- **The real power of accreditation is as a gate.** The FT only ranks accredited programmes (Master Grad Schools, 2026) [practitioner consensus], so unaccredited schools cannot appear at all. Accreditation also screens unknown schools: if a school you have never heard of has none of AACSB, EQUIS or AMBA and no national state recognition, treat that as a red flag.
- **Employer or government requirements.** I found no European employer that states on the record that it requires AACSB/EQUIS/AMBA. Claims that some governments or employers (e.g. in India, China or the Gulf) value triple crown for hiring or recognition are in Claims to verify.

**Germany.** German bachelor's and master's programmes at state or state-recognised universities are accredited through the Stiftung Akkreditierungsrat. A university hires an approved agency; a peer panel, in which professors hold the majority of votes and which includes a practitioner, reviews the programme. The Akkreditierungsrat then decides, publishes the report and grants accreditation for eight years (Akkreditierungsrat, 2026) [data]. Universities can instead obtain "system accreditation" and accredit their own programmes. The practical check for a German private school is whether it is staatlich anerkannt (state-recognised) and whether the programme carries the Akkreditierungsrat seal.

## 7. Degree recognition

**Bologna.** All the European schools on the Admetia list sit in the European Higher Education Area (EHEA), so a 120-ECTS master's after a 180-ECTS bachelor's is structurally comparable. Bologna makes recognition easier but not automatic, especially for the legal uses below.

**France: grade vs visa vs label.**
- The **grade de master** is a state degree level. It can be conferred on national master's degrees and on "certain business schools'" own diplomas (Wikipédia "Grade de master", 2026) [data].
- The ministry's 25 June 2026 order (BO n°28, 9 July 2026) lists which private and consular management-school diplomas are "visé" (state-endorsed) and which also confer the grade de licence or master. In that list, 94 entries carry the grade de master and 21 carry only a Bac+5 visa (my count, BO n°28) [data]. Examples: HEC Programme Grande École, grade de master 2024–2029; HEC "Diplôme en finance internationale", grade de master 2025–2030; HEC "Diplôme en innovation et entrepreneuriat", visa Bac+5 only; EDHEC Programme Grande École, grade de master 2025–2030; an EDHEC "Diplôme supérieur en gestion des affaires et des équipes", visa Bac+5 only [data].
- The **CEFDG**, which evaluated these programmes, "est supprimée par le décret n°2026-551 en date du 26 juin 2026" (CEFDG, 2026) [data]. What replaces its evaluation role was not clear on the page; watch this for 2027 (Claims to verify).
- The **CGE "MSc-Master of Science" label** is awarded by the Conférence des grandes écoles (an association) to a school's own Bac+5 diploma with at least 50% of teaching in a foreign language; about 150 programmes hold it. The **Mastère Spécialisé** label (340+ programmes) is a post-Bac+5 specialisation (CGE, 2026) [data]. Neither label is, by itself, the state grade de master.

**Italy: valore legale.**
- Italian degrees are conferred "in nome della Legge". The 1933 Testo unico states that foreign academic titles have no legal value in Italy "salvo il caso di legge speciale" (Wikipedia IT, citing R.D. 1592/1933) [data]. Italy ratified the Lisbon Recognition Convention by Law 148/2002, which replaced old-style *equipollenza* with purpose-specific recognition (CIMEA, 2026) [data].
- By purpose (CIMEA, 2026) [data]:
  - *Public competitions (concorsi):* equivalence from the Presidenza del Consiglio, Dipartimento della Funzione Pubblica, after an opinion from MUR (art. 38 D.Lgs. 165/2001; DPR 189/2009).
  - *Points in public-competition rankings / public-sector career progression:* MUR, via the administration concerned.
  - *Regulated professions (e.g. accountant or engineer registers):* the ministry that oversees the profession.
  - *Non-regulated jobs in the private sector:* no formal procedure listed.
- **Exception worth knowing:** a double degree that includes an Italian university can end in a laurea magistrale (for example the ESCP–Politecnico di Torino double degree for engineering students; see decisions/programme-choice.md section 4). A standard ESCP MiM does not.
- **The 300-ECTS expectation in Germany.** The KMK's common structural guidelines expect a master's to bring the total, including the bachelor's, to 300 ECTS; a three-year bachelor's (180) plus a one-year master's (60 to 90) falls short. The guidelines allow deviations, and doctoral admission is decided by each university's doctoral regulations, so a shortfall usually means extra coursework or a case-by-case decision rather than a legal bar; check the specific Promotionsordnung before relying on a one-year master's for a German PhD (KMK structural guidelines and university commentaries) [data, interpretation practitioner consensus]. A claim that it legally bars German PhD admission or the higher civil service was not confirmed.
- **Online degrees and work rights.** A degree studied online from abroad does not give a UK Student visa and therefore no Graduate route (which requires a current Student visa held in the UK), and a US online degree gives no F-1 status and therefore no OPT. Online formats also usually exclude on-campus recruiting. They can make sense for people who already have a job and the right to work where they are [data for the UK eligibility rule; the rest practitioner consensus].
- **Practical rule:** an Italian choosing between Bocconi and, say, ESCP or HSG does not lose anything with private employers. They do add a bureaucratic step if they later want Banca d'Italia, CONSOB, ministries or other concorsi (see places/italy-playbook.md).

## 8. Private-school quality signals

**For-profit groups.** Galileo Global Education describes itself as a player in "independent higher education" with 300,000 students, 65 schools and universities, 110+ campuses and 20 markets (GGE, 2026) [data]. Omnes Education is a similar group (Claims to verify for its scale and ownership). For-profit ownership is not in itself a quality verdict: the FT 2026 table ranks Paris School of Business 17th (Claims to verify: PSB's current group ownership). But PSB is also a warning case. It ranks 17th with a careers-service rank of 99 and an alumni-network rank of 99 out of 100, and only 47 students, yet a career-progress rank of 2 and a 66.83% salary increase (FT 2026) [data]. A small, self-reported alumni sample can push a school up the table even when the parts that matter for job-hunting are weak.

**How to spot a "cash cow" MSc at a big-name school** (checklist built from the FT columns and the French BO data):
1. **Is it the flagship?** For French schools, the PGE carries the grade de master. Many specialised MSc and "Diplôme" programmes also do, but some are visa-only (BO n°28) [data]. Ask for the diploma's exact status and the RNCP/visa line.
2. **Class size vs the flagship.** Several PGEs enrol 900–1,200 students per year (FT 2026) [data]. A standalone MSc with a large intake and a one-year format is more likely to be revenue-led. Ask for the intake and number of applicants.
3. **Coverage of the employment report.** Prefer reports that give the response rate and follow a recognised reporting standard (such as MBA CSEA's). Discount any "100% employed" figure that covers under about 70% of the class (e.g. Bocconi 100 (55), FT 2026) [data].
4. **Concentration of one nationality.** A programme where most students come from a single overseas market may be recruiting for fee income. The FT publishes only the overall "international students %", so ask the school for the nationality split [practitioner consensus; see Claims to verify].
5. **Is the programme itself in the FT or the school's accreditation scope?** The FT ranks one programme per school per ranking (e.g. HEC's MiM, not its other MSc programmes). The brand's rank does not transfer to sister programmes.
6. **State recognition in the home country:** grade de master (France), staatlich anerkannt plus the Akkreditierungsrat seal (Germany), a MUR-recognised title (Italy).

## Hypotheses tested

**H1. "FT rank differences of less than ~15 places are noise."** Partly supported. Within the top 30, year-on-year moves are small (median 4), so a 15-place gap there (e.g. 5th vs 20th) is probably real. Below 30th, 15+ place swings are common (27 of 85 schools in 2026), so gaps of 15–20 places are within noise. Fails when one school is Tier I and the other Tier III. Evidence: FT 2026 table, my calculation [data]; Poets&Quants 2025, 39 of 100 moved by double digits [practitioner consensus].

**H2. "A low-tuition public school at similar selectivity matches private schools on outcomes."** Partly supported. It holds in Germany (Mannheim, Cologne, TUM ≈ Frankfurt School and EBS; below WHU/HHL) and Switzerland (HSG 1st), and for Polimi vs Luiss in Italy. It fails for Polimi vs Bocconi (PPP salary $74k vs $109k), where Bocconi's selectivity and pipeline dominate. Conditions: the public school must itself be the national elite option (HSG, Mannheim, WU), not a mass university. Evidence: FT 2026 [data]; Mannheim, Bocconi and Polimi fee pages [data].

**H3. "Big-name schools run lower-selectivity MSc 'cash cows' that trade on the brand."** Insufficient evidence for selectivity (I could not fetch acceptance rates). There is supporting indirect evidence: very large French PGE and MSc intakes, and some Bac+5 diplomas at top schools that do not confer the grade de master (HEC and EDHEC examples, BO n°28) [data]. It fails as a blanket claim: many specialised MScs at HEC and EDHEC do carry the grade de master.

**H4. "Employers rarely check accreditation."** Partly supported. I found no employer stating on the record that it requires AACSB/EQUIS/AMBA, and top recruiting schools (LSE, Oxford, Cambridge) lack the triple crown (Wikipedia list, 2026) [data]. Accreditation still matters indirectly as the gate to the FT and as a screen for unknown schools (EFMD, 2026) [data]. Fails for some non-EU contexts, where it may be checked (unverified).

**H5. "The FT's PPP salary adjustment flatters schools in low-cost countries."** Supported. IIM programmes show $136k–$200k PPP (FT 2026) [data] against actual starting pay under $45k for IIMA (Poets&Quants, 2025) [practitioner consensus]; Rabat, Kozminski and Ljubljana also rank well on salary increase. The reverse also holds: PPP deflates Swiss salaries. Conditions: comparing across countries. It does not distort comparisons within one country.

**H6 (own). "Employment-rate columns are unreliable because coverage varies hugely."** Supported. Coverage runs from 100% (HSG, ESCP) to 55% (Bocconi), 46% (HEC Lausanne) and 15% (TUM) (FT 2026) [data]. Where coverage is low, the headline rate is not comparable.

**H7 (own). "FT position tracks survey and diversity criteria more than recruiter prestige, especially in finance."** Supported. Skema ranks 2nd in the MiF, above HEC (7th) and LBS (9th) (Poets&Quants, 2026) [practitioner consensus]. PSB ranks 17th in the MiM despite careers-service and alumni-network ranks of 99 (FT 2026) [data]. About 44% of the weight is school-supplied non-career data [practitioner consensus]. Where it fails: the top 5–10 do broadly match recruiter perception.

**H8 (own). "In France, the state grade de master separates programmes even within the same top school."** Supported. HEC and EDHEC each have at least one Bac+5 diploma with a visa but no grade, while their PGEs carry the grade (BO n°28, 2026) [data]. It matters for PhD entry, French public-sector competitions and some foreign recognition. It matters little for private-sector hiring.

**H9 (own). "Swiss applied-sciences universities (HAW/FH) are a cheap route into Swiss salaries."** Partly supported. ZHAW's MSc International Business posts a $103,114 PPP salary, level with HEC Lausanne, with a value-for-money rank of 3 (FT 2026) [data]. But the FT covers only one ZHAW programme, and graduates' access to top consulting and banking firms is unverified (see places/countries-and-cities.md and places/visas-and-work-rights.md for Swiss work rights).

## Common mistakes and myths

- **"Ranked 12th beats ranked 20th."** Within one FT tier, differences are mostly noise, and a single year's move can be 10–30 places for unchanged programmes (FT 2026; Poets&Quants 2025) [data / practitioner consensus].
- **Reading the FT salary column as cash.** It is PPP-adjusted and measured three years after graduation. Use national statistics or school reports for cash pay (money/salaries-and-roi.md) [data].
- **"Not in the FT means weak."** LSE, Oxford, Cambridge, UZH, ETH and Dauphine are absent from the FT MiM. Absence usually means the school did not enter or the programme does not fit the format (FT 2026) [data].
- **"Triple crown = top 1%, so it's elite."** 149 schools hold it, while several of the most sought-after programmes do not (Wikipedia, 2026) [data].
- **Confusing "MSc" or "Master" titles in France.** A "Master of Science" can be a CGE-labelled school diploma, a visa-only Bac+5, or a grade de master programme. Only the last is the state master's level (CGE; BO n°28) [data].
- **Italians assuming a foreign master's counts for concorsi automatically.** It needs a Funzione Pubblica equivalence (CIMEA, 2026) [data].
- **Trusting "100% employed" without the coverage figure** (FT 2026: Bocconi 100 (55)) [data].
- **Using the MiM ranking to choose a finance master's, or vice versa.** The FT MiF and MiM have different participants and results. Neither shows front-office finance placement (see careers/finance.md) [practitioner consensus].

## Decision rules

1. **If two schools are in the same FT tier and differ by fewer than 15 places, ignore the rank gap** and decide on target-employer access, city and cost (because year-to-year noise is that large below the top 30).
2. **If a school's FT employment coverage (the bracketed figure) is below 70%, discount its employment rate** and ask for the full employment report with response rate (because non-respondents are disproportionately unplaced).
3. **If comparing salaries across countries using FT data, compare only within the same country**, or convert with national data (because PPP inflates cheap-country figures and deflates Swiss ones).
4. **If the programme is French, check the latest Bulletin officiel list for "grade Master"**, and prefer it over a visa-only Bac+5 when you might pursue a PhD, French public-sector jobs or foreign recognition (because only the grade is the state master's level).
5. **If you are Italian and a public-sector career (Banca d'Italia, CONSOB, ministries, public competitions) is a realistic option, prefer an Italian degree, or budget time for Funzione Pubblica equivalence** (because foreign titles have no automatic legal value).
6. **If a school is unknown to you and has neither AACSB/EQUIS/AMBA nor national state recognition, drop it** (because accreditation is the minimum screen and the gate to all major rankings).
7. **If a school is well known to your target employers, do not pay extra for accreditation badges** (because recruiters use pipelines, not accreditation lists).
8. **If choosing between German public (Mannheim, Cologne, TUM) and German private (Frankfurt School, EBS, ESMT) as an EU citizen, default to public unless the private school has a named pipeline you want** (because FT PPP salaries are similar or higher at the public schools and fees are about EUR 194 per semester vs five-figure private fees).
9. **If choosing between Bocconi and Politecnico for a management master's, pick Bocconi for finance or consulting and Polimi for operations, tech or industrial roles and cost** (because the FT salary gap is large while Polimi is roughly a fifth of the price for high-ISEE families; see money/costs-and-funding.md).
10. **If a ranking is dominated by one country's schools (France in the FT MiF), check what recruiters in your target city think** before trusting it (because participation and diversity criteria drive placement in the table).
11. **If a big-name school offers you a standalone MSc with a large intake, a one-year format and rolling admissions, ask for that programme's own employment report and its visa/grade status** (because the brand's FT rank belongs to its flagship programme).
12. **If you are a non-EU applicant comparing unfamiliar European schools, use FT tier plus accreditation as a first filter**, then check the visa rules in places/visas-and-work-rights.md (because this is where rankings genuinely help people who do not know the schools).
