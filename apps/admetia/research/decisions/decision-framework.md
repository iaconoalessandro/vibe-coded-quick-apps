# Decision framework

> **Not legal, tax or immigration advice.** This file summarises official sources as of the dates given. Rules change and individual cases differ: confirm with the competent authority or a qualified adviser before acting.

## Bottom line

1. **Decide in this order: constraints → target career → target work country → programme type and format → school → timing → funding → execution calendar.** Most students do it backwards: they pick the highest-ranked school they can get into, then discover it doesn't recruit into the job, city or language they need. The research consistently shows that *where you can work* and *which pipeline you enter* move outcomes more than a few ranking places do.
2. **Two constraints, citizenship and languages, prune more options than any other factor.** Since Brexit, an EU citizen needs a UK visa exactly like an Indian or Brazilian graduate. German-speaking MBB offices treat fluent German as mandatory. Only 2–3% of German job postings waive German (places/visas-and-work-rights.md; careers/consulting.md; places/countries-and-cities.md).
3. **The internship is the hiring channel, not the degree.** In France, 42.3% of grande-école graduates were hired through their final internship or apprenticeship host (CGE 2026). Goldman's intern class (~2,500) is the same size as its whole entry-level intake (Fortune, Jun 2026). Choose programmes by how they get you an internship in your target city in time.
4. **Rank differences below the top 30 of the FT are mostly noise.** Across the FT MiM table the median programme moved 7 places in one year (top 30: 4), and 27 of 85 moved 15 or more. The FT's salary figure is a PPP-adjusted mean three years out, not a starting salary (decisions/school-types-and-accreditation.md; money/salaries-and-roi.md).
5. **The pre-experience window is narrow and closes on graduation date, not age.** Graduate-scheme caps run 2–3 years after graduation (Unilever 24 months, L'Oréal UK 2 years, LVMH 3 years), as does the LBS MiM window (2 years). Deferred-MBA schemes (HBS 2+2, Stanford, Wharton Moelis) exclude anyone who worked full-time between degrees (decisions/timing-and-sequencing.md; careers/marketing.md).
6. **For an EU citizen, cost differs by up to 100× for programmes in the same league**: CBS/SSE €0, Mannheim ~€194 a semester, RSM €2,771, HEC MiM €58,970, LBS MiM £52,950. Starting pay differs more by *country of work* than by school. A Swiss public-university economics master's earns a median CHF 87,100 one year out, against an LBS MiM UK mean base of £44,675 (money/costs-and-funding.md; money/salaries-and-roi.md).
7. **The market has tightened at the entry level, and the channel matters more than ever.** There are 140 applications per UK graduate vacancy (290 in FMCG). French six-month employment fell to 76%. One in three employers report replacing some entry-level work with AI (GMAC 2026). Referrals, internship conversion and school pipelines beat the open funnel (evidence/trends.md; getting-in/breaking-in.md).
8. **Time-sensitive (as of 1 October 2026):**
   - HEC MiM round 1 closes on 7 October 2026.
   - Bocconi's international round 1 closes on 29 October, with the test booked by 26 October.
   - Bocconi's round for Italian non-Bocconi graduates closes on 2 November.
   - UK Graduate visa applications made on or before 31 December 2026 get 24 months instead of 18.
   - London 2027 summer internship windows are closing now: Bank of America London IB closes on 11 October 2026.

   Sources: getting-in/admissions.md, places/visas-and-work-rights.md, getting-in/recruiting-calendar.md.

## Step −1. Check whether you need a master's at all

On the Continent (Italy, France, DACH), a master's is the usual entry credential for business roles. In the UK and US, many schemes hire bachelor's graduates at the same grade, so the master's mainly buys access, timing and a visa. Read decisions/should-you-do-a-masters.md before committing tuition. Read every school claim with evidence/how-numbers-mislead.md at hand.

## Step 0. Write down your constraints first

| Constraint | Why it comes first | Where the detail is |
|---|---|---|

| **Languages at B2/C1+** | DACH MBB requires fluent German. P&G Germany wants "solid German". Ferrero wants Italian. Milan and Frankfurt IB expect the local language. | **Budget, and whether you can borrow in euros** | Tuition ranges from €0 to about €60k. Living costs often exceed tuition on two-year programmes. FX-denominated loans add risk if you'll earn euros | money/costs-and-funding.md |
| **Graduation date and work history** | Experience caps count from graduation. Any full-time job between degrees ends deferred-MBA eligibility | decisions/timing-and-sequencing.md |

## Step 1. Pick a target career family (or two adjacent ones)

The master's should serve a career, not the other way round. If you can't choose, choose *adjacent* families (consulting + corporate strategy; IB + corporate banking; FMCG brand + consulting). A generalist MiM keeps those open.

**Rules for Step 1**
- R1.1 **If** you rank two families close, **pick** the one whose entry door you can still reach this cycle (internship windows, experience caps), because missing a door costs a year. Doors that stay open longer (corporate programmes, Big 4, off-cycle boutiques) are better second choices than doors that close (spring weeks, deferred MBA).
- R1.2 **If** junior work in your target family is mostly production (audit, content, routine reporting), **pair** it with a skill AI complements (client work, SQL/modelling on real data, sales), because hiring is falling through fewer hires, not lower wages (evidence/trends.md; Stanford "Canaries", rev. Aug 2026: −19% relative employment for 22–25s in exposed occupations).
- R1.3 **If** pay is your main criterion, **compare** "net minus rent" in the city you'll work in, not gross salary (money/salaries-and-roi.md R9).

## Step 2. Pick the country or city where you want to *work*, then study there or in a feeder

Graduates mostly work where they studied, unless they're in a mobile finance track. Five years out, the Netherlands keeps only 20.3% of EEA international graduates (Nuffic 2025). Only 38% of SSE's international MSc graduates work in Sweden. At Bocconi, 63.9% of employed MSc Finance graduates work abroad, against 38.4% of Marketing Management graduates (places/countries-and-cities.md; getting-in/employer-pipelines.md).

**Rules for Step 2**
- R2.1 **If** you're an EU citizen without the local language and want Germany, France, Italy or Spain, **choose** a school there only if you'll reach B2/C1 before recruiting starts. Otherwise **target** London, Dublin, Amsterdam, Luxembourg or your home country (places/countries-and-cities.md R1).
- R2.2 **If** your target is London finance or consulting, **study in London or at a school whose finance graduates mostly go abroad** (Bocconi MSc Finance, HEC, HSG), and budget for the UK visa route as an EU citizen (places/visas-and-work-rights.md).
- R2.3 **If** you want Swiss pay, **study at a Swiss public university or recruit into Swiss offices from one**, because the premium is a *location* premium. Swiss applied-sciences bachelor's graduates earn CHF 80,300, against CHF 80,900 for university master's graduates (BFS 2024). Non-EU graduates without experience can't count on Swiss permits (money/salaries-and-roi.md H1, H7).
- R2.4 **If** you're Italian and plan to return, **prefer** a foreign school with Milan placement, or a Bocconi/Polimi double degree. Keep in mind the impatriati regime, now art. 225 of D.Lgs. 117/2026: 50% of income taxable (40% with a minor child, i.e. 60% exempt; see `visas_immigration/italy/italy_visas_immigration_guide.md` §Caso 9) for five years, capped at €600k. It requires at least 3 tax years abroad and a 4-year commitment to stay. **Returning to the same employer or group needs 6–7 years abroad**, so the classic "London office → Milan office" transfer after 3 years does *not* qualify; switch employer when you return (places/italy-playbook.md).
- R2.5 **If** you're non-EU, **weight post-study work rights above ranking**:
  - Germany: 18 months of open job search.
  - Netherlands and France: 1 year each.
  - Ireland: 12+12 months after a master's.
  - Switzerland: only 6 months, then quotas.
  - UK: 18 months from 2027.

  The UK's sponsor-free HPI route covers TUM, ETH, PSL, Amsterdam and many US schools, but *not* HEC, Bocconi, St. Gallen or any UK school (places/visas-and-work-rights.md).

## Step 3. Choose the programme type and format

**Rules for Step 3** (decisions/programme-choice.md unless noted)
- R3.1 **If** undecided between consulting, finance and tech, **prefer** a generalist MiM. In GMAC's 2026 survey, 73% of employers hired MiM graduates in 2025, the highest of any pre-experience specialised master's (MFin 68%, business analytics 68%, marketing 66%). Note: GMAC also shows employers over-predict business-master's hiring every year.
- R3.2 **If** the target is London front-office IB and you're admitted to both a MiM and a finance master's at the same school, **prefer** the finance master's (LBS 2025: MFA 50% to IB vs MiM 14%; mean base £53,866 vs £41,229).
- R3.3 **If** the target is marketing, **don't assume** an MSc Marketing is required. FMCG schemes accept all disciplines. At Warwick, MSc Management reported 91% employed at $67k PPP against 72% at $60k for MSc Marketing & Strategy (careers/marketing.md).
- R3.4 **If** you have fewer than 2 relevant internships, **prefer** a two-year continental programme with a gap year, internship semester or final stage over a one-year UK MSc, unless you can recruit from week one. London windows open in July and fill on a rolling basis (getting-in/recruiting-calendar.md).
- R3.5 **If** you already have ≥6 months of relevant experience, **prefer** the one-year format. It saves a year of cost and is designed for you.
- R3.6 **If** your home school is a CEMS member and you want international consulting/FMCG/industry in Europe or Asia, **add CEMS**. If you target US banks, skip it.
- R3.7 **If** a double degree adds a year and a second fee, **take it only** to work in the partner's country.
- R3.8 **If** you want luxury, sports or sustainability, **prefer** a specialisation inside a strong generalist programme over a stand-alone niche master's at a weaker school.
- R3.9 **If** you're French or French-speaking and admitted to ESSEC's Flexible track or emlyon, **evaluate the apprenticeship** before borrowing (tuition paid plus a salary). Note that only 37.9% of management apprentices are hired by their host (CGE 2026), so choose the host as if it were your employer.

## Step 4. Choose the school: pipeline first, then cost, then rank

**Rules for Step 4**
- R4.1 **If** two schools are in the same FT tier and within ~15 places, **ignore the rank gap** and decide on target-employer access, city and cost (decisions/school-types-and-accreditation.md R1).
- R4.2 **If** a school's FT employment coverage is below 70%, **discount** its employment rate. Bocconi's "100% employed" covers 55% of the class (FT 2026).
- R4.3 **If** comparing salaries across countries via the FT, **compare within a country only**, because the FT figure is PPP-adjusted.
- R4.4 **If** you're an EU citizen choosing between German public (Mannheim, TUM, Cologne) and private schools, **default to public** unless the private school has a named pipeline you want. FT PPP salaries are similar or higher, and fees are about €194 a semester.
- R4.5 **If** you're choosing between Bocconi and Politecnico for a management master's, **pick Bocconi** for finance or consulting, and **Polimi** for operations, tech, industry or cost.
- R4.6 **If** you want German industry (auto, suppliers, machinery), **prefer** a German-language master's near the cluster plus a Werkstudent role or company thesis at the target firm. These firms hire from their own interns and dual students (about 70% of DHBW dual graduates are taken on by their partner) (getting-in/employer-pipelines.md).
- R4.7 **If** you want Swiss banking, **prefer** HSG over a higher-ranked non-Swiss school (HSG Banking Days and similar pipelines).
- R4.8 **If** you choose RSM, **expect** a Dutch Big 4/bank pipeline (top five employers: Deloitte, PwC, EY, KPMG, Rabobank), not a London IB one.
- R4.9 **If** a big-name school offers a large-intake standalone MSc with rolling admissions, **ask for that programme's own employment report**. The FT rank belongs to the flagship.
- R4.10 **If** a programme is French, **check** the Bulletin officiel for "grade de master" when a PhD, French public sector or foreign recognition matters. A visa-only Bac+5 isn't the state master's level.
- R4.11 **If** you're Italian and might sit public competitions (Banca d'Italia, CONSOB, ministries), **prefer** an Italian degree or budget time for Funzione Pubblica equivalence. A foreign degree has no automatic legal value there.

## Step 5. Timing: now, later, or twice?

**Rules for Step 5** (decisions/timing-and-sequencing.md unless noted)
- R5.1 **If** you're in your bachelor's final year, have strong grades and want a top US MBA eventually, **go straight into a MiM and apply to deferred MBAs in its final year**. Any full-time work in between ends eligibility. HBS 2+2 had 1,463 applicants and 131 committed (2025).
- R5.2 **If** a pre-master's year would be unrelated work, **prefer** a programme with built-in experience (HEC gap year, ESCP gap semesters, Imperial 16-month option).
- R5.3 **If** you're Italian with an on-time triennale and aim for London or Paris finance or consulting, **prefer** going abroad straight away over a laurea magistrale followed by a foreign master's. The second route adds about two years of age without adding work experience. Italian economics LM graduates average 26.3 at graduation (AlmaLaurea).
- R5.4 **If** you already hold a laurea magistrale, **do a second master's only** if it opens a named pipeline you can't reach otherwise (London banking recruiting, a UK Graduate visa, quant finance).
- R5.5 **If** a recession hits while you're deciding, **don't start** a master's you wouldn't otherwise do; **do bring forward** one you'd planned. Recession entry costs about 9% of early earnings, halving in 5 years and gone by about 10, mostly through a weaker first employer (Oreopoulos, von Wachter & Heisz).
- R5.6 **If** you finish without an offer, **don't take** more than 12–18 months of unrelated work before reapplying to graduate schemes. Unilever caps experience at 24 months after graduation, and P&G reroutes anyone with 2+ years (careers/marketing.md).
- R5.7 **If** you graduated more than 18 months ago and want the LBS MiM, **apply this cycle**. The window closes 2 years after graduation.

## Step 6. Fund it

**Rules for Step 6** (money/costs-and-funding.md unless noted)
- R6.1 **If** cost binds and you're an EU citizen, **shortlist at least one** of Mannheim, TUM, CBS, SSE or RSM (tuition €0–2,771).
- R6.2 **If** you're Italian and considering Bocconi, **apply** for Bocconi4Access (20/60/100% waivers) and the regional ISU/DSU grant even if you doubt you'll qualify.
- R6.3 **If** you want merit money, **apply in the first round**. Bocconi, Oxford MFE and ESSEC's Eiffel nominations favour or require early rounds.
- R6.4 **If** you'll earn in euros, **prefer** a euro loan (e.g. Bocconi–Intesa, the French state-guaranteed loan) over GBP/USD debt.
- R6.5 **If** you hold two offers with deposits, **price the forfeit** before paying the second (LBS £8,000; ESSEC €6,000; HEC €5,000; LSE 10–15% non-refundable). Bocconi's first instalment is refundable within windows (getting-in/admissions.md).
- R6.6 **If** a private-school award is under ~25% and you have a competing offer, **ask for a review politely with documents but budget as if the answer is no**. No school we read commits to matching offers, and Bocconi calls decisions "final and unquestionable" (thin).
- R6.7 **If** comparing a one-year and a two-year programme at similar fees, **add 11 months of living costs** to the two-year one.

## Step 7. Execute: work backwards from the recruiting calendar

**Rules for Step 7** (getting-in/recruiting-calendar.md, getting-in/breaking-in.md)
- R7.1 **If** you start a one-year UK MSc in September and target London IB or consulting, **finish** your CV, a 25–40-firm target list and online-test practice **by 31 July** before arrival.
- R7.2 **If** a firm recruits on a rolling or first-come basis, **apply within 2–3 weeks of opening** (direction is employer-stated; the size of the advantage is unquantified).
- R7.3 **If** you haven't practised a firm's test format, **practise first**. A failed test can bar you for 6–12 months (EY, P&G).
- R7.4 **If** you're from a non-target school applying to a test-gated scheme, **get one referral per firm plus heavy test prep**, not mass cold emails. Referred candidates were 6% of applicants but 21% of interviewees and 29% of hires in Brown, Setren & Topa (2016).
- R7.5 **If** choosing whom to contact, **prefer** moderately weak ties (about 10 mutual connections: alumni, former employer, home town). In the MIT/LinkedIn experiment (Science 2022) these produced the most job moves.
- R7.6 **If** you apply to UK or US offices, **use** a one-page CV with no photo and no date of birth. **If** you apply to a local German employer, **follow German norms** (reference letters; a photo is common).
- R7.7 **If** your name signals a visible-minority origin and you apply in France or the UK, **plan** for more applications per callback, and front-load third-party signals (known employers, references). The UK CSI Nuffield field experiment found callback ratios of up to ~2× by origin.
- R7.8 **If** you missed the bulge-bracket summer window, **pivot** to graduate analyst applications (Sept–Nov) plus boutique and off-cycle applications from January.

## If you are still in your bachelor's (years 1–3): what to do now

This was added after the gap audit, because a first-year student's real question is "what should I do *this year*?". Every rule draws on facts in the named files.

- B1. **Grades in every exam count, not just the final mark.**
  - Bocconi ranks Italian applicants on the weighted average of exams registered by the round deadline, not on the final 110 mark.
  - LSE's published 2:1 floor for Italians is 104/110, but most MSc Finance admits hold a First, equated to 110/110.

  (getting-in/admissions.md)
- B2. **Collect the credits the gates ask for.** Statistics and quantitative ECTS (e.g. RSM 10 EC of quantitative methods, Stockholm MSc Finance 30 ECTS) and business ECTS are pass/fail gates that no test score repairs. Choose electives with them in mind. The examples come from the calculator's own published gates: data/masters-model.js and docs/VERIFICATION.md §5, sourced OFF to each school's page. See also getting-in/admissions.md §6.
- B3. **Apply to spring weeks and insight programmes while you are an undergraduate.** They are undergraduate-only (Deloitte "Spring into Deloitte", Deutsche Bank insight programmes). UK spring-week return offers fell from 31% to 20% but still exist (getting-in/recruiting-calendar.md; careers/finance.md).
- B4. **Get a language to B2/C1 if you want DACH, France, Italy or Spain.**
  - Fluent German is mandatory at McKinsey DACH.
  - P&G Germany asks for "solid German".
  - Only 2.4–3.4% of German postings waive German.

  (careers/consulting.md; careers/marketing.md; places/countries-and-cities.md)
- B5. **Do at least one exchange or internship abroad of ≥4 months.** Siemens and Bosch list it as a requirement for their graduate programmes (careers/accounting-and-corporate.md).
- B6. **Do one internship before the master's if you can.** A one-year UK MSc assumes you arrive ready to recruit (ESSEC's one-year track asks for ≥6 months' experience), and London windows open in July (decisions/programme-choice.md; getting-in/recruiting-calendar.md).
- B7. **Graduate on time.** Italian economics LM graduates average 26.3 at graduation, and only 61.3% of triennale graduates finish on time (AlmaLaurea). Lost semesters push you towards experience caps and age norms (decisions/timing-and-sequencing.md).
- B8. **If you might want a top US MBA**, don't take a full-time job between bachelor's and master's. Deferred-MBA schemes accept final-year master's students only if they had no full-time work in between (decisions/timing-and-sequencing.md).
- B9. **If you are 18–19, German-speaking and want German industry**, compare dual study at a target firm with a university bachelor's. About 70% of DHBW dual graduates are taken on by their partner company (getting-in/employer-pipelines.md).

## If your school is not a "target" (and for readers outside the elite schools this data covers)

The library's school-level data comes mostly from the ~70 elite programmes the calculator scores. Most students attend other schools. What transfers:

- N1. **Channel beats brand.** Referrals moved candidates from 6% of applicants to 29% of hires in one large-firm study, and 42.3% of French grande-école hires came through the final internship or apprenticeship. These channels are open to non-targets (getting-in/breaking-in.md; getting-in/employer-pipelines.md).
- N2. **Use moderately weak ties.** Contact alumni and second-degree connections with about 10 mutual connections first (getting-in/breaking-in.md).
- N3. **For IB, go through boutiques and off-cycle roles, one tier at a time** (boutique → middle market → bulge bracket), and do it within 1–3 years. Boutique offers take a few dozen to 50+ contacts, against 150–200 at large banks (practitioner source, anecdotal). For non-EU candidates this route is weaker, because boutiques rarely sponsor (getting-in/breaking-in.md).
- N4. **A target master's "re-targets" you only with an internship before or during it**, ideally in the hiring city (getting-in/breaking-in.md H1, practitioner consensus).
- N5. **Regional and industrial pipelines don't care much about business-school prestige.** German industry, French corporates via alternance, and Swiss and Dutch firms recruit from their own interns and local schools. A strong local school plus a company thesis can beat a distant brand for those employers (getting-in/employer-pipelines.md, H2).
- N6. **Fill in contextual-recruitment forms in the UK.** Firms using Rare (e.g. EY) credit over-achievement relative to school context, but still screen tests and A-levels (getting-in/employer-pipelines.md).
- N7. **Expect "skills-based hiring" to change little.** In the US, 45% of firms that dropped degree requirements changed nothing in practice (getting-in/employer-pipelines.md).

## The myths that most often derail students (consolidated, ranked by damage)

| # | Myth | Reality (evidence) | File |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "As an EU citizen I can just work in London after my LSE MSc." | You need a Graduate or Skilled Worker visa. From 1 Jan 2027 the Graduate visa lasts 18 months. A UK–EU youth scheme isn't agreed. | places/visas-and-work-rights.md |
| 2 | "I'll do the master's first, then look for an internship." | London windows open in July and fill on a rolling basis. Goldman's intern class ≈ its entry class. | careers/finance.md, getting-in/recruiting-calendar.md |
| 3 | "An English-taught master's means an English-speaking job market." | 2.4–3.4% of German postings waive German. McKinsey DACH requires fluent German. | places/countries-and-cities.md, careers/consulting.md |
| 4 | "Ranked 12th beats ranked 20th." | Rank gaps of <15 places below the top 30 are noise. The FT salary is a PPP mean three years out. | decisions/school-types-and-accreditation.md |
| 5 | "I need an MSc Marketing (or Finance) for that career." | FMCG schemes accept any discipline. Specialist degrees often lead elsewhere: only 15% of Imperial Strategic Marketing graduates went into marketing roles. | decisions/programme-choice.md, careers/marketing.md |
| 6 | "I'll work for a year, then do a MiM, then a deferred MBA." | Work between degrees ends deferred-MBA eligibility. Experience caps count from graduation. | decisions/timing-and-sequencing.md |
| 7 | "Big 4 is a safe, guaranteed entry." | UK Big 4 intake is down about a sixth since 2023, with substitution towards school leavers. | careers/accounting-and-corporate.md |
| 8 | "Comparing gross salaries tells me where I'm better off." | Net minus rent reorders cities. At €60k: Dubai > Paris ≈ Madrid ≈ Amsterdam (with the 30% ruling) > Munich ≈ Milan ≈ Zurich > London. | money/salaries-and-roi.md |
| 9 | "A referral gets you the job." | Referrals mainly win interviews. Tests and interviews still decide. | getting-in/breaking-in.md |

## How the calculator fits

The admissions calculator answers Step 4's admissibility question ("can I get in?") and part of Step 0 (gates: ECTS, test floors, experience caps, C1 English). Every other step (career fit, country, format, pipeline, cost, timing, calendar) is decided elsewhere, and each needs its own tool or guide. See product/product-map.md.

## Claims to verify (synthesis-level)

1. That rank gaps of under ~15 places are "noise" rests on one year-pair of FT data (2025→2026). It needs a multi-year volatility check (decisions/school-types-and-accreditation.md).
2. The "apply within 2–3 weeks of opening" advantage is unquantified anywhere we found (getting-in/recruiting-calendar.md).
3. Luxury entry pay and tech product-marketing pay are unverified (careers/marketing.md).
4. City pay ratios for consulting (MBB vs Big 4 by country) come from a single aggregator family (careers/consulting.md).
5. All source-file "Claims to verify" sections are consolidated in verification/claims-to-verify.md.
