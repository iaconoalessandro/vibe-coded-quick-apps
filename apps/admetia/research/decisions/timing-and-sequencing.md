# Timing and sequencing

## Bottom line

1. **The pre-experience window is short and hard-edged.** LBS MiM wants graduation within the last two years and at most two years of post-graduation work; HEC Paris MiM requires less than three years; Imperial MSc Management treats more than two years "case by case"; LBS MFA says 0 to 2 typical years (school pages, 2026) [data]. Internships during studies do not count against the LBS limit. Plan on the window closing roughly two years after your bachelor's.
2. **Doing the MiM straight after the bachelor's keeps the deferred-MBA door open; working first closes it.** HBS 2+2, Stanford deferred enrolment, Wharton Moelis, MIT Sloan MBA Early, Booth Scholars and Darden Future Year Scholars all accept final-year master's students, but only if the master's followed the bachelor's with no full-time work in between. Stanford explicitly rules out anyone who took a gap year between degrees (school pages, 2026) [data]. This is the single most non-obvious sequencing fact in this file.
3. **Deferred MBA is a realistic target only for a thin top slice.** HBS 2+2 had 1,463 applicants and 131 committed students in 2025, from 72 undergraduate institutions (HBS, 2026) [data]; Wharton's 2026 Moelis cohort was 114 fellows from 68 universities (Wharton, 2026) [data]. Applying costs little (HBS fee $100, Booth and Kellogg waive fees), so for a strong final-year MiM student the expected value of applying is positive even at a low hit rate.
4. **Graduating into a recession hurts, but the hurt fades, and it fades fastest for strong graduates.** A typical recession cut Canadian graduates' earnings by about 9% at entry, halving within 5 years and fading by 10 (Oreopoulos, von Wachter & Heisz) [data]. In US data a large recession cuts first-year earnings about 10%, mostly gone within 7 years, with smaller losses for high-paying majors (Altonji, Kahn & Speer) [data]. The loss runs through a worse first employer, so the cure is to move up quickly, not to stay in school indefinitely.
5. **"Hiding in grad school" is a partial hedge, not a strategy.** Graduate enrolment rises only modestly in downturns and the response differs by gender, major and degree type (Bedard & Herman 2008; Johnson 2013) [data]. A one-year MiM defers entry by about 12 months, which is shorter than most recessions' hiring trough plus the recovery lag, and it costs a full year of fees and living costs (see money/costs-and-funding.md). It works best when you would have done the master's anyway and merely bring it forward.
6. **Italians are older at every step, and that matters for norms, not rules.** The average Italian first-level (triennale) graduate is 24.5 and the average laurea magistrale graduate 27.2 (26.3 in economics); only 61.3% of first-level graduates finish on time (AlmaLaurea, 2023 graduates) [data]. ESCP reports an average MiM entry age of 22, ESSEC and Warwick an average age of 23 (school pages, 2026) [data]. An Italian doing a triennale, then a laurea magistrale, then a UK MSc can arrive at the first job at 27–28, several years older than the analyst cohort. No major school or employer page read today sets an age cap, but experience caps and "recent graduate" wording bite instead.
7. **A second master's works when it opens a specific pipeline you cannot reach otherwise.** Examples: a laurea magistrale plus a one-year LSE, LBS or Imperial finance MSc to reach London front-office recruiting, or a MiM plus a specialised MiF. It is weak when it repeats content in the same labour market. Evidence here is mostly structural (who is eligible and who recruits where) rather than hard outcome data, so treat it as a judgement call.
8. **The UK Graduate visa is shrinking, which makes timing matter for non-UK readers.** It lasts 2 years if you apply on or before 31 December 2026 and 18 months if you apply on or after 1 January 2027 (GOV.UK, 2026) [data]. EU citizens without settled status need it too. See places/visas-and-work-rights.md.
9. **A business PhD is a real, funded alternative for the academically inclined.** LBS funds all PhD students with a fee waiver and a tax-free stipend of £32,154 (September 2026) for five years (LBS, 2026) [data]; newly hired business-school doctorates earned a mean of $139,659 in 2025–26, with finance assistant professors averaging $180k (AACSB, 2026) [data]. EMEA pay is far lower (new-doctorate median $77,664, small sample).

## 1. Experience caps and what schools say about working first

### The caps, school by school

| Programme | Rule (as stated) | Source |
|---|---|---|
| LBS Masters in Management | "graduated within the last two years, with a maximum of two years of postgraduate work experience"; internships during studies "do not count, even if you have completed several" | LBS MiM FAQ, 2026, https://www.london.edu/masters-degrees/masters-in-management/faqs [data] |
| LBS Masters in Financial Analysis | "0 to 2 typical years of experience"; people with more than 2 years are pointed to other programmes | LBS MFA page, 2026, https://www.london.edu/masters-degrees/masters-in-financial-analysis [data] |
| Imperial MSc Management | "designed for new and recent graduates. Candidates with more than two years' work experience will be considered on a case-by-case basis" | Imperial entry requirements, 2026, https://www.imperial.ac.uk/business-school/programmes/msc-management/admissions/entry-requirements/ [data] |
| HEC Paris MiM (Grande Ecole) | "less than three years of work experience"; admissions page says "little to no professional experience by the start of the program" | HEC MiM FAQ and admissions, 2026, https://www.hec.edu/en/master-s-programs/master-management/faq ; https://www.hec.edu/en/master-s-programs/master-management/admissions [data] |
| ESCP MiM | No cap found on the page; average entry age 22 | ESCP MiM, 2026, https://escp.eu/programmes/master-in-management [data] |
| ESSEC MiM (Grande Ecole, admission with degree) | No numeric cap found; a 1-year Intensive Track is "designed for students with prior professional and international experience", a 2–3-year Flexible Track for others; average age 23 | ESSEC, 2026, https://www.essec.edu/en/program/master-in-management-admission-with-degree/ [data] |
| Warwick MSc Management | Cap not confirmed today; 2025 intake 272 students, average age 23 | WBS, 2026, https://www.wbs.ac.uk/courses/masters/management/ [data] |

Two details matter more than the headline numbers.

- **The LBS clock starts at graduation, not at the first job.** "Graduated within the last two years" means a gap year spent travelling or unemployed still uses up the window. An Italian who finished a triennale in July 2024 and then started a laurea magistrale is a grey case: the page does not say which degree counts. Ask admissions in writing before relying on it (see Claims to verify).
- **HEC deferrals are rare.** HEC grants deferral "only in exceptional cases", for at most one year, and only after financial confirmation of the place (HEC MiM FAQ, 2026) [data]. So "get in now, work a year, then start" is not a plan you can count on at HEC.

### What the evidence says about 1–2 years of work before a MiM

No school report read today splits MiM salaries by prior experience, so there is no clean data on the payoff. What we can say:

- **Class composition shows that many MiM students already take a year or two out.** Of the 12 LBS MiM2026 student ambassadors listed on the "who attends" page, 5 finished their bachelor's in 2023, 3 in 2024 and 4 in 2025, for a programme starting in 2025 (LBS, 2026, https://www.london.edu/masters-degrees/masters-in-management/who-attends) [anecdotal: a school-chosen sample of 12, not a class statistic]. They include graduates of Bocconi, LSE and IE who waited two years. The class itself was 299 students from 65 nationalities.
- **Top MiMs build the experience in rather than asking for it first.** HEC offers a gap year between M1 and M2, and students must have 8 weeks (direct M1 entry) or 16 weeks (L3 entry) of professional experience by the start of M2 to be exempt from the professional gap year (HEC, 2026) [data]. ESCP says graduates leave with at least 9 months of professional experience, allows up to 2 gap semesters and runs an apprenticeship track with over 200 apprentices a year (ESCP, 2026) [data]. Imperial offers an optional extended term to 16 months for a longer work placement (Imperial, 2026) [data]. The design implies that schools see in-programme experience as a substitute for pre-programme experience.
- **Pre-experience roles are pitched at the MiM level regardless.** LBS says the MiM prepares students for "pre-experience roles and graduate schemes" (LBS MiM FAQ, 2026) [data]. A year of full-time work before the MiM will not usually let you skip the analyst or graduate-scheme level afterwards; it mainly buys a clearer story and better internship odds.

**Working rule:** a year of *relevant* work (a banking or consulting off-cycle internship, a start-up job with real responsibility) before a MiM is worth it if it changes which employers will interview you. A year of unrelated work mostly spends the eligibility window and a year of earnings in the high-paying later career.

## 2. Gap years and internship years

### Structures at the schools in the calculator

| School | Structure | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| HEC Paris MiM | Optional gap year between M1 and M2: a professional year (internships) or a personal project (volunteering, a start-up) | Exempt from the professional gap year only with 8 or 16 weeks of experience by M2 (HEC, 2026) [data] |
| ESCP MiM | 2 to 3 years; up to 2 gap semesters before and during the final year; 24-month or 12–14-month apprenticeship options | Minimum 9 months' experience at graduation (ESCP, 2026) [data] |
| Imperial MSc Management | Optional extended term to 16 months with an extended work placement | (Imperial, 2026) [data] |
| ESSEC MiM Flexible Track | 2–3 years; at least 8 months of professional experience during studies, in France or abroad, with apprenticeship among the options; some previous experience may count | (ESSEC, 2026) [data]. Separate gap-year rules not confirmed today |
| German universities (Mannheim, TUM) | The usual German route is a leave semester (Urlaubssemester) or a planned internship semester (Praxissemester) | Not confirmed from a university page today; see Claims to verify |

### Why the in-programme gap year beats the pre-programme one

- **You keep student status.** In France, internships of more than two months need a tripartite internship agreement (convention de stage) with a school, which is one reason the HEC and ESCP gap years sit inside the programme. This is standard French practice [practitioner consensus], but check the detail with the school; it is not on the pages read today.
- **You stay eligible for deferred MBAs.** A gap year *inside* a programme is not "full-time work between degrees". Yale Silver Scholars explicitly considers applicants with "gap years for internships/volunteering" and integrated bachelor's/master's programmes, while Stanford rules out gap years between undergraduate and graduate study (Yale SOM and Stanford GSB, 2026) [data]. Read each programme's rule before you take time out.
- **You do the internship at the moment banks and consultancies recruit.** A gap year between M1 and M2 gives two or three internships (for example a spring-week-to-summer sequence), each one a potential return offer. See getting-in/recruiting-calendar.md for when those processes open.

### The cost

A gap year adds a year of living costs and delays full-time earnings by a year. In the later career (where a top MiM graduate's pay is highest), that lost year is worth far more than the entry-level salary. So the gap year pays only if it moves you into a better first employer, which is exactly the mechanism the recession research finds matters most (section 6).

**Visa note for non-EU readers:** a gap year inside a UK programme is rare because Student visa sponsorship is tied to the course; placements must be part of the course. For EU citizens in France, Germany, Italy or Spain there is no visa issue. See places/visas-and-work-rights.md.

## 3. A second master's

### How it plays out

The evidence on how employers view two master's degrees is thin and mostly anecdotal. There is no recruiter survey read today that addresses it directly. What we can establish:

- **Schools do not bar it.** HEC asks applicants "currently pursuing a master's degree" to upload both master's and bachelor's transcripts (HEC admissions, 2026) [data], which shows they admit people with a master's in progress. LBS and Imperial caps are written in terms of work experience, not prior degrees (school pages, 2026) [data].
- **Deferred-MBA programmes treat a straight-through second degree as fine.** Stanford, Wharton and MIT accept graduate students who went straight from undergraduate to graduate study. None of the pages read today addresses a *second* master's specifically.
- **Age is the real cost.** An Italian who completes a triennale (average 24.5 at graduation) and a laurea magistrale (26.3 in economics) and then a UK MSc arrives at the first job at about 27 (AlmaLaurea, 2023 graduates) [data]. Most classmates in a pre-experience MiM are 22–23 (ESCP, ESSEC, Warwick pages, 2026) [data], so a 27-year-old will be the oldest in the room at interviews for the same entry-level roles. No age cap appeared on any page read, but the profile needs a clear story.

### When it works

| Situation | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Italian laurea magistrale, then a 1-year LSE/LBS/Imperial finance MSc to reach London banking | Often works | The second degree buys access to the London on-campus recruiting pipeline and the UK Graduate visa (for non-UK nationals). Opens a pipeline the first one did not. |
| Non-business bachelor's, then a MiM, then a specialised MiF | Can work | The MiF fills a technical gap for asset management or quant roles; but a MiM alone suffices for most corporate finance roles. |
| MiM, then a second MiM in another country | Weak | Same content, same employer level; mainly a costly country switch. A job search in the target country is usually cheaper. |
| Master's, then a second master's to wait out a weak market | Weak to moderate | Only if the second degree opens a stronger pipeline; otherwise it is a deferral that costs fees and a year (section 6). |

### Cost-benefit rule of thumb

A second one-year master's costs a year of fees (see money/costs-and-funding.md for levels) plus a year of forgone salary. It pays back only if it changes the tier of your first employer or the country you can work in. If you already have interview access to the employers you want, a second master's is mostly a costly signal.

**The master's-student clause is the key sequencing insight.** A European student who goes straight from a bachelor's into a one- or two-year MiM can apply to most of these programmes in the MiM's final year. Most deadlines fall in April, which fits the calendar of a September-to-August one-year MiM. A student who works for a year before the MiM has lost eligibility everywhere in the table.

**How selective is it?** HBS's 2025 numbers imply that roughly 9% of applicants ended up committed (1,463 applicants, 131 committed), though HBS does not publish an admit rate (HBS, 2026) [data]. The 2025 2+2 cohort majored in maths and physical sciences (37%), engineering (24%), economics (17%) and business (15%) (HBS, 2026) [data], a much more quantitative mix than the regular class. An older P&Q report put 2+2 at 79% domestic (US) students, but that data dates from 2017 and is stale (Poets&Quants for Undergrads, 2017) [data, stale]. Wharton's 29-country cohort suggests international applicants are welcome.

### The regular MBA route after a MiM

- **Experience is the currency.** HBS's Class of 2027 averages 4.9 years of work experience, middle 80% between 3 and 7 years, 943 students from 9,409 applications (HBS class profile, 2026) [data]. A MiM graduate who starts work at 23 reaches that experience band at 26–30, a normal MBA age.
- **Does the MBA "discount" MiM content?** No school page read today says so. The common advisor view is that MBA admissions judge career progression and leadership, not prior coursework, and that a MiM graduate must explain why they need an MBA after a business master's [practitioner consensus, not confirmed from a primary source]. There is no evidence that a MiM *hurts* MBA chances; it may shorten the "why MBA" story if your MiM already taught the core.
- **Share of MBA classes holding a prior master's:** not found on the HBS or Stanford profile pages read today (see Claims to verify).

## 5. PhD in finance, marketing or accounting (short)

- **Funding is standard at research schools.** At LBS all PhD students get a fee waiver and a tax-free stipend (£32,154 from September 2026, £33,762 from September 2027) for five years, subject to progress; students normally finish in 5–6 years (LBS PhD, 2026, https://www.london.edu/programmes/phd) [data].
- **The academic job market pays well, especially in the US.** For 2025–26, AACSB reports newly hired doctoral faculty earning a mean of $139,659 (median $128,000; 287 hires at 95 schools). Americas mean $152,550; EMEA mean $117,066, median $77,664 (only 23 hires at 8 schools) (AACSB Trend Watch: Faculty Salaries, 2026) [data]. Assistant professor means by discipline: finance $180k, accounting $170k, marketing $147k.
- **Timing fit:** a PhD suits someone who enjoys research, has top grades in quantitative subjects and is willing to spend 5–6 years on a modest stipend. It is not a "wait out the recession" option. For Europeans, the best-paid academic jobs are in the US, which means a US PhD or a top European one with US placement records.

## 6. Graduating into a recession

### The academic evidence

| Study | Data | Main finding | Persistence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Oreopoulos, von Wachter & Heisz (AEJ Applied 2012; NBER WP 12159, 2006) | Canadian college graduates 1982–1999, matched university–employer–employee data | A typical recession (a rise in unemployment of about 4 percentage points in their data) implies an initial earnings loss of about 9% | Halves within 5 years, fades to zero by 10. A lower-quality first employer explains 30–40% of the initial loss; recovery comes by moving to better firms, and advantaged graduates move faster [data] |
| Altonji, Kahn & Speer (NBER WP 20531, 2014; JOLE 2016) | US graduates, classes of 1974–2011 | A large recession cuts first-year earnings about 10% (about 4% through lower hourly wages); higher-paying majors lose less; earnings gaps between majors widen by almost a third | Most effects fade over the first 7 years. The Great Recession hit harder than earlier recessions and differed less by major [data] |
| Kahn (Labour Economics 2010) | US white male college graduates, 1979–1989 (NLSY) | Large negative wage effects, lower-level occupations, slightly higher tenure, higher educational attainment | Persist for the entire period studied [data, small sample, old] |
| Schwandt & von Wachter (JOLE 2019; NBER WP 25141) | US labour-force survey data, 1976–2015 | Persistent earnings and wage losses, especially for less advantaged entrants | Government support only partly offsets them [data] |
| von Wachter (Journal of Economic Perspectives 2020) | Review | Large initial effects on earnings, labour supply and wages | Fade after 10–15 years; later effects on health and mortality in middle age [data] |

**Reading the evidence for our reader:** the European top-MiM graduate is close to the "advantaged, high-paying major" group, whose losses are smallest and shortest. The mechanism is the first employer: a worse first job, then gradual climbing. So the real risk in a downturn is accepting a much weaker first employer and staying there.

All of these studies are North American and most predate 2023. They are the best evidence available, but European labour markets (with stronger employment protection and more fixed-term contracts, notably in Italy) could show more persistent effects. Treat the numbers as indicative.

### Does "hide in grad school" work?

- **Enrolment responds only a little.** Graduate enrolment patterns "vary across the business cycle by undergraduate major, GPA, gender, and advanced degree type" (Bedard & Herman, Economics of Education Review 2008) [data]. Overall US graduate enrolment is countercyclical for women and acyclical for men, and bad markets shift people from full-time to part-time study (Johnson, Economics of Education Review 2013) [data]. Graduates of high-paying majors are *less* likely to take an advanced degree when graduating into a recession, because their opportunity cost rises relative to others (Altonji, Kahn & Speer 2014) [data].
- **Timing arithmetic.** A one-year MiM applied for in a downturn means applying around October to March and starting in September, graduating about 20 months after the decision. If the weak hiring market lasts longer than that, you still graduate into it, now with fees paid (how long hiring troughs last is not quantified in the studies read; see Claims to verify). It works best when (a) you planned the master's anyway and bring it forward, (b) the programme has a strong placement pipeline that holds up in downturns, or (c) a two-year MiM with internships lets you enter the full-time market later.
- **Bottom line for the calculator:** do not recommend a master's purely to wait out a downturn. Do recommend bringing forward a master's you would do anyway, and recommend programmes with internships inside them so the student is in the employer pipeline throughout.

### Timing relative to the hiring cycle

For banking, consulting and graduate schemes, applications for the September start usually open about 9–12 months ahead (see getting-in/recruiting-calendar.md). A one-year MiM starting in September leaves only a few weeks before the main autumn recruiting season. Two consequences follow: (1) prepare applications before the MiM starts, and (2) programmes with a gap year or a longer structure (HEC, ESCP, Imperial's 16-month option) give a second recruiting cycle. This is the timing argument for two-year MiMs that most rankings do not show.

## 7. Age norms

- **MiM entry age:** ESCP reports 22 on average at entry; ESSEC's MiM reports an average age of 23 and Warwick's MSc Management 23 for its 2025 intake (school pages, 2026) [data]. FT MiM ranking tables list average age by school, but the ranking page could not be read today (see Claims to verify).
- **MBA entry age:** US M7 classes average about 5 years of work experience (HBS Class of 2027: 4.9 years) (HBS, 2026) [data], which implies a typical entry age of about 27–28 for someone who started work at 22–23.
- **"Too old" for a pre-experience master's** is set by experience caps, not age. Someone of 27 with a laurea magistrale and no full-time work is still eligible under LBS's and HEC's wording, but will be several years older than the class. **"Too young" for an MBA** is set by experience: with fewer than about 3 years' work you fall below the middle 80% band at HBS, which makes the deferred route the only realistic early path.
- **Graduate schemes rarely state age limits**; no employer page read today did. They tend to use phrases like "recent graduate" or a graduation-date window [practitioner consensus; the legal basis, EU and UK age-discrimination law, is in Claims to verify]. No employer page was read today to confirm a specific window; see getting-in/employer-pipelines.md and Claims to verify.

The averages are "gross": they include mature students, so the typical economics student at a selective university is younger than the mean.

### What it means for MiM and scheme timing

- **An on-time Italian triennale graduate is 22, the same as the ESCP average entrant.** The problem is the roughly 4 in 10 who finish late, and the common habit of adding a laurea magistrale first. The typical economics LM graduate is 26.3.
- **Doing the laurea magistrale first, then a foreign master's, stacks up age without adding work experience.** Experience caps are not breached (study is not work), but you arrive at the entry level at 27–28, when most pre-experience master's classmates are 22–23 (ESCP, ESSEC, Warwick, 2026) [data].
- **Better sequences for an Italian aiming abroad:** (a) triennale on time, then a foreign MiM or MSc straight away (keeps deferred-MBA eligibility too); or (b) a Bocconi or other Italian LM with a double degree or exchange abroad, rather than an LM followed by a second foreign master's. HEC runs MiM double degrees with Bocconi and St. Gallen, among others (HEC MiM course-content pages, 2026) [data]. See places/italy-playbook.md.

## Hypotheses tested

**H1. "One year of full-time work before a MiM improves outcomes more than it costs."**
Verdict: **not supported as a general rule; partly supported for relevant work.**
Holds when the year is spent in the target industry (an off-cycle banking or consulting role) and it changes which employers will interview you. Fails when the work is unrelated, when it pushes you past the LBS two-years-since-graduation window, or when you want a deferred MBA (full-time work before the master's makes you ineligible at Stanford, HBS, Wharton and MIT). Evidence: caps (LBS, HEC, Imperial pages, 2026) [data]; built-in experience at HEC, ESCP and Imperial (2026) [data]; no school report found that splits outcomes by prior experience.

**H2. "A second master's is mostly a costly signal of indecision unless it opens a specific pipeline."**
Verdict: **partly supported, insufficient outcome evidence.**
Holds when both degrees serve the same labour market at the same level. Fails for a clear pivot or country switch (for example an Italian LM plus a London finance MSc, which adds on-campus London recruiting and a UK Graduate visa). Evidence is structural: schools admit master's holders (HEC admissions, 2026) [data], and age norms (AlmaLaurea, 2023 graduates) [data]. No recruiter survey found.

**H3. "Graduating into a downturn has persistent pay effects, so delaying via a master's can pay off."**
Verdict: **first half supported, second half mostly not supported.**
Losses are real (about 9–10% at entry) but fade in 7–10 years, and they are smallest for high-paying fields (Oreopoulos et al.; Altonji et al.) [data]. A one-year master's usually delays entry by less than the trough lasts, and costs fees. It holds only when the master's was planned anyway or it opens a better first employer. High-paying-major graduates actually reduce advanced-degree take-up in recessions (Altonji et al. 2014) [data].

**H4. "Italian graduates are older at MiM entry, which pushes them against experience caps and graduate-scheme age norms."**
Verdict: **partly supported.**
They are older (24.5 at first-level graduation on average, 26.3 for economics LM graduates) [data], but caps are about work, not age, so the cap itself rarely bites. What bites is the LBS "graduated within the last two years" clause after a gap, and informal analyst-age norms. Fails for on-time triennale graduates from selective universities, who are 22.

**H5. "Deferred MBA admission is a realistic target for top MiM and undergraduate students."**
Verdict: **supported for a narrow group; the MiM route is under-used by Europeans.**
Eligibility explicitly includes straight-through master's students at HBS, Stanford, Wharton, MIT, Booth and Darden [data]. Volume is small (HBS 131 committed in 2025; Wharton 114 in 2026) [data] and the HBS 2025 cohort was heavily quantitative. Holds for students with top grades, a strong GMAT or GRE, and substantive internships (Booth names "a history of substantive internships") [data]. Fails for anyone who has already worked full-time.

**H6 (own). "Two-year MiMs with a built-in gap year give a timing advantage over one-year MSc programmes for recruiting."**
Verdict: **supported on structure, not yet on outcomes.**
HEC and ESCP place internships or gap semesters inside the degree and require or expect months of experience at graduation [data]. A one-year UK MSc starting in September sits right on top of autumn recruiting (see getting-in/recruiting-calendar.md). No data found comparing offer rates by programme length.

**H7 (own). "The UK Graduate visa cut makes it better for non-UK students to finish a UK master's before 2027."**
Verdict: **supported, narrowly.** Applications on or before 31 December 2026 get 2 years; from 1 January 2027 they get 18 months (GOV.UK, 2026) [data]. Any one-year MSc starting in September 2026 finishes after that date, so for current applicants the 18-month period is effectively the new normal. The rule matters for the Graduate-visa strategy, not for choosing when to start.

## Common mistakes and myths

1. **"I'll work for a year and then do a MiM, and apply for a deferred MBA later."** Full-time work before the master's ends deferred-MBA eligibility (Stanford, HBS, Wharton and MIT pages, 2026) [data]. Choose one route.
2. **"Internships count against the experience cap."** At LBS they don't: only post-graduation work counts (LBS MiM FAQ, 2026) [data].
3. **"A gap year only costs a year."** At LBS the two-year window runs from graduation, so a year off consumes half your eligibility (LBS, 2026) [data].
4. **"I can defer my HEC offer to work first."** Only in exceptional cases, for a maximum of one year, with financial confirmation (HEC FAQ, 2026) [data].
5. **"A recession graduate is scarred for life."** For college graduates in high-paying fields the loss mostly fades in 7–10 years (Oreopoulos et al.; Altonji et al.) [data]. The lasting damage comes from staying in a weak first job.
6. **"I'll hide in a master's until the market recovers."** Graduate enrolment is only weakly countercyclical, and a one-year programme may end before recovery (Bedard & Herman; Johnson) [data].
7. **"The laurea magistrale is a free extra year of polish."** It adds about two years of age without work experience; for students aiming at London or Paris analyst roles, a foreign MiM after an on-time triennale often gets there earlier (AlmaLaurea age data) [data; the strategic judgement is practitioner consensus].
8. **"A PhD is a fallback."** It is a 5–6-year commitment on a stipend (LBS, 2026) [data]; the pay is attractive mainly in the US academic market (AACSB, 2026) [data].

## Decision rules

1. **If** you are in the final year of a bachelor's, want an M7 MBA eventually and have strong grades, **prefer** going straight into a MiM and applying to deferred MBAs (HBS 2+2, Stanford, Wharton Moelis, MIT MBA Early, Booth, Darden) in the MiM's final year, **because** any full-time work before the master's ends eligibility.
2. **If** you graduated more than 18 months ago and target the LBS MiM, **then** apply in the current cycle, **because** the window closes two years after graduation.
3. **If** you have more than two years' full-time work, **prefer** Imperial (case by case) or HEC (under three years) over LBS MiM, and above about three years consider MBA-type programmes instead, **because** those are the stated caps.
4. **If** a pre-MiM year would be spent in unrelated work, **prefer** going straight to a MiM with built-in internships (HEC gap year, ESCP gap semesters, Imperial 16-month option), **because** in-programme experience keeps student status and deferred-MBA eligibility.
5. **If** you are Italian with an on-time triennale and aim for London or Paris finance or consulting, **prefer** a foreign MiM or MSc straight away over an Italian laurea magistrale followed by a foreign master's, **because** the second route adds about two years of age without adding work experience.
6. **If** you already hold a laurea magistrale, **consider** a second (one-year) master's only if it opens a named pipeline you cannot reach now (London banking recruiting, a UK Graduate visa, a quant finance track), **because** otherwise it adds fees and a year with little new access.
7. **If** a recession hits while you are deciding, **do not** start a master's you would not otherwise do; **do** bring forward one you had planned, and prefer programmes with internships inside them, **because** losses fade in 7–10 years and come mainly through a weak first employer.
8. **If** you graduate into a downturn and take a weaker first job, **then** plan to move within 1–2 years, **because** moving to better firms is how recession graduates recover (Oreopoulos et al.).
9. **If** you are a non-UK student relying on the UK Graduate visa, **then** budget for 18 months of post-study work, not 2 years, for any application from 1 January 2027.
10. **If** you have top quantitative grades and like research, **consider** a funded PhD in finance or accounting, **because** it is fully funded at top schools and US assistant professor pay averages $170k–$180k in those fields (AACSB, 2026).
11. **If** your MiM is one year long and starts in September, **then** prepare graduate-scheme and banking applications before the programme starts, **because** autumn recruiting begins within weeks (see getting-in/recruiting-calendar.md).
