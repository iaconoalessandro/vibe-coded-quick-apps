# Gulf and CEE hubs

> **Not legal, tax or immigration advice.** This file summarises official sources as of the dates given. Rules change and individual cases differ: confirm with the competent authority or a qualified adviser before acting.

## Bottom line

1. **The Gulf's best graduate doors are closed to Europeans by design.** ADIA's graduate cycles are for "UAE National candidates" (ADIA careers, read in full, accessed Oct 2026) [employer-stated]. PIF's Graduate Development Program is "aimed at Saudi nationals" and "open to Saudi nationals" (PIF page, read 2 Oct 2026), Mubadala's Maseeraty is "open to all UAE Nationals" (Mubadala brochure, linked from its careers page), Aramco's graduate route sits under "For Saudi applicants" while international applicants are sought with "five to 10 years" of experience, SABIC's early-career programmes "attract and nurture young Saudi talents", QIA's Journey Program needs a Qatari national, and Emirates NBD, FAB and ADNOC run their graduate programmes under UAE-national headings [employer-stated, own pages read 2 Oct 2026]; PwC's Watani programme and EY MENA's standard consulting programmes (UAE or MENA nationals) rest on search summaries of the postings [employer-stated, snippet only]. Saudi Arabia raised localisation quotas in 2025-26 (consulting 40% since 2024, marketing and sales 60%, engineering 30%) (Clyde & Co, 9 Feb 2026; Consultancy-ME, 25 Mar 2024) [practitioner consensus].
2. **H-a ("Gulf consulting and finance hire European graduates straight from a pre-experience master's") is not supported.** The documented open channels are Big 4 and similar programmes in Dubai and Doha, subject to visa feasibility, plus transfers. I found no employer-stated evidence of an MBB, sovereign-fund or investment-bank pre-experience intake of EU master's graduates. This is an absence of evidence in an area where employers rarely publish numbers, so the verdict is "not supported on what is publishable", not "proven impossible".
3. **Security is a live, dated risk.** The FCDO pages (UAE, 24 Jul 2026; Qatar, 19 Aug 2026; Saudi Arabia, 20 Sep 2026) report US-Iran strikes and retaliatory attacks since 8 July and Houthi missiles and drones aimed at Saudi cities including Riyadh since 13 July [data]. Check the Farnesina and FCDO pages on the day you decide.
4. **Tax-free pay helps, but the pay figures are weak.** At an assumed AED 15,000 a month, a Dubai graduate keeps about EUR 1,440 after central rent, against about EUR 915 for London (LBS) and EUR 1,260 for Paris (CGE) [author calculation, with anecdotal pay and rent]. The Dubai pay figure is not employer-stated for expats, so H-b is supported only conditionally.
5. **For Italians, AIRE and tax residence are a real trap.** Italy presumes you remain a tax resident if you do not register with AIRE, and a specialist source says moves to the UAE can keep the presumption even after registering unless you prove your life moved (Italian foreign ministry note, 18 Mar 2024) [data]; (Fiscomania, snippet only) [practitioner consensus, unverified].
6. **CEE shared-service centres are real, large and deep in Italian speakers, but entry pay is low and the entry rung is thinning.** 488,700 people work in Polish centres (Q1 2025), about 19.6% foreigners, Italy 8.3% of those. Junior specialist base pay is EUR 1,744 in Poland, 2,133 in Czechia, 1,598 in Hungary, 1,625 in Romania (ABSL 2025, citing Mercer 2024) [data]. 74.1% of centres use intelligent process automation, and KPMG and PwC Poland partners describe junior transactional roles shrinking (ACCA, May 2026) [practitioner consensus].
7. **After rent, a CEE centre job leaves less than Paris and about the same as or less than London for most cities.** About EUR 190 (Warsaw centre) to EUR 805 (Brno) a month after a one-bedroom in the centre (author calculation). The saving comes from Wrocław, Brno, Prague and Bucharest rents, not from pay.
8. **H-c (SSC as a viable first job with progression) is partly supported.** The sector is moving up to mid-office work (54.2% in 2025), turnover is low (9.1% voluntary in 2024), and centre careers exist. No source gives the transfer rate into regional or Western roles, so progression to Europe cannot be promised.
9. **The Gulf is a second-job move for most Italians.** ISTAT's 2024-25 report names no Gulf state or Poland among the origins of returning Italians, though its lists are top-10 only (ISTAT, 3 Aug 2026) [data].

## 1. Gulf work rules for graduates

Scope note: this section answers "what rules apply to an EU graduate, and to which employers". Italian and other EU citizens need an employer-sponsored work permit in every Gulf state. No free movement applies. Generic EU and non-EU permit rules are in places/visas-and-work-rights.md.

**UAE: Golden Visa and Work Rules.** *(All United Arab Emirates immigration, work permit, Golden/Green visa, and health insurance rules are centralized and verified in [`visas_immigration/uae/uae_visas_immigration_guide.md`](../../visas_immigration/uae/uae_visas_immigration_guide.md))*. The official portal lists a 10-year Golden Visa for outstanding university students (top-100 world universities with bachelor's GPA $\ge 3.5$ within 2 years, or top UAE universities with GPA $\ge 3.5$ in Class A or $\ge 3.8$ in Class B) and skilled professionals (MoHRE level 1/2 with salary $\ge$ AED 30,000/month) [data]. The practical route for a first job is an employer-sponsored work permit (mainland or free zone) or a 5-year Green Visa (salary $\ge$ AED 15,000/month).

**UAE: Emiratisation (MoHRE).** The rule applies to private-sector companies with 50 or more employees, which must raise the number of Emiratis in skilled jobs by 2% a year, split 1% in each half-year. For the first half of 2026 the deadline was 30 June, and from 1 July 2026 non-compliant firms pay AED 10,000 a month (AED 120,000 a year) for each Emirati position not filled: "MoHRE is set to begin imposing financial contributions on non-compliant companies starting from 1st July 2026, amounting to AED10,000 per month (AED120,000 annually) for each position they fail to fill with an Emirati citizen" (WAM, the UAE state news agency, 22 Jun 2026, https://www.wam.ae/en/article/c0uqocb-mohre-reaffirms-june-deadline-for-private-sector; verified in round 3g; Gulf News of the same month reports the same figure) [employer-stated, government]. The UAE Government portal (u.ae, "Employing Emiratis in the private sector", updated 12 Aug 2026, read 2 Oct 2026) states the same 2% annual rise for skilled jobs in private establishments with 50 or more employees and the 20-49-employee rule in 14 sectors (one Emirati hired by end 2024 and a second by end 2025), but gives the contribution as "AED 6,000 monthly for every citizen not employed ... These contributions increase by AED 1,000 annually until 2026", and, for 20-49-employee firms, AED 96,000 in January 2025 and AED 108,000 in January 2026, which is 8,000 x 12 and 9,000 x 12 (my arithmetic). Read together, the rate was about AED 9,000 a month from January 2026 and MoHRE's 22 June 2026 statement moves it to AED 10,000 from 1 July 2026, so the sources are consistent rather than conflicting (MoHRE's own press page was not reachable; check it on the day, and see whether the AED 10,000 rate continues in 2027) [data for the rule and the AED 6,000 base; the AED 9,000 figure is inferred]. The same page sets a minimum monthly wage for Emiratis in the private sector of AED 6,000 from 1 Jan 2026 and says employers must not advertise Emiratisation vacancies with unskilled or misleading roles. MoHRE's own pages (Wayback captures of 13 Jan 2026 and 27 Oct 2025) confirm the 2% rule, the 20-49 rule and that contributions apply from 1 January 2026 to firms missing their 2025 target [employer-stated, government]. A November 2025 report adds that selected firms with 20 to 49 workers in targeted high-growth sectors must hire at least one Emirati (Gulf News, 25 Nov 2025, https://gulfnews.com/uae/government/private-sector-warned-to-meet-emiratisation-goals-or-face-penalties-1.500359140) [practitioner consensus]. The two Gulf News articles do not mention free zones, and neither does the u.ae page. What it means for an expat graduate: the quota does not bar expats from being hired. It means a 50+ employee mainland company pays a charge if it does not keep adding Emiratis to skilled roles, and the biggest graduate schemes are ring-fenced for nationals (section 2).

**Saudi Arabia: Nitaqat and Saudisation.** *(All Saudi immigration and visa rules are centralized in [`visas_immigration/saudi_arabia/saudi_arabia_visas_immigration_guide.md`](../../visas_immigration/saudi_arabia/saudi_arabia_visas_immigration_guide.md))*. The ministry (MHRSD) runs the Developed Nitaqat system; its own news pages (read 2 Oct 2026) announce a new three-year phase of the programme from 2026 aimed at localising more than 340,000 additional private-sector jobs (HRSD, 19 Feb 2026), 60% Saudisation in marketing professions (10 roles, minimum wage SAR 5,500) and sales professions (8 roles listed), both announced as effective 19 Jan 2026 with three months' grace and establishments of 3+ workers in those roles (HRSD, 26 Jan 2026), 30% in 46 engineering professions (minimum wage SAR 8,000, establishments with 5+ engineering workers, dated 31 Dec 2025 with six months' grace) and 70% in 12 procurement professions (30 Nov 2025 with six months' grace, 3+ workers) (HRSD, 21 Jan 2026), and the second consulting phase at 40% in force from 25 Mar 2024 (HRSD, 6 May 2024) [employer-stated, government; the news items give no per-role consulting list]. The law-firm and trade-press readings that follow are consistent with these. A law-firm review dated 9 Feb 2026 lists the 2025-26 localisation decisions: marketing 60% (from 19 Apr 2026, establishments with 3+ staff, 10 professions), sales 60% (19 Apr 2026, 9 roles), engineering 30% (30 Jun 2026, establishments with 5+ engineers), dental 55%, and 100% bans on foreigners in some narrow roles such as housing supervisors and spa administrators. The Nitaqat framework grew from 37 to 41 sectors (Clyde & Co, 9 Feb 2026, https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2026/02/the-first-saudisation-updates-of-2026-key-changes) [practitioner consensus, law firm]. A search summary of other 2026 sources adds a 70% quota for procurement and the removal of the Yellow tier (secondary, not opened; Claims to verify 2). For management consulting, a 40% Saudi quota applied from March 2024 to business consultants, financial-advisory specialists, cybersecurity advisers and project managers (up from 35%), and the ministry said it expected the figure to rise (Consultancy-ME, 25 Mar 2024, https://www.consultancy-me.com/news/8734/saudi-kicks-off-2nd-phase-of-saudization-in-consulting-industry) [practitioner consensus, trade press; dated, check the current level]. Practical meaning: in a Riyadh consulting office, two of every five seats in those roles must go to Saudi nationals, so the share open to a European graduate shrinks as the firm grows.

**Saudi Arabia: Regional HQ (RHQ) programme.** Since 1 January 2024, multinationals without a licensed regional HQ in the Kingdom are generally barred from contracting with Saudi government entities, with exceptions including contracts under SAR 1 million. An RHQ must hire at least 15 full-time employees within a year of licensing, at least 3 of them C-level. RHQs get a 10-year exemption from Saudisation requirements and 0% corporate tax on eligible activities for up to 30 years (DLA Piper, 7 Mar 2024, https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/gulf-tax-insights/2024/gulf-tax-insights-february-2024/saudi-regional-headquarters-program-an-overview-of-tax-and-non-tax-incentives) [practitioner consensus, law firm]. The Ministry of Investment released draft RHQ regulations for consultation on 10 Sep 2025 (DGA Group, 2025, https://dgagroup.com/insight/saudi-arabia-issues-new-draft-regional-headquarters-rules-for-public-consultation/) [practitioner consensus], so the final text may differ from DLA Piper's 2024 summary. What it means for graduates: the programme creates Riyadh jobs inside multinationals, but the mandated hires are senior (3 C-level of 15), and RHQs are exempt from the quota that would otherwise force Saudi hiring. Treat it as demand for experienced managers, not as a graduate pipeline.

**Saudi Arabia: Premium Residency.** Special-talent tracks are reported to need more than 3 years of experience and monthly pay of SAR 14,000 (researchers), SAR 35,000 (healthcare and scientists) or SAR 80,000 (executives) (KSA Expats, 2025-26, https://ksaexpats.com/gifted-special-talent-residency-saudi-arabia/, search snippet only) [anecdotal]. The official portal (pr.gov.sa) needs JavaScript and could not be read. No track fits a new graduate.

**Qatar.** All labour law provisions, Qatarisation rules (Law No. 12 of 2024), sponsorship and visa requirements are fully verified in the single source of truth: [`visas_immigration/qatar/qatar_visas_immigration_guide.md`](../../visas_immigration/qatar/qatar_visas_immigration_guide.md). One Big 4 posting says Qatar positions may be sponsored by the local PwC firm, while Saudi positions require the candidate to be already eligible to work without sponsorship (PwC Middle East postings via ACCA Careers, accessed Oct 2026, https://jobs.accaglobal.com/job/8428097/deals-graduates-qatar/, search snippet only) [employer-stated, unverified].

## 2. Gulf hiring evidence and pay

The pattern across the evidence is a **two-tier market**. The prestigious graduate schemes (sovereign funds, government entities, many Big 4 and consulting graduate programmes) are built for nationals. Non-nationals are mostly hired into the same firms as experienced hires, as intra-firm transfers, or through a smaller number of open international-friendly programmes.

**What the Saudi consulting rules add.** Saudi consulting firms must keep 40% of the quota roles Saudi (section 1). Trade press reports a royal order that bars government entities from using foreign consultancy firms for some work, and secondary reports put the Saudi-staffing share on government projects at about 33-35% (PrepLounge forum, Oct 2026) [anecdotal]. I could not open the original text of the order (the law-firm page returned 404 or 403), so I do not state its terms. The direction is clear from the 40% quota: the Riyadh consulting offices are being staffed with Saudi graduates first.

**Hypothesis H-a verdict in short.** There is no employer-stated evidence that Gulf consulting or finance houses run a mass pre-experience intake of European master's graduates. The firms that publish graduate programmes ring-fence the biggest ones for nationals, and the open ones (Big 4 in Dubai, Doha and sometimes Riyadh) depend on visa feasibility. Some European graduates do land Gulf entry roles, but the channel I can document is Big 4 audit/advisory, bank and corporate roles, not MBB or sovereign funds. See the hypotheses section.

**Pay: what is stated, and by whom.** I found no employer-stated entry salary for expat graduates in the Gulf. The numbers available are weak:

- Recruiter listings for UAE-national graduate trainee roles show about AED 15,000 a month plus Nafis support (Robert Walters listing, Oct 2026, https://www.robertwalters.ae/businesssupport/jobs/administrative/1890892-uae-national-hiring---multiple-opportunities.html, snippet only) [anecdotal]. - For tax-free pay versus cost of living, the one rigorous cross-check I have is the expat survey: the UAE ranks 1st for career prospects, but 18th for personal finance and 24th for fair pay, with a 46.3-hour average week (InterNations Expat Insider 2026, cited in places/countries-and-cities.md) [data, survey]. The comparison method is in section 6.
- Gemini's "Riyadh hardship premium of 25-40% over Dubai" and "$70,000-$100,000 saved in year one" have no source in its file. I found no source for them in my searches and treat them as unconfirmed (see Corrections). Saudi Arabia's 2026 localisation drive points the other way for expat entry roles: fewer seats, not premium pay.

This section is descriptive. It gives what official sources say and the date of each, without advice on whether a person should go.

I did not retrieve the Italian Farnesina "Viaggiare Sicuri" pages (the site is script-driven) or the US State Department pages. Italian readers should check Viaggiare Sicuri directly (Claims to verify 4).

Several UK press pieces from March 2026 report that the FCDO "advises against all but essential travel" to the UAE and Qatar. InterNations' 2026 survey also states that its fieldwork straddled the outbreak of the US-Iran war (cited in places/countries-and-cities.md) [data, survey]. This is a live risk that a graduate would carry for at least the first two years of a Gulf job, and it can change within weeks.

**End-of-service gratuity (UAE private sector).** After one year of continuous service a worker is entitled to 21 days' basic salary per year of service for years 1 to 5 and 30 days per year thereafter, capped at two years' wage in total. It is calculated on basic pay, not allowances (UAE Government portal, undated page, https://beta.government.ae/en/information-and-services/jobs/end-of-service-benefits-for-employees-in-the-private-sector, read in full 1 Oct 2026) [data]. A graduate who spends 2 years in a UAE job with a basic salary of AED 15,000 a month would therefore accrue about 42 days of basic pay, roughly AED 21,000 (author calculation; check whether your employer uses a free-zone or DIFC savings scheme instead, which is not covered by this page). For Qatar, Law No. 14 of 2004 Art. 54 sets an end-of-service gratuity of at least 3 weeks' basic salary per year of service after one continuous year, verified in [`visas_immigration/qatar/qatar_visas_immigration_guide.md`](../../visas_immigration/qatar/qatar_visas_immigration_guide.md) [QA-SRC-08]. Saudi gratuity rules were not retrieved.

**Labour-law basics.** Employment in the UAE, Saudi Arabia and Qatar is employer-sponsored: your residence is tied to your contract. For Qatar, notice periods (1–2 months), probation (max 6 months) and non-compete rules are fully verified in [`visas_immigration/qatar/qatar_visas_immigration_guide.md`](../../visas_immigration/qatar/qatar_visas_immigration_guide.md) [QA-SRC-03, QA-SRC-08].

**Tax residence for Italians.** The Italian foreign ministry's note on AIRE and tax (updated 18 Mar 2024) states that failing to register with AIRE creates a rebuttable presumption of Italian tax residence, and that someone who keeps Italian civil registration for more than 183 days a year remains an Italian tax resident and must declare foreign income in Italy (Ambasciata d'Italia Maputo, "AIRE e fisco", 18 Mar 2024, https://ambmaputo.esteri.it/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/AIRE-fisco.pdf) [data, official summary]. A specialist site adds that Italian citizens who cancel their registration and move to a state on the 1999 "privileged tax" list (the UAE has been on it, per that site) are presumed Italian tax residents unless they prove they moved their centre of life, and that the Cassation Court confirmed this for indirect moves in order 14240/2021 (Fiscomania, accessed Oct 2026, https://fiscomania.com/doppia-residenza-italia-dubai-cosa-fare/, search snippet only) [practitioner consensus, unverified]. The practical consequence for a young graduate with a Dubai job: AIRE registration is necessary but not enough, the burden of proof sits with the taxpayer, and the 0% UAE tax is only real if Italy accepts you have left. The "exit tax" regimes I know of in Italian law concern company assets and unrealised gains of businesses, not employees' salaries, but I did not verify this and do not state it as fact (Claims to verify 6). Anyone planning to return to Italy should also read the impatriati regime point in places/italy-playbook.md, since the return regime has its own conditions.

**Cultural and legal points the sources support.** I found no official source in this research that I can cite for dress, alcohol or social-conduct rules, so I leave them out. The two facts that employers and governments themselves publish and that matter most to a new graduate are: your employer sponsors your visa, and the national-quota systems (section 1) shape which jobs are open.

## 4. CEE shared-service and nearshoring centres

**Scale (employment).** The employer association ABSL counts 2,081 business service centres run by 1,258 investors from 50 countries in Poland at the end of Q1 2025, with 488,700 people employed in dedicated centres (+6.2% year on year, from 164,600 in 2015). Foreign-owned centres hold 84.3% of the employment, and Warsaw (429 centres) and Kraków (324) are followed by Wrocław (258), with Warsaw and Kraków each above 100,000 jobs (ABSL, "Business Services Sector in Poland 2025", 24 Jun 2025, https://absl.pl/en/news/p/absl-report-polands-business-services-sector-grows-value-and-strategic-importance, and the full report PDF read in full, https://invest.lodz.pl/files/invest/public/ABSL-2025-EN.pdf) [data, industry association survey]. A search summary of ABSL and Colliers material quotes 2,179 centres and 1,303 companies at Q1 2026, but I could not open that 2026 release (Claims to verify 7). Neighbouring countries, from investment-agency material reported by search summaries only: Hungary about 245 centres and about 120,000 employees in 2025 (HIPA via BBJ, search summary, unverified), and Czechia at least 350 centres and more than 150,000 employees (KPMG/CzechInvest material, search summary, unverified) [anecdotal, snippet only]. Employer figures need care: HSBC's own page says its Service Delivery centre in Kraków has "nearly 6,000" professionals working in 11 languages, but the page is undated (it cites a 2016 ranking), so treat it as a possibly stale figure (HSBC Poland, https://www.about.hsbc.pl/hsbc-service-delivery, read 1 Oct 2026) [employer-stated]. Goldman Sachs' Warsaw office is described as its "largest office in continental Europe" (eFinancialCareers, 28 Jan 2026, https://www.efinancialcareers.com/news/goldman-sachs-warsaw-hiring) [practitioner consensus, trade press]. Headcounts I saw for JPMorgan, UBS and Citi in Poland came from search summaries of old or unnamed pages and are not used.

**Who is in the workforce.** Foreigners made up 19.6% of centre employment in 2024-25. Ukraine was the biggest origin (23.4% of foreigners), then India (10.9%), Belarus and **Italy (8.3% each)**. On those two figures, I estimate roughly 8,000 Italians work in Polish centres (488,700 x 19.6% x 8.3%, author calculation, treating survey shares as sector-wide) (ABSL 2025 report) [data, author calculation]. 46% of employees are 35 or older, and the share of junior staff is falling as the sector matures.

**Entry pay against Western Europe.** The ABSL report reproduces Mercer's 2024 surveys of monthly gross base pay for a junior specialist (up to 2 years' experience, entry level) in BPO/SSC/IT/R&D centres: Poland EUR 1,744 mean (1,666 median), Czechia EUR 2,133 (2,071), Romania EUR 1,625 (1,645), Hungary EUR 1,598 (1,554), Bulgaria EUR 1,517 (ABSL 2025 report, Table 2.1, citing Mercer 2024 SSC surveys) [data, employer-survey]. The same table shows Polish team leaders at EUR 3,888 and managers at EUR 6,127, so the pay ladder roughly triples from junior to manager. These are base salaries before language premiums and are from 2024 surveys, so they are 1-2 years old. Two checks on the size of the gap, both from Eurostat: net annual earnings for a single worker on the country's *average* wage in 2025 were EUR 19,086 in Poland, EUR 20,901 in Czechia, EUR 15,432 in Hungary and EUR 13,877 in Romania, against EUR 36,535 in Germany, EUR 32,346 in France, EUR 27,788 in Italy and EUR 27,041 in Spain (Eurostat earn_nt_net, API read 1 Oct 2026, https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/api/dissemination/statistics/1.0/data/earn_nt_net) [data]. Poland's statutory minimum wage was EUR 1,139 a month in the first half of 2026, Czechia EUR 924, Hungary EUR 838, Romania EUR 795, against Germany EUR 2,343, France EUR 1,823 and Spain EUR 1,425 (Eurostat earn_mw_cur, 1 Oct 2026) [data]. A search summary of Statistics Poland puts the average gross monthly wage in Q2 2026 at PLN 9,233 (about EUR 2,145), up 5.5% year on year (Polskie Radio, July 2026, citing GUS; I could not read the GUS page itself) [data, secondary]. A junior centre salary of EUR 1,744 gross is therefore about 81% of the national average, which is typical for entry jobs.

**Language premiums and language demand.** Mercer's December 2024 poll shows most centres pay a language allowance: PLN 600 to 2,000 a month depending on the language, with the Nordic languages and Dutch at the top (PLN 2,000, about EUR 470) and most Western European languages near PLN 1,000 to 1,400 (EUR 235 to 330) (ABSL 2025 report, Figure on language allowances, citing Mercer PL Shared Services Spot Poll Dec 2024) [data, chart-read: the text layer lists the language names and PLN values in separate rows, so I give only the range, not a language-by-language mapping]. HSBC's Kraków centre works in 11 languages including Italian, German and French (HSBC Poland page above) [employer-stated]. The practical reading: native Italian plus fluent English is a paid skill in these centres, not a gate, and it makes Italians over-represented among the foreigners ABSL counts. I did not find a source on whether Polish, Czech or Hungarian is required inside the centres themselves, so I make no claim on that (Claims to verify 9).

**Is it a viable first job with a path upward (H-c)?** The evidence cuts both ways.
- For: the sector is moving up the value chain. Mid-office work rose to 54.2% of process work in 2025 from 46.1% in 2021, back-office fell to 42.7% from 52.9% (ABSL data via ACCA, "From back office to value add", May 2026, https://abmagazine.accaglobal.com/content/abmagazine/global/articles/2026/may/practice/from-back-office-to-value-add.html) [practitioner consensus, trade magazine citing ABSL]. Specialist grades are growing, and ABSL's tenure data show 27.5% of staff at 3-5 years and 14.4% above 10 years, which means careers inside centres exist. Voluntary turnover fell from 18.3% in 2022 to 9.1% in 2024 (ABSL 2025) [data].
- Against: ABSL's own figures show intelligent process automation in 74.1% of centres and a falling junior share, and a PwC Poland partner is quoted saying career structures are shifting "from a traditional pyramid towards a diamond shape" as junior transactional roles decline; a KPMG Poland partner says "India will always be cheaper" for the transactional tasks (ACCA, May 2026) [practitioner consensus, executives quoted on the record]. In plain terms, the entry rung is being thinned from below while the middle widens.
- Search summaries of Randstad's Workmonitor and ABSL material report that 39% of Polish employers use automation to offset costs and fewer than one in five plan to raise headcount by more than 10% by Q1 2027 (secondary, unverified; Claims to verify 8).
- **What I could not find:** a published statistic on how many centre employees are promoted into regional or European roles. ABSL describes centres as a "good place to start a career", but that is a trade-body description, not a conversion rate. So H-c is only partly supported.

**Where the Western banks actually sit.** HSBC's Kraków centre is a service-delivery and technology hub (risk, business analysis, IT applications support across 27 countries) (HSBC Poland page) [employer-stated]. Goldman's Warsaw office is a technology and engineering base, and its latest senior hire covers "global banking, markets and engineering" (eFinancialCareers, 28 Jan 2026) [practitioner consensus]. These are delivery and engineering locations. My inference, not a sourced finding: the roles these centres advertise (risk, finance operations, engineering) are not the client-facing M&A or trading jobs, which the banks' own pages place in their main financial centres. I found no employer page offering a pre-experience investment-banking analyst role in Warsaw or Kraków (Claims to verify 10).

## 5. CEE finance and consulting

The evidence base for this section is thinner than for sections 1 to 4. I looked for primary pages on Warsaw's finance and consulting graduate market and found mostly trade press and directory entries. What follows separates what I could document from what I could not.

**What is documented.**
- **Warsaw and Kraków are delivery hubs for foreign banks, not their client-facing centres** (section 4). Goldman Sachs describes its Warsaw base through technology and engineering hiring, and HSBC's Kraków centre through risk, analysis and application support [employer-stated and trade press, sources in section 4].
- **Local banking is large but local.** PKO Bank Polski is the largest bank in Poland and listed in Warsaw (Wikipedia and directory pages found in search, not a primary source) [anecdotal]. I found no PKO graduate-programme page stating language or nationality rules, so I cannot say how open it is to non-Polish graduates (Claims to verify 11).
- **Tech employers are arriving, which changes the competition.** Bending Spoons opened a Warsaw office in September 2026 and reports about 50,000 applications from Poland in the first nine months of 2026, more than twice the whole of 2025; more than half of recent Polish hires were still studying (Bending Spoons investor newsroom, 21 Sep 2026, cited in careers/tech-business-and-startups.md) [employer-stated]. It shows that Polish graduates are strong, numerous and aimed at the same top-tier employers.

**EU citizens and free movement.** An Italian or other EU citizen needs no permit in Poland, Czechia, Hungary or Romania. The rule is stated in places/visas-and-work-rights.md for every EU/EEA country, and I did not re-read national immigration pages for these four. For **non-EU** readers, I did not research permit routes for the four countries, so I make no claim; see places/visas-and-work-rights.md and Claims to verify 12.

**Does a Western master's matter?** I found no source that measures it. The ABSL report describes a mature sector hiring from Polish universities (ABSL 2025) [data]; the report's university-student table by city could not be mapped reliably from the PDF text, so I give no student figure. The inference is that centres do not need a foreign master's to fill entry roles, since local supply is large, and their pay premium is for languages and specialist skills. Your master's helps you reach the *West-facing* roles (the part of the Warsaw or Kraków operation that serves a London or Frankfurt desk) only if it comes with an internship or a clear route into the same firm's graduate scheme. This is an inference and not evidence of causation.

**Consulting.** I did not find any primary evidence on MBB, Big 4 or boutique graduate hiring in Warsaw, Prague, Budapest or Bucharest. The only Big 4 evidence is that KPMG Poland and PwC Poland partners comment on shared-service career structures (section 4). There are no numbers on entry cohorts. Treat consulting in CEE as unresearched here.

**Progression from a centre to a European role.** The only documented routes are internal: a centre employee can apply for roles in the parent bank, and tenure data show people stay (27.5% have 3 to 5 years; 14.4% have 10 or more) (ABSL 2025) [data]. I have no source for a rate of transfer to London, Frankfurt or other Western offices. If your goal is a Western European career at a named bank, a first job inside that bank's CEE centre is an option only if you can verify an internal-mobility policy in writing (ask the recruiter for the share of last year's centre graduates who moved to a Western office; this is a practical step, not a finding).

## 6. Purchasing power

**Method.** I reuse the "net minus central one-bedroom rent" method of money/salaries-and-roi.md (author calculation, single person, no other living costs, so the residual is before food, transport and leisure). Net pay is gross multiplied by each country's Eurostat net-to-gross ratio for a single worker on the average wage in 2025: Poland 71.7%, Czechia 78.1%, Hungary 66.5%, Romania 58.5% (Eurostat earn_nt_net, gross and net series, API read 1 Oct 2026) [data]. Because tax is progressive, entry-level workers pay a little less than the average-wage ratio, so this slightly understates net pay. Gross pay is the Mercer 2024 junior-specialist base from the ABSL report (section 4), a centre salary. Rents are Numbeo's crowdsourced one-bedroom figures, collected 23 Sep to 1 Oct 2026 [anecdotal]. For Gulf cities I cannot use a ratio, because there is no income tax, so net equals gross.

| Case | Gross / month | Net / month | Rent, centre (outside) | Left after centre rent (outside) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Warsaw, SSC junior specialist | EUR 1,744 | about EUR 1,250 | EUR 1,064 (857) | about EUR 190 (390) |
| Wrocław, same pay | EUR 1,744 | about EUR 1,250 | EUR 747 (592) | about EUR 500 (660) |
| Prague, SSC junior | EUR 2,133 | about EUR 1,670 | EUR 995 (857) | about EUR 670 (810) |
| Brno, same pay | EUR 2,133 | about EUR 1,670 | EUR 861 (711) | about EUR 805 (955) |
| Budapest, SSC junior | EUR 1,598 | about EUR 1,060 | EUR 726 (561) | about EUR 335 (500) |
| Bucharest, SSC junior | EUR 1,625 | about EUR 950 | EUR 611 (407) | about EUR 340 (545) |
| Dubai, assumed AED 15,000 | about EUR 3,570 | about EUR 3,570 | EUR 2,130 (1,339) | about EUR 1,440 (2,230) |
| Dubai, forum figure AED 23,000 (McKinsey BA, anecdotal) | about EUR 5,480 | about EUR 5,480 | EUR 2,130 (1,339) | about EUR 3,350 (4,140) |

Dubai rows use an assumed AED 4.2 per euro, which I did not verify (Claims to verify 13); the AED 15,000 base is the pay listed for a graduate-trainee role aimed at UAE nationals (Robert Walters listing, snippet only) [anecdotal] and is *not* a documented expat entry salary. Comparison points already in the library: London LBS MiM UK mean about EUR 915 left per month, Paris CGE Île-de-France mean about EUR 1,260, Milan AlmaLaurea national one-year average about EUR 0 (centre) to EUR 460 (outside centre) (money/salaries-and-roi.md) [data inputs, author calculation].

**What the table says.**
1. **A CEE shared-service job pays like a modest Italian or Milanese job, not like London.** In Prague and Brno the centre rent leaves about EUR 670 to 800 a month, and in Warsaw only about EUR 190 if you rent in the centre. That is below both Paris (EUR 1,260) and the London graduate case (EUR 915). The saving is rent, not pay.
2. **Wrocław and Brno beat Warsaw and Kraków on residual** because the same SSC salary meets a lower rent (Wrocław EUR 747 against Warsaw EUR 1,064). ABSL counts 258 centres in Wrocław against 429 in Warsaw, so supply of jobs is still deep (ABSL 2025) [data].
3. **Dubai leaves the most if the pay assumption holds.** Even at the low AED 15,000 case a Dubai graduate keeps about EUR 1,440 after central rent, above every CEE row and above London and Milan graduate cases. At the forum figure for a McKinsey analyst it is about EUR 3,350. The result depends entirely on a pay figure I cannot confirm from an employer. It also excludes the 46-hour average week that expats report in the UAE and the lack of a pension in the base case (InterNations 2026) [data, survey]. A UAE gratuity of about 21 days' basic pay per year adds a small amount (section 3).
4. **Riyadh rent is low (EUR 1,043 centre, EUR 732 outside) but I have no entry-pay evidence for expats there.** Doha is EUR 1,678 and Abu Dhabi EUR 1,740 (Numbeo, Sept to Oct 2026) [anecdotal].
5. **Romania shows how tax changes the picture.** Gross pay of EUR 1,625 looks close to Poland's EUR 1,744, but net is about EUR 950 against EUR 1,250, because only 58.5% of gross is kept at the average wage. ABSL, citing Mercer, makes the same point for senior accountants: Romanian net pay is "much lower", with a tax burden above 40% of gross (ABSL 2025) [data].
6. **H-b (Gulf tax-free pay beats London and Paris net pay at entry after living costs) is supported in the residual calculation but not in the underlying data.** See the hypotheses section.

## 7. Test against places/countries-and-cities.md and ISTAT

places/countries-and-cities.md says (bottom line 7 and section 5) that young Italian graduates leave mainly for the UK, Germany, France, Switzerland and the US, with no Gulf state named, and that the Gulf is a second-job or MBA-stage move for most Europeans. I re-read the underlying ISTAT report (ISTAT, "Migrazioni interne e internazionali della popolazione residente, anni 2024-2025", 3 Aug 2026, https://www.istat.it/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Statistica-report_Migrazioni-interne-e-internazionali-della-popolazione-residente_ANNI-2024-2025.pdf, 14 pages) [data].

- **The claim is confirmed but weaker than it sounds.** The report's text and figures name Germany, the UK, Switzerland, the US, France, Brazil, Spain, Argentina, the Netherlands and Australia as origins of returning Italians. A text search for "Emirati", "Golfo", "Arabia", "Qatar" and "Polonia" returns no hits. So no Gulf state or Poland is named. But the report only names the top countries in its charts, so absence from a top-10 list is not proof of zero flow.
- **Likely undercount.** ISTAT's figures are built on registry cancellations (AIRE). Section 3 shows why Gulf-bound Italians have a reason not to register: the UAE has been treated as a "privileged tax" destination for the presumption of Italian residence, so a registered move invites scrutiny (Fiscomania via search snippet, unverified), and people on short postings may never deregister. That is an inference from the AIRE rules, not a measured effect. It means the ISTAT counts probably under-represent short Gulf postings. Gemini's file implies the Gulf is a mainstream destination ("massive relocations"); ISTAT does not show that for Italians, and I found no evidence of volume for either claim.
- **What the gap means for the site.** It supports putting the Gulf in the second-job bracket for Italian readers. It does not support dropping it: the Gulf matters for people who already have an employer-sponsored route (intra-firm transfers, Big 4 programmes in Dubai or Doha) or who are non-EU with Gulf ties. For Poland, the ISTAT report names no Italian flow either, yet ABSL counts Italians as 8.3% of foreigners in Polish centres (about 8,000 people by my estimate, section 4). That suggests many Italians in CEE are not captured in the ISTAT top-destination lists, again perhaps because they register late or not at all. This is also an inference.

## Hypotheses tested

**H-a (assigned). Gulf consulting and finance hire European graduates directly out of a pre-experience master's.** Verdict: **not supported on available evidence (insufficient to prove none).**
- Holds where: Big 4 offices in Dubai, Abu Dhabi and Doha run graduate programmes where sponsorship "may be" available (PwC via ACCA Careers postings; Deloitte "subject to visa and work permit feasibility") [employer-stated, snippet only]; BCG Riyadh advertises "Visiting Associate" roles for students graduating Nov 2026 to Sep 2027, with nationality rules unknown (Sabbar copy of the posting) [employer-stated, snippet only].
- Fails where: sovereign wealth and government entities (ADIA, Mubadala, PIF) and the flagship national programmes (PwC Watani; EY standard consulting) are for nationals [employer-stated]. Saudi consulting must meet a 40% Saudi quota in roles that a graduate would fill [practitioner consensus]. Saudi Big 4 postings require existing work eligibility (PwC postings, snippet only).
- Key evidence: ADIA posting (read in full); Consultancy-ME 2024; Clyde & Co 2026.

**H-b (assigned). Gulf tax-free pay beats London and Paris net pay at entry after living costs.** Verdict: **partly supported, conditional on pay I cannot confirm.**
- Holds where: pay is at or above about AED 15,000 a month, you rent outside the centre or share, and you stay long enough for the move to pay back. Dubai leaves about EUR 1,440 (centre rent) to EUR 2,230 (outside) against London about EUR 915 and Paris about EUR 1,260 (author calculation) [anecdotal inputs].
- Fails where: expat entry salaries are lower than assumed, a housing allowance is not provided, the 46-hour average week is counted as an hourly cost, or Italian tax residence is not shed (the 0% tax is then not real). InterNations ranks the UAE 18th for personal finance despite no income tax (2026) [data, survey].
- Key evidence: Numbeo rents, 23 Sep to 1 Oct 2026 [anecdotal]; Robert Walters listing (nationals-only trainee role) [anecdotal]; PrepLounge forum figure [anecdotal].

**H-c (assigned). CEE shared-service centres are a viable first job with progression to regional or European roles.** Verdict: **partly supported (job: yes; progression: unproven).**
- Holds where: you are an Italian, German, French or other Western-language speaker, you want a European city with low rent (Wrocław, Brno, Prague, Bucharest), and you target a specialist track (FP&A, risk, analytics) rather than transactional processing.
- Fails where: your aim is a client-facing banking or consulting seat in Western Europe, or the role is transactional work that automation and India-based centres are absorbing (KPMG Poland quote, ACCA May 2026) [practitioner consensus].
- Key evidence: ABSL 2025 (data); ACCA May 2026; no transfer-rate source found.

**H-d (mine). Gulf national-quota systems are the main reason expat graduate intake is thin, more than a lack of demand.** Verdict: **partly supported.**
- Evidence: 2% annual Emirati skilled-job targets (u.ae, updated 12 Aug 2026) with a per-position monthly charge of AED 6,000 rising by AED 1,000 a year to 2026 (u.ae) then AED 10,000 from 1 Jul 2026 (MoHRE statement carried by WAM, 22 Jun 2026) [data / employer-stated, government]; a Saudi 40% consulting quota (HRSD, 6 May 2024), 60% marketing and sales quotas (HRSD, 26 Jan 2026), 30% engineering and 70% procurement (HRSD, 21 Jan 2026) [data, government]. Fails because I could not test demand directly: no employer reports expat vacancy counts.

**H-e (mine). For a European student, a CEE centre job adds more value as a paid language-and-finance apprenticeship than as a destination.** Verdict: **insufficient evidence.** Language premiums of about PLN 1,000 to 1,400 a month for most Western languages (Mercer via ABSL) [data, chart-read] and a 2-year junior band support the idea; no source tracks where people go afterwards.

**H-f (mine). The Gulf data gap in ISTAT reflects registration incentives, not absence of Italians.** Verdict: **insufficient evidence.** The AIRE tax presumption (Italian foreign ministry note, 18 Mar 2024) [data] gives a reason; no measured undercount.

**H-g (mine). Romania and Hungary look cheap in gross terms but give poorer net outcomes than Poland and Czechia for a graduate.** Verdict: **partly supported.** Net-to-gross at the average wage is 58.5% for Romania and 66.5% for Hungary against 71.7% for Poland and 78.1% for Czechia (Eurostat 2025) [data]; but Bucharest and Budapest rents are lower (EUR 611 and 726 against Warsaw EUR 1,064), so after-centre-rent residuals for Warsaw, Budapest and Bucharest are similar (about EUR 190 to 340), while the Czech cities are higher (author calculation).

## Common mistakes and myths

1. **"Tax-free means rich."** Expats rank the UAE 18th for personal finance and 24th for fair pay, with a 46.3-hour week (InterNations 2026, via places/countries-and-cities.md) [data, survey]. Central Dubai rent is about EUR 2,130 a month (Numbeo, Sept 2026) [anecdotal].
2. **"The Golden Visa is a graduate route."** The u.ae page lists a 10-year track for "top university students" with certificates of excellence and recommendations, but states no GPA, no list of universities, and no sign it fits a foreign master's graduate looking for a job (u.ae, updated 28 Jul 2026) [data]. Plan on an employer-sponsored visa.
3. **"The big Gulf names will hire me after a European master's."** The sovereign funds' graduate cycles are for nationals (ADIA read in full) [employer-stated]. People move to the Gulf at these firms via transfers or experienced hiring [practitioner consensus, forum signals].
4. **"Moving to Dubai ends my Italian tax duties on day one."** AIRE registration is a legal presumption tool, and moves to listed low-tax states can invite an Italian review (Italian foreign ministry, 2024; Fiscomania, snippet only) [data; practitioner consensus, unverified].
5. **"CEE is where the junior banking jobs are."** Foreign banks' CEE sites are service-delivery and technology bases (HSBC, Goldman) [employer-stated]. I found no pre-experience IB analyst scheme there.
6. **"Shared service centres are a safe stepping stone."** ABSL figures show 74.1% of centres have intelligent process automation and a falling junior share, and a PwC Poland partner calls the career shape "a diamond" (ABSL 2025; ACCA May 2026) [data; practitioner consensus].
7. **"Prague, Warsaw, Budapest all pay the same."** Czech junior pay (EUR 2,133) is 22% above Poland (EUR 1,744) in the Mercer data and Czech net-to-gross is higher (ABSL 2025, Eurostat 2025) [data].

## Decision rules

1. If you are an EU citizen and the Gulf is on your list, treat it as a second-job move via an employer you already work for (a London, Paris or Milan transfer), not a first-job plan (because the graduate schemes of sovereign funds and government entities are for nationals, and the open ones depend on visa feasibility).
2. If you want a Gulf first job anyway, target Big 4 or similar Dubai and Doha graduate programmes that state visa sponsorship, and apply on the firm's international graduate page before assuming any quota-ring-fenced scheme is open to you.
3. If the role is in Saudi Arabia and the posting asks for "right to work" without sponsorship, do not apply unless you already hold the right (PwC postings state this for Saudi positions).
4. If the offer is in the UAE or Saudi Arabia and you are an Italian citizen, get tax advice on AIRE registration and Italian residence before accepting, because the 0% tax only works if Italy accepts that you left.
5. If you compare a Gulf offer with London or Paris, compare net minus rent, and subtract the 46-hour week and any missing housing allowance, because tax-free gross hides cost and hours.
6. If you are choosing a Gulf city for security reasons, read the FCDO or Farnesina page dated within the last month for each country, because the Saudi advisory changed on 20 Sep 2026 and regional attacks were reported from 8 and 13 July 2026.
7. If you speak a Western European language (Italian, German, French) and want a European first job with low rent, consider a CEE centre in Wrocław, Brno or Prague over Warsaw (because the same pay leaves EUR 500 to 800 against about EUR 190 in a Warsaw centre flat).
8. If you take a CEE centre job, pick a specialist track (FP&A, risk, analytics) and ask the recruiter in writing what share of last year's cohort moved to a Western office, because ABSL shows automation thinning the junior rung and no source gives transfer rates.
9. If you are a non-EU graduate considering Poland, Czechia, Hungary or Romania, check national work-permit pages before applying, because I did not research them.
10. If you are targeting client-facing banking or MBB in Western Europe, do not treat a CEE bank centre as the route, because the banks' Warsaw and Kraków sites hire for technology, risk and operations.
11. If you are comparing a Romanian and a Polish offer on gross pay, compare net, because Romania keeps 58.5% of gross at the average wage and Poland 71.7% (Eurostat 2025).
