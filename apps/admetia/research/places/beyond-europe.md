# Destinations beyond Europe

> **Not legal, tax or immigration advice.** This file summarises official sources as of the dates given. Rules change and individual cases differ: confirm with the competent authority or a qualified adviser before acting.

## Bottom line

1. **The US STEM list is narrow in business.** Of the 52.xx business codes only 52.1301, 52.1302, 52.1304 and 52.1399 are on the DHS list (July 2024), plus 27.0305 Financial Mathematics; general finance (52.0801) is not. Check the CIP code on the I-20 (ICE; US guide, Caso 7) [data].
2. **US results differ sharply for international graduates.** Duke MMS Class of 2025: 71% offers for temporary-work-authorisation seekers vs 85% for permanent-authorised; median base $75,000 vs $82,500; about 87% of jobs in the US (Duke report) [data, school-stated]. MIT MFin is strong (97.1% offers, $125,000 median) and 20.6% of accepted offers are in Europe [data, school-stated].
3. **US costs are the highest on the list**: MIT MFin tuition $96,884 (12-month), Duke MMS $69,900 with total cost of attendance $105,421, Vanderbilt MS Finance $76,700 [data].
4. **Canada is a stronger work route than its reputation, a weaker PR route.** Public-university master's are PAL-exempt from 1 Jan 2026 and get a 3-year PGWP with no field rule (CLB 7 needed); Ontario's no-job-offer master's PR stream closed 30 May 2026 (IRCC; Ontario) [data]. Beware that Rotman's MFin is mid-career.
5. **Australia is the weakest for international business master's**: 2 years of 485 work rights; in 2025 only 53.3% of international business and management postgraduate coursework graduates were in full-time work (domestic 92.2%) and median pay was A$65,000 vs A$130,000 (QILT) [data].
6. **Singapore is gated by salary and school list, not only by COMPASS points.** EP floor S$5,600/6,200 now, S$6,000/6,600 from 1 Jan 2027; 40 points are reachable with a list school, near-benchmark pay and a non-dominant nationality. NUS GES figures are bachelor's-level and cannot be used for master's (MOM; MOE) [data].
7. **Hong Kong and Japan have sponsor-free entry for top-100 graduates**: IANG gives 24 months without a job offer to recent graduates of Hong Kong programmes; TTPS Category C gives 24 months to top-100 graduates (quota-limited); Japan's J-Find gives two years (2023 source). HKUST's own survey shows median pay of HK$25,000 a month for MSc/MA graduates and 65.5% returning home [data; Japan secondary].
8. **A European master's alone does not give US work rights**: OPT needs a US degree, and the H-1B advanced-degree cap is for US master's holders. Routes that need no US degree exist (J-1 Intern/Trainee, L-1, E-2 for Italian and UK nationals; US guide, Caso 2 and 3). A European master's does open Singapore (C2 list), Hong Kong (TTPS) and possibly Japan [data].
9. **Several Gemini figures were outdated or unsupported** (PAL for master's, H-1B odds, Singapore S$4,746 as a master's benchmark, MOE bond); see Corrections.

## 1. United States

Full official guide and single source of truth: `visas_immigration/united_states/united_states_visas_immigration_guide.md` (verified 6 October 2026). This section adds the business-programme specifics.

### 1.1 Which business master's are STEM-designated

The STEM OPT extension is tied to the programme's CIP code on the DHS list, not to how quantitative the course feels. I read the DHS STEM Designated Degree Program List (last updated 22 July 2024; ICE, https://www.ice.gov/doclib/sevis/pdf/stemList2024.pdf) [data]. In the whole 52.xx (Business) family only four codes are on it:

| CIP code | Title | On the DHS list? |
|---|---|---|
| 52.1301 | Management Science | Yes |
| 52.1302 | Business Statistics | Yes |
| 52.1304 | Actuarial Science | Yes |
| 52.1399 | Management Sciences and Quantitative Methods, Other | Yes |
| 27.0305 | Financial Mathematics | Yes |
| 30.7102 / 30.7104 / 30.7101 | Business Analytics / Financial Analytics / Data Analytics | Yes |
| 45.0603 | Econometrics and Quantitative Economics | Yes |
| 52.0201 General business, 52.0801 Finance, 52.1401 Marketing, 52.0301 Accounting | Not on the list (no 52.02, 52.03, 52.08, 52.14 codes appear) | No |

Practical consequences (inference from the list, [data] for the codes):
- A programme called "Master of Finance" is STEM only if the school registered it under a quantitative code. Princeton's Bendheim Center states that its MFin "is STEM-eligible for visa purposes" (Bendheim Center, https://bcf.princeton.edu/academic-programs/master-in-finance/common-questions/, fetched 2026-10-01) [employer-stated, i.e. school-stated]. MIT Sloan states that its Master of Finance is "a rigorous, STEM-designated program" (https://mitsloan.mit.edu/mfin/explore-program, read 2 Oct 2026) [employer-stated].
- Duke Fuqua's MMS: Foundations of Business "carries the official STEM designation" since August 2022, "instead of the prior MSTeM track" (Duke Fuqua MMS FOB Employment Report 2025, https://www.fuqua.duke.edu/sites/default/files/media/programs/mmsfob/2025%20MMS%20FOB%20Employment%20Report_FINAL.pdf) [data, school-stated]. So a general-management master's can be STEM if registered as 52.1301.
- The list is not frozen, and schools can re-code. CIP 03.0204 (Environmental/Natural Resource Economics) is on the DHS list of 22 July 2024 (US guide, Caso 7); an earlier note here that it was an unconfirmed later addition was wrong. A DHS update after July 2024 is not verified. Always ask the school: "What CIP code is on my I-20?" It is on the I-20, and that code decides eligibility.
- Checked in round 3b (2 Oct 2026; details in verification/round-3b.md): **Berkeley MFE is CIP 27.0305 Financial Mathematics, on the DHS list** (UC Berkeley International Office memo) [data]. **Vanderbilt MS Finance, all three WashU Olin MS Finance tracks, MIT MFin, Princeton MFin, Duke MMS: FoB and Yale MAM state on their own pages that they are STEM-designated** [employer-stated], but none publishes its CIP code. **Michigan Ross Master of Management is not STEM-designated** ("No. The MM is not a STEM-designated program.", Ross MM FAQ) [employer-stated]. So the calculator's STEM flag should read "yes (school-stated; confirm CIP on I-20)" for those seven and "no" for Ross MM.

### 1.2 OPT in numbers (SEVIS by the Numbers, calendar 2024, published 5 June 2025)

From the DHS/ICE report (https://www.ice.gov/doclib/sevis/btn/25_0605_2024-sevis-btn.pdf, read in full) [data]:
- 194,554 students had OPT work authorisation and reported an employer in 2024 (+21% on 2023). 95,384 had STEM OPT authorisations (+54%). 165,524 students participated in STEM OPT, 48.0% from India and 20.4% from China.
- Master's students are 46.5% (667,622) of the post-secondary F-1/M-1 population.
- Among the top majors, Management Science has 40,403 students, "Management Sciences and Quantitative Methods, Other" 30,301 and Business Analytics 20,645. These are exactly the STEM-coded business majors. "Business Administration and Management, General" has 88,257 (not STEM-coded).
- Italy has 11,134, France 14,673, Germany 15,513 and Spain 15,116 student records. India has 422,335 and China 329,541. So Europeans are a small slice of the pool and the STEM OPT pool is dominated by two nationalities.
- Caveat: the report counts authorisations and people working, not what share of graduates stay on after OPT. There is no official series of "OPT to H-1B conversion" in this report. Check anything quoting a conversion rate for its source.

### 1.3 H-1B odds under the weighted lottery

- Full official guide and single source of truth: `visas_immigration/united_states/united_states_visas_immigration_guide.md` (Caso 2, section 3.2). FY2027 was the first weighted lottery: a Level IV wage gets 4 entries, III gets 3, II gets 2 and I gets 1. DHS projects per-person selection chances of 15.29% at Level I, 30.58% at Level II, 45.87% at Level III and 61.16% at Level IV; these are projections, not results [data]. USCIS has not published FY2027 registration counts, so no FY2027 odds figure is usable (US guide, OQ-09). The FY2026 registration figures (343,981 eligible, 120,141 selected, 34.9%) are not comparable with per-person odds.
- Entry-level hires are the people the weighting hurts. A new graduate paid a Level I or II wage gets one or two tickets while a Level IV registrant gets four. I could not confirm from a primary source which OEWS level typical finance/consulting entry salaries fall into, so I do not give a graduate-specific odds figure (Claims to verify #3).
- The $100,000 payment: Proclamation 11069 runs to 21 September 2027, but the implementing policies were vacated by the District of Massachusetts (8 June 2026) and by N.D. California (30 September 2026), and the payment is not being collected for now; appeals are pending (US guide section 3.2) [data]. A change of status from F-1 inside the US is outside the payment only if it is granted; it applies if the change is denied or the person leaves the US before the decision. DHS has also proposed a separate $103,265 fee on all cap-subject petitions (25 August 2026; not law; the text read has no exception for in-country changes of status).
- Gemini's "~35-40% for a master's" single-cycle figure used the old unweighted table. Under the weighted rule it is no longer a single number: it depends on the wage level of the offer.

**Two US rules the lottery figures leave out (added 4 Oct 2026, external audit).**
- **Cap-exempt employers.** The annual H-1B cap and lottery do not apply to petitions from institutions of higher education, their affiliated or related non-profit entities, and non-profit or government research organisations (INA §214(g)(5)), so universities, many teaching hospitals and research institutes can sponsor at any time of year. For a business graduate this matters mainly for university administration, research, health-system and policy roles, not for banks or consultancies [data: statute; practical scope practitioner consensus].
- **Curricular practical training (CPT) can cost you OPT, and is now narrower.** A student who accumulates 12 months or more of full-time CPT is not eligible for post-completion OPT (8 CFR 214.2(f)(10)). Since SEVP Broadcast Messages 2608-01 (12 August 2026) and 2608-02 (24 August 2026), CPT is allowed only where practical training is required for all students in the curriculum; an optional course is not enough. A challenge to these messages (*AAU v. DHS*, 1:26-cv-14520, D. Mass.) was filed on 5 October 2026 and has no ruling yet. Programmes that build long full-time internships into the degree should say how they handle it (US guide, Caso 3 and section 3.3) [data: regulation and SEVP messages].

### 1.4 What the Duke and MIT reports show about internationals

Duke Fuqua MMS: Foundations of Business, Class of 2025 (school report, MBACSEA-style) [data, school-stated]:
- 257 graduates: 137 with permanent US work authorisation and 120 with temporary (mostly F-1 internationals).
- Seeking employment: 99 permanent vs 91 temporary. Total offers at 6+ months: 85% vs 71% (full-time 85% vs 66%). Median base: $82,500 vs $75,000 (81% reported salary).
- Denominator check: offers were 78% of the 190 seekers, but seekers are only 74% of 257 graduates. An international graduate's chance of "a full-time offer by 6 months" over the whole cohort of 120 is roughly 0.66 x 91 / 120, about 50%. That is my arithmetic from the report's table, not a school-stated figure.
- Only 16% of full-time acceptances were school-facilitated; 84% were graduate-facilitated. 51 US companies hired students with non-permanent US work authorisation (of 126 employers overall).
- Location: about 87% of jobs are in the US (Northeast 37%), Asia 10%, Europe 1%. So a Duke MMS is a US-career degree, not a portable global one.
- Industry: financial services 33%, technology 15%, consulting 11%. Function: finance/accounting 42%.

MIT Sloan Master of Finance, Class of 2025 (MIT CDO, https://cdo.mit.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/67/2026/02/2025-MFin-Employment-Report.pdf) [data, school-stated]:
- 117 graduates; 89% international; 104 sought employment; 97.1% of seekers had an offer within six months (94.2% accepted). 100% response, but only 83.1% reported salary.
- Mean base $122,858, median $125,000; median signing bonus $25,000.
- Accepted offers by region: US 62.9% (NYC 38.1%), Europe 20.6% (London 9.3%), Asia 15.5% (Hong Kong 9.3%). With an 89% international class, that means roughly a third left the US.
- 47.2% of February 2025 graduates who accepted rejoined their summer internship employer.

Princeton Bendheim MFin: about 40 students a year, acceptance about 5%, "100%" placement record 2017–2023, but the page I could read gives no salary, location or visa data (Bendheim Center pages, fetched 2026-10-01) [data, school-stated]. Treat "100%" as a coverage claim until the denominator is shown.

Reading across: the Duke and MIT reports show different populations. MIT MFin is a very quantitative 12-18 month finance degree with a US-wide recruiting machine (97% offer rate). Duke MMS recruits mainly 22-year-olds with six months' experience or less and places about half of its international graduates in full-time jobs by six months. Both are STEM-coded, which gives three lottery attempts.

## 2. Canada

Full official guide and single source of truth: `visas_immigration/canada/canada_visas_immigration_guide.md`.

Already verified in places/visas-and-work-rights.md: a master's of at least 8 months earns a 3-year PGWP with no field-of-study rule, and CLB 7 is needed. I re-read both IRCC pages on 1 Oct 2026 and they still say so (IRCC, "About the PGWP" modified 2026-03-09, https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/study-canada/work/after-graduation/about.html; "PGWP eligibility" modified 2026-06-24, https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/study-canada/work/after-graduation/eligibility.html) [data]. The application window is 180 days after programme completion is confirmed.

### 2.1 Caps: what actually binds a master's student

- 2026 national target: 408,000 study permits, down from 437,000 (2025) (IRCC allocations notice, 25 Nov 2025, https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/news/notices/2026-provincial-territorial-allocations-under-international-student-cap.html) [data]. Of these, 155,000 are new arrivals and 253,000 extensions. The notice splits them: 180,000 applicants who need a provincial attestation letter (PAL/TAL), 49,000 master's and doctoral students at public institutions (exempt), 115,000 K-12 and 64,000 other exempt applicants. Provincial PAL allocations: Ontario 104,780, Quebec 93,069, BC 32,596, Alberta 32,271.
- **From 1 January 2026, degree-granting master's and doctoral programmes at public institutions no longer need a PAL/TAL** (IRCC PAL page, https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/study-canada/study-permit/get-documents/provincial-attestation-letter.html, fetched 2026-10-01) [data]. So the cap that choked 2024–25 is largely lifted for public-university master's students, but a separate allocation of 49,000 permits exists for them, so it is not unlimited. Private institutions and non-degree master's-level programmes are different cases I did not check.
- The levels plan therefore cuts new arrivals mostly in colleges and undergraduate programmes. A European master's applicant faces a competitive public-university admission, not a lottery.

### 2.2 Graduate-to-PR: the route is narrower than the permit suggests

- Ontario's Masters Graduate stream, one of the few no-job-offer provincial routes, "was closed as of May 30, 2026, as part of the OINP redesign" (Government of Ontario, https://www.ontario.ca/page/oinp-masters-graduate-stream, fetched 2026-10-01) [data]. A secondary summary says the eight streams were consolidated into an employer-driven Ontario Workforce Priority stream from 26 June 2026 (silaws.com, 14 June 2026; unverified, secondary).
- BC's International Post-Graduate stream closing on 7 January 2025: I did not re-verify it (Claims to verify #4).
- Express Entry Canadian Experience Class draws in August and September 2026 had minimum CRS scores of 523 (18 Aug), 521 (1 Sep) and 519 (15 Sep), 2,000 invitations each (immigration.ca and cicnews, Sept 2026; unverified, secondary: the IRCC rounds page I could read was blank) [secondary]. A CEC candidate needs a year of skilled Canadian work, which comes after the PGWP starts. Express Entry is a points system where age, language scores (CLB) and Canadian experience dominate; I did not compute a typical Italian master's graduate's score, because the exact CRS table was not read.
- Net effect: Canada's guaranteed part is the 3-year PGWP. The uncertain part is converting to PR. This is the same shape as the UK/US route but with a longer, lower-stakes work window.

### 2.3 Which master's are actually pre-experience

Key finding: many "Master of Finance" programmes in Canada are mid-career degrees.
- Rotman MFin is 15 months, hybrid, and requires "2+ years of professional finance experience"; one third of graduates work at Canada's top five banks (Rotman, https://www.rotman.utoronto.ca/MFin/, fetched 2026-10-01) [data, school-stated]. It is not a pre-experience programme.
- Pre-experience options: Ivey MSc Management (16 months; business analytics, digital management, international business; CEMS MIM link), Schulich MF (12 months, 3 terms; I did not verify its experience requirement), and others. I read Ivey's report and Schulich's tuition letter below.
- Ivey MSc Class of 2023/24: overall offer rate 85%; combined median base C$75,000 (mean C$73,037; range C$30,000 to C$135,000); financial services 30%, consulting 14%, technology 11% (Ivey MSc Employment Report 2023/24, https://www.ivey.uwo.ca/media/y3odhxzg/ivey-msc-employment-report.pdf) [data, school-stated]. Business Analytics class of 2023 (71 students, 63% international): 97% of seekers received an offer within six months; average base C$78,001 (68% salary reporting); 80% of jobs in Toronto/GTA, 6% London (Ontario), 11% elsewhere in Canada and 4% abroad (France, Norway), chart-read and rounded. Coverage note: the "97%" is of those seeking; the salary rate is 68%.

### 2.4 Verdict on H-c ("a weak study-to-work route for business master's")
- Not supported for the work stage: public-university master's students are exempt from the PAL, get a 3-year PGWP regardless of field, and the 12-month Schulich MF or 16-month Ivey MSc turn into the same 3-year permit as a longer degree (IRCC) [data].
- Supported for the PR stage: the Ontario no-job-offer master's stream is gone, and CEC cut-offs in Sept 2026 were 519–523 [secondary]. The route is "three years of work rights, PR uncertain". See Hypotheses tested.

## 3. Australia

Full official guide and single source of truth: `visas_immigration/australia/australia_visas_immigration_guide.md`.

### 3.1 Temporary Graduate visa (subclass 485), Post-Higher Education Work stream
Read on the Home Affairs page, "last updated 24/09/2026" (https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/visas/getting-a-visa/visa-listing/temporary-graduate-485/post-higher-education-work; fetched with curl and read from the page's embedded JSON, 2026-10-01) [data]:
- Age: 35 or under at application. Under 50 only if the qualifying degree is a Masters (research) or PhD (the page lists further exceptions I did not read in full).
- Length: bachelor 2 years; **master's by coursework (and extended) 2 years**; master's by research 3 years; PhD 3 years. Hong Kong and BNO passport holders may stay up to 5 years. Indian nationals under AI-ECTA get 3 years for coursework master's, 4 for PhD.
- Conditions: you "must find your own employment"; you must be in Australia when applying and have held a Student visa in the last six months; the visa cannot be extended.
- For a 22–25-year-old European the age cap is irrelevant. It matters for career changers (see decisions/mba-and-career-switchers.md).

### 3.2 Caps
- The 2026 National Planning Level (NPL) is 295,000 new international students, 25,000 above 2025 and "still 8 per cent below the immediate post-COVID peak" (Dept of Education / IEAA summary of the 4 Aug 2025 ministerial release, https://ieaa.org.au/IEAA/News/2025/Australian-Government-2026-National-Planning-Level.aspx) [data]. For 2027 the government kept it at 295,000 and says it "is not a cap or a limit on student numbers" but a prioritisation system for Student visa applications. Commencements in 2026 are 8% below the same period of 2025 (StudyAustralia.gov.au summary of the Education Department notice, https://www.studyaustralia.gov.au/en/tools-and-resources/news/australia-confirms-international-education-settings-for-2027; I did not read the department's own page, which timed out) [data, secondary]. So the cap is unlikely to bind a postgraduate applicant to a Go8 university in 2026–27; the allocation is per provider.
- All official fees (Student visa subclass 500 A$2,500 base from 1 July 2026 [AU-SRC-01], subclass 485 A$5,750 [AU-SRC-04], financial capacity A$29,710/yr [AU-SRC-13]) and official processing times are verified in `visas_immigration/australia/australia_visas_immigration_guide.md`.

### 3.3 Outcomes of international business master's: the QILT 2025 International Report
QILT, 2025 Graduate Outcomes Survey, International Report, June 2026 (https://www.qilt.edu.au/docs/default-source/default-document-library/2025-gos-international-report.pdf, read in full via PDF text) [data]:

| Postgraduate coursework, 2025 | International | Domestic |
|---|---|---|
| Full-time employment rate, all areas | 52.2% (2023: 60.7%) | 88.3% |
| Overall employment rate, all areas | 73.7% | 93.6% |
| Median annual full-time salary, all areas | A$69,400 | A$104,700 |
| Business and management: full-time employment | 53.3% | 92.2% |
| Business and management: median full-time salary | A$65,000 | A$130,000 |
| Perceived overqualification (full-time employed) | 38.7% | 28.3% |

Reading notes (denominator and coverage):
- International salary figures "only include data for international graduates working in Australia". Those who left for home or a third country are not in the salary median, so the survivorship runs in the optimistic direction.
- The 2025 report changed how the labour force is defined; full-time rates for 2025 are "not directly comparable" with earlier years, though the Department's analysis puts the effect on postgraduate coursework at under 1 percentage point.
- Further full-time study and age differences explain part of the gap (the report says so). Business and management shows a "large gap" between domestic and international, and the report suggests jobs there may require "a deeper cultural and environmental understanding of Australia".
- 22.8% of postgraduate coursework graduates cite "do not have permanent residency" as the main reason for working in a job that does not fully use their education.
- The Gemini file's A$35,300 gap and 38.7% figures are correct against this report (it just attributed them to the wrong URL).

### 3.4 Fees
- University of Melbourne Master of Finance 2026: A$65,984 per EFTSL, 1.5 EFTSL, indicative total A$102,276 (aggregator unienrol.com via search snippet; the official page returned 403. unverified, secondary) [secondary].
- University of Sydney Master of Commerce: the Gemini file's A$54,500 a year could not be confirmed (page content did not load). Claims to verify #6.

### 3.5 Verdict
Australia is a weak study-to-work route for business master's: 2 years of work rights with a 52–53% full-time employment rate for international business/management postgraduate coursework graduates, versus 92% for domestic. It is a strong choice only for people who plan to leave afterwards or who come with an employer-linked route.

## 4. Singapore

Full official guide and single source of truth: `visas_immigration/singapore/singapore_visas_immigration_guide.md`.
Thresholds are already in places/visas-and-work-rights.md. I re-read the MOM page (updated 28 April 2026, https://www.mom.gov.sg/passes-and-permits/employment-pass/eligibility, fetched 2026-10-01) [data]: the qualifying salary is S$5,600 a month (S$6,200 in financial services) now, and S$6,000 (S$6,600) from 1 January 2027; it is "benchmarked to the top 1/3 of local PMET salaries by age". COMPASS needs 40 points. There is no fresh-graduate exemption. Candidates on S$22,500 a month or more, and intra-corporate transferees, skip COMPASS.

### 4.1 COMPASS arithmetic for a European master's graduate
Scoring rubric (MOM, COMPASS rubric, https://www.mom.gov.sg/-/media/mom/documents/press-releases/2023/annex-a---compass-scoring-rubric.pdf; rubric dated 2023, check against the 2026 page) [data]:
- C1 Salary: 20 points at or above the 90th percentile of local PMET salaries in the sector for the candidate's age, 10 points at the 65th to 90th, 0 below 65th.
- C2 Qualifications: 20 for a top-tier institution, 10 for a degree-equivalent qualification.
- C3 Diversity: 20 if the candidate's nationality is under 5% of the firm's PMETs, 10 at 5% to under 25%, 0 at 25% or more.
- C4 Local employment: 20 if the firm's local-PMET share is at or above the 50th percentile of its subsector, 10 at the 20th to 50th, 0 below.
- Bonus: +20 for a Shortage Occupation List job (+10 if the candidate's nationality is a third or more of the firm's PMETs); +10 for firms meeting strategic-priority criteria. Small firms with fewer than 25 PMETs get 10 on both C3 and C4 by default.

A graduate paid close to the qualifying salary is roughly at the 65th percentile of the local PMET distribution (my inference from "top 1/3" benchmarking), so C1 is about 10. With a top-tier degree (C2 = 20) that gives 30. The missing 10 comes from either C3 or C4, which most firms with a mixed workforce give a European. So the sums work for a European graduate of a top-tier school at a normal employer. They fail only when salary is below the benchmark, the degree is not on the list, or the firm is dominated by the candidate's own nationality (the Gemini file's example was an Indian or Chinese national at a firm with many compatriots).

C2 list (MOM, "List of institutions awarded 20 points under COMPASS Criterion 2", released Nov 2025, applicable from 1 Jan 2026, https://www.mom.gov.sg/-/media/mom/documents/work-passes-and-permits/compass/compass-c2-list-of-top-tier-institutions-upcoming.pdf, read via PDF text) [data]:
- Group A (all faculties) includes NUS, NTU, SMU, Oxford, Cambridge, LSE, Imperial, Warwick, Manchester, MIT, Princeton, Duke, Michigan, Columbia, Berkeley, TUM, Copenhagen, Uppsala, Amsterdam, Politecnico di Milano, Sciences Po (politics faculties) and PSL institutions including Paris-Dauphine.
- Group B (specific faculties only) lists HEC Paris, ESSEC, IESE, IE, INSEAD, IMD and LBS under "Business Administration (for MBAs - master's degrees)", and St. Gallen for accounting, finance, economics and business. Whether a MiM or MSc at these schools counts is not obvious from the list's wording (Claims to verify #7).
- I did not find Bocconi, Mannheim, Erasmus/RSM, Vanderbilt or WashU in the extracted text. A degree outside the list scores 10, not 20 (rubric), which makes the C3/C4 contribution decisive. Text extraction can miss names, so check the PDF.
- Consequence: the "does my school count" question is a real, programme-specific Singapore filter, and a Bocconi graduate is not automatically at a disadvantage in practice (small firms and good diversity scores still pass), but the margin is thinner.

### 4.2 What graduates actually earn: do not use the headline S$4,746
- The NUS Graduate Employment Survey 2025 is published by bachelor's degree. Survey: 7,567 fresh graduates, 68.2% response, target 70% (MOE, https://www.moe.gov.sg/api/media/a43cdd0b-4f2d-44f8-a98c-7ff48a7670ac/Web-Publication-NUS-GES-2025.pdf, read in full) [data]. NUS BBA (non-honours) median gross S$6,000 (25th percentile S$4,000, 75th S$9,000, 82.5% in full-time permanent jobs, 62% response); BBA (Hons) median S$4,669 basic, S$4,860 gross, 80.2% in full-time permanent jobs.
- The survey has no master's rows, and it does not say how many respondents are foreign nationals. The Gemini file used the all-NUS S$4,746 median as if it were the benchmark a master's graduate faces. That is a bachelor's-level, all-nationality figure and cannot answer an EP question. I found no published Singapore master's coursework outcomes for business.
- NUS MSc Finance, August 2026 intake: programme fee S$69,000 before GST, S$75,210 with 9% GST (programme site, mscfin.nus.edu.sg, via search result; page did not render for my fetch; unverified, secondary) [secondary].

### 4.3 The "tuition grant bond" claim does not apply to these masters
The MOE Tuition Grant page says the scheme covers "selected full-time diploma or undergraduate studies" and that international recipients "must serve a 3-year bond" (MOE, https://www.moe.gov.sg/financial-matters/tuition-grant-scheme, fetched 2026-10-01) [data]. Coursework master's fees like NUS MSc Finance are published as full fees, with no grant. The Gemini file's 10% compound-interest penalty could not be confirmed on the page I read; the claim also assumed the grant applies to masters.

### 4.4 Verdict on H-b ("COMPASS makes a fresh-graduate EP unrealistic outside finance/tech")
Partly supported, in a different way than stated. COMPASS points are not the main barrier for a European with a top-tier degree: the salary floor is. S$5,600 to S$6,200 (S$6,000 to S$6,600 from 2027) is above the median bachelor's graduate pay (NUS and NTU overall medians S$4,500 to S$4,750 in 2025 [data, bachelor's]), so it is realistic mainly in finance, tech and consulting where entry pay exceeds it, and unrealistic in most other sectors. See Hypotheses.

## 5. Hong Kong

Full official guide and single source of truth: `visas_immigration/hong_kong/hong_kong_visas_immigration_guide.md`.

### 5.1 Rules (read on the Immigration Department pages, 2026-10-01, verified 2026-10-05 [HK-SRC-06..10])
- **IANG (non-local graduates)**: a recent graduate (applying within six months of graduation) needs no job offer and "may be granted 24 months' stay on time limitation only without other conditions of stay". Non-recent graduates need a job offer "at a level commonly taken up by degree holders" with market-level pay. The scheme is "quota-free and non-sector specific". Processing is about two weeks for recent graduates and four for non-recent, longer in the June to August rush (ImmD IANG page, related-links date 29-04-2026, https://www.immd.gov.hk/eng/services/visas/IANG.html) [data]. A top-tier employment route to a six-year extension exists for people earning at least HK$2 million in the previous tax year (same page).
- **Top Talent Pass Scheme (TTPS)**: Category A (income of at least HK$2.5 million in the past year, initial stay 36 months), B (graduates with a Bachelor's degree from eligible universities with at least three years' experience in the past five years, 24 months) and C (graduates with a Bachelor's degree from eligible universities in the past five years with under three years' experience, 24 months, subject to an annual quota of 10,000, first come first served). Note: ImmD FAQ Q15/Q16 explicitly mandates an eligible **Bachelor's degree**; a master's or PhD alone from a top-100 school does not qualify without an eligible Bachelor. Eligibility depends on an aggregate list of 200 eligible institutions (including Politecnico di Milano). Processing about four weeks (ImmD TTPS page, 10-07-2026, https://www.immd.gov.hk/eng/services/visas/TTPS.html) [data].
- Consequence for a European: a graduate with a Bachelor's degree from a top-100 European school on the list (Category C) can go to Hong Kong without studying there, with 24 months and no sponsor, while the 10,000 quota lasts. A degree from a Hong Kong university gives the same 24 months through IANG, quota-free. At renewal (month 24), both require a degree-level job offer or an active business. See `visas_immigration/hong_kong/` for the complete 10-case guide.

### 5.2 Costs and outcomes
- HKUST MSc in Finance: total programme fee HK$460,000 for the 2027-28 intake (tuition up to 34 credits, textbooks; living costs excluded; no on-campus housing) (HKUST Business School MFin site, https://mfin.hkust.edu.hk/admissions/program-fee-and-expenses, fetched 2026-10-01) [data]. The Gemini file's HKU MFin fee of HK$462,000 could not be confirmed; a search snippet suggested HK$231,000 a year for HKU, which would be about the same total over two years (Claims to verify #9).
- HKUST Graduate Employment Survey 2025 (taught postgraduates, https://career.hkust.edu.hk/web/public/documents/SurveyFindings_TPG_2025.pdf, read in full) [data, school-stated]:
  - 2,452 MSc/MA respondents, of whom only 21.8% were full-time employed in the survey and **65.5% are classed as "emigrated or returned to homeland"**. The cohort is mostly non-local students who go home, mainly to the Chinese mainland.
  - Of employed MSc/MA graduates (n=420 with a base location), 93.3% were based in Hong Kong.
  - Gross monthly pay, all MSc/MA full-time employed: median HK$25,000 (mean HK$29,019). Banking/finance: mean HK$36,894, median HK$30,000. School of Business and Management MSc/MA: mean HK$29,273, median HK$25,000. MBA median HK$49,250 (42 respondents).
  - Coverage check: these averages mix every MSc/MA programme and are for the whole school, not for finance. Hong Kong employers' stated analyst pay (for example an investment bank analyst) is much higher but is not what a typical business master's reaches. The Gemini file's HK$60,000 to HK$75,000 analyst figure came from a news site I did not verify.
- Language: Gemini states that Mandarin fluency is a "non-negotiable" for front-office roles, citing a recruiter. I did not verify this from a primary source (Claims to verify #10). The MIT Sloan report shows 9.3% of its accepted offers in Hong Kong and 4.1% in Shanghai (MIT MFin 2025 report) [data], so non-Chinese-speaking finance graduates do get Hong Kong jobs, but the sample is a top quant programme.

### 5.3 Verdict
Hong Kong is the easiest Asian permit (24 months, no sponsor, quota-free through a local degree), but a local business master's mostly yields median pay near HK$25,000 to HK$30,000 a month in the school's own survey, with most students leaving. It works for finance-hub entry only if you are placed in banking, markets or asset management, usually via a summer internship. Treat it as a high-variance route with weak published evidence for Europeans.

## 9. Home-field comparison: what a non-European master's does for a later European career

**What I could verify (data)**

## Hypotheses tested

**H-a (assigned). "A non-STEM US master's gives internationals one year of work rights and a low chance of staying."**
- Verdict: partly supported.
- Holds when: the programme's CIP code is not on the DHS list (finance 52.0801, general business 52.0201, marketing 52.1401 are not among the four 52.xx codes on the list I read). Then OPT is 12 months (places/visas-and-work-rights.md) and the lottery has one or two attempts (registration is possible in the March before graduation and in the March during OPT; inference, consistent with the visas file) [data for the list and OPT length].
- Fails or needs care: (1) "low chance" is not measured. USCIS publishes selected/eligible (FY2026: 34.9%) but not OPT-to-H-1B conversion, and the FY2027 weighted lottery depends on wage level, not programme. (2) STEM status is cheap to obtain at some schools: Duke registered its MMS under the STEM list in 2022 [data, school-stated]. (3) Employer green-card and O-1 routes exist outside the lottery (not researched here).
- Key evidence: DHS STEM list (ICE, July 2024) [data]; USCIS registration table [data]; Duke 2025 report [data, school-stated].

**H-b (assigned). "Singapore's COMPASS makes a fresh-graduate Employment Pass unrealistic outside finance/tech."**
- Verdict: partly supported, with a different mechanism.
- Holds when: the offer is below the qualifying salary (S$5,600, S$6,200 in financial services, rising to S$6,000 and S$6,600 on 1 Jan 2027). Median bachelor's pay at NUS, NTU and SMU in 2025 is S$4,500 to S$4,750, below the line, so most non-finance, non-tech entry jobs cannot sponsor an EP (MOM; MOE GES 2025) [data, bachelor's level].
- Fails when: the candidate has a degree on the C2 list (20 points), salary near the benchmark (about 10 points) and works for a firm where their nationality is under 25% of PMETs (10 points) or a small firm (10 points on C3 and C4 by default): 40 points is reachable without a "finance/tech" label (MOM rubric) [data].
- Not tested: no primary data on EP approval rates for fresh graduates; the S$4,746 figure in the Gemini file is not a master's figure.

**H-c (assigned). "Canada's post-2024 caps and PGWP field rules make it a weak study-to-work route for business master's."**
- Verdict: not supported for study-to-work; supported for work-to-PR.
- Holds (for PR): the Ontario Masters Graduate stream is closed (30 May 2026), CEC cut-offs were 519-523 in Aug-Sept 2026 [secondary].
- Fails (for permits): public-university master's are PAL-exempt from 1 Jan 2026, field of study does not matter for master's, and 8+ months earns a 3-year PGWP; only the CLB 7 language test binds [data].
- Condition: the programme must be at a public designated learning institution and the applicant must reach CLB 7 in all four skills.

**H-d (own). "STEM designation belongs to the CIP code on the I-20, not to the school's brand, so two programmes at one school can differ."**
- Verdict: supported.
- Evidence: the DHS list contains only 52.1301, 52.1302, 52.1304 and 52.1399 of the 52.xx business codes; Duke's report says MMS moved to STEM designation in August 2022, replacing the earlier MSTeM track, which shows that classification is programme-specific [data].
- Fails: nowhere found; round 3b adds a live example: Duke's MMS (general management) is STEM-designated while Michigan Ross's Master of Management is not [employer-stated].

**H-e (own). "In Canada, programmes named 'Master of Finance' are mid-career degrees, so a pre-experience European should look at MSc Management, not the MFin."**
- Verdict: supported for Rotman, not generalisable.
- Evidence: Rotman MFin requires 2+ years' professional finance experience (Rotman site) [data]; Ivey's MSc is explicitly pre-experience, and its 2023/24 report shows 85% offers (Ivey) [data, school-stated]. I did not check Schulich's requirements.

**H-f (own). "Sponsor-free permits (Canada PGWP, Hong Kong IANG/TTPS) give more optionality than US OPT for the same money."**
- Verdict: partly supported.
- Evidence: PGWP 3 years, IANG 24 months with no job offer, TTPS 24 months; vs OPT 12 (+24 STEM) months followed by a lottery [data]. Optionality is not the same as earnings: HKUST median pay is HK$25,000 a month across MSc/MA (HKUST 2025) and Ivey median C$75,000, versus MIT median $125,000 and Duke $80,000 (school reports) [data, school-stated]. US pay is highest where the work rights are most fragile.

**H-g (own). "In Australia, the international penalty in business is larger than in other fields."**
- Verdict: partly supported.
- Evidence: international business and management postgraduate coursework graduates have 53.3% full-time employment vs 92.2% for domestic, and a median salary of A$65,000 vs A$130,000, against all areas at 52.2% vs 88.3% and A$69,400 vs A$104,700 (QILT 2025) [data]. The employment gap (38.9 vs 36.1 points) is only slightly larger; the salary gap is much larger. QILT itself suggests that business jobs require local cultural understanding.

## Common mistakes and myths

Each item is a reading error I can correct from an official or school source. I did not find advisor or recruiter statements for these, so none is tagged [practitioner consensus].
1. **"Any US master's gets 3 years."** Only programmes whose CIP code is on the DHS STEM list get the 24-month extension; the 52.xx list is just four codes (ICE list, July 2024) [data].
2. **"The H-1B lottery is about 25% (or 35-40% for masters)."** The FY2026 selected/eligible ratio was 34.9%, but FY2027 used a weighted lottery by wage level, so entry-level offers get fewer entries (USCIS) [data].
3. **"A US degree means a US job."** Duke MMS: 71% offers for international seekers by six months, vs 85% for permanent-authorised; roughly half of the international class had a full-time offer (my arithmetic) [data, school-stated].
4. **"Canadian masters need a provincial attestation letter."** Not from 1 January 2026 for public-university degree programmes (IRCC) [data].
5. **"Canadian master's degree means PR."** The Ontario no-job-offer route closed on 30 May 2026 (Ontario.ca) [data].
6. **"Singapore's median graduate salary shows what a master's earns."** The GES is by bachelor's degree and has no master's rows (MOE GES 2025) [data].
7. **"The Australian age limit is 50."** For the 485 post-higher-education stream it is 35, except for research masters and PhD (Home Affairs, 24 Sept 2026) [data].
8. **"Hong Kong visas need a job offer."** Not for recent graduates under IANG (ImmD) [data]. Whether finance jobs there need Mandarin is unverified here.
9. **"A top-ranked school automatically scores 20 under COMPASS."** The list is institution- and faculty-specific. Business schools such as HEC and ESSEC are listed under "Business Administration (for MBAs - master's degrees)"; Bocconi was not found in the extracted list (MOM, Nov 2025) [data].

## Decision rules

1. If you are an EU citizen and your target is a US career, and the programme is not STEM-coded on the I-20, prefer a STEM-designated alternative or accept a one-year, one-or-two-lottery runway (DHS list; USCIS) because the extension is what buys extra attempts.
2. If you are weighing two US finance programmes, prefer the one that publishes an international-student outcome split (like Duke's temporary-authorisation columns) because overall rates hide a lower rate for F-1 graduates (Duke 2025: 71% vs 85%).
3. If the US total cost (tuition plus living) is $100,000 or more (Duke $105,421; MIT 12-month tuition alone $96,884) and you would return to Europe within a year, prefer a European programme unless the school's own report shows 20% or more of offers in Europe (MIT: 20.6%).
4. If you want permit security more than pay, prefer Canada at a public university (3-year PGWP, no field rule) over the US non-STEM route, because the PGWP does not depend on a lottery (IRCC) and the PAL cap no longer applies to public master's.
5. If you plan Canadian PR after the PGWP, do not rely on a provincial no-job-offer stream and plan for an employer job or French, because the Ontario Masters Graduate stream closed on 30 May 2026 and CEC cut-offs were around 519-523 in Sept 2026 [secondary].
6. If a Canadian "Master of Finance" asks for 2+ years' experience (Rotman), treat it as mid-career and compare pre-experience MSc Management programmes (Ivey) instead.
7. If you are 35 or under and set on Australia, expect two years of work rights and a business job market where international graduates have about 53% full-time employment (QILT 2025); prefer Australia only with a clear plan to return or a sponsor.
8. If your target is Singapore and your degree is from a school on the MOM C2 list (check the list by faculty), a finance or tech offer at or above S$6,200 (S$6,600 from 2027 in financial services) is realistic; if your school is not listed, aim for firms with few compatriots and small firms (C3, C4).
9. If your degree is from a top-100 university and you want Hong Kong without studying there, apply under TTPS Category C within five years of graduation and with under three years' experience, and check the quota is open on the ImmD page before relying on it.
10. If you plan to study in Hong Kong, assume the school's survey includes mostly returners and that median pay is HK$25,000 to HK$30,000 a month in its own data; treat banking placement as an internship-led route, not a default.
11. If you are considering Japan, use J-Find only if your degree is from a top-100 university; do not plan on specific Japanese salary or language figures from this file (unverified).
12. If you would apply for a US H-1B later with a European master's, note that the 20,000 advanced-degree slots are for US master's holders and you compete in the 65,000 regular pool (USCIS).

- **STEM flag on every US programme in the calculator**: store the CIP code and a "STEM-designated: yes/no/unverified" field, sourced from the I-20 or school page, with the DHS list date (22 July 2024) shown. Warn that general finance (52.0801) and business administration (52.0201) are not on the DHS list.

## Corrections to the Gemini leads

1. **Master's PAL requirement (Canada)**: the lead says master's and PhD students need a PAL. From 1 January 2026, public-university degree programmes at that level do not (IRCC PAL page).
2. **Ontario stream closure**: the lead says it closed in June 2026 and quotes a page URL I did not verify. Ontario's own page says the Masters Graduate stream "was closed as of May 30, 2026".
3. **US H-1B odds**: the lead's 36% single-cycle and 73.7% three-cycle figures use unweighted odds and assume independent draws. FY2027 selection is weighted by wage level. FY2026 selected/eligible was 34.9%. The lead says a non-STEM student has "exactly 1" lottery attempt; the visas file says "one or two".
4. **Singapore EP**: correct for 2026, but it omits the increase from 1 January 2027 (S$6,000 and S$6,600). Its S$4,746 median is an all-NUS bachelor's-level figure, not a master's benchmark, and its "automatic rejection at 30 points" assumed zero diversity and local-support points.
5. **MOE tuition grant bond and 10% interest**: the MOE page says the grant covers "selected full-time diploma or undergraduate studies"; I could not confirm a master's bond or the 10% interest.
6. **Hong Kong**: IANG 24 months is right. The 10,000 TTPS Category C quota is confirmed on ImmD [HK-SRC-08], the 2026 Aggregate List has 200 institutions (including Politecnico di Milano) [HK-SRC-09], and HKU MFin fee of HK$462,000 is verified on the HKU Business School site [HK-SRC-25]; see `visas_immigration/hong_kong/`.
7. **Japan**: the ISA URL it cites returns 404; its numbers are unverified here.
8. **Australia**: the age limit (35), 2-year coursework period and QILT figures (A$35,300 gap, 38.7% overqualification) are correct. It missed that research masters get 3 years and that Hong Kong/BNO holders get 5.
9. **Tables with "short quote" columns**: I could not confirm several quoted phrases word for word on the pages cited. Do not reuse them.

## Numbers to treat with care

- **Chart-read or rounded**: Ivey Business Analytics location split (80% Toronto, 6% London, 11% other Canada, 4% abroad) is read from a chart and rounded; the parts sum to 101%.
- **Own arithmetic, not school-stated**: Duke "about 50% of internationals had a full-time offer" (0.66 x 91 / 120); MIT "about 84% of the class accepted an offer" (0.942 x 104 / 117); the H-1B FY2026 ratio selected/eligible (34.9%, not comparable with per-person odds). Label as "Admetia calculation".
- **School self-reported**: all employment-report figures (MIT, Duke, Ivey, HKUST, Princeton). MIT and Duke follow reporting standards and show response data; Princeton's "100%" shows no denominator.
- **Single-source or secondary**: CEC cut-offs of 519-523 (immigration.ca/cicnews); Melbourne fee (aggregator); NUS MSc fee (search summary); Rotman and Ivey tuition (search summaries); Princeton and Michigan tuition (search summaries, pages blocked); J-Find details (2023 article); Singapore and HK items marked "secondary".
- **Dated**: the COMPASS rubric document is from 2023 (the C2 list is Nov 2025); DHS STEM list is from July 2024 and may have been updated since; Home Affairs, ImmD and MOM pages change often (dates given in text).
- **Not comparable across rows**: HKUST's survey covers all taught postgraduates; QILT covers all postgraduate coursework or all business and management. Neither is a specific finance master's.
- **Salary currencies**: left as published (USD, CAD, AUD, SGD, HKD). The site should convert at a dated rate and show the date.
