# Student logistics

> **Not legal, tax or immigration advice.** This file summarises official sources as of the dates given. Rules change and individual cases differ: confirm with the competent authority or a qualified adviser before acting.

## Bottom line

1. **Budget a cash buffer for the first two months, not just tuition.** Deposits are capped by law at up to 3 months' net rent in Germany (§551 BGB), 1 month (unfurnished) or 2 months (furnished) in France (service-public, Jun 2026). Add the first month's rent, visa and health charges, and furniture. A €3–6k buffer on arrival is a reasonable planning figure for one person in a major city. That figure is an author estimate built from the caps above, with rents in money/costs-and-funding.md.
2. **UK visas are paid upfront for the whole stay.**
   - **Student visa:** the Immigration Health Surcharge is £776 per year.
   - **Graduate visa:** £937 application fee plus £1,035 per year of surcharge. That's ≈ £2,490 for 18 months, or ≈ £3,007 for 24 months, due when you apply, typically in the weeks after your degree is confirmed (GOV.UK, 2026).
   - This applies to EU citizens too.
3. **Work limits while studying differ by a factor of two or more.**
   - UK degree-level students: 20 hours a week in term time, full-time in vacations (Appendix Student).
   - Germany: 140 full working days a year (§16b AufenthG).
   - France: 964 hours a year (60% of a full-time year) without a separate authorisation (service-public F2728).

   EU citizens studying in another EU country don't face these limits.
4. **The French apprenticeship route is open to non-EU students on a student permit.** The employer is exempt from requesting a work authorisation once the apprenticeship contract is validated by the OPCO (private sector) or Dreets (public sector). The employer must notify the préfecture 2 working days before hiring (service-public F2728, updated 18 Jun 2026). The binding constraint is usually French-language interviews, not law. (A claim in the Gemini library that non-EU first-year students are legally barred is contradicted by this page.)
5. **Switzerland caps non-EU work permits nationally:** 8,500 for 2026 (4,500 B + 4,000 L), plus a separate 3,500 for UK nationals (Federal Council, 19 Nov 2025). Non-EU graduates of Swiss universities get a 6-month job-search permission with up to 15 hours a week of work. A job then needs to be of "high economic interest" and fall within quotas (places/visas-and-work-rights.md). For non-EU students, Switzerland is a place to study, not a reliable place to start a career, unless a large employer sponsors.
6. **Health cover rules differ.**
   - In France, EU/EEA/Swiss students with an EHIC valid for the whole academic year stay covered by their home insurer and needn't register.
   - Non-EU students register on etudiant-etranger.ameli.fr (ameli.fr, Jan 2026).
   - In the UK, the surcharge replaces insurance for NHS access.
   - In Germany, statutory student health insurance is a monthly cost (amount to verify).

**The month-zero cliff at graduation (UK).** To stay on the Graduate visa, you must apply before your Student visa expires and pay £937 plus the surcharge for the whole period, about £2,490 for 18 months, at once (GOV.UK). Plan the cash before your final term ends.

## 2. Working while studying

| Country | Non-EU student work limit | EU students | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| UK | 20 h/week in term time; full-time outside term (degree level); no self-employment | Same as non-EU (EU citizens need a Student visa since Brexit) | GOV.UK Appendix Student |
| Germany | 140 full working days a year ("Arbeitstagekonto"); student jobs at the university don't count | No limit | §16b(3) AufenthG |
| France | 964 hours a year (60% of the legal annual working time) without a separate authorisation; apprenticeships exempt once validated | No limit | service-public F2728 |
| Netherlands | Commonly reported as 16 h/week or full-time in June–August, with an employer-obtained work permit (TWV) | No limit | To verify (IND page not reached) |
| Switzerland | Commonly reported as 15 h/week in term after 6 months of residence; graduates 15 h/week during the 6-month search | Free movement | Graduate rule: places/visas-and-work-rights.md; term rule to verify |
| Spain, Italy | To verify | No limit | — |

**Why it matters.** An internship *during* term can breach the UK cap unless it's part of the course. In Germany, the 140-day account counts every working day, so a long internship can use most of it. Plan internships inside the programme structure (getting-in/recruiting-calendar.md; decisions/timing-and-sequencing.md).

## 3. Health cover

- **France:** EU/EEA/Swiss students with an EHIC valid until the end of the academic year stay covered by their home insurer and don't register in France. Non-EU students register on etudiant-etranger.ameli.fr (ameli.fr, updated 19 Jan 2026) [data]. Whether affiliation is free wasn't stated on the page read (verification/claims-to-verify.md).
- **UK:** the IHS (£776/yr for students; £1,035/yr on the Graduate visa) gives NHS access (GOV.UK) [data].
- **Germany:** statutory student health insurance is required for enrolment. The monthly amount wasn't verified here (verification/claims-to-verify.md).

## 4. Permits that change what's possible

- **France (non-EU):** apprenticeship contracts are allowed on a student permit, with no employer work-authorisation request once validated by the OPCO or Dreets. Ordinary jobs are capped at 964 hours a year. After graduation, jobs must "correspond to the diplomas and experience acquired" (service-public F2728) [data]. For the post-study job-search card, see places/visas-and-work-rights.md.
- **Switzerland (non-EU):** 2026 quotas of 8,500 (B 4,500 + L 4,000), plus 3,500 for UK nationals (Federal Council, 19 Nov 2025) [data]. Employers must show the hire is in the economic interest and that no suitable resident or EU/EFTA candidate is available (SEM; places/visas-and-work-rights.md).
- **UK (everyone except UK/Irish citizens):** Graduate visa of 24 months if you apply by 31 Dec 2026, 18 months from 1 Jan 2027. The Skilled Worker floor is £41,700, or £33,400 for new entrants (GOV.UK) [data].

## 5. Housing reality

Housing shortages in Amsterdam, Dublin, Munich, Zurich and London are widely reported. Some Dutch universities advise students not to come without accommodation (Gemini lead; not verified here). National vacancy statistics weren't fetched (places/countries-and-cities.md claims). **Rule:** secure housing before paying non-refundable programme deposits where possible, or choose programmes with guaranteed first-year housing.

## 6. Arrival paperwork traps (added 4 October 2026)

These are the circular rules that stall students in the first weeks. Sources are university and official pages surfaced by search on 4 October 2026 unless stated; local rules and fees change.

- **Germany: no landlord form, no registration.** Registration at the residents' registration office (Anmeldung) is mandatory within two weeks of moving in and needs your passport plus the landlord's confirmation of move-in (Wohnungsgeberbestätigung). University and DAAD guidance says the registration certificate unlocks the tax ID, a bank account and contracts, while hostels and some short-term rentals refuse to issue the form, so choose your first accommodation by whether it will [data]. If you have no permanent address yet, guidance allows registering the temporary lodging address where the provider issues the form. Some banks and blocked-account providers open an account before registration; for a student-visa application the blocked account comes first, so check with the provider rather than assuming it is impossible.
- **Netherlands: the BSN and the housing shortage.** A citizen service number (BSN) normally needs registration at a municipality, which needs a lease or host who permits registration. Subletting is common and often comes without registration rights, which blocks the BSN, payroll, a bank account and student finance [practitioner consensus, university and student press]. For stays under four months, registering as a non-resident at one of 19 designated municipalities issues a BSN. Ask any landlord in writing whether you can register at the address before paying a deposit.
- **France: VISALE.** Action Logement's VISALE is a free rent guarantee that can replace a French guarantor for students up to about 30, including international students with a long-stay student visa (VLS-TS) or residence permit; if you default, Action Logement pays the landlord and you repay it (123loger and other summaries of Action Logement's rules; terms to check on Action Logement's site) [practitioner consensus]. It matters because French landlords usually ask for a French guarantor.
- **UK: advance rent.** Since 1 May 2026 English landlords cannot require more than one month's rent in advance (see money/costs-and-funding.md section 2); expect to be asked for a UK guarantor or a paid guarantor service instead.
- **Italy: health cover for non-EU students.** Voluntary registration with the national health service for a student with a study permit costs a minimum of €700 for the calendar year, up from about €150, under the 2024 Budget Law (L. 213/2023); the sum is paid with form F24 and is not reduced for part of a year, and €2,000 applies when family dependants are covered (University of Bologna guidance and press summaries, 2024 to 2026) [data, secondary]. EU students use their EHIC or the equivalent.

## Hypotheses tested

**SL-1. "Visa and health charges are a material upfront cash shock for UK Graduate visa applicants."** Verdict: **supported.** £937 + £1,035/yr, due at once: ≈ £2,490 for 18 months (GOV.UK) [data].

**SL-2. "Non-EU students are legally barred from French apprenticeships in their first year."** Verdict: **not supported.** service-public F2728 (Jun 2026) exempts validated apprenticeship contracts from the work-authorisation request and states no first-year condition [data].

**SL-3. "Swiss quotas make Switzerland a weak career start for non-EU graduates."** Verdict: **supported in direction.** The national cap is 8,500 third-country permits, plus the economic-interest test. The share of graduates affected isn't published.

## Decision rules

1. **If** you'll need a UK Graduate visa, **then** set aside about £2,500 (18 months) before your final term ends, **because** the fee and surcharge are paid upfront when you apply.
2. **If** you're non-EU in the UK, **then** keep term-time work under 20 hours a week, and fit internships into the course structure or the vacations.
3. **If** you're non-EU in Germany, **then** track your 140-day work account before accepting a long internship outside the curriculum.
4. **If** you're non-EU and want a French apprenticeship, **then** focus on French-language interview readiness and get the employer to validate the contract with the OPCO early, **because** the legal route is open but the interviews are in French.
5. **If** you're non-EU and considering HSG or another Swiss university, **then** plan Germany, Austria or the Netherlands as your career fallback, **because** of the 8,500 national cap and the economic-interest test.
6. **If** you're renting in Germany or France, **then** budget the legal maximum deposit (3 months net / 1–2 months) plus the first month, and don't pay more than the legal cap.
