# Origin-country playbooks for EU (non-Italian) students

> **Not legal, tax or immigration advice.** This file summarises official sources as of the dates given. Rules change and individual cases differ: confirm with the competent authority or a qualified adviser before acting.

## Bottom line

1. **Grade conversion can cost more than a test gap, but only at schools that convert mechanically.** Mannheim ranks applicants with a linear points formula (60 points at German 1.0, 0 at 4.0). Applying the KMK modified Bavarian formula to LSE's own 2:1 lines, a French applicant (13/20) scores 18 grade points, a Spaniard (7.0/10) 24, a Dutch applicant (7.0/10) 20, a Pole (4.5/5) 46 and an Italian (104/110) 52. The gap between France and Italy, 34 points, exceeds the whole GMAT Focus range from 645 to 685 (30 points). This is the author's calculation from the statute and the KMK text, assuming Nmin equals the national pass mark (Mannheim selection statute, 24 March 2026, https://www.uni-mannheim.de/media/Einrichtungen/zula/Auswahlsatzungen_master/satzung_ma_mmm_en.pdf) [data].
2. **LSE's published equivalences differ sharply in strictness by origin.** 2:1 equals 7.0/10 in Spain and Greece, 13/20 in France, 15/20 in Portugal (a single threshold), 4.5/5 in Poland, 2.2 in Germany and 8.5/10 in Romania (LSE country pages, 2026) [data].
3. **Greece is the one origin where official data show a clear push to leave.** Tertiary unemployment is 14.8% at 25-29, recent-graduate employment is 66.9% against 86.9% for the EU, and Germany's tertiary mean earnings are 1.87 times Greece's in PPS (Eurostat 2025 and 2022; author's calculation) [data]. Spain's penalty is real at 25-29 (10.0% unemployment) but gone by 30-34 (88.0% employment against 89.6%).
4. **Every verified returner tax regime needs about five years abroad (three in Poland), so none helps a "master's abroad, then home" plan.** Spain art. 93 LIRPF (flat 24%, five prior non-resident periods), France art. 155 B CGI (five calendar years, up to eight years of benefit), Portugal IFICI (20% for 10 years, five prior years, and a qualifying-role list that appears to exclude fresh graduates), Poland's ulga na powrot (PLN 85,528 a year tax-free for four years after three years abroad) (BOE, Portal das Financas, podatki.gov.pl, Berton Associes) [data, one secondary]. Greece's art. 5C needs non-residence in 5 of the previous 6 years (AADE FAQ, 2 Oct 2025; the "7 of 8" figure in some summaries belongs to art. 5A, the investor regime), so it also fails a one-to-three-year stay abroad (AADE, aade.gr) [data].
5. **The pay gap from leaving shrinks a lot for CEE origins once prices are counted.** Germany's tertiary mean earnings are 3.38 times Poland's in euros but 1.86 times in PPS, and 3.02 times Romania's in euros but 1.51 times in PPS (Eurostat earn_ses22_30, 2022; author's calculation) [data].
6. **Funding for going abroad is thin and nationality-specific.** The la Caixa postgraduate-abroad fellowship (100 awards in the 2026 call, Spanish or Portuguese nationals, January deadline, tuition plus allowance) and the Onassis Foundation (Greek nationals, February deadline, according to an aggregator summary) are the named schemes; German Auslands-BAfoeG covers up to EUR 5,600 of tuition as a grant for one year, per a university page; I found no French or Polish equivalent (UMH call summary; Regensburg page) [data, secondary hosts].
7. **Emigration is not mainly a UK story any more.** Portuguese entries to the UK fell 37% to 2,766 in 2024, while Switzerland (12,388), Spain (11,332), France (8,088) and Germany (7,410) took most of 65,000 departures (Observatorio da Emigracao, March 2026) [data].
8. **Not tested for lack of evidence:** home public masters versus foreign private programmes (H-c), French, Polish and German emigration of graduates, Greek and Spanish home alternatives. The file covers six origins in full and three briefly; Belgium, Austria, the Nordics and the rest of CEE were skipped for lack of research budget.
## 1. Which origins are covered, and which are skipped

**Full playbooks (6):** Spain, France, Germany, Portugal, Greece, Poland. They were chosen because they differ most on the three things that drive the decision: how the home grade converts (sections 2b and each country), how good the home labour market is for a young graduate (section 2), and whether a return tax regime exists (section 10).

**Short notes (3):** Netherlands, Ireland and Romania (section 9). For these I have LSE equivalences, Eurostat data and the Mannheim calculation, but no funding, emigration or return-regime evidence, so I do not claim more.

**Skipped, and why:** Belgium, Austria, the Nordics, Czechia, Hungary, Bulgaria, Croatia and the Baltics were not researched. The brief asks for the six or seven origins with the strongest evidence, and I ran out of research budget (a usage-limit interruption mid-task, then a reduced cap). This is a gap in coverage, not a finding that they are similar to the countries above. Italy has its own file (places/italy-playbook.md); non-EU origins are in places/origin-countries-non-eu.md.

**How to read the structure of each section.** Degree structure and grade conversion first, then home alternatives, funding abroad, home pay, emigration, return incentives and national pitfalls. Where I could not source a point, I say "not researched" rather than fill it with background knowledge. Statements about degree structure marked "(author background)" are standard Bologna-process facts I did not re-source and should be treated as unverified.

### Mannheim: a points formula that turns pass-mark and ceiling into a score (author's calculation)

Mannheim's Master in Management selection statute (5th amendment, 24 March 2026, English convenience translation) ranks applicants on: bachelor's final or current average grade (maximum 60 points, 60 for German grade 1.0 and 0 for 4.0, linear in steps of 0.1), a semester abroad (22 points), GMAT/GRE (maximum 60 points; GMAT Focus 685+ = 60, 665 = 50, 645 = 30, 625 = 20, 605 = 10, 555 = 1), and relevant experience (maximum 6 points for work). Foreign grades are "converted according to the recommendations of the ZAB". Application deadline for the autumn semester is 15 May (https://www.uni-mannheim.de/media/Einrichtungen/zula/Auswahlsatzungen_master/satzung_ma_mmm_en.pdf) [data].

The conversion uses the "modified Bavarian formula" x = 1 + 3 x (Nmax - Nd) / (Nmax - Nmin), truncated (not rounded) to one decimal. The text I read is the Kultusministerkonferenz resolution of 15 March 1991 "in the version of 18 November 2004", reproduced by a German university of applied sciences (https://hsbi.de/media/hochschulverwaltung/dezernat-vi/hochschuluebersetzerin/information-on-converting-grades-of-certificates-issued-abroad.pdf) [data]. Nmax and Nmin are the "upper and lower parameters according to the suggestions of the ZAB" (the Central Office for Foreign Education, database anabin), not necessarily your university's own pass mark. The resolution is written for foreign university *entrance certificates*, but German universities apply the formula to foreign degree grades (Mannheim's statute does so explicitly). A technical university's page confirms the tool is "guidance only, not binding" (TU Darmstadt, https://www.tu-darmstadt.de/studieren/studieninteressierte/internationale_studieninteressierte/bewerbung_und_zulassung_international/notenumrechnung.de.jsp) [data].

I applied the formula to LSE's own 2:1 and First equivalents, assuming Nmin equals the national pass mark (Spain, Greece, Romania 5/10; France and Portugal 10/20; Poland 3/5; Italy 66/110; Netherlands 5.5/10). This is the author's calculation, not a school figure.

Read-across: a French applicant at LSE's 2:1 line (13/20) scores 18 grade points, an Italian at LSE's 2:1 line scores 52, a 34-point gap. For scale, the GMAT component rises from 30 points at 645 to 60 points at 685, so 34 points is more than the whole 645-to-685 range. A Spanish or Greek applicant at "First" level (9.0 or 8.0) scores 48 or 36. The formula is linear between pass mark and ceiling, so systems where almost nobody approaches the ceiling (French 20-point scale, Dutch 10-point scale, Portuguese 20-point scale) are compressed. This is the known complaint with the formula and is also reflected in German university guidance that strict application disadvantages French, Belgian and Dutch certificates (reported in the search results for the KMK text; I could not open the university page that states it, so treat as secondary, see Claims to verify, item 5).

## 3. Spain

**Degree structure and recognition (author background).** A Spanish *grado* is normally 240 ECTS over four years, followed by a one- or two-year *master*. Four-year grados clear Mannheim's 180-ECTS minimum and LSE's requirement without any "three-year degree" question (see getting-in/admissions.md for the Italian three-year issue, which Spaniards do not face).

**Grade conversion.** LSE treats 7.0/10 as a 2:1 and 9.0/10 as a First (LSE Spain page, 2026) [data]. Spanish marks cluster in the "notable" band, so a 9.0 average is rare while 7.0 is common (author background; I could not retrieve a primary distribution, see Claims to verify, item 4). The practical consequence is a cliff: a Spanish student with an 8.0 is, in LSE's table, between 2:1 and First, with no published rule for the gap. At Mannheim an 8.0 converts to 2.2 (36 of 60 grade points) and a 7.0 to 2.8 (24 points) (author's calculation from the KMK formula, section 2b). Whether Nmin is 5 for Spain in the ZAB database was not verified.

**Home alternatives and reputation.** Spain is itself a destination for the schools in the calculator (IE, Esade, IESE). Public masters (for example at Carlos III or Pompeu Fabra) exist, but I found no primary data on their pay or recruiter reputation, so hypothesis H-c is not tested for Spain (see Hypotheses).

**Funding for nationals going abroad.** The Fundacion "la Caixa" postgraduate-abroad fellowship is the largest named scheme I found. The 2026 call (deadline 19 January 2026, results 21 May 2026) offered 100 fellowships for a master's, doctorate or pre-doctoral project at an EHEA, North American or Asia-Pacific institution, in any field, open to Spanish or Portuguese nationals with a first degree obtained between January 2017 and July 2026. It covers tuition, a monthly allowance by destination country, EUR 1,600 for setting-up costs, travel, visa and insurance, for one year and renewable to a maximum of 24 months, and the application must be written in English (call summary hosted by Universidad Miguel Hernandez, https://sgi.umh.es/files/2025/12/Becas-Posgrado-en-el-Extranjero-2025.pdf, read in full) [data, secondary host]. Two points matter for timing: the deadline falls in January of the year you enrol, so you must apply before you have most offers, and the 2027 call dates were not published when I looked (the next call is probably in January 2027, an assumption). The foundation's own pages returned errors, so the monthly amount is not verified (the snippets said about EUR 1,400, unverified).

**Home market.** Spanish tertiary-educated 25-29-year-olds have a 79.6% employment rate and 10.0% unemployment, against 83.1% and 6.8% for the EU; by 30-34 employment is 88.0% against 89.6% for the EU (Eurostat lfsa_ergaed, lfsa_urgaed, 2025) [data]. So the Spanish penalty is concentrated in the mid-20s and mostly gone by 30. Mean annual gross earnings of tertiary-educated employees were EUR 40,809 in 2022 (PPS 43,761), against EUR 75,732 (PPS 67,684) in Germany (Eurostat earn_ses22_30) [data]. In PPS terms, Germany's tertiary average is 1.55 times Spain's (author's calculation).

**Emigration.** INE counted 137,719 emigrations of Spanish nationals in 2024, but 49,476 of them were born abroad (naturalised or dual nationals), so only 88,243 were Spain-born (INE press release, 11 December 2025, https://www.ine.es/dyngs/Prensa/EMCR2024.htm) [data]. The press release did not give destination or education; the "27.6% to the UK" figure in an unrelated snippet is unverified (see Claims to verify, item 7).

**Return regime.** Art. 93 of the Ley 35/2006 (IRPF), as consolidated by the BOE on 9 September 2026, lets an individual who becomes tax resident in Spain choose non-resident-style taxation for the year of arrival plus five more tax periods, with a flat 24% on employment income up to EUR 600,000 (47% above), if (a) they were not resident in Spain in the five tax periods before arrival and (b) they arrive because of an employment contract (or remote work for a foreign employer under the teleworking visa, as an administrator, entrepreneur, or highly qualified professional serving start-ups or doing R&D) (https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2006-20764) [data]. The law does not mention nationality, so Spaniards returning qualify. The binding constraint is the five prior non-resident periods: a master's year plus two or three years of work abroad does not meet it. Whether 24% beats the ordinary progressive scale at a graduate salary was not calculated (see Claims to verify, item 8).

**Pitfalls.** (1) Fundacion la Caixa applications are due in January, before most admissions decisions. (2) Spanish nationals who were born abroad and hold dual nationality are counted in INE's emigration numbers, which inflates "brain drain" headlines. (3) The Spanish *Titulo Propio* versus *Master Universitario* distinction (an official, registered degree versus a university's own diploma) is cited by Gemini as the key credential trap. I did not verify it here; it matters mostly for Spaniards considering domestic private masters (see Claims to verify, item 9).

## 4. France

**Degree structure and recognition.** The French route that LSE spells out is unusual because it counts the two *classes preparatoires* years: LSE requires "a minimum of three years at a Grande Ecole", which "can include the two year classes preparatoires period", with an average of 13.0/20 (or "A") for a 2:1 and 15/20 for a First; a Master 1 is accepted "with a pass mark" for a 2:1 and 13.0/20 for a First (LSE France page, 2026) [data]. A French licence from a public university needs 13.0/20 averaged across all years studied. French students going to a Spanish, Italian, German or UK school therefore have three possible credentials, and the one that converts best depends on the destination.

**Grade conversion trap.** The French scale is compressed: 13/20 is already a 2:1 for LSE. Under the KMK modified Bavarian formula with Nmin = 10, 13/20 gives German grade 3.1 (18 of 60 points at Mannheim), and even a First-level 15/20 gives 2.5 (30 points) (author's calculation, section 2b). A French applicant at the same relative standing as an Italian at 104/110 (52 points) therefore starts 34 points behind in Mannheim's ranking. German university guidance reportedly acknowledges this disadvantage for French certificates (secondary, see Claims to verify, item 5). Mitigation: Mannheim lets you submit a current average and GMAT points; a GMAT Focus of 685 gives 60 points, so a strong test can offset an unfavourable conversion, but 34 points is more than the whole 645-685 range.

**Home alternatives and reputation.** France's own business schools (HEC, ESSEC, ESCP, EDHEC, emlyon, SKEMA) are the home option. The CGE's 2026 survey (34th edition, collected November 2025 to March 2026) put net employment of grandes ecoles graduates six months out at 76%, down 4.2 points from 2025, with 74.5% for management schools; for management graduates working in France the mean gross annual salary was EUR 40,825 (EUR 42,799 in the Ile-de-France, EUR 36,511 elsewhere) and 42.3% of hires came through the final internship or the apprenticeship employer (reported by L'Express Education, https://lexpress-education.com/articles/enquete-cge-2026/, and Diplomeo, https://diplomeo.com/actualite-cge_resultats_enquete_insertion_2026; the CGE's own report was not opened) [data, secondary]. The 76% and the 42.3% are the library's verified figures; the management-school salary comes from the secondary reports only. A median of EUR 40,000 appears in Diplomeo; L'Express gives a different "overall" median of EUR 39,600, so I use only the mean. 

**Funding for nationals going abroad.** I found no French scheme comparable to la Caixa. A search snippet said that a CROUS means-tested grant (EUR 1,454 to EUR 6,335 a year in the snippet's scale) is open to French students under 28 who are enrolled in an EU institution, but I could not open the official page, so treat this as unverified (see Claims to verify, item 10).

**Home market.** French tertiary-educated 25-29-year-olds have 84.4% employment and 8.7% unemployment; recent graduates (1-3 years out, 20-34) have 82.0% employment against 86.9% for the EU (Eurostat, 2025) [data]. Mean annual gross earnings for tertiary employees were EUR 50,736 in 2022, PPS 46,713, close to the EU average of EUR 50,948 (Eurostat earn_ses22_30) [data]. That puts France roughly at the EU average, with no obvious pay premium for leaving except in London or Zurich, which this file does not measure.

**Emigration.** Not researched in this session.

**Return regime.** Art. 155 B of the Code general des impots: a person not fiscally domiciled in France during the five full calendar years before taking up the post, recruited from abroad or transferred within a group, can exempt a prime d'impatriation (or 30% of net remuneration on a flat basis), capped at 50% of total remuneration, until 31 December of the eighth year after arrival; nationality is irrelevant, so a French national returning after five years abroad qualifies (Berton Associes, updated 17 February 2026, https://www.berton-associes.fr/blog/droit-fiscal/regime-fiscal-impatries-france-guide-complet-article-155b/) [practitioner consensus, secondary]. I could not open the Legifrance or BOFiP text for the current wording: the BOFiP page I did open (BOI-RSA-GEO-40-10-20-20151120) was withdrawn in 2017 and described a five-year regime, which shows how much the rule has changed. Contrast with Italy: France allows intra-group transfers, whereas Italy requires 6 years if you return to the same group (places/italy-playbook.md).

**Pitfalls.** (1) Do not assume your *classe preparatoire* years are invisible: LSE counts them towards its three-year Grande Ecole test (other schools' treatment was not checked). (2) The French 20-point scale looks low to a German points formula. (3) Between CGE employment 76% (2026) and 90.5% (2023), the market for French graduates has deteriorated sharply; the data are for French-trained graduates, not those who studied abroad.

## 5. Germany

**Degree structure and recognition.** A German bachelor's is typically six semesters (180 ECTS); Mannheim itself requires "a standard period of study of at least 6 semesters or 3 years" and 36 ECTS of general business (Mannheim selection statute, 2026) [data]. LSE accepts bachelor's degrees from a Universitaet or Fachhochschule with 2.2 for a 2:1 and 1.4 for a First (LSE Germany page, 2026) [data]; the page prints "2.2/5" and "1.4/5", which is LSE's wording (the German scale runs 1.0 best to 4.0 pass, 5.0 fail).

**Grade direction and conversion.** Lower is better, so a German 1.4 is the First. At Mannheim a German applicant uses the native grade directly: 2.2 is 36 points and 1.4 is 52 points (author's calculation from the statute's linear rule of 60 points at 1.0 and 0 at 4.0). Germans applying out are not penalised by a conversion formula; it is the incoming students who are, which is why Spanish, Greek, Portuguese and French applicants to German schools are the section 2b population.

**Home alternatives and reputation.** Germany has the strongest home market of the six in the data: recent-graduate employment 92.6%, tertiary unemployment 4.5% at 25-29 and mean tertiary earnings EUR 75,732 (PPS 67,684) against the EU's EUR 50,948 (Eurostat, 2022 and 2025) [data]. Mannheim, TUM and the other German schools are in the calculator; I did not research German public versus private master's reputation, so H-c is not tested.

**Funding for nationals going abroad.** Auslands-BAfoeG is the German state scheme: a Regensburg university page says it covers "necessary tuition fees" up to EUR 5,600 per year, as a full grant, for a maximum of one year, while the living allowance is half grant and half interest-free loan (https://www.uni-regensburg.de/international/wege-ins-ausland/finanzierung-auslandsstudium/staatliche-foerderung) [data, secondary]. Another university summary (snippet only) says a complete master's within the EU or Switzerland can be funded for the standard period of study; I could not open the official bafoeg.de page (404), so the full-degree rule and the treatment of the UK (not EU) are unverified (Claims to verify, item 11). The scheme is means-tested on parental income.

**Emigration.** A news summary of Destatis data reports 269,986 departures of German nationals in 2024 against 189,107 arrivals (net minus 80,879), with Switzerland, Austria and Spain the main destinations; the figures are not graduate-specific and the page is secondary (unverified, see Claims to verify, item 12).

**Return regime.** I found no returner or expat regime in Germany; this is a "not found" rather than a verified absence.

**Pitfalls.** (1) Note inversion: when you convert, remember the German scale runs the other way. (2) Mannheim's statute truncates rather than rounds, so 1.69 becomes 1.6 (a tiny edge) and a foreign mark just under a threshold falls the wrong way. (3) Fachhochschule and university bachelor's are both accepted by LSE, but the German schools in the calculator (Mannheim, TUM) have their own selection rules.

## 6. Portugal

**Degree structure and recognition.** Portuguese qualifications are usually a three-year *licenciatura* plus a two-year *mestrado* (author background). LSE accepts "the licenciado/licenciatura, or the DESE awarded after four to five years of study" and the *mestrado*, "grades of 15/20, bom com distincao or good with distinction, or above"; one threshold, no separate First (LSE Portugal page, 2026; 39 Portuguese graduate students joined in 2025) [data]. At Mannheim, 15/20 converts to 2.5 (30 points) and 16/20 to 2.2 (36 points) (author's calculation).

**The national pitfall.** 15/20 is a demanding mark in Portuguese higher education (author background; Claims to verify, item 4). A Portuguese applicant should check where their average sits against the published threshold before choosing between a one-year UK master's and a two-year continental one, since LSE publishes one threshold only.

**Home alternatives and reputation.** Nova SBE and Catolica Lisbon are in the calculator and are, in my background knowledge, Portugal's leading business schools (not source-checked); ISEG is the main public alternative. I did not research their placement data or recruiter perception, so H-c is not tested for Portugal.

**Funding for nationals going abroad.** The same Fundacion la Caixa fellowship (see section 3) is open to Portuguese nationals with a first degree dated January 2017 to July 2026 (UMH call summary) [data, secondary host]. Nothing else was researched.

**Home market.** Portuguese tertiary 25-29-year-olds: 86.2% employment and 8.1% unemployment; recent graduates 88.0% employed; mean tertiary earnings EUR 35,273 in 2022, PPS 41,834 (Eurostat) [data]. In PPS terms, Germany's tertiary average is 1.62 times Portugal's (author's calculation). The unemployment rate at 25-29 is higher than the EU's 6.8% while recent-grad employment is above the EU average, a mixed picture.

**Emigration.** About 65,000 Portuguese emigrated in 2024 (down from 70,000 in 2023). The main destinations were Switzerland (12,388), Spain (11,332), France (8,088), Germany (7,410), Belgium (5,471), the Netherlands (4,795), Luxembourg (3,469) and the UK (2,766, down 37% after Brexit-era declines); the stock abroad is about 1.8 million, around 17% of the resident population (Observatorio da Emigracao, Emigracao Portuguesa 2025, published March 2026, https://observatorioemigracao.pt/np4/file/10517/OEm_EmigracaoPortuguesa2025.pdf, read in full for these pages) [data]. The report notes that flows to France and Switzerland are mostly low-skilled and flows to other destinations include more qualified professionals. For a business graduate this means the destinations that matter are Switzerland, Spain, the Netherlands and Germany, with the UK now a minor flow.

**Return regime.** IFICI (Incentivo Fiscal a Investigacao Cientifica e Inovacao), art. 58-A of the Estatuto dos Beneficios Fiscais, regulated by Portaria 352/2024/1 of 23 December 2024: 20% flat IRS on category A and B income from qualifying roles for 10 consecutive years, for people who become resident and were not resident in any of the previous five years; Portuguese nationals returning qualify if they meet the five-year test; registration is due by 15 January of the year after becoming resident (Portal das Financas FAQ, https://info.portaldasfinancas.gov.pt/pt/apoio_contribuinte/questoes_frequentes/Pages/faqs-01018.aspx) [data]. The qualifying roles listed on that page include academic and research posts, R&D at certified entities, start-up posts and "highly qualified" professions defined by a doctorate or a master's plus three years of experience. That last condition means a fresh master's graduate would not meet it, so entry-level eligibility must be checked case by case (I did not read the full list of qualifying roles; Claims to verify, item 13). IFICI is the successor to the older NHR regime (author background).

**Pitfalls.** (1) IFICI is a research-and-innovation incentive, not a generic returner scheme. (2) The five-year non-residence test excludes a typical "master's plus three years" return.

## 7. Greece

**Degree structure and grade conversion.** LSE takes a Greek *ptychio* from an AEI with 7.0/10 for a 2:1 and 8.0/10 for a First (LSE Greece page, 2026; 49 Greek graduate students joined in 2025) [data]. At Mannheim these convert to 2.8 (24 points) and 2.2 (36 points). Greek marks run from 5 (pass) to 10 (author background).

**Mismatch between schools.** A secondary summary of the 2026 Onassis Foundation scholarship call says it requires a bachelor's average of at least 8.00/10 "or equivalent, such as a 2:1 in the UK", which would equate Greek 8.0 with a 2:1, one whole class above LSE's own equivalence (secondary, https://www2.fundsforngos.org/education/call-for-applications-onassis-foundation-scholarships-greece/, search summary only; Claims to verify, item 14). If correct, a Greek applicant at 7.5 passes LSE's 2:1 test but misses the Onassis test, which is the sort of cross-institution mismatch hypothesis H-a describes.

**Home alternatives.** Not researched (no primary data on Greek public or private business masters).

**Funding for nationals going abroad.** The same summary reports up to 80 Onassis scholarships in the 2026 call (deadline 28 February 2026), a monthly grant of about EUR 1,000 in Europe, about EUR 980 in the UK and about EUR 1,110 elsewhere, and a one-off payment of EUR 5,000 (GBP 3,500 in the UK) toward fees and relocation, for master's study of up to 24 months; applicants had to be born in 1991 or later for master's programmes (secondary, unverified). The Onassis site returned no text when fetched.

**Home market.** The Greek numbers are the weakest in the data set: tertiary unemployment 14.8% at 25-29 and 9.3% at 25-39, recent-graduate employment 66.9% against 86.9% for the EU, and mean tertiary earnings of EUR 29,706 in 2022 (PPS 36,215) (Eurostat, 2025 and 2022) [data]. Germany's tertiary average is 1.87 times Greece's in PPS terms (author's calculation), the largest ratio among the six playbooks. By 30-34 Greek employment is 86.1%, 3.5 points below the EU.

**Emigration.** Secondary press summaries say about 600,000 mostly educated Greeks left between 2010 and 2021, with the UK, Germany and the UAE leading, and that roughly 350,000 had returned by 2024, with fewer than 10% of emigrants surveyed in London and the Netherlands planning to return within three years (reported through search snippets of TO BHMA, ProtoThema and similar; unverified, Claims to verify, item 15). The Bank of Greece study "The Escape of Human Capital" (Lazaretou) is the primary source I did not open.

**Return regime.** Art. 5C of the Greek Income Tax Code (Law 4172/2013, inserted from 1 January 2021): 50% reduction of income tax on Greek-source employment or business income for up to seven years, for people who transfer tax residence to Greece, have not been Greek tax resident in **the previous 5 of the last 6 years**, move from an EU/EEA state (or a state with a tax administrative-cooperation agreement), work for a Greek legal entity or the Greek permanent establishment of a foreign firm, and declare a stay of at least two years. The relief lasts 7 tax years, and the person is also exempt from the annual "objective expenditure" tax base for housing and cars for 7 years. Apply by the end of the year of starting work if work starts on or before 2 July, otherwise by the end of the following year (AADE, "Application for inclusion under the special tax regime (Article 5C)", read 2 Oct 2026, https://www.aade.gr/en/psifiakes-ypiresies/eisodima/ypoboli-aitisis-ypagogis-se-eidiko-tropo-forologisis-arthro-5g-toy-kfe ; AADE FAQ of 2 Oct 2025, https://www.aade.gr/sites/default/files/2025-10/FAQS_5G_KFE.pdf ; consolidated article text on Taxheaven, https://www.taxheaven.gr/law/4172/2013/arthro/5%CE%93) [data]. The article was added by Law 4758/2020 (art. 40) and applies from tax year 2021. **The "7 of the last 8 years" figure that some summaries give (including the Forvis Mazars page, which also quotes an old 31 July deadline) is the test in art. 5A, the separate EUR 100,000 flat-tax regime for investors**, not in 5C. Neither the statute nor the AADE FAQ contains a nationality condition, so a returning Greek citizen can qualify. AADE Decision A.1096/2026 extends the regime to people who move to Greece to serve the Greek State under public-sector payroll rules, covering applications since 28 July 2025 (AADE page above) [data]. I did not trace which later law changed the 7-of-8 wording of the 2020 enactment, and the Government Gazette copy was not opened.

**Pitfalls.** (1) The headline "50% tax reduction for returnees" is conditional on several years abroad and on employment in Greece. (2) Greek unemployment among recent graduates is the one case in this file where a push-out effect is visible in official data. (3) Cross-institution grade mismatches (LSE 7.0 versus Onassis 8.0).

## 8. Poland

**Degree structure and grade conversion.** LSE accepts a *licencjat* or *inzynier* with 4.5/5 (or 5.0/6) for a 2:1 and 5.0/5 (or 5.5/6) for a First (LSE Poland page, 2026; 20 Polish graduate students joined in 2025) [data]. At Mannheim a 4.5/5 converts to 1.7 (46 points) and a 4.0/5 to 2.5 (30 points) with Nmin = 3 (author's calculation). Poland is one of the countries that gains from the formula: because the pass mark is 3 on a 2-5 scale, a 4.5 sits high on the linear scale.

**Home market.** Polish tertiary 25-29-year-olds have the best employment data in the set: 91.1% employment, 2.8% unemployment, recent-graduate employment 92.4% (Eurostat, 2025) [data]. Mean tertiary earnings were EUR 22,401 in 2022 but PPS 36,454; Germany's tertiary average is 3.4 times Poland's in euros but 1.9 times in PPS (author's calculation). This is a smaller gap than the headline.

**Home alternatives.** The Warsaw School of Economics (SGH) is the leading economics school; a snippet from SGH's own news says it was the only Polish public university in the FT Masters in Finance 2025 ranking (unverified, snippet only). I did not retrieve ELA (the Polish graduate-earnings system) figures; the SGH document found covers relative earnings indicators by programme for the 2022 cohort, which I could not use for absolute pay.

**Funding for nationals going abroad.** Not researched.

**Emigration.** Not researched.

**Return regime.** *Ulga na powrot* (return relief), art. 21 ust. 1 pkt 152 of the personal income tax act (ustawa o PIT), in force since 1 January 2022: no personal income tax on employment, contract and business income up to PLN 85,528 a year for four consecutive tax years, for people who move their residence to Poland, after three calendar years without a place of residence in Poland (and, per the official page, an earlier period of at least five years' Polish residence); the limit is shared with four other reliefs (seniors, 4+ families, young people), and management contracts are excluded (Ministry of Finance, podatki.gov.pl, https://www.podatki.gov.pl/ulgi/ulga-na-powrot-pit/, read 1 October 2026) [data]. Poland is the only one of the six where the required time abroad is three years rather than five, so it is the only regime that a "master's plus about two years of work" path could meet. Whether the earlier five-year Polish residence is cumulative or an alternative is ambiguous in my reading of the page (Claims to verify, item 17).

**Pitfalls.** (1) The Polish 2-5 scale makes 4.5/5 look modest, but LSE's threshold is exactly 4.5. (2) Euro pay comparisons exaggerate the pull of Western Europe by about 1.8 times (author's calculation above).

## Hypotheses tested

**H-a (assigned). "A grade-conversion mismatch costs EU applicants more than a test score gap."**
Verdict: **partly supported**.
- Holds when: the school ranks applicants on a points formula that converts foreign grades mechanically. At Mannheim, a French applicant at LSE's own 2:1 line (13/20) scores 18 grade points, an Italian at LSE's 2:1 line (104/110) scores 52, a Pole at LSE's line (4.5/5) scores 46, a Spaniard at 7.0 scores 24 (author's calculation from the KMK formula and Mannheim's statute, section 2b). The GMAT component rises by only 30 points from 645 to 685, so the conversion gap between a French and an Italian applicant of equal standing (34 points) exceeds the whole upper GMAT band [data for the statute, author's calculation for the gap].
- Fails when: the school publishes country tables (LSE does for every origin in this file), because then the mismatch is small and mostly in the "between 2:1 and First" zone. I did not test HEC, ESSEC, Bocconi or Imperial conversion rules, and I could not verify the ZAB parameters Mannheim actually applies.
- Mismatches also occur between institutions: LSE's Greek 2:1 is 7.0/10, whereas a secondary summary of the Onassis call treats 8.0/10 as the 2:1 equivalent.

**H-b (assigned). "For Spanish, Greek and Portuguese graduates going abroad pays more than staying, but return incentives narrow the gap."**
Verdict: **partly supported (pay gap yes; narrowing only for long stays)**.
- Pay: mean tertiary earnings (PPS, 2022) in Germany are 1.55 times Spain's, 1.62 times Portugal's and 1.87 times Greece's (Eurostat earn_ses22_30; author's calculation) [data]. These are stock averages of all tertiary employees in the destination, not starting salaries of foreign business graduates, and Germany is only one of several destinations (for Portuguese emigrants, Switzerland, Spain, France and Germany lead, OEm 2025) [data].
- Return incentives: Spain's flat 24% (art. 93 LIRPF), Portugal's 20% IFICI and Greece's 50% reduction all require roughly five years abroad before you come home, and Portugal's list of qualifying roles appears to exclude a fresh master's graduate (section 6). A master's year plus two or three years of work does not qualify, so the incentives narrow the gap only for people who stay abroad for most of a decade.
- Fails when: the destination is the same country as the home labour market's neighbour (Portuguese moving to Spain, 11,332 in 2024) where the pay gap is small (Spain 43,761 against Portugal 41,834 PPS).

**H-c (assigned). "Public master's at home match private foreign programmes for domestic employers."**
Verdict: **insufficient evidence**. I found no primary data (employer statements or placement reports) comparing home public masters with foreign private ones for any of the six origins. The only related datapoints are national-level: Polish and German graduates have strong home labour markets (Eurostat), which says nothing about school brands.

**H-1 (own). "Euro pay comparisons overstate the pull of the West for Central and Eastern European graduates by about half."**
Verdict: **supported**. Germany's tertiary mean earnings are 3.38 times Poland's in euros but 1.86 times in PPS, and 3.02 times Romania's in euros but 1.51 times in PPS (Eurostat earn_ses22_30; author's calculation) [data]. Fails when: the graduate plans to consume or save in the origin country (for example, send money home or buy property there), since PPS then understates the gain. Data are for 2022 and all tertiary graduates.

**H-2 (own). "The Spanish graduate-unemployment problem is an early-career problem, not a permanent one."**
Verdict: **supported on the 2025 data**. Tertiary 25-29 unemployment is 10.0% (EU 6.8%), but employment at 30-34 is 88.0% against 89.6% for the EU (Eurostat, 2025) [data]. This implies the Spanish "push" is strongest for the exact audience of pre-experience master's applicants and fades by the early 30s. Greece differs: its employment gap persists at 30-34 (86.1%).

**H-3 (own). "Return tax regimes are designed for people who spend five or more years abroad, so they do not change a study-then-return plan."**
Verdict: **supported for Spain, France, Portugal; partly for Poland; unresolved for Greece**. Spain requires five non-resident tax periods, France five full calendar years, Portugal five years; Poland three calendar years plus an earlier-residence condition; Greece requires 5 of the previous 6 years non-resident (AADE FAQ, 2 Oct 2025; section 7) [data]. Fails when: the person was abroad for school and work for five or more years (for example a bachelor's abroad plus a master's plus work), which is typical of Erasmus-plus-master's students who do not go home between degrees.

**H-4 (own). "Intra-group transfers qualify in France but not (without a long wait) in Italy, so the return route for a consultant differs by origin."**
Verdict: **partly supported**. French sources say intra-group transfers qualify (Berton Associes, 2026) while Italy requires six years if you return to the same group (places/italy-playbook.md). I could not verify the French position on the primary text, and I did not check the Spanish regulation on intra-group transfers.

## Common mistakes and myths

1. **"My home 2:1 equivalent is the same as an Italian's."** The same standing converts differently: 15/20 in Portugal or France is 2.5 in German grades and 30 Mannheim points; 104/110 in Italy is 52 [author's calculation].
2. **"A one-year master's abroad plus a couple of years' work earns me the returner tax break."** The five-year (three in Poland) non-residence tests make that false for Spain, France and Portugal [data].
3. **"Greece's 50% tax reduction is simple."** It needs 5 of the previous 6 years as a non-resident, a move from an EU/EEA or tax-cooperation state, a Greek employer and a signed two-year stay declaration; some summaries wrongly quote 7 of 8 years, which is the investor regime (AADE FAQ, 2 Oct 2025) [data].
4. **"IFICI is Portugal's generic returner regime."** It targets research, innovation and "highly qualified" roles; entry-level eligibility is doubtful [data].
5. **"Spanish emigration figures show a pure brain drain."** 49,476 of the 137,719 Spanish emigrations in 2024 were of people born abroad [data].
6. **"The Mannheim conversion is the same as a transcript GPA conversion."** The KMK resolution is written for entrance certificates and the conversion parameters are the ZAB's, not yours; the university converts [data].
7. **"The German scale is higher-is-better."** It is lower-is-better, and Mannheim truncates rather than rounds [data].

## Decision rules

1. **If you are French or Dutch and applying to a points-based German school (Mannheim) with a 13/20 or a 7.0, prefer a test score of 685 or higher or a second target school**, because the formula puts you at about 18-20 grade points against 46-52 for Polish and Italian applicants of equal standing (Mannheim statute, author's calculation).
2. **If you are Spanish with an average between 7.0 and 9.0, prefer schools that publish a Spanish table or give conditional offers, and weight your GMAT**, because LSE leaves the middle band without a rule.
3. **If you are Portuguese, check whether your average is at or above 15/20 before choosing LSE**, since LSE publishes a single threshold at that level.
4. **If you are Greek, check each scheme's own equivalence rather than assuming 8.0 = First**, because LSE treats 7.0 as a 2:1 and a secondary summary of the Onassis call treats 8.0 as the 2:1.
5. **If you are Spanish or Portuguese and applying for the la Caixa fellowship, plan to apply in January of the year you enrol**, with a first degree dated no earlier than the call's cut-off (January 2017 in the 2026 call) and an English-language application.
6. **If you plan to return to Spain, France or Portugal and want the tax regime, prefer paths that give you five full years abroad before you come home**, and avoid assuming the one-year master's route qualifies.
7. **If you plan to return to Poland within three years, check the ulga na powrot early**, because Poland's three-year test is the only one in this file reachable after a master's and two years' work.
8. **If you are Portuguese and considering IFICI, ask your future employer whether the post is on the qualifying list**, because "master's plus three years" is the stated profile for "highly qualified" roles.
9. **If you are Greek or Spanish and young (25-29), treat the home job market as a real push factor**, because tertiary unemployment is 14.8% and 10.0% in those groups against 6.8% for the EU [data].
10. **If you are German, Dutch or Irish, do not choose abroad for pay reasons**, because the mean tertiary earnings at home (EUR 75,732, 68,139 and 66,790 in 2022) are among the highest in the set.
11. **If you are Polish or Romanian, compare pay in PPS rather than euros**, because the apparent gap to Germany halves.
12. **If you are French and applying to LSE, use the prepa years in your favour**, because LSE counts the two prepa years towards its three Grande Ecole years.
