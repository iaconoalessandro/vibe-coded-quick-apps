# Italy playbook: business master's decisions for Italian students

> **Not legal, tax or immigration advice.** This file summarises official sources as of the dates given. Rules change and individual cases differ: confirm with the competent authority or a qualified adviser before acting.

## Bottom line

1. **Abroad pays far more, but few go and fewer come back.** Italian second-cycle graduates working abroad earn about EUR 2,900 net a month five years after graduating, against about EUR 1,800 for those in Italy (+61.7%). Only 4.6% of employed Italian-citizen magistrale graduates work abroad at five years, and 71.9% of them say returning within five years is unlikely or very unlikely (AlmaLaurea, Rapporto 2025, survey 2024, https://www.almalaurea.it/sites/default/files/comunicati/2025/cs_rapporto-almalaurea-2025.pdf) [data]. The gap is in nominal euros and not adjusted for London, Zurich or Paris living costs.
2. **An Italian public economics magistrale is a safe but low-paying baseline.** Economics LM graduates (AlmaLaurea survey 2025): 86.3% employed at 1 year on EUR 1,581 net a month, and 96.3% employed at 5 years on EUR 2,006 (AlmaLaurea per-course data, https://www2.almalaurea.it/cgi-php/universita/statistiche/tendine.php?anno=2025&config=occupazione) [data]. Finance (LM-16) pays best at both points (EUR 1,756 and EUR 2,126).
3. **The best Italian business schools are invisible in AlmaLaurea.** Bocconi, Politecnico di Milano, LUISS and Cattolica are not on AlmaLaurea's list of 78 member universities for the 2025 survey, so national averages describe the public mass market, not the Milan elite (same source, list of atenei) [data]. For Bocconi you must use its own placement reports (see getting-in/employer-pipelines.md).
4. **Among public universities, international exposure predicts pay more than city.** Bologna, Pavia, Padova and Trento economics LM graduates have 10–16% of their five-year employed working abroad and earn EUR 2,150–2,280 net. Federico II, Sapienza and Tor Vergata are at 4–8% abroad and EUR 1,965–2,025 (AlmaLaurea 2025 survey, per university) [data].
5. **"Abroad first, return later" works for tax only if you plan the timeline.** The impatriati regime (art. 5 D.Lgs. 209/2023 until 31 December 2026; recodified word for word as art. 225 of the Testo unico imposte sui redditi, D.Lgs. 117/2026, which applies from 1 January 2027) taxes only 50% of employment income (40% with a minor child, i.e. 60% exempt; see `visas_immigration/italy/italy_visas_immigration_guide.md` §Caso 9) for 5 tax years, up to EUR 600,000 a year. It needs 3 prior tax years of non-residence, rising to **6 years if you return to the same employer or group** (7 if you had worked for it in Italy before leaving). You must stay 4 years, or the benefit is clawed back with interest (https://www.normattiva.it/uri-res/N2Ls?urn:nir:stato:decreto.legislativo:2026-06-19;117~art225) [data].
6. **The intra-group transfer trap is the non-obvious killer.** The usual return route for consultants, Big 4 staff and bankers (London office to Milan office) does not qualify until year 6. To use the regime after about 3 years abroad, you have to change employer when you come home (Agenzia delle Entrate, Risposta a interpello 263/2025, summarised at https://www.fiscoetasse.com/new-rassegna-stampa/2895-impatriati-periodo-residenza.html) [data].
7. **A "Master universitario di I/II livello" is not a degree.** Under DM 270/2004 art. 3, universities award only the laurea, the laurea magistrale, the diploma di specializzazione and the dottorato as titoli. Masters di I/II livello are "corsi di perfezionamento scientifico e di alta formazione" (art. 3 c. 9, https://www.normattiva.it/uri-res/N2Ls?urn:nir:ministero.universita.e.ricerca.scientifica.e.tecnologica:decreto:2004-10-22;270~art3) [data]. Never take one instead of a laurea magistrale or a foreign MSc if you want a Bologna second-cycle qualification.
8. **Emigration of young graduates is real but slowing.** Italy had a net loss of 78,000 graduates aged 25–34 in 2019–24. Expatriations of Italians fell from a 2024 peak of 141,000 to 109,000 in 2025. The UK and Germany together took just under a third of young graduate leavers in 2019–25 (ISTAT, 3 Aug 2026, https://www.istat.it/comunicato-stampa/migrazioni-interne-e-internazionali-della-popolazione-residente-anni-2024-2025/) [data].

## 1. Degree types: laurea magistrale vs Master universitario vs business-school "Master" vs foreign MSc

| Credential | Legal status in Italy | Bologna level | Typical use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Laurea magistrale (LM-77 Scienze economico-aziendali, LM-56 Scienze dell'economia, LM-16 Finanza), 120 ECTS | A *titolo di studio* under DM 270/2004 art. 3 c. 1 [data] | Second cycle (EQF 7) | Default route. Gives access to dottorato, concorsi pubblici and abilitazioni such as dottore commercialista |
| Master universitario di I livello (after a triennale) / II livello (after an LM), usually 60 ECTS | "Corso di perfezionamento scientifico e di alta formazione permanente e ricorrente" (DM 270/2004 art. 3 c. 9) [data]. Not in the list of titoli | A I livello master is **not** a second-cycle degree. It sits on top of a first-cycle degree | Specialisation, career change, employer-sponsored training |
| Business-school "Master" (SDA Bocconi, 24ORE Business School, MIP, LUISS Business School, etc.) | Either a university Master di I/II livello issued through the parent university, or a private certificate with no legal value | Depends: check the diploma wording | Specialist (luxury, fashion, finance), often with an internship |
| Bocconi / Polimi / LUISS / Cattolica MSc | These are lauree magistrali, so legally the same as a public LM | Second cycle | Elite route; the brand rather than the legal status is what differs |
| Foreign MSc/MiM (HEC, LSE, ESCP, etc.) | Foreign qualification. Usable in the private sector as is. For public competitions it needs equivalence (see decisions/school-types-and-accreditation.md on the Funzione Pubblica procedure) | Second cycle if a national master's degree (e.g. French *grade de master*, UK MSc) | International careers and Milan multinationals |

**Who actually does Masters universitari?** Among economics LM graduates surveyed five years after graduating (survey 2024), 5.9% had done a Master di I livello, 6.7% a Master di II livello and 8.5% "another type of master". By comparison 33.3% had done a company stage (AlmaLaurea per-course data, survey 2024, gruppo Economico, LM biennale, 5 years) [data]. So a Master universitario is a minority add-on after an LM, not a substitute for one.

**Non-obvious:** the *same* school brand can sell both kinds of product. Bocconi University awards lauree magistrali, while SDA Bocconi School of Management runs masters that are not lauree magistrali. 24ORE Business School and many "Master in Luxury/Fashion Management" programmes are short, expensive and often pre-experience, but they are not second-cycle degrees. Check three things on any brochure: (a) does the diploma say "Laurea magistrale classe LM-xx"? (b) how many ECTS? (c) does admission require a triennale (I livello) or an LM (II livello)? The legal status is verified above. How employers perceive these products could not be verified from primary sources (see Claims to verify, item 1).

## 2. Italian options vs going abroad: what the data covers

**Coverage gap.** AlmaLaurea's 2025 occupational survey lists 78 member universities. They include Bologna, Padova, Torino, Politecnico di Torino, Ca' Foscari, Tor Vergata, Sapienza, Federico II, Milano Statale, Bicocca, Trento, Pavia and LIUC. Bocconi, Politecnico di Milano, LUISS and Università Cattolica are **not** members (AlmaLaurea ateneo selector, 2025 survey) [data]. Any "Italian graduates earn X" headline therefore leaves out most graduates of the schools an ambitious business student shortlists. For Bocconi programme-level placement (for example, 63.9% of MSc Finance graduates working abroad vs 38.4% for Marketing Management), see getting-in/employer-pipelines.md. For FT ranks and salaries (Bocconi USD 109k vs Polimi USD 74k weighted salary; LUISS level with Polimi), see decisions/school-types-and-accreditation.md.

## 3. Italian graduate pay vs abroad

**National AlmaLaurea headline (Rapporto 2025, survey 2024, all second-cycle graduates with Italian citizenship)** (https://www.almalaurea.it/sites/default/files/comunicati/2025/cs_rapporto-almalaurea-2025.pdf) [data]:

| Metric | 1 year after the LM | 5 years after the LM |
|---|---|---|
| Net monthly pay, all second-cycle graduates | EUR 1,488 | EUR 1,847 |
| Net pay, working in Italy | under EUR 1,500 | almost EUR 1,800 |
| Net pay, working abroad | over EUR 2,200 (+54.2%) | almost EUR 2,900 (+61.7%) |
| Share of employed working abroad | 4.1% | 4.6% |
| Employment rate (all second-cycle) | 78.6% | 89.7% |

Other figures from the same release [data]:
- Who goes abroad: more men than women (5.6% vs 3.8% at five years) and more of the "most brilliant" graduates by exam grades and regularity of studies.
- Why they left (five-year cohort abroad): 32.0% received an attractive offer from a foreign company; 31.1% lacked adequate opportunities in Italy.
- Return intentions: 38.2% say returning within five years is very unlikely, 33.7% unlikely, and 15.3% very likely.
- More than 30% of employed graduates say their pay is inadequate for their role (35.0% of second-level graduates at one year).

**Update from the Rapporto 2026 (survey 2025, presented 11 June 2026).** For all second-level graduates, employment is 80.8% at 1 year and 94.4% at 5 years. Net pay is EUR 1,495 at 1 year (down 0.9% in real terms) and EUR 1,903 at 5 years. Graduates working in the North earn on average only EUR 68 net a month more than those working in the South. 66.9% of graduating students would not accept less than EUR 1,500 net for a full-time job, against 24.4% in 2016 (AlmaLaurea press release, 11 June 2026, https://www.almalaurea.it/sites/default/files/comunicati/2026/cs-rapporto-almalaurea-2026.pdf) [data]. The 2026 release does not repeat the Italy-vs-abroad pay split. That split dates from the 2025 Rapporto. **Non-obvious:** the North–South *pay* gap inside Italy (about EUR 68 a month) is tiny compared with the Italy–abroad gap (about EUR 1,100 a month at 5 years). Moving to Milan mainly improves your chances of getting hired (+34.8% probability for northern residents) and your career path, not your starting net pay.

**Economics graduates specifically.** Within economics, 5.7% of five-year employed work abroad. The share is higher for LM-56 (10.1%) and LM-16 (9.5%) than for LM-77 (4.2%) (AlmaLaurea 2025 survey) [data]. AlmaLaurea publishes the Italy-vs-abroad pay split only nationally in its press release. The per-course tool shows where graduates work but not pay by location, so an economics-only abroad premium could not be extracted (see Claims to verify, item 3).

**Why the premium overstates real gains.** The comparison is in nominal net euros. A London or Zurich net salary buys much less housing than the same figure in Turin or Padua. The people abroad are also positively selected (higher grades, more often men, more often from ICT and science). Treat +60% as an upper bound on the causal premium of leaving. For Milan vs London net pay after rent, see money/salaries-and-roi.md.

## 4. Brain drain: ISTAT 2024–2025

From ISTAT's *Migrazioni interne e internazionali della popolazione residente, anni 2024/2025* (published 3 Aug 2026, https://www.istat.it/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Statistica-report_Migrazioni-interne-e-internazionali-della-popolazione-residente_ANNI-2024-2025.pdf) [data]:

- **Expatriations of Italians:** 141,000 in 2024 (a peak) and 109,000 in 2025 (−22.7%, provisional). The net migration balance of Italian citizens improved from −83,000 to −53,000. Returns (rimpatri) fell slightly to 56,000.
- **Why 2024 spiked:** ISTAT links the peak to stricter AIRE rules. Law 213/2023 introduced a fine of EUR 200–1,000 per year (up to 5 years) for Italians abroad who fail to register in AIRE. Part of the 2024 "exodus" is therefore people regularising earlier moves.
- **Young graduates:** in 2019–24, Italy recorded a net loss of 78,000 graduates aged 25–34 (at least a triennale). Inflows of foreign graduates (+88,000) more than offset this. Among all Italians aged 25–34: 247,000 left and 86,000 returned.
- **Destinations of young graduates (2019–25):** the UK and Germany together took just under a third (over 38,000). France and Switzerland took about 12,000 each, and the United States about 7,000.
- **2025 shift:** for all expatriations, Spain became the first destination (12.2%). Flows to the UK fell 38.5% (18,000 to 11,000) and to Germany 36.8% (20,000 to 13,000).
- **South:** in 2019–25 the Mezzogiorno lost 150,000 graduates aged 25–34: 122,000 to the Centre-North and 28,000 abroad. For a southern student, the realistic "brain drain" is to Milan first.
- **Returns by origin (2025):** Germany is the first source of returning Italians (about 9,000), then the UK (just under 7,000) and Switzerland (about 5,000).

**Implication:** the post-Brexit UK has fallen as a destination for Italians overall. Still, the UK plus Germany remains the largest bloc for young graduates. For visa consequences for EU citizens in the UK and Switzerland, see places/visas-and-work-rights.md. Country-level comparisons are in places/countries-and-cities.md.

## 5. Returning: the impatriati regime (2024 rules, recodified in 2026)

**Legal basis.** Article 5 of D.Lgs. 209/2023 created the regime for people moving tax residence to Italy from tax year 2024. It is re-enacted word for word as **art. 225 of the Testo unico delle disposizioni legislative in materia di imposte sui redditi (D.Lgs. 19 June 2026, n. 117, GU n. 152, 3 July 2026, S.O. n. 26)**. The decree entered into force on 4 July 2026, but art. 377 of the Testo unico says its provisions "si applicano a decorrere dal 1° gennaio 2027", and art. 376(1)(eeeeee) repeals art. 5 D.Lgs. 209/2023 from that date. So **art. 5 remains the operative text until 31 December 2026 and art. 225 applies from 1 January 2027**; the conditions are identical in the two (compared on Normattiva, 2 Oct 2026) (https://www.normattiva.it/uri-res/N2Ls?urn:nir:stato:decreto.legislativo:2026-06-19;117~art225) [data]. Many 2024–25 guides, and the Agenzia delle Entrate's own 2026 rulings, still cite art. 5. The substance is the same.

**The rules (art. 225, read in full):**

| Element | Rule |
|---|---|
| Income covered | Employment income, income treated as employment income, and self-employed professional income produced in Italy. Business income is excluded |
| Relief | Only **50%** of that income is taxable, up to **EUR 600,000 a year** |
| With children | Only **40%** is taxable (i.e. 60% exempt) if you move with a minor child, or a child is born or adopted during the regime. The child must be resident in Italy |
| Duration | The year of the move plus the next 4 tax years (5 in total). No extension |
| Prior non-residence | Not tax-resident in Italy in the **3** tax years before the move |
| Same employer or group | **6** tax years abroad if you return to work for the same employer or group you worked for abroad. **7** years if you had also worked for it in Italy before leaving. "Group" means control under art. 2359(1)(1) of the civil code |
| Commitment | You must remain tax-resident in Italy for at least **4 years**. If you don't, you lose the benefit and what you saved is **recovered with interest** |
| Qualification | "Elevata qualificazione o specializzazione" under D.Lgs. 108/2012 and 206/2007, or applied AI research. In practice: a higher-education degree of at least 3 years plus a job in ISTAT occupational levels 1–3 (managers, professionals, technicians) (Dottrina Lavoro e-book 2024, citing Agenzia Entrate circ. 17/2017, https://www.dottrinalavoro.it/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/Ebook-IMPATRIATI-2024-rev.-11.04.pdf) [practitioner consensus] |
| Work location | You must work in Italy for most of the tax year |
| Proof of non-residence | For tax years before 29 Dec 2023, AIRE registration or treaty residence abroad counts for Italian citizens (art. 225 c. 6) |
| EU cap | Applies within the EU de minimis state-aid rules (Reg. 2023/2831) (art. 225 c. 7). The practical effect on individual employees is unclear (Claims to verify, item 6) |

These rules are more restrictive than the pre-2024 regime: 50% taxable instead of 30%, 3 years abroad instead of 2, a 4-year stay commitment instead of 2, a new qualification test and a new EUR 600k cap (Dottrina Lavoro e-book 2024 comparison table) [practitioner consensus].

**Agenzia delle Entrate on the same-employer rule (Risposta 263/2025).** A taxpayer moved abroad in January 2023 and planned to return in 2026 to a *new, unrelated* Italian employer. Meanwhile he kept a co.co.co. teaching contract with an Italian university that he had held since before he left. The Agency allowed the regime on the new job's income. It refused it on the university income, because that relationship was with an employer he had worked for in Italy before leaving, which triggers the 7-year rule (Risposta n. 263/2025 read on the Agenzia delle Entrate site, https://www.agenziaentrate.gov.it/portale/documents/20143/9354001/Risposta+n.+263_2025/3b85b278-713f-22f9-1a7a-4a429877bfa2; also FiscoeTasse, 14 Oct 2025, https://www.fiscoetasse.com/new-rassegna-stampa/2895-impatriati-periodo-residenza.html) [data]. The Agency's own words extend the longer test to any contract type: the period "è aumentato a sei o sette anni in tutte le ipotesi in cui il contribuente (lavoratore dipendente, assimilato o lavoratore autonomo) al rientro in Italia presti l'attività lavorativa per il medesimo soggetto (datore/gruppo)", so freelance work for a former employer counts too. **Lesson:** the 3-year route applies only to genuinely new employers. Ongoing side contracts with Italian entities you worked for before leaving stay outside the regime.

**What this does to the "MSc abroad, work 3 years, come back" plan:**
- **Counting the years.** The test is about *tax years* (calendar years) of non-residence. Suppose you finish a triennale in July 2027, start an MSc abroad in September 2027 and register in AIRE. 2027 is probably still an Italian residence year, because you were in Italy for most of it. Non-resident years would then be 2028, 2029 and 2030, and the earliest qualifying return is in 2031. Your **master's year abroad can count** if it falls in a full non-resident tax year, but the year you leave usually does not. (This is the author's reading of the rule. Edge cases depend on the art. 2 TUIR residence test, so confirm with a commercialista.)
- **Group transfers kill it.** Joining McKinsey, BCG, Bain, a Big 4 firm or an investment bank in London or Paris and then transferring to its Milan office means a 6-year wait. If you had interned with the Italian entity before leaving, it may be 7. Whether a pre-departure *internship* counts as being "impiegato" is not settled (Claims to verify, item 7). The practical routes are: switch employer when you return, or stay abroad for 6+ years.
- **Size of the prize (illustration only, not verified tax advice).** Take a EUR 60,000 gross Milan salary for a returner with about 4 years' experience. Halving taxable income cuts IRPEF by roughly EUR 9–11k a year on 2025 IRPEF brackets (23/35/43%) before regional and municipal surcharges. Over 5 years that is about EUR 45–55k. Social contributions are not reduced. This is the author's calculation, and the brackets were not re-verified this session (Claims to verify, item 8).
- **Who it helps most:** high earners returning to a new employer, especially with children (40% taxable). It does not change the 1–3 year decision of whether to go abroad at all. It improves the economics of coming back after year 3–5 for people who would otherwise stay away.
- **Who it does not help:** people who want to return within 2 years, people who stay with the same group, and people who might leave Italy again within 4 years. In that last case clawback with interest applies.

**Impatriati and the flat tax do not combine.** The Revenue Agency has said that the regime for returning workers is incompatible with opening a VAT position under the flat-rate regime for the self-employed (regime forfettario, 5% or 15%). Choosing the forfettario at the time of return does not allow the impatriati benefit in later years, and the position carries over to art. 5 of D.Lgs. 209/2023 (Revenue Agency positions as summarised by Arletti Partners, Fiscal Focus and RSM, 2025) [practitioner consensus, secondary]. The impatriati regime applies to employment or self-employment income as the law defines; a freelancer who wants both has to choose, so get a tax adviser's opinion before opening a VAT number.

**Thirteenth and fourteenth months.** Many Italian national contracts pay 13 or 14 monthly instalments a year, with the extra months paid in December and, where it exists, in the summer. A "monthly net" figure from a survey is only comparable with another country's 12-month figure if you know whether the extra months are included. AlmaLaurea's published methodology note on this point was not found, so the AlmaLaurea net figures in section 3 are not adjusted either way: multiplying them by 12 may understate annual income if respondents quoted a regular month, and multiplying by 13 may overstate it if they averaged the year. When you compare an Italian offer, convert the gross annual figure on the contract (RAL) with the same tax calculation used abroad, not a monthly figure [author reasoning; AlmaLaurea definition to verify].

## 7. Grade conversion for foreign admissions

These are structural facts about the Italian system:
- Final degree marks are out of **110** (110 e lode is the top). Single exams are graded out of **30** (18 to pass, 30 e lode the top).
- A triennale is **180 ECTS**. That satisfies the Bologna first-cycle requirement, but some UK one-year MScs and many US programmes judge it against 3- or 4-year degrees with different grade cultures.
- **"Fuori corso"** means taking longer than the nominal duration. AlmaLaurea's average graduation ages are 24.5 for the triennale and 26.3 for economics LM, and 61.3% graduate on time (see decisions/timing-and-sequencing.md). Among economics LM graduates interviewed at five years (survey 2024), the average final mark was 106.8/110, with a mean study duration of 2.6 years for a 2-year programme and a delay index of 0.31 (AlmaLaurea per-course data) [data]. **Implication:** a 106–107 LM mark is *average* in Italy, not distinctive. Admissions committees that know Italy read the transcript average out of 30 more than the 110 mark.
- UK class-equivalence tables for Italian marks (for example, a 2:1 equivalent) are set by each school and vary. See getting-in/admissions.md. School-specific cut-offs could not be verified here (Claims to verify, item 10).

A foreign MSc generally needs private, bank or foundation funding. This raises the effective cost gap between, say, Bologna (ISEE-linked fees) and an MSc in London or Paris.

## 9. Internships (stage / tirocini extracurricolari)

Not re-verified in this session (Claims to verify, item 12). The structural facts are these. Extracurricular internships are regulated by the regions under the 2017 State–Regions guidelines, and each region sets a minimum monthly *indennità*. Curricular internships done while enrolled have no legal minimum. Apprendistato di alta formazione e ricerca lets an employer hire a student who is completing a degree, under a training contract. In AlmaLaurea, 33.3% of economics LM graduates at five years had done a company stage after graduating, and 13.7% a tirocinio or praticantato (survey 2024) [data]. That is a reminder that a post-LM stage is a normal Italian entry step, not a failure.

## Hypotheses tested

**H1. "Italian magistrale graduates working abroad earn about 50% or more net than those in Italy at 5 years."**
Verdict: **supported** in nominal terms. +61.7% at five years (about EUR 2,900 vs EUR 1,800) and +54.2% at one year (AlmaLaurea Rapporto 2025) [data]. It holds for all second-cycle graduates with Italian citizenship. It fails as a *causal* or *real* claim: there is no cost-of-living adjustment, those abroad are positively selected (higher grades, more often men and STEM), and no economics-specific split is published.

**H2. "Among Italian public universities, the choice of university matters less for pay than the choice of country."**
Verdict: **supported.** The five-year pay range across the 11 public universities sampled is EUR 1,965–2,283, a spread of about 16%. The abroad premium is about 60% (AlmaLaurea 2025 survey) [data]. It holds for public economics LMs. It may fail for Bocconi and Polimi, which are outside AlmaLaurea and whose graduates have much higher abroad shares (see getting-in/employer-pipelines.md).

**H3. "Finance (LM-16) graduates out-earn management (LM-77) graduates in Italy."**
Verdict: **partly supported.** LM-16 earns EUR 1,756 vs EUR 1,576 at one year (+11%), and EUR 2,126 vs EUR 2,011 at five years (+6%). Its five-year employment is 97.7% vs 96.9%. LM-16 graduates are also more than twice as likely to work abroad (9.5% vs 4.2%) (AlmaLaurea 2025 survey) [data]. The premium is small and partly reflects the abroad share and selection. LM-16 classes are much smaller (442 vs 5,857 interviewed).

**H4. "The impatriati regime makes 'abroad first, return later' financially rational for many."**
Verdict: **partly supported.** It holds for people who (a) stay at least 3 full tax years abroad, (b) come back to an *unrelated* employer, (c) earn well enough for a 50% exemption to matter (tens of thousands of euros over 5 years), and (d) are sure to stay 4 years (art. 225; Risposta 263/2025) [data]. It fails for the common consulting, Big 4 and banking path of transferring within the same firm (6–7 years needed). It also fails for anyone unsure about staying, given clawback with interest. Behaviourally, 71.9% of graduates abroad think returning within 5 years is unlikely (AlmaLaurea 2025) [data], so the regime affects a minority's decision.

**H5. "A Master universitario di I livello is legally weaker than a laurea magistrale."**
Verdict: **supported** on legal status: it is not a titolo of DM 270/2004 art. 3 c. 1–2 but a corso di perfezionamento/alta formazione under art. 3 c. 9 [data]. **Insufficient evidence** on employer perception: no primary source was read on how Italian employers screen it.

**H6 (own). "National averages describe the elite route."**
Verdict: **not supported.** Bocconi, Polimi, LUISS and Cattolica are not AlmaLaurea members [data]. National economics averages describe public universities only.

**H7 (own). "The 2024 emigration surge means the brain drain is accelerating."**
Verdict: **not supported.** ISTAT attributes the 2024 peak partly to stricter AIRE enforcement. Expatriations fell 22.7% in 2025, and young-adult departures fell 29.2% (ISTAT, Aug 2026) [data].

**Assigned but not testable this session:** "A Bocconi or Polimi master's beats a mid-ranked UK MSc for a career in Milan" and "Polimi Management Engineering matches Bocconi in consulting placement at a fraction of the cost" have **insufficient evidence** here. The FT salary gap (Bocconi USD 109k vs Polimi USD 74k, in decisions/school-types-and-accreditation.md) argues *against* parity on pay. Consulting-specific placement shares were not verified.

## Common mistakes and myths

1. **"AlmaLaurea says economics graduates earn EUR 1,600, so a Bocconi MSc is overpriced."** AlmaLaurea excludes Bocconi, Polimi, LUISS and Cattolica [data]. Compare like with like.
2. **"A Master di I livello is a master's degree."** In Italian law it is a postgraduate *corso di perfezionamento*, and the I livello is open to triennale holders [data]. Abroad it will usually not count as a second-cycle degree.
3. **"I'll go to London with McKinsey and transfer back to Milan after 3 years with the impatriati discount."** Same-group returns need 6–7 years abroad [data].
4. **"The impatriati regime gives 70% or 90% off."** Those were the pre-2024 rules (30% taxable, or 10% in the South). Since 2024 it is 50% taxable (40% with a child), with a EUR 600k cap and a 4-year stay [data].
5. **"I can skip AIRE registration while abroad."** That brings fines of EUR 200–1,000 per year since Law 213/2023 (ISTAT note) [data], and it weakens your evidence of non-residence for the impatriati test.
6. **"110 out of 110 makes me stand out abroad."** The average economics LM mark is 106.8/110 [data]. Grade inflation at the top of the 110 scale is well known, so the 30-point exam average and class rank matter more. Specific committee behaviour is unverified (Claims to verify, item 10).
7. **"Everyone talented leaves."** Only 5.7% of economics LM graduates work abroad at five years (survey 2025) [data]. Leaving is a minority, self-selected path.

## Decision rules

1. **If** you want a legal second-cycle degree (for a dottorato, concorsi or the commercialista track), **prefer** a laurea magistrale (LM-77/56/16) or a foreign national master's degree **over** any Master universitario di I livello, **because** the latter is a corso di perfezionamento, not a titolo (DM 270/2004 art. 3).
2. **If** you compare Italian public options on outcomes, **use** AlmaLaurea per-class and per-ateneo data for the same survey year, and **weight pay differences under about EUR 200 a month as noise**, **because** the five-year spread across major public universities is only about 16%.
3. **If** you compare against Bocconi, Polimi, LUISS or Cattolica, **use** their own employment reports and FT data (not AlmaLaurea), **because** these schools are not in AlmaLaurea.
4. **If** you prioritise pay over location and your profile is strong (high exam average, quantitative), **plan** for 3–5 years abroad, **because** graduates abroad earn about 60% more net in nominal terms. **Then** discount for housing costs before you decide (money/salaries-and-roi.md).
5. **If** you plan to return to Italy and use the impatriati regime, **then** (a) register in AIRE, (b) count *full calendar tax years* of non-residence (at least 3), and (c) **prefer** returning to a different, unrelated employer, **because** same-group returns need 6–7 years.
6. **If** you are not sure you will stay in Italy 4 years after returning, **do not** budget the impatriati saving, **because** leaving early triggers recovery with interest.
7. **If** you have or expect children around the return date, **raise** the expected benefit (40% taxable instead of 50%), **because** art. 225 c. 4–5 gives the larger relief while the minor child is resident in Italy.
8. **If** your target is finance and you stay in a public university, **prefer** LM-16 over LM-77, **because** it shows higher pay (+6–11%), higher employment and twice the abroad share. **But** if the LM-16 at your university is small, check the per-course numbers.
9. **If** you are from the South and want a business career, **assume** the first move is to Milan or the Centre-North (122,000 of the 150,000 young graduates the South lost in 2019–25 went there), **and** compare Milan-based options before foreign ones.
10. **If** a business-school "Master" (SDA, 24ORE, MIP, LUISS BS) appeals to you, **check** whether the diploma is a university Master di I/II livello or a private certificate, and whether it requires a laurea, **before** comparing its price with an MSc.
11. **If** your LM mark will be around 106/110, **present** your 30-point exam average and any class ranking in foreign applications, **because** 106.8 is the Italian average for economics LMs.
