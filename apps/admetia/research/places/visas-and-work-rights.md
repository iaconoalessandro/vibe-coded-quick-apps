# Visas, post-study work rights and employer sponsorship

## Bottom line

1. **For an EU citizen, the UK is the only European country on Admetia's list that needs a visa.** Across the EU/EEA an Italian can work without a permit; in Switzerland, under the AFMP, an Italian needs no labour-market test or sponsor, but must register with the commune within 14 days and before starting work to receive an L or B permit (SEM; see single source of truth in `visas_immigration/switzerland/switzerland_visas_immigration_guide.md`) [data]. In the UK the same Italian follows the Student, Graduate and Skilled Worker route like any Indian or Chinese graduate. No UK–EU youth scheme had been agreed by late September 2026 (section 1.1).

2. **The UK Graduate visa drops from 2 years to 18 months for applications made on or after 1 January 2027** (GOV.UK, fetched 2026-09-30, https://www.gov.uk/graduate-visa) [data]. Anyone starting a one-year UK MSc in autumn 2026 finishes in 2027 and gets 18 months.

3. **UK sponsorship after the Graduate visa has a pay floor.** A new entrant needs at least £33,400 and 70% of the occupation's going rate; everyone else needs £41,700 and the full going rate (GOV.UK, 2026) [data].
   - Finance analysts (SOC 2422): new-entrant floor £33,400.
   - Management consultants and business analysts (SOC 2431): new-entrant floor £35,100 (the 70% column of the going-rate table).
   - Bank, MBB and Big 4 graduate pay clears these floors. Many marketing and SME offers do not.

4. **The UK's sponsor-free High Potential Individual (HPI) visa covers TUM, ETH, EPFL, PSL, Amsterdam, KU Leuven and 33 US universities, including MIT, Princeton, Duke, Michigan, Vanderbilt, WashU and Berkeley** (GOV.UK list for qualifications awarded Nov 2025 to Oct 2026) [data]. It does not cover HEC, Bocconi, St. Gallen, ESSEC, IE, Esade or any UK school.

5. **US: the $100,000 H-1B payment is not being collected for now, but the lottery now favours higher pay.** Full detail, dates and sources: `visas_immigration/united_states/united_states_visas_immigration_guide.md` (section 3.2).
   - Proclamation 11069 extends the $100,000 restriction to 21 September 2027. Its implementing policies were vacated by two courts (District of Massachusetts, 8 June 2026; N.D. California, 30 September 2026, *Global Nurse Force v. Trump*), and the second order says the fee is no longer in effect. Appeals are pending. USCIS's page (updated 21 September) predates the 30 September order; it says DHS "will comply" but intends to collect if the vacatur is lifted [data].
   - A change of status from F-1 or OPT to H-1B inside the US is not covered by the payment if it is granted. It is covered if the change is denied, or if the person leaves the US before the decision or asks for consular notification (USCIS) [data].
   - DHS has proposed a separate $103,265 fee on all cap-subject H-1B petitions, including those for US master's graduates (proposed rule, 25 August 2026; comments closed 24 September 2026; not law). The text read has no exception for in-country changes of status [data].
   - Also pending: DHS proposed on 11 September 2026 to remove the discretionary 60-day grace period after a job ends for certain nonimmigrants including H-1B holders (comments to 10 November 2026; proposal only). A 9-11 biometric fee has applied since 9 September 2026 to "covered" employers (50 or more staff, more than half on H-1B or L-1) [data]. Information only, not immigration advice.
   - The weighted lottery (in force for FY2027) gives higher wage levels more entries. DHS projects per-person selection chances of about 15% at Level I and 31% at Level II against over 61% at Level IV; these are projections, not results, and the FY2026 figure of 34.9% of eligible registrations selected is not comparable with them (US guide, Caso 2) [data].
   - So STEM OPT's extra 24 months, which means more lottery attempts, matters more than ever.

6. **For non-EU graduates, the continental job-search permits are long, need no job offer and allow full-time work.**
   - Germany: up to 18 months (§20 AufenthG).
   - Netherlands: 1 year (orientation year).
   - France: 1 non-renewable year.
   - Austria: 12 months.
   - Ireland: up to 24 months after a master's.
   - Switzerland is the outlier: 6 months, 15 hours a week, then quotas.

7. **Salary floors for graduate hires are far lower on the continent than in London.**
   - Netherlands: reduced criterion €3,122 a month in 2026.
   - Germany: Blue Card new-entrant floor €45,934.20 a year.
   - France: talent card €39,582.
   - Sweden: 90% of the median salary, SEK 34,470 a month, since June 2026.
   - Sources are in the country sections [data].

8. **Canada is the most generous permit on the list.** Any university master's of at least 8 months earns a 3-year PGWP with no field-of-study restriction, but CLB 7 language is required (IRCC, 2026) [data].

9. **Under half of UK Graduate-visa holders move onto a work visa.** In the first cohort, 50% switched to a work or study visa by the end of the Graduate visa; 86% of those switched to Skilled Worker. Median monthly pay in year one was £1,750 (MAC, 2024) [data]. The Graduate visa is a search window, not a job guarantee.

## 1. United Kingdom

> **Single Source of Truth:** All United Kingdom immigration, visa, and work permit rules are centralized and verified in [`visas_immigration/united_kingdom/united_kingdom_visas_immigration_guide.md`](../../visas_immigration/united_kingdom/united_kingdom_visas_immigration_guide.md).

### 1.1 EU citizens: same route as everyone else
- **The route is unchanged by nationality.** An Italian arriving in 2026–27 needs a Student visa, pays the immigration health surcharge (IHS) and pays international fees; international students on taught master's (MSc/MiM/MBA) cannot bring dependants (Appendix Student ST 31.1). The EU Settlement Scheme protects only people resident before the end of the transition period (Claims to verify #1).
- **EU student numbers in the UK halved** between 2020/21 and 2023/24 (HESA data cited by EPRS, March 2026, https://www.europarl.europa.eu/RegData/etudes/ATAG/2026/782680/EPRS_ATA(2026)782680_EN.pdf) [data]. The reasons given: the visa, the IHS and higher fees.
- **Youth Experience Scheme (YES): not agreed.**
  - The EU opened negotiations by Council Decision (EU) 2025/1286 of 20 June 2025 (https://publications.europa.eu/resource/celex/32025D1286) [data]. The UK Cabinet Office said on 15 January 2026 that any scheme "will be time-limited and subject to an allotted number of places" and that "the exact parameters of any scheme are subject to ongoing negotiations" (letter to Lord Clancarty, UK Parliament deposited paper DEP2026-0031) [data]. The 18–30 age band and a "reasonable timeframe (e.g. 4 years)" appear only as illustrative examples in the Commission's April 2024 draft negotiating directives (COM(2024) 169, Annex, points 12–13) [data], not as agreed terms. No agreed cap, age band or start date exists in any official text read on 2 October 2026.
  - Envisaged for ages 18–30, with stays of "up to two to four years" (EPRS, secondary).
  - The UK and its universities oppose home tuition fees for EU students (EPRS, March 2026) [data].
  - The UK rejoins Erasmus+ in 2027, but that covers exchanges only, not degrees or work.
  - As of 11 September 2026, "no final UK-EU Youth Experience Scheme agreement has been published". The cap is still disputed, with proposals of 50,000–100,000 reported in the press only (West End Consultants, 2026-09-11, https://www.westendconsultants.co.uk/blog/uk-eu-youth-experience-scheme/) [practitioner consensus]; no official figure was found.
  - The July 2026 summit was postponed after Keir Starmer resigned. It is expected "next month at the earliest" under Prime Minister Andy Burnham (Connexion, 2026-09-25, https://www.connexionfrance.com/news/pressure-mounts-on-burnham-to-deepen-uk-eu-ties/817092) [practitioner consensus].
- **Implication:** even a deal agreed in autumn 2026 would still need rules and a capped application system. Do not plan a 2027 UK entry around it.

### 1.2 Graduate visa
- **Duration:** 2 years if you apply on or before 31 December 2026; 18 months from 1 January 2027; 3 years for PhD graduates.
- **Cost:** fee £937, plus IHS "usually £1,035 for each year".
- **Rules:** cannot be extended; switching (for example to Skilled Worker) is allowed (GOV.UK, fetched 2026-09-30, https://www.gov.uk/graduate-visa) [data].
- **Legal basis:** Appendix Graduate, GR 8.1 (2 years if the application is made before 1 January 2027; 18 months on or after; PhD 3 years), introduced by Statement of Changes HC 1333 of 14 October 2025 (Explanatory Memorandum paras 5.25–5.26) (https://www.gov.uk/guidance/immigration-rules/immigration-rules-appendix-graduate; https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/statement-of-changes-to-the-immigration-rules-hc-1333-14-october-2025/explanatory-memorandum-to-the-statement-of-changes-to-the-immigration-rules-hc-1333-14-october-2025-accessible) [data].
- **Upfront cost for 18 months:** about £2,490: £937 plus IHS of 1.5 × £1,035 (GOV.UK: "the yearly cost plus half the yearly cost, if it's for 18 months or less") (https://www.gov.uk/healthcare-immigration-application/how-much-pay) [data]. For a 2-year grant it is £937 plus £2,070.
- **Timing:** a one-year MSc that ended in September–December 2026 can still get the 2-year version if the application goes in by 31 December 2026.

### 1.3 Skilled Worker visa since July 2025
- **General rule:** the higher of £41,700 or the occupation's going rate (GOV.UK, fetched 2026-09-30, https://www.gov.uk/skilled-worker-visa/your-job) [data].
- **New-entrant rule:** applies if you are under 26, or hold or recently held a Student or Graduate visa. You need 70% of the going rate, with a floor of £33,400, for at most 4 years in total (https://www.gov.uk/skilled-worker-visa/when-you-can-be-paid-less) [data].
- **The 4-year clock includes Graduate-visa time.** Law-firm and university summaries state that the discounted rate lasts four years in total counting any time already spent on the Graduate route or an earlier new-entrant Skilled Worker visa, so a 24-month Graduate visa leaves two years at the discounted rate and an 18-month one (from 2027) leaves two and a half; after that the employer must pay the full general rate or the occupation's going rate (Bath and Warwick university guidance, Davidson Morris, 2026) [practitioner consensus; the GOV.UK page was not read in full]. Ask a prospective employer whether it will sponsor you at the full rate when the clock ends, because some will not.
- **Graduate-visa timing.** You must apply from inside the UK, while holding a Student visa, after your education provider has told the Home Office you completed the course. You need not wait for the ceremony or certificate. Applying before that notification does not meet the eligibility rule (GOV.UK Graduate visa eligibility, read 4 Oct 2026) [data]; whether the fee is refunded was not checked, so wait for the provider's confirmation.

**Going rates (Appendix Skilled Occupations, Table 1, RQF 6+), fetched 2026-09-30 from https://www.gov.uk/guidance/immigration-rules/immigration-rules-appendix-skilled-occupations [data]:**

| SOC | Occupation | Going rate | New-entrant pay needed* | Standard pay needed* |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2422 | Finance and investment analysts/advisers | £45,800 | £33,400 | £45,800 |
| 2421 | Chartered and certified accountants | £49,200 | £34,500 | £49,200 |
| 2423 | Taxation experts | £48,500 | £34,000 | £48,500 |
| 2431 | Management consultants and business analysts | £50,200 | £35,100 | £50,200 |
| 2134 | Programmers and software developers | £54,700 | £38,300 | £54,700 |
| 1131 | Financial managers and directors | £75,100 | £52,600 | £75,100 |

*New-entrant column: the going-rate table's own "70% of going rate (option E)" column, which never falls below £33,400 (re-read 2 Oct 2026; the earlier version of this table used my own arithmetic, which differed by up to £100). "Standard" is the going rate column; the general £41,700 minimum also applies. The table also has a lower "25th percentile" set of rates (for example £40,000 for SOC 2134); those apply only to Health and Care jobs and to people already on Skilled Worker permission granted before 4 April 2024, not to new graduates (Appendix Skilled Worker SW 4.2(b); GOV.UK going-rates page, "lower rate").

- **Marketing:** several marketing and sales associate codes are only in the transitional Table 1a (below RQF 6). New applicants cannot use them unless the role is on the Temporary Shortage List (same source) [data]. A MSc Marketing graduate needs the employer to code the job as a graduate-level professional or manager role.
- **The 4-year clock (non-obvious):** someone hired at 24 on £34,000 must reach the full going rate by year 5 to renew.

### 1.4 Employer cost: Immigration Skills Charge
- Rose 32% on 16 December 2025: £1,320 for 12 months or less for large sponsors (£660 per extra 6 months), and £480 for small and charitable sponsors. That is £6,600 for a 5-year hire at a large firm (£2,400 at a small one). Source: The Immigration Skills Charge (Amendment) Regulations 2025, SI 2025/1324, made 15 December 2025, in force 16 December 2025, Schedule table ("More than 54 months, but no more than 60 months £2400 £6600"); the Explanatory Note says the amounts rise "by 32%" (https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2025/1324/made) [data]. The same instrument exempts the research, higher-education-teaching, clergy and sports SOC codes it lists; business, finance and software codes are not exempt [data].

### 1.5 High Potential Individual (HPI) visa

> **Settlement (ILR), added 4 Oct 2026.** Time on a Student or Graduate visa does not count towards the five-year Skilled Worker route to settlement, because neither is a settlement route; a one-year MSc plus two years on the Graduate route therefore adds nothing to that clock. The government's "earned settlement" consultation, which proposed a ten-year baseline qualifying period and the removal of the separate long-residence route, closed on 12 February 2026; the GOV.UK consultation page read on 4 Oct 2026 says the response is still being analysed. Some secondary sources describe the ten-year rule as already in force from April 2026; that was not confirmed on GOV.UK, so check the Immigration Rules before planning on any settlement date [data for the consultation; practitioner consensus for the rest].
- **Terms:** no sponsor or job offer needed. You must have graduated within the last 5 years from a listed university. UK universities are never eligible (GOV.UK, fetched 2026-09-30, https://www.gov.uk/high-potential-individual-visa/eligibility) [data].
- **2025 list (qualifications awarded 1 Nov 2025 to 31 Oct 2026)** (https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/high-potential-individual-visa-global-universities-list/high-potential-individual-visa-global-universities-list-2025) [data]:
  - **France:** IP Paris, PSL, Sorbonne, Paris-Saclay.
  - **Germany:** Heidelberg, **TUM**, Bonn, LMU.
  - **Netherlands:** Delft, Amsterdam, Groningen.
  - **Nordics:** Copenhagen; Karolinska, KTH, Lund, Uppsala.
  - **Switzerland:** ETH, EPFL, Zurich.
  - **Belgium:** KU Leuven.
  - **US:** 33 universities, including MIT, Princeton, Duke, Michigan, Vanderbilt, WashU, Berkeley, Columbia, Penn, Chicago, NYU and Stanford.
  - **Not listed:** HEC, ESSEC, ESCP, EDHEC, emlyon, SKEMA, Bocconi, IE, Esade, IESE, St. Gallen, Mannheim, Erasmus/RSM, CBS, SSE, WU, Nova, Católica.
- **Recent changes:** in November 2025 the list roughly doubled, to about 80 universities, with a cap of 8,000 applications a year (Statement of Changes HC 1333, 14 October 2025, Explanatory Memorandum para 5.24: "doubling the list of eligible institutions ... a cap placed on the route of 8,000 applications per year") [data]. The cap "is not expected to be filled" (Free Movement, 2026-01-29, https://freemovement.org.uk/apply-for-a-high-potential-individual-visa/) [practitioner consensus]. The English requirement for the HPI, Skilled Worker and Scale-up routes rose from B1 to B2 for initial grants from 8 January 2026 (same Explanatory Memorandum, paras 5.22–5.23) [data]. HPI duration: Claims to verify #3.
- **Non-obvious points:**
  - The list that applies is the one for the year your degree is awarded, not the current one.
  - A master's awarded by a listed university qualifies even if it is taught in its business school, for example TUM School of Management or Dauphine within PSL, if PSL awards it (Claims to verify #4).

## 2. Switzerland

> **Single Source of Truth:** All Switzerland immigration, visa, work permit, student logistics, quotas, and health insurance rules are centralized and verified in [`visas_immigration/switzerland/switzerland_visas_immigration_guide.md`](../../visas_immigration/switzerland/switzerland_visas_immigration_guide.md).

- **EU/EFTA citizens:** Free choice of job and residence under the AFMP (EPFL) [data]. No labour-market test or sponsor, but mandatory registration with the commune within 14 days and *before starting work* to receive an L or B permit. The 14 June 2026 "No to 10 million" initiative, which could ultimately have ended free movement, was rejected by 54.8% (54.79% official, admin.ch) [data].
- **Non-EU graduates of Swiss universities (St. Gallen, ETH, EPFL):**
  - 6-month job-search permit L that cannot be extended, with work limited to 15 hours a week. Proof of graduation, funds (CHF 10,500 Zurich to CHF 12,000 Geneva/Vaud) and housing required (Art. 21(3) FNIA / AIG) [data].
  - To hire, the employer can skip the Swiss/EU priority test only if the job is of "predominant scientific or economic interest" (Art. 21 para 3 of the Foreign Nationals and Integration Act). **Skipping the priority test is not the same as skipping the quota**: the permits still count against the cantonal and federal quotas below. The separate proposal to exempt Swiss master's and doctorate graduates from the quotas (Business 22.067 / Dobler motion) was referred back by Parliament and was NOT enacted into law; graduates remain subject to federal/cantonal quotas [data].
- **2026 quotas, unchanged from 2025:**
  - Third countries: 8,500 permits (4,500 B and 4,000 L) (Federal Council decision, 19 Nov 2025) [data].
  - UK citizens: a separate 3,500 (2,100 B and 1,400 L). Only 17% of this UK quota had been used by September 2025 (Vischer, 2025-12) [practitioner consensus].
- **Health insurance (KVG/LAMal):** Mandatory within 3 months of taking up residence, retroactive to arrival [data].
- **Implication:** A non-EU graduate realistically gets into Switzerland only through an employer willing to argue "predominant economic interest" and obtain a cantonal/federal quota allocation. Plan a second country from day one.

## 3. Germany

> **Single Source of Truth:** All German immigration, visa, residence permit, and work rules are centralized and verified in [`visas_immigration/germany/germany_visas_immigration_guide.md`](../../visas_immigration/germany/germany_visas_immigration_guide.md).

- **EU citizens:** Free movement under FreizügG/EU. No visa or work permit required; only local address registration (*Anmeldung*) within 14 days (§17 BMG).
- **Job search after graduation (§20 AufenthG):**
  - Up to 18 months after a German degree (§20(1) Nr. 1, §20(2) S. 2 AufenthG), with full unrestricted work rights (*Erwerbstätigkeit uneingeschränkt gestattet*, §20(4)).
- **EU Blue Card 2026 (§18g AufenthG):**
  - Standard threshold: **€50,700 gross/year** (€4,225/month; 50% of unified pension contribution ceiling).
  - Reduced threshold: **€45,934.20 gross/year** (€3,827.85/month; 45.3% of ceiling) for shortage occupations (*Mangelberufe*), recent graduates within 3 years, and IT specialists with 3+ years experience.
- **Settlement (Niederlassungserlaubnis §18c AufenthG):**
  - German-university graduates: after **2 years (24 months)** in skilled employment with pension contributions and German B1 (§18c(1) S. 2).
  - Blue Card holders: after **27 months** (German A1) or **21 months** (German B1) (§18c(2)).
  - Other skilled workers (§18a, §18b): after **3 years** (36 months).
- **Chancenkarte / Opportunity Card (§20a AufenthG):**
  - For candidates with foreign qualifications: up to 1 year of search with 20 h/week work rights and 2-week trial jobs; requires basic German A1 or English B2 + 6 points unless recognized as Fachkraft; proof of funds **€1,091/month (€13,092/year)** via Sperrkonto in 2026.
  - Useful for a HEC or Bocconi non-EU graduate who wants to search in Germany. A Mannheim or TUM graduate should use §20 instead.

## 4. France

> **Single Source of Truth:** All French immigration, visa, and work permit rules are centralized and verified in [`visas_immigration/france/france_visas_immigration_guide.md`](../../visas_immigration/france/france_visas_immigration_guide.md).

- **Card "recherche d'emploi ou création d'entreprise" (RECE):**
  - 1 year, non-renewable. Open to graduates with a French master's, CGE-labelled MSc, mastère spécialisé or doctorate.
  - During the 12-month search period, any work is permitted freely to support oneself.
  - To switch to an ordinary "Salarié" residence permit without the labour market test (*inopposabilité de l'emploi*), the employment contract must relate to the studies and pay at least **1.5× SMIC (€2,800.53 gross a month / €33,606 a year)** (CESEDA art. L. 422-11; Service-Public F17319) [data].
  - Graduates living abroad can apply within 4 years of graduating. Cost in France: €150 (€100 tax + €50 stamp).
- **Talent card (Passeport Talent):**
  - **Salarié qualifié:** requires a master's-level diploma, contract > 3 months, and gross salary $\ge$ **€39,582 a year** (Arrêté du 21 août 2025; Service-Public F16922) [data]. Valid up to 4 years; tax/stamp €350. Exempt from work authorisation and employer tax.
  - **Carte bleue européenne (EU Blue Card):** contract $\ge$ 6 months, 3+ years higher education, and gross salary $\ge$ **€59,373 a year** (1.5× average reference salary).
  - Accompanying family (*Talent famille*) receives immediate, unrestricted work rights.

## 5. Netherlands

See the single source of truth: `visas_immigration/netherlands/netherlands_visas_immigration_guide.md` (verified 5 Oct 2026).

- **Orientation year (Zoekjaar hoogopgeleiden):**
  - 1 year, cannot be extended; usable once per programme; free work with no employer work permit (TWV).
  - Open within 3 years of graduating from a Dutch programme, or from a foreign institution ranked in the top 200 of at least two of THE, QS and Shanghai (IND, https://ind.nl/en/residence-permits/work/residence-permit-for-orientation-year) [data].
- **2026 gross monthly thresholds** (IND, https://ind.nl/en/required-amounts-income-requirements) [data]:
  - Highly skilled migrant (*Kennismigrant*): €5,942 at 30 or older; €4,357 under 30; **€3,122 reduced criterion for recent graduates** (employer must be an IND-recognised sponsor).
  - EU Blue Card: €5,942, or €4,754 reduced (the employer does *not* need to be an IND-recognised sponsor, Dir. 2021/1883).
- **Non-obvious point:** the orientation year is not only for Dutch graduates. A non-EU graduate of a top-200 foreign university can move to Amsterdam and search freely. Whether a given business school's parent university passes the ranking test depends on the IND's designated list for that year.

## 6. Other EU countries

**EU citizens need no permit in any of them.** The notes below are for non-EU graduates.

- **Sweden:**
  - Full official guide and single source of truth: `visas_immigration/sweden/sweden_visas_immigration_guide.md`.
  - Graduates with at least two completed semesters (60 ECTS credits, Bachelor or Master) can apply for a permit to look for work or start a business of up to 12 months (18 months for PhD) before the student permit expires (Migrationsverket, 2026; Utlänningsförordningen 5:1b) [data].
  - **From 1 June 2026, a work permit needs pay of at least 90% of the median salary: SEK 34,470 a month** since 16 June (SCB median SEK 38,300), with a 75% shortage exemption (SEK 28,725) (Migrationsverket news 2026-06-16; Prop. 2025/26:87) [data].
  - The EU Blue Card threshold is SEK 53,625 (1.25× average gross pay), issued for up to 4 years with accelerated permanent residence in 3 years [data].
  - An SSE graduate at a Stockholm bank or consultancy clears 90% of the median easily. Junior marketing roles may not.
- **Austria:**
  - Full official guide and single source of truth: `visas_immigration/austria/austria_visas_immigration_guide.md`.
  - Non-EU graduates: after an Austrian degree, the student permit can be renewed for 12 months to search for a job or start a business (§ 64 Abs. 4 NAG).
  - A matching offer leads to a Red-White-Red Card for graduates (§ 12b Z 1 AuslBG), with no labour-market test and no points. The pay must be "the locally customary gross minimum salary which comparable Austrian graduates" receive under the applicable collective agreement (Kollektivvertrag - KV).
  - For other skilled workers: RWR Card for Key Workers (*Sonstige Schlüsselkräfte*) threshold is €3,465/month gross (x 14 = €48,510/year); EU Blue Card threshold is €55,678/year gross (€3,977/month x 14).
  - After 21 months of such work within 24 months, the holder can switch to a Red-White-Red Card plus, with open labour-market access (§ 41a NAG) (migration.gv.at, verified 2026-10-05) [data].
  - For WU graduates this is one of the lowest-friction routes on the list, but German is usually needed outside multinationals.
- **Ireland:**
  - Full official guide and single source of truth: `visas_immigration/ireland/ireland_visas_immigration_guide.md`.
  - Non-EU graduates: Stamp 1G gives 12 months (Level 8 Honours Bachelor) or up to 24 months (Level 9 Master's / Level 10 PhD, in two 12-month blocks) with 40 h/week full-time work rights and no self-employment.
  - Critical Skills Employment Permit (CSEP): €40,904/yr for listed occupations (€36,848 for degrees < 12 months old) or €68,911 for unlisted occupations, exempt from LMNT; immediate spouse Stamp 1G and fast-track to Stamp 4 after 21 months. General Employment Permit (GEP): €34,000/yr (rising to €36,605 in Roadmap); LMNT required (28 days online). Under the Employment Permits Act 2024, permit holders can change employers after 9 months. Student visa proof of funds is €10,000/yr; IRP registration fee €300 at Burgh Quay, Dublin. [data]
- **Spain:**
  - Full official guide and single source of truth: `visas_immigration/spain/spain_visas_immigration_guide.md`.
  - Non-EU graduates: under DA 17ª Ley 14/2013, higher education graduates (EQF 6+) get a 12-month non-renewable residence permit for job search or business creation, or can switch directly to work residency under Art. 190 RD 1155/2024 (amended by RD 316/2026) immediately upon graduation with provisional full-time work rights upon admission.
  - Students may work up to 30 hours a week automatically for higher education studies without administrative work authorization (BOE-A-2024-24099). EU Blue Card threshold is €41,356.36/yr (standard) or €33,085.09/yr (reduced for under-30 / shortage occupations); SMI 2026 is €1,221/mo in 14 payments (€17,094/yr; RD 126/2026); IPREM 2026 is €600/mo (€7,200/yr). [data]
- **Italy:**
  - Full official guide and single source of truth: `visas_immigration/italy/italy_visas_immigration_guide.md`.
  - Non-EU graduates of Italian degrees can obtain a 9-to-12-month permit to look for work or start a business (art. 39-bis.1 TUI). ## 9. Employer sponsorship behaviour

- **UK outcomes** (MAC rapid review, May 2024, https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6745b2eecdd295aea88098b6/MAC+Rapid+Review+of+Graduate+Route+-+FINAL+Nov+24__1_.pdf) [data]:
  - Of the first cohort whose Graduate visas had expired (started July–December 2021), 50% had switched to a work or study visa. Of those, 86% went to Skilled Worker, 1% to other work visas and 13% back to Student visas.
  - Median monthly pay in year one was £1,750 (£21,000 a year). Only 19% earned £2,500 or more in their first month, rising to 38% by month 12.
  - 91% of Graduate-visa holders in 2023 had completed a postgraduate taught course.
  - These are all-university averages, and data before 2023 are possibly stale. Graduates of LSE, LBS or Imperial into banks and MBB will do far better, but the base rate is a warning.
- **Cost:** about £6,600 ISC per 5-year hire at a large sponsor, plus fees (section 1.4). Large banks, MBB and Big 4 absorb this routinely. Small firms often decline (5% of firms under £250k turnover had sponsored against 18% of those over £50m; HEPI/IoD, Jan 2023) [data, pre-2023].
- **Quotas are rarely the binding constraint:** the UK-citizen Swiss quota was only 17% used (Vischer, 2025) [practitioner consensus]. Employer willingness and pay floors bind first.
- **US:** the weighted lottery gives higher wage levels more entries (DHS final rule, 90 FR 60864; US guide, Caso 2) [data]. That it therefore favours banks, MBB and big tech over small firms paying Level I wages is my inference, not a published finding.
- **Which employer types sponsor (checked 2 Oct 2026, round 3d):**
  - UK sponsor register (GOV.UK, file dated 2 Oct 2026): 127,606 unique organisation names, 122,238 of them licensed on the Skilled Worker route. The file has no sector or size field, so a count by sector is not possible. Name matching finds the large banks (Goldman Sachs International, JPMorgan Chase Bank N.A., Morgan Stanley UK, Barclays, HSBC, Citi Group, UBS AG), elite boutiques (Lazard, Rothschild, Evercore, PJT, Moelis, Houlihan Lokey, Jefferies, Perella Weinberg), McKinsey, BCG, Bain, Oliver Wyman, the Big 4 and Unilever, L'Oréal, LVMH and Google. A licence is necessary, not sufficient: the role must also clear the pay floor, and the licence does not show that the firm sponsors graduates (https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/register-of-licensed-sponsors-workers) [data].
  - Home Office grants of Skilled Worker entry clearance by sponsor-declared industry, year ending June 2026: finance and insurance 22.1% (5,933 of 26,798), professional, scientific and technical 19.8%, information and communications 13.6%, manufacturing 8.6%. These cover applications made outside the UK only, so they exclude Graduate-visa holders switching inside the UK; the industry field is self-assigned by the sponsor (Home Office, "Sponsored work entry clearance visas by occupation and industry (SOC 2020)", published 27 Aug 2026, https://www.gov.uk/government/statistical-data-sets/immigration-system-statistics-data-tables) [data].
  - Firm size: in an Institute of Directors poll (656 members, fieldwork Oct 2022; HEPI Policy Note 43, 5 Jan 2023), 5% of firms with turnover under £250,000 had sponsored a visa against 18% of firms over £50 million; 60% of non-sponsors said they could find skills without the visa system and 21% were put off by hassle, cost or waiting time (https://www.hepi.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/Not-heard-of-this-Employers-perceptions-of-the-UKs-Graduate-Route-visa.pdf) [data; IoD members, pre-2023, not representative].
- **Does needing a visa lower hiring odds?** No audit or correspondence study isolating visa need was found. The indirect evidence is employer willingness: GMAC's 2026 survey (621 employers, 39 countries) found four in five Western European employers willing to hire graduates who need additional legal documentation (up 17 points on 2025) against just under one in three US employers (26 points below 2022) (GMAC Corporate Recruiters Survey 2026, p.40, https://www.gmac.com/-/media/files/gmac/research/employment-outlook/2026-corporate-recruiters-survey/report.pdf) [data; stated willingness, not realised hiring; Western Europe not split into UK and EU; employers that already recruit from business schools]. This is general information, not immigration advice.

## Hypotheses tested

**H1. "Post-Brexit, an Italian graduate faces the same UK visa barrier as an Indian graduate."**
- **Verdict:** supported.
- **Holds:** all UK routes are nationality-neutral, and YES is not agreed (GOV.UK; West End Consultants, 2026-09-11) [data / practitioner consensus].
- **Fails:** for Italians with EU Settlement Scheme status from before 2021, and if YES is agreed later.

**H2. "For a non-EU MiM graduate, a 2-year continental programme plus the local job-search permit gives more total time than a 1-year UK MSc."**
- **Verdict:** supported for Germany, Austria and Ireland; partly for France and the Netherlands; not for Switzerland.
- **Total time in the country (my arithmetic from the sources above) [data]:**
  - Mannheim or TUM: 24 months of study + 18 months of search, about 42 months.
  - HEC or ESSEC: about 24 + 12 months.
  - St. Gallen: about 24 + 6 months.
  - UK 1-year MSc: 12 + 18 months, about 30 months, with only one pre-graduation recruiting autumn.
- **Fails:** when the target is London banking or MBB, where sponsor-ready employers make the shorter UK window enough.

**H3. "A STEM-designated US MS Finance is worth more to an international than a higher-ranked non-STEM programme."**
- **Verdict:** partly supported.
- **Why:** STEM OPT gives 36 months against 12, so roughly three H-1B lottery attempts instead of one or two.
- **Illustration (my arithmetic, assuming the DHS projected rates):** at Level II (30.58% per draw), three draws give about a 67% cumulative chance against 31% for one draw. At Level I (15.29%), three draws give about 39% against 15%.
- **Fails:** when the higher-ranked programme feeds employers that transfer staff abroad, such as MBB offices or banks sending hires to London. It also fails for EU citizens with no intent to stay in the US.
- **Evidence:** USCIS STEM OPT; Federal Register 2025 [data].

**H4. "A HEC or Bocconi degree gives an easier route into London than a TUM or ETH degree."**
- **Verdict:** not supported for sponsor-free entry. TUM and ETH are on the HPI list; HEC and Bocconi are not (GOV.UK) [data].
- **Holds in practice for HEC and Bocconi:** because their pipelines feed licensed sponsors (see getting-in/employer-pipelines.md).

**H5. "The $100,000 H-1B charge makes US master's pointless for internationals."**
- **Verdict:** not supported, but not a clean escape either. The payment is not being collected while the courts' vacatur stands, and an in-country change of status from F-1 is outside it if granted (not if denied, or if the person leaves the US first); see US guide section 3.2 (USCIS, 2026-09-21) [data].
- **Real remaining risk:** the weighted lottery, the extension of the proclamation to 21 September 2027 (appeals are pending), and the proposed $103,265 fee on all cap-subject petitions, which in the text read has no exception for in-country changes of status (proposed rule, 25 August 2026; comments closed 24 September 2026; not law) [data].

**H6. "The Netherlands is the cheapest continental country in which to be sponsored."**
- **Verdict:** partly supported. The €3,122 a month reduced criterion (about €37,500 a year) is below the German Blue Card (€45,934) and the French talent card (€39,582) [data].
- **Fails:** Austria's graduate RWR card and Germany's general skilled-worker route set no fixed floor, only "customary" or comparable pay.

## Common mistakes and myths

1. **"The UK Graduate visa is 2 years."** Only for applications made by 31 December 2026 (GOV.UK) [data].
2. **"As an EU citizen I can work in London after my LSE MSc."** No. You need the Graduate and then Skilled Worker route (GOV.UK; EPRS) [data].
3. **"Any top school qualifies for HPI."** HEC, Bocconi, IE, St. Gallen and all UK schools are out (GOV.UK list) [data].
4. **"The French job-search card can be renewed."** It is 12 months, one time only (Service-Public) [data].
5. **"The Dutch orientation year is only for Dutch graduates."** Graduates of top-200 foreign universities also qualify (IND) [data].
6. **"The $100k fee applies to every H-1B."** It is not being collected at present (the implementing policies were vacated by two courts; appeals are pending), and an in-country F-1 to H-1B change of status is outside it only if the change is granted; it applies if the change is denied or the person leaves the US before the decision (US guide section 3.2; USCIS) [data].
7. **"£33,400 is always enough for a UK visa."** A £34,000 consultant offer fails, because the new-entrant rate for SOC 2431 is £35,100 (GOV.UK) [data].
8. **"Swiss graduates escape quotas."** The priority test can be waived, but quotas still apply unless the pending reform passed (Blick; SEM) [practitioner consensus].

## Decision rules

1. **If you are an EU citizen targeting London,** get the Graduate visa application in by 31 December 2026 where you can; otherwise plan for 18 months and about £2,500 in fees (GOV.UK).
2. **If you are an EU citizen open to continental careers,** do not pay a premium for UK "post-study rights". Any EU/EEA or Swiss school gives unlimited work rights in 31 countries.
3. **If you are non-EU and your target employers are start-ups or small firms in London,** prefer an HPI-listed university (TUM, ETH, PSL, US schools) over HEC, Bocconi or St. Gallen. Check the list for your graduation year.
4. **If you are non-EU and considering a US master's,** prefer a STEM-designated programme (CIP 52.1301, 30.7102 or 27.0305). It triples your lottery attempts (USCIS; DHS list).
5. **If you are on OPT and have an H-1B offer,** stay in the US through the change of status, because leaving can trigger the $100,000 rule if collection resumes (USCIS; the proclamation now runs to 21 September 2027).
6. **If a UK offer pays below max(£33,400, 70% of the going rate),** it cannot become a Skilled Worker visa. Renegotiate the pay or the SOC code before accepting.
7. **If you are non-EU and considering St. Gallen,** treat Switzerland as possible only through large employers. Plan Germany, Austria or the Netherlands as your fallback.
8. **If you are non-EU with a German master's,** count 18 months of free search and settlement after 2 years of skilled work (§20 and §18c AufenthG).
9. **If you are non-EU and have a Dutch offer,** confirm the firm is an IND-recognised sponsor and pays at least €3,122 a month gross in 2026.
10. **If you are non-EU and targeting Sweden,** check the offer is at least SEK 34,470 a month (90% of the median) (Migrationsverket).
11. **If you are non-EU and weighing Canada against the US,** Canada's 3-year PGWP is guaranteed on graduating, while US work beyond OPT depends on a lottery. Prefer Canada if long-term residence matters more than US pay.
12. **If a UK–EU youth scheme is announced,** ignore it for planning until GOV.UK publishes the rules.
