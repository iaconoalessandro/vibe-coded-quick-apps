# Countries and cities: where to study and where to work

## Bottom line

1. **Studying in a country gives you access to its jobs, not a job. Without the local language, most EU graduates leave.** Five years after graduating, the Netherlands keeps only 20.3% of its EEA international graduates (38.5% of non-EEA ones), and 22.6% of international economics graduates from research universities (Nuffic, 2025) [data]. Leavers mainly cite failing to find suitable work (51%), housing (37%) and Dutch (30%) (Nuffic, 2023) [data].
2. **The strongest graduate labour markets are Germany, the Netherlands, Ireland, Luxembourg, Switzerland and Austria; the weakest are Italy, Greece and France.** In 2025, 92.6% of recent tertiary graduates (1–3 years out) were employed in Germany and 92.1% in the Netherlands, against 82.0% in France and 78.7% in Italy (Eurostat, 2025) [data].
3. **Italy and Spain have converged much faster than their reputation suggests, while France and Sweden have weakened.** Unemployment among tertiary-educated 25–29-year-olds fell in Italy from 16.2% (2019) to 8.2% (2025), now below France's 8.7%. Sweden rose from 5.2% to 8.3% (Eurostat) [data].
4. **London remains Europe's finance capital by a wide margin; Paris is the fastest riser, not the leader.** In 2025, London attracted 59 financial-services FDI projects, against 30 for Paris, 20 for Milan, 17 for Luxembourg and 15 for Madrid. France's FDI-linked finance jobs rose 136% to 1,349, while the UK's rose to 4,372 (EY, June 2026) [data]. The UK handles 38% of global FX trading (BIS via TheCityUK, 2025) [data].
5. **The UK graduate market is the tightest in years, and international master's graduates bear the brunt.** Graduate hiring fell 8% in 2025 and there were 140 applications per vacancy (ISE, 2025) [data]. Among 2022/23 postgraduate-taught leavers, 13% of internationals were unemployed and looking for work, against 3% of UK graduates (Jisc, 2026) [data]. Since Brexit, EU citizens count as internationals for this purpose.
6. **The Netherlands is the most English-tolerant large continental job market; Germany is among the least.** 7.8% of Dutch job postings explicitly say Dutch is not required, compared with ~4% in Italy and France and 2.4–2.8% in Germany, the UK and Ireland (Indeed Hiring Lab, 2024) [data]. Even in the Netherlands, Nuffic concludes that students "underestimate the importance of speaking Dutch" [data].
7. **Italian graduates leave mainly for the UK and Germany, then France and Switzerland, not the Gulf.** Between 2019 and 2025 the UK and Germany together took over 38,000 young Italian graduates (just under a third of the total), France and Switzerland about 12,000 each, and the US 7,000 (ISTAT, Aug 2026) [data]. Net loss 2019–24: 78,000 young Italian graduates [data].
8. **Inside Italy, the labour market is Milan and the North.** The South lost 150,000 Italian graduates aged 25–34 in 2019–2025, 122,000 of them to the Centre-North, and Lombardy takes almost 3 in 10 South-to-North movers (ISTAT, 2026) [data]. Milan was Europe's 3rd city for financial-services FDI projects in 2025 (EY, 2026) [data].
9. **The Gulf ranks top for career momentum but carries a live security risk.** Expats rank the UAE 1st in the world for career prospects, and Saudi Arabia 4th. InterNations flags that its 2026 survey ran across "the outbreak of the US-Iran war" (InterNations Expat Insider, 2026) [data, survey]. Treat Dubai and Riyadh as a second-job or MBA-stage move for most Europeans, not a first-job default.

How to read it:
- **Tight markets (DE, NL, IE, LU, CH, AT).** More than 90% of recent graduates are in work, and young-graduate unemployment is 4–5%. Here the constraint on an international graduate is language and fit, not the number of jobs.
- **Improving southern markets (IT, ES, PT).** Much better than in 2019, but still the weakest in the EU for young graduates. Eurostat names Greece and Italy as the only EU countries where fewer than 80% of recent tertiary graduates were employed in 2025 (Eurostat Statistics Explained, 2026, https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/statistics-explained/index.php?title=Employment_rates_of_recent_graduates) [data].
- **Softening markets (FR, SE, FI, and DE from a high base).** France is flat at 82% and its tertiary 25–29 unemployment rose to 8.7%. This is consistent with the fall in grande-école six-month net employment reported by the CGE (see product/landscape-map.md) [data, secondary]. Germany's vacancy rate fell from 4.3% (2022) to 2.6% (2025) [data]. Nuffic's UWV analyst notes that by 2024 labour-market tightness in Germany had fallen below the Netherlands' (Nuffic, 2025) [data + expert].
- **Sweden is a trap for headline-readers.** Its recent-graduate employment rate is high (91%), but youth unemployment is 24.3% and tertiary 25–29 unemployment is 8.3%, the fastest rise in the table [data].
- The Netherlands and Belgium have the EU's highest vacancy rates in professional, scientific and technical services (5.2% and 5.8%) [data].

### UK and London

- ISE members cut graduate hiring by 8% in 2024–25, the first fall since 2020. There were 140 applications per vacancy, and about 290 in retail/FMCG (ISE Student Recruitment Survey 2025, https://ise.org.uk/_userfiles/pages/files/reports/student_recruitment_survey_2025.pdf) [data].
- Graduate Outcomes 2022/23: graduate unemployment rose from just under 5.5% to 6.1%, and professional-level employment fell from 75.1% to 71.9%. Of international PGT leavers, 74% were employed (UK PGT: 77%), but 13% were unemployed and seeking work (UK PGT: 3%) (Charlie Ball, Jisc, "The graduate labour market in Spring 2026", https://www.imperial.ac.uk/media/imperial-college/staff/education-development-unit/public/The-graduate-labour-market-in-Spring-2026.pdf) [data].
- UK vacancies were about 721,000 in Dec 2025–Feb 2026, including about 35k in financial services and 77k in business services. The same deck cites the Bank of England (March 2026): some large professional-services firms are cutting early-career hiring because AI and automation lower the volume of routine entry-level work (Jisc deck, citing ONS and BoE) [data, secondary].
- UK financial and related professional services employ almost 2.5 million people, two-thirds outside London (TheCityUK Key Facts 2025, https://www.thecityuk.com/media/woaizj2o/key-facts-about-the-uk-as-an-international-financial-centre-2025.pdf) [data]. Non-obvious: Edinburgh, Glasgow, Manchester, Leeds and Birmingham are large back- and middle-office centres, but front-office banking, consulting and asset-management entry remains London-centred [practitioner consensus].

## 2. Language as a hiring barrier

### What employers ask for: the job-posting evidence

Indeed Hiring Lab measured the share of job postings that explicitly say the local language is not required (Sept 2023–Aug 2024) (Indeed Hiring Lab, 10 Oct 2024, https://hiringlab.indeed.com/uk/blog/2024/10/10/how-language-flexibility-shapes-job-opportunities-for-migrants/) [data]:

| Country | Share of postings saying the local language is not required | Standardised to UK occupational mix |
|---|---|---|
| Netherlands | 7.8% (and about 5% of postings are written in English) | 8.7% |
| Spain | 5.8% | 6.0% |
| France | ~4% | 4.2% |
| Italy | ~4% | 4.1% |
| Germany | 2.4–2.8% | 3.4% |
| UK | 2.8% | 2.8% |
| Ireland | 2.4–2.8% | 2.5% |

Two caveats matter for a business graduate:
- **Language flexibility is concentrated in low-paid work** (cleaning, driving, beauty). Higher-paid occupations are less flexible, and advertised wages correlate negatively with "language not required" [data]. So the 4–8% is an upper bound for graduate roles, not a floor.
- **The exceptions are the international hubs.** Indeed notes that in France and Spain some skilled occupations show non-trivial "language not required" shares, and gives the examples of an English-speaking investment banker in Paris and a software developer in Barcelona (same source) [data]. This supports the practitioner view that English-only entry is realistic in IB, trading, the international arms of consulting, and tech, but rare in corporate functions, audit, sales and marketing [practitioner consensus].

### Netherlands (Nuffic 2025, graduation cohorts 2013–14 to 2022–23)
- 25% of international graduates still live in the Netherlands five years after graduating, and about 80% of those are employed (Nuffic, "Stay rate and labour market position of international graduates 2013–2022", 15 May 2025, https://www.nuffic.nl/en/research-facts-and-figures/research/stay-rate-and-labour-market-position-of-international-graduates; PDF: https://www.nuffic.nl/sites/default/files/2025-05/nuffic-stay-rate-and-labour-market-position-of-international-graduates-2013-2022.pdf) [data].
- **EEA graduates stay at about half the non-EEA rate: 20.3% vs 38.5% at five years.** Germany, Italy and Romania are among the largest origin countries, but only Romania (44.4%) makes the top-15 stay-rate list (same report) [data].
- Economics produces the most stayers but has a low stay rate: 22.6% (research universities) and 20.6% (universities of applied sciences) at five years. Of the staying graduates from the research-university MSc Business Administration, 92% are in paid work (same report) [data].
- **The first year is the hard part.** One year after graduation, 41.8% of international stayers have a paid job against 64.9% of Dutch graduates. About 30% are in further study. The gap narrows to 79.6% vs 89.6% by year five. A UWV labour-market analyst quoted in the report says internationals take longer to find substantial jobs, earn less, and names Dutch-language requirements as one likely reason (same report) [data + expert].
- Master's graduates who also did their bachelor's in the Netherlands are more likely to stay than master's-only internationals (same report) [data]. That is a direct measure of the value of local roots.
- In a 2023 Nuffic survey of 680 current and former students, leavers cited struggling to find suitable work (51%), housing (37%), financing (37%) and the Dutch language (30%). Stayers cited quality of life (84%) and career opportunities (82%) (Nuffic, 5 Dec 2023, https://www.nuffic.nl/en/news/this-is-why-international-students-stay-or-leave-after-graduating-in-the-netherlands) [data].
- An earlier Nuffic survey (Nov 2021) found only 45% of EEA students saying they were likely to stay, against 59% of non-EEA students. It put the actual stay rate at about 50% after one year and about 25% after five (https://www.nuffic.nl/nieuws/nederland-blijft-aantrekkelijk-voor-internationale-afgestudeerden) [data; older than 2023].

### Germany (DAAD, Destatis and IW, 2025)
- 46% of international students are still in Germany ten years after starting their studies. That is among the highest retention rates in the OECD, alongside Canada's (Destatis, quoted by Dr Jessica Schüller of the DAAD, Wissenschaft weltoffen, 15 Jul 2025, https://www.wissenschaft-weltoffen.de/en/2025/07/15/international-students-want-to-stay-which-is-a-huge-opportunity-for-the-german-labour-market/) [data].
- Two-thirds of more than 20,000 surveyed international students intend to stay, with the strongest intention in economics, engineering and computer science. The DAAD calls "adequate German language skills" another important factor for a career start, "particularly" for graduates of English-only programmes (same source) [data].
- **Read the 46% carefully.** The largest origin countries are India and China, and STEM enrolment by internationals rose 95% in ten years (same source) [data]. The headline is therefore a non-EU, STEM, often visa-motivated figure, not a guide for an Italian MiM graduate from Mannheim or TUM [interpretation].
- IW, for the DAAD, estimates that if 40% of a cohort stays, their taxes cover the cost of their education within three years, which is why German policy actively courts graduates (Wissenschaft weltoffen, 10 Jun 2025, https://www.wissenschaft-weltoffen.de/en/2025/06/10/we-analysed-to-what-extent-international-students-strengthen-the-skilled-labour-workforce-in-the-country-especially-after-completing-their-studies/) [data].
- Expat experience: 55% of expats in Germany named the language barrier as a top concern before moving (global average 32%), and 62% find German difficult to learn. Germany ranks 30th of 31 countries overall and 19th for working abroad (InterNations Expat Insider 2026, ~8,000 respondents, https://www.internations.org/expat-insider/2026/best-and-worst-countries-2026) [data, survey].

### France, Italy, Spain
No fetched primary stay-rate source comparable to Nuffic's or Destatis's exists for these countries (see Claims to verify: Campus France). What the evidence supports:
- About 4% (France, Italy) and 5.8% (Spain) of postings explicitly waive the local language (Indeed, 2024) [data].
- Italy ranks 30th and Portugal 29th of 31 in InterNations' 2026 Working Abroad Index. Only 38% of expats in Italy say the move improved their career prospects, and 37% disagree. Portugal ranks last for career prospects, and one respondent says the market is "even worse for English jobs" (https://www.internations.org/expat-insider/2026/working-abroad-2026) [data, survey; quote anecdotal].
- Practitioner consensus is that in Paris and Madrid, graduates of HEC/ESSEC/ESCP or IE/Esade without French or Spanish do find work in IB, trading, consulting (international staffing), luxury HQ roles with global scope and tech. Outside those niches they are largely shut out [practitioner consensus]. For Italy, the equivalent English-only niches in Milan are much smaller; see places/italy-playbook.md.

- Investors surveyed on the next three years rank **Paris as the most attractive city globally** for financial-services investment. Hong Kong and New York are joint second and London fourth, though the UK stays the most attractive European country (same source) [data, survey of intentions].
- US firms made 101 FS projects in Europe in 2025 (+40%), and 34% of UK FS projects were US-backed (same source) [data].
- London hosts 160+ foreign banks, and the UK accounts for 38% of global FX turnover (BIS, April 2025) against 19% for the US (TheCityUK Key Facts 2025) [data].

**Interpretation for a graduate:** the relocations after Brexit moved regulated entities, senior staff and some trading desks. Graduate volume in IB and markets remains London-heavy. Paris is now the clear continental number two, with the strongest momentum. Frankfurt is losing FDI momentum but remains the supervision capital, since the ECB and its Single Supervisory Mechanism are there. Milan is rising, mostly as a national market. See careers/finance.md and getting-in/employer-pipelines.md for desk-level detail.

## 4. Study country = work country?

The evidence splits sharply by who is studying.

- **For non-EU students the link is strong**, because the study visa converts to a job-search permit. Non-EEA stay rates are 38.5% (NL, 5 years), and 46% of international students remain in Germany ten years after starting (Nuffic 2025; Destatis via DAAD 2025) [data]. See places/visas-and-work-rights.md.
- **For EU students studying abroad the link is weak.** Only 20.3% of EEA graduates remain in NL five years on [data]. ISTAT counts Italians "returning" from abroad: 9,000 from Germany, just under 7,000 from the UK and about 5,000 from Switzerland in 2025, with returns from the Netherlands up 20.8% (ISTAT, 2026) [data]. Returnees include graduates coming home after studying or starting careers abroad, though ISTAT does not split them by reason.
- **Local roots multiply the effect.** Nuffic finds that internationals who did their bachelor's in the Netherlands too are more likely to stay [data]. In Nuffic's 2021 survey, students who studied fully remotely were less likely to plan to stay (46%) than those with an in-person component (60%) [data; 2021].
- **Where in the country you study matters.** In NL, 40% of internationals still working five years on work in Greater Amsterdam, and Eindhoven keeps nearly 50% of its international graduates (Nuffic, 2025) [data]. Campus recruiting is local: a school's city usually becomes your first-job city.
- **UK after Brexit.** International PGT graduates are four times as likely as UK PGT graduates to be unemployed and seeking work (13% vs 3%) (Jisc, 2026) [data]. For an EU student, a one-year UK MSc now works like any international degree: the job needs a sponsor or the Graduate Route. The Graduate Route shrinks to 18 months for applications from 1 January 2027 (see places/visas-and-work-rights.md).
- School-level evidence (share of each school's graduates who start in the school's country) could not be retrieved from primary reports in this pass. HEC shows its "International Job Locations" only as an embedded graphic. See Claims to verify.

## 5. Italy: Milan vs the rest, and why Italians leave

- **Emigration peaked in 2024 and fell in 2025.** Expatriations of Italian citizens were 141,000 in 2024 (inflated by stricter AIRE registration rules) and 109,000 in 2025 (−22.7%). The net migration balance of Italians was −83,000 in 2024 and −53,000 in 2025 (ISTAT, "Migrazioni interne e internazionali della popolazione residente, anni 2024–2025", 3 Aug 2026, https://www.istat.it/comunicato-stampa/migrazioni-interne-e-internazionali-della-popolazione-residente-anni-2024-2025/; report PDF: https://www.istat.it/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Statistica-report_Migrazioni-interne-e-internazionali-della-popolazione-residente_ANNI-2024-2025.pdf) [data].
- **The graduate brain drain in numbers.** In 2019–2024, 247,000 Italians aged 25–34 emigrated and 86,000 returned. Counting those with at least a bachelor's degree, Italy had a net loss of 78,000 young graduates, offset by a net gain of 88,000 foreign young graduates (same report) [data].
- **Where young Italian graduates go (2019–2025, including provisional 2025 data):** the UK and Germany together took over 38,000 (just under a third), France and Switzerland about 12,000 each, and the US 7,000 as the top non-European destination (same report) [data]. No Gulf country is named among the main destinations.
- **Brexit and the 2025 shift.** Among all Italian expatriates, flows to the UK fell from 18,000 to 11,000 (−38.5%) and to Germany from 20,000 to 13,000 in 2025. Spain became the first destination for the first time, with 12.2% of Italian emigrants. However, 34.9% of Italians moving to Spain and 35.9% of those moving to the UK were born abroad (naturalised or dual citizens moving on), against 72–85% Italian-born for Germany, Switzerland, the Netherlands and Belgium (same report) [data]. **Non-obvious:** the "Italians flocking to Spain" headline is partly re-emigration by foreign-born Italian citizens. The graduate labour flow still points to the UK, Germany, France and Switzerland.
- **Internal migration is the bigger story.** The South lost 150,000 Italian graduates aged 25–34 in 2019–2025: 122,000 to the Centre-North and 28,000 abroad. Lombardy receives almost 3 in 10 South-to-North moves, Emilia-Romagna has the highest in-migration rate, and the North-East and North-West have positive internal balances (+1.6 and +1.3 per 1,000) (same report) [data].
- **Why they leave.** Italy has the EU's second-lowest recent-graduate employment rate (78.7%) (Eurostat, 2025) [data], and expats rank it 30th of 31 for working abroad (InterNations, 2026) [data, survey]. For pay gaps and the impatriati tax regime, see money/salaries-and-roi.md and places/italy-playbook.md.
- **Milan vs Rome.** Milan is the national centre for finance, consulting, FMCG/luxury marketing and FS FDI (20 EY projects in 2025) [data]. Rome's graduate market is shaped by the public sector, the energy and utilities HQs (Eni, Enel) and the state-owned groups [practitioner consensus]. City-level salary evidence is in money/salaries-and-roi.md.

## 6. The Gulf and Singapore

- **Career momentum is real.** In InterNations' 2026 Working Abroad Index, the UAE ranks 3rd overall and 1st for career prospects: 80% say the move improved their career prospects (global average 55%), and 55% moved for work (33% globally). Saudi Arabia ranks 9th overall and 4th for career prospects (InterNations Expat Insider 2026, https://www.internations.org/expat-insider/2026/working-abroad-2026) [data, survey].
- **The trade-offs are hours and fair pay.** The average full-time week in the UAE is 46.3 hours (global average 42.2). The UAE ranks 24th for work-life balance and 24th for fair pay (52% positive), and 18th for personal finance despite the absence of income tax, because living costs absorb the gain (same source) [data, survey]. For after-tax comparisons, see money/salaries-and-roi.md.
- **Security risk in 2026.** InterNations notes that its February–March 2026 fieldwork straddled "the outbreak of the US-Iran war", so its UAE and Saudi results only partly reflect it (https://www.internations.org/expat-insider/2026/best-and-worst-countries-2026) [data]. Jisc's Spring 2026 labour-market deck lists "Iran/energy supplies" as a major shock risk [data]. This is a fast-moving factor; check current travel advice before committing.
- **Openness for Europeans.** Practitioner consensus is that Gulf offices of MBB, the Big 4 and banks prefer experienced hires, Arabic speakers or GCC nationals (Saudisation and Emiratisation quotas), plus mobility transfers from London and Paris. Direct entry by a pre-experience European master's graduate is possible but less common than a transfer after 2–3 years [practitioner consensus; primary data not retrieved, see Claims to verify].
- **Singapore.** Expats rank Singapore 7th overall and 2nd for quality of life, but 30th for cost of living (InterNations 2026) [data, survey]. The UK and US are first and second for FX, with Singapore third at 12% of global turnover (BIS via TheCityUK, 2025) [data]. The Employment Pass and the COMPASS points test are covered in places/visas-and-work-rights.md. Practitioner view: Singapore is the APAC hub for banking, private markets and consulting, but recruits mainly from local universities (NUS, NTU, SMU) and from INSEAD and ESSEC Asia-Pacific campuses. A European graduate usually arrives via an internal transfer [practitioner consensus].

## Hypotheses tested

**H1 (assigned). Studying in country X is the strongest predictor of working in country X.** Verdict: **partly supported.**
- Holds: for non-EU students (visa conversion), for students with local roots (a local bachelor's, the local language), and at city level (campus recruiting). Nuffic: non-EEA 38.5% five-year stay rate. NL-bachelor holders stay more often. 40% of NL stayers work in Greater Amsterdam [data].
- Fails: for EU students without the language. 79.7% of EEA graduates have left the Netherlands within five years, and economics has a 22.6% stay rate (Nuffic, 2025) [data]. For an EU citizen, studying in X buys an internship pipeline into X, not residence.

**H2 (assigned). An English-taught master's in Germany without German leads to a low stay rate for business graduates.** Verdict: **partly supported (direction supported, no business-specific stay rate found).**
- Evidence: the DAAD calls German skills a key factor, especially for English-only programmes [data]. Only 2.4–3.4% of German postings waive German [data]. 62% of expats find German difficult [data, survey].
- Fails or unknown: the 46% ten-year stay rate is high but dominated by non-EU STEM students. No Destatis split by field and language was retrieved (Claims to verify).

**H3 (assigned). Post-Brexit, Paris has overtaken London for EU graduates' finance entry.** Verdict: **not supported on volume; supported on momentum.**
- London had 59 FS FDI projects vs Paris's 30, and 4,372 vs 1,349 FDI-linked jobs in 2025. The UK has 38% of global FX [data].
- Paris had the fastest job growth (+136%) and is investors' top city for the next three years (EY, 2026) [data].
- For an EU citizen, the visa cost of London is the real tie-breaker (see places/visas-and-work-rights.md).

**H4 (assigned). Milan is the only Italian city where a foreign-master's graduate earns a competitive salary.** Verdict: **partly supported (job concentration supported; salary claim insufficient evidence here).**
- Milan is Italy's only city in the European FS FDI top 5 [data], and Lombardy is the main destination of internal graduate migration [data].
- No city-level Italian salary data was fetched for this file. See money/salaries-and-roi.md and places/italy-playbook.md. Rome remains a real market for energy, public-sector and state-owned-group careers [practitioner consensus].

**H5 (assigned). The Gulf is the fastest-growing destination for European business graduates.** Verdict: **not supported for Italians by volume; insufficient evidence on growth.**
- ISTAT's 2019–2025 destinations for young Italian graduates are the UK, Germany, France, Switzerland and the US. No Gulf state is named [data].
- Expat career ratings for the UAE and Saudi Arabia are the world's best (InterNations, 2026) [data, survey], but 2026 conflict risk tempers the case.

**H6 (own). A tight labour market does not translate into access for non-speakers of the local language.** Verdict: **supported.**
- The Netherlands has one of the EU's highest vacancy rates (4.1% overall, 5.2% in professional services) [data]. Yet one year out, only 41.8% of international stayers have paid jobs against 64.9% of Dutch graduates, and EEA stay rates are 20% [data].
- Demand is necessary but not sufficient. Language and local networks decide access.

**H7 (own). Italy is no longer the worst place in the EU for a young graduate to look for work.** Verdict: **partly supported.**
- On unemployment, Italy's tertiary 25–29 rate (8.2%) is now below France's (8.7%), Sweden's (8.3%) and Spain's (10.0%) [data].
- On employment of recent graduates, Italy (78.7%) is still second-worst in the EU [data]. More Italian graduates are in work or searching, but many remain inactive or in further study. Italy is no longer a uniquely bad market, but it is still a low-pay one (see money/salaries-and-roi.md).

**H8 (own). Brexit has diverted Italian graduate emigration away from the UK.** Verdict: **partly supported.**
- All-Italian expatriation to the UK fell 38.5% in 2025, and Spain became the top destination [data].
- But the UK and Germany together still took just under a third of young Italian graduate emigrants over 2019–2025 [data]. The Spanish surge is partly foreign-born Italians (34.9%) [data].

**H9 (own). Switzerland and the Nordics are high-pay but low-belonging choices, which increases the odds of leaving after 2–3 years.** Verdict: **partly supported (the belonging deficit is measured; the exit rate is not).**
- Norway, Sweden, Austria, Germany and Switzerland occupy the bottom six of the 2026 Ease of Settling In Index [data, survey].
- No retention data was fetched to prove the effect on actual departures.

**H10 (own). France's graduate market has softened more than the rankings imply.** Verdict: **supported.**
- Recent-graduate employment is flat at 82.0% (2019 → 2025), and tertiary 25–29 unemployment rose from 7.2% to 8.7% [data].
- The CGE grande-école six-month net employment rate fell from 90.5% (2023) to 76% (2026) (CGE Enquête Insertion 2026 report, verified by the lead 2026-10-01) [data, secondary].
- Pre-2023 advice about "guaranteed" jobs from a French MiM is stale.

## Common mistakes and myths

1. **"An English-taught master's means an English-speaking job market."** The Netherlands, the most tolerant market, still loses about 80% of EEA graduates within five years. Leavers cite jobs (51%) and Dutch (30%), and Nuffic warns that students underestimate Dutch (Nuffic, 2023/2025) [data].
2. **"Germany keeps half its international students, so it will keep me."** The 46% is mostly non-EU STEM. The DAAD itself flags German as a key hurdle for English-programme graduates [data].
3. **"Paris has replaced London."** Not on volume. London had twice Paris's FS FDI projects and three times its FDI-linked jobs in 2025 (EY, 2026) [data].
4. **"The UK is still easy for Europeans."** Since Brexit, EU citizens are internationals, and international PGT unemployment was 13% vs 3% for UK PGT graduates (Jisc, 2026) [data].
5. **"Italy has no jobs for graduates."** The national picture is better than in 2019: tertiary 25–29 unemployment is 8.2%, down from 16.2% [data]. The problem is concentration in the North and low pay, not an absence of jobs.
6. **"Everyone Italian is moving to Spain."** It is true for all expatriates in 2025, but over a third of them were born abroad, and young graduates still go mostly to the UK, Germany, France and Switzerland (ISTAT, 2026) [data].
7. **"Tax-free Dubai means more money."** Expats rank the UAE 18th for personal finance and 24th for fair pay, with a 46-hour average week (InterNations, 2026) [data, survey]. Tax-free gross pay is not the same as higher savings.
8. **"Luxembourg is a tax haven with few jobs."** It is Europe's largest fund domicile, with €6.69tn in net assets (ALFI, 2026) [data], and ranks 1st for expat job security (InterNations, 2026) [data, survey]. The fund-operations career path is underrated by students.
9. **"Sweden and Denmark are easy wins because everyone speaks English."** Sweden's youth unemployment is 24.3%, and both countries score poorly for settling in and local career prospects (Denmark ranks 22nd for local job market) [data; data, survey].

## Decision rules

1. **If you are an EU citizen without the local language and want to work in Germany, France, Italy or Spain, choose a school there only if you will reach B2/C1 before the recruiting cycle** (because only 2.4–6% of postings waive the language and the DAAD names German as a key hurdle). Otherwise target London, Dublin, Amsterdam or Luxembourg.
2. **If your goal is IB, S&T or asset management and you are an EU citizen, prefer a school that recruits into London or Paris; pick London only if you can fund the visa route** (because London has twice Paris's FS projects, and Paris is the only EU city with comparable momentum).
3. **If you want to stay in the Netherlands, start Dutch in year one and secure a Dutch-employer internship** (because international stayers trail Dutch graduates by 23 points on employment in year one, and prior contact with Dutch employers raises stay odds).
4. **If you are a non-EU student, weight post-study work rights and local stay rates above ranking** (non-EEA stay rates are 38.5% in NL and 46% at ten years in Germany; see places/visas-and-work-rights.md).
5. **If you are Italian and plan to return, prefer a foreign school with Milan placement or a Bocconi or Polimi double degree** (because the Italian graduate market is concentrated in Lombardy, and returnees mostly come back from Germany, the UK and Switzerland; see places/italy-playbook.md).
6. **If you are choosing between the UK and a continental one-year MSc, check the school's international-graduate unemployment, not the headline employment rate** (UK international PGT unemployment is 13% vs 3% for UK students).
7. **If your target is fund operations, private-markets back office or depositary banking, add Luxembourg to the list** (because assets grew ~20% year on year and job security ranks 1st).
8. **If your target is commodity trading, study or intern in Geneva or Zug, or take the SuisseNégoce pipelines** (because the 215+ member firms cluster there and run their own training programmes).
9. **If you are considering the Gulf as a first job, prefer to enter via a London or Paris office and transfer after 2–3 years** (because practitioner consensus is that pre-experience direct entry is rare, and 2026 security risk is elevated).
10. **If you compare countries on "graduate employment", use Eurostat's 1–3-year recent-graduate rate and tertiary 25–29 unemployment together** (because Sweden and Italy look very different on the two measures).
11. **If you need quality of life and a social life, discount Germany, Switzerland, Austria and the Nordics by their settling-in scores** (bottom six worldwide in 2026).
12. **If you want an English-speaking continental job, rank Amsterdam > Luxembourg > Dublin (full English) > Paris/Madrid finance niches > Berlin tech > the rest** (based on the share of postings that waive the local language and on InterNations working-abroad ranks).
