# Origin-country playbooks for non-EU students

> **Not legal, tax or immigration advice.** This file summarises official sources as of the dates given. Rules change and individual cases differ: confirm with the competent authority or a qualified adviser before acting.

## Bottom line

1. **UK visa risk is now a nationality problem, not a school problem.** Refused share of decided UK study visas by quarter, Q4 2025 to Q2 2026: Pakistan 34-41%, Nigeria 17-25%, India 7-9%, Turkey 1-7%, China 0.1-1%, US 0.2-0.8% (Home Office datasets, author calculation) [data]. The tables do not split by sponsor, so we cannot say whether LSE or Imperial applicants fare better.
2. **UK master's-level visa grants fell for India (-32% from 2022) and China (-23% from 2023), while American grants reached 7,597 in 2025, the highest of the years tabulated** (Home Office, author calculation) [data]. Nigeria fell 70% from 2022 to 2024, partly rebounded in 2025, and is being refused again at high rates.
3. **Currency, not tuition, drives affordability for Nigeria and Turkey but not for India or China.** A fixed GBP 41,000 programme cost 6.8x as many naira and 12.5x as many lira in 2025 as in 2015; rupees 1.17x, yuan 1.00x, reais 1.45x (World Bank, author calculation) [data]. H-a is supported for NGN and TRY and not supported for INR.
4. **The same grades clear one school and fail another.** India 7.6/10 clears LSE's 2:1 equivalent, Imperial's mainstream bar is 8.0/10; China needs 85% from a "highly regarded" (LSE) or Project 211 (Imperial) university, otherwise 90% at LSE; Turkey's 3.3/4 at LSE versus 3.0-3.25/4 at Imperial (school pages, 2026) [data].
5. **Home funding rules mostly do not help a pre-experience master's abroad.** PM-Vidyalaxmi is for Indian institutions only; Chevening needs two years of work; CSC and YLSY are aimed at doctoral/academic or public-sector staff; US Grad PLUS ended for new borrowers on 1 July 2026 (USD 20,500 a year) [data].
6. **Foreign-exchange rules are paperwork in India and China, a real cap in Nigeria.** India: USD 250,000 a year under LRS; China: USD 50,000 is a convenience quota with documents for more; Nigeria: USD 25,000 per semester and USD 5,000 per quarter from 1 June 2026 (RBI, SAFE, press on CBN) [data / secondary].
7. **Home-country degree recognition is a hidden condition.** China's CSCSE and Turkey's YÖK matter for state employers; India's AIU for study; online or hybrid components are the main risk (CSCSE, YÖK press, AIU) [data / secondary].
8. **UK and US passport holders are third-country nationals for EU schools.** Germany lets UK nationals apply for the permit after arrival; Swiss quotas for UK nationals are separate (German Embassy London; Vischer) [data / practitioner consensus].
9. **No regulator evidence on agents.** We found no UK or EU statement on agent quality or fraud; UKCISA warns about impersonators (3 Feb 2026) [data]. H-c is insufficiently evidenced.
10. **Confidence is limited by secondary sources for CBN, YÖK, SAFE tuition rules and CSCSE;** see Numbers to treat with care.

## 1. Which origins, and which were skipped

**Covered (6):** India, China, Nigeria, Turkey, United Kingdom, United States. Selection rule: official pages from LSE or Imperial for grade conversion, plus Home Office visa data, plus at least one official home-country rule (RBI, SAFE, CBN, YÖK, FSA) that we could open.

**Skipped:**
- **Brazil.** Home Office data are in section 8 (304 master's visas in 2025, refusals 0.6%) and the real moved 1.45x against sterling over ten years (section 9), but we found no official page on CAPES or Science without Borders successors, and no LSE or Imperial Brazil page was opened. The Gemini lead on Fundação Estudar (5-90% of costs) is unverified.
- **Gulf (GCC) nationals.** Only Home Office numbers: Saudi Arabia 1,355 master's grants in 2025, refusals 1.5%; Kuwait 4,887 study visas issued in the year to June 2026, refusals 0.3%; Qatar 888; UAE 230 (section 8 dataset; all course levels for the last three). Saudi KASP and similar bonded scholarships were not verified. Content on Gulf destinations is in places/gulf-and-central-eastern-europe.md.
- **Pakistan and Bangladesh** are not on the brief's list, but the Home Office data show them as the highest-refusal nationalities (Pakistan 34-41% per quarter since Q4 2025). We flag them in section 8 but give no playbook.

**What each playbook covers:** degree structure and grade conversion (from LSE and Imperial pages), tests, home-country funding and foreign-exchange rules, UK visa outcomes (section 8 for all), return outcomes and national pitfalls. Continental stay rights for these nationalities are in places/visas-and-work-rights.md; we did not find nationality-specific stay-rate statistics from Nuffic, DAAD or Campus France that we could open (Campus France publishes country sheets, for example for Nigeria, which we did not open).

**Evidence strength by origin (our judgement).** Best: UK and US (official school and FSA pages, Home Office data). Good: India, China (school pages, RBI, SAFE). Moderate: Nigeria (CBN rule via press only), Turkey (YÖK via press only).

## 2. India

### 2.1 Degree structure and grade conversion

- **A three-year Indian bachelor's is accepted by the UK schools we could read.** LSE requires "a bachelor's degree (BA, BSc, BCom or BTech) awarded after a minimum of three years of study" for taught master's, and Imperial Business School accepts a "three to four-year Bachelor's degree from a mainstream university (state/federal or 'deemed' institution)" (LSE India page, 2026, https://www.lse.ac.uk/study-at-lse/international-students/india; Imperial Business School international entry requirements, 2026, https://www.imperial.ac.uk/business-school/programmes/international-entry-requirements) [data]. The "three years is not enough" worry belongs to the US and a few EU schools (see getting-in/admissions.md, which could not confirm the US rule), not to the UK pages read here.
- **Published thresholds differ by school and are not "a 2:1".** LSE publishes CGPA cut-offs by scale:

| LSE taught master's, India | 3.x/4 | /7 | /8 | /9 | /10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2:1 equivalent | 3.3 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 7.6 |
| First-class equivalent | 3.5 | 5.5 | 6.5 | 7.5 | 8.5 |

(LSE India page, 2026) [data]. Imperial's business school asks for at least 70% or CGPA 8.0/10 from a mainstream university, falling to 7.5/10 or 65% for what it calls elite institutions (Imperial, 2026) [data]. So a 7.6/10 is enough for LSE's 2:1 equivalent but not for Imperial's mainstream threshold. Check the scale printed on your own transcript; many Indian universities do not use a 10-point scale in the same way.
- **LSE enrolled 506 graduate students from India in 2025** (LSE India page, 2026) [data]. Germany hosted 49,008 Indian students in winter semester 2023/24, the largest origin group, all levels, not only business (DAAD/DZHW, Wissenschaft weltoffen kompakt 2025, https://www.pedocs.de/volltexte/2025/33602/pdf/Kercher_et_al_2025_Wissenschaft_weltoffen_kompakt.pdf) [data]. A DZHW press release cites about 58,800 for winter 2024/25 (search snippet only; not opened, see Claims to verify).

### 2.2 Tests and language

LSE says that for many quantitative programmes a GRE or GMAT score is needed, and that an English test is not needed at first application (LSE India page, 2026) [data]. See getting-in/admissions.md for GMAT Focus scales and waivers. We found no school-published GMAT cut-off by Indian university tier. The claim that Indian "Tier-3" graduates need a GMAT Focus of 665 or 685 is a Gemini lead with no traceable source; treat it as unverified (see Corrections).

### 2.3 Funding and foreign exchange

- **Collateral-free state-backed lending (PM-Vidyalaxmi) does not cover study abroad.** The scheme covers students "admitted on their own merit to one of the designated Quality Higher Educational Institutions (QHEIs) in the country", 1,425 institutions at the date of the Ministry's note. It offers a 75% government credit guarantee on loans up to INR 7.5 lakh and a 3% interest subvention for family income up to INR 8 lakh on loans up to INR 10 lakh (PM Vidyalaxmi Scheme factsheet, Ministry of Education/PIB, 29 July 2026, https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2026/jul/doc2026729936401.pdf) [data]. A family hoping to use it for a London MSc will not qualify.
- **Overseas study loans come from banks under the Indian Banks' Association model scheme, from non-bank lenders, or from cross-border lenders.** Search snippets quote an IBA ceiling of about INR 20 lakh abroad, collateral-free only up to INR 7.5 lakh, and a priority-sector classification for loans up to INR 20 lakh abroad. We could not open an official page for these (the Tripura government copy refused the connection), so they are in Claims to verify, not the body. Check current terms on the lender's own page. Interest rates and Prodigy/MPOWER terms are in money/costs-and-funding.md.
- **Remittance rules.** Residents may remit up to USD 250,000 per financial year under the Liberalised Remittance Scheme, and "studies abroad" is a listed purpose (RBI FAQ on LRS, https://www.rbi.org.in/commonman/english/Scripts/FAQs.aspx?Id=1834, read 2026-10-01) [data]. Since 1 April 2025 the tax collected at source on LRS remittances is nil up to INR 10 lakh and 5% above for self-funded education, and nil for education financed by a loan from a notified financial institution (Business Standard on Budget 2025, https://www.business-standard.com/amp/budget/news/tcs-on-foreign-remittances-up-to-rs-10-lakh-none-on-foreign-education-loan-125020100857_1.html, Feb 2025) [practitioner consensus, secondary]. For a loan-free family, a 5% TCS on tuition above INR 10 lakh is a cash-flow cost recoverable against tax, not a lost cost, but it must be pre-funded. Confirm with the bank.
- **Rupee path.** World Bank official rates: INR per GBP 98.0 in 2015, 89.9 in 2019, 114.8 in 2025, so a fixed sterling price cost 17% more rupees in 2025 than in 2015, and 28% more than in 2019 (World Bank PA.NUS.FCRF, updated 13 July 2026, author calculation; see section 9) [data, author calculation]. That is a slow drift, not a collapse.

### 2.4 UK visa outcomes for Indians

From section 8: 78,511 master's-level study visas granted to Indians in 2025, down 32% from the 2022 peak of 115,445. Refusals were about 2% in mid-2025 but 6.7%-9.1% in each of the last three quarters (Q4 2025 to Q2 2026), for all course levels [data, author calculation]. That is still far below Nigeria and Pakistan. Post-study routes and salary floors: see places/visas-and-work-rights.md. Indians' continental alternatives (Germany's 18-month permit, the Dutch orientation year, France's non-renewable year) are also covered there.

### 2.5 Return outcomes and pay

- **Employer-stated data on Asia is thin and favourable on purchasing power, not on pounds.** In LBS's MiM 2025 report, 15% of the class is from South Asia and 31% from South East/East Asia (94% international, 52 nationalities). 54% went to jobs in the UK, 21% elsewhere in Europe, 18% in Asia and 5% in Africa/Middle East. Mean base pay was £44,402 in the UK, £38,643 in Europe and £33,540 in Asia (min £7,997, max £98,538); in the report's PPP-adjusted "international dollars" the Asia mean is $99,528 against $67,125 for the UK (London Business School, MiM Employment Report 2025, https://assets.london.edu/hxo16fanegqh/7oC5DZdSFwJChIaAb4i9AE/e62b2c069b9c86ab5d75c841c006ee0e/mim_employment_report.pdf) [employer-stated]. It does not break out India separately, so we cannot give an LBS-to-India salary.
- **A degree earned abroad may need an equivalence certificate in India for study or some public jobs.** The Association of Indian Universities' Evaluation Division issues equivalence certificates for foreign qualifications (an embassy page summarising the procedure gives a fee of the equivalent of US$200; Indian High Commission, Malaysia, https://indianhighcommission.com.my/equivalence_certificate.html, undated) [practitioner consensus, secondary]. The page frames it for admission to Indian universities; we did not establish when employers require it. Private-sector employers generally do not ask; check any public-sector or regulated-profession target.

### 2.6 Pitfalls

- **Refusal risk is now not zero for Indians** and is likely to be concentrated at lower-compliance sponsors, but the Home Office tables do not show it (section 8.2).
- **Agents.** We found no UK or EU regulator warning about education agents in Indian-specific terms. UKCISA's scams page (updated 3 February 2026) warns about callers impersonating the Home Office, or an agent, demanding money (https://www.ukcisa.org.uk/student-advice/life-in-the-uk/frauds-and-scams/) [data]. That is impersonation fraud, not evidence on agent quality. See H-c.

## 3. China

### 3.1 Degree structure and grade conversion

- **Chinese bachelor's degrees are four years, so the three-year question does not arise.** What matters is the institution and the percentage mark. LSE sets two tiers. For a 2:1 equivalent it requires "85 per cent from applicants who have attended a highly regarded institution in China", and at least 90% from all other applicants. For first-class it asks 88% and 93% respectively (LSE China page, 2026, https://www.lse.ac.uk/study-at-lse/international-students/china) [data]. LSE does not publish the list of "highly regarded" institutions on that page.
- **Imperial Business School asks for "a final overall weighted average mark of at least 85% from a Project 211 university"** (Imperial Business School international entry requirements, 2026, https://www.imperial.ac.uk/business-school/programmes/international-entry-requirements) [data]. Project 211 is the older state list of key universities; the page does not mention the newer "Double First-Class" list. A Gemini lead said Manchester, Bristol and Warwick publish 80% for 985/211 and 87-90% for others; we did not open those pages, so that is not used here (see Corrections).
- **Practical reading.** The same Chinese mark means different things at different sources. A student with 86% from a 211 university clears both LSE (85%) and Imperial (85%); the same 86% from a non-listed university fails LSE's 90% tier and Imperial's 211 rule. Continental schools read the transcript more holistically in our experience, but we found no official page quantifying that, so it is not asserted.

### 3.2 Tests and language

Same GMAT/GRE logic as for India (LSE says quantitative programmes need GRE or GMAT; getting-in/admissions.md). The GRE General Test costs US$231.30 in China against US$249 elsewhere (money/costs-and-funding.md, from ETS). No school-published language waiver specific to China was verified.

### 3.3 Funding and foreign exchange

- **The USD 50,000 annual personal quota is a "convenience" threshold, not a ceiling.** SAFE states the principle as "small amounts convenient, large amounts regulated": individuals have an annual convenience quota of USD 50,000-equivalent, and "large amounts must provide genuineness materials" (SAFE reply to NPC proposal no. 5467, 2024, https://www.safe.gov.cn/safe/file/file/20240301/67efecf61b05467399e235bb9f5794d9.pdf; the reply is about settlement of overseas-Chinese remittances, so it states the principle but not the study-abroad rule) [data]. For tuition, Chinese press reporting of the SAFE rules says study-abroad purchases above the quota are allowed on presentation of a passport, valid visa, the overseas admission letter and a tuition or living-cost proof (Zhejiang Online, 2017, https://china.zjol.com.cn/gnxw/201701/t20170103_2599075.shtml, snippet only) [practitioner consensus, secondary, not opened]. So a Gemini lead calling USD 50,000 a "cap" is misleading. A £45,000 MSc fee plus living costs can exceed USD 50,000, but it is a paperwork step, not a bar.
- **CSC scholarships are mostly doctoral or joint-training.** The China Scholarship Council's state-sponsored programmes as described by partner universities concentrate on PhD and joint PhD study, with a return-to-China expectation whose terms "vary by scholarship category and have changed over time" (Leiden University CSC page, 2026, https://www.universiteitleiden.nl/en/scholarships/saz/csc-scholarship, via search summary) [practitioner consensus]. We found no CSC route for a self-selected one-year business master's. The Gemini claim of a "100% refund plus 30% liquidated damages" is unverified.
- **Renminbi path.** CNY per GBP: 9.51 in 2015, 8.82 in 2019, 9.47 in 2025; a fixed sterling price cost about the same in yuan in 2025 as in 2015 (World Bank PA.NUS.FCRF, updated 13 July 2026, author calculation) [data, author calculation]. The yuan has been the most stable of the five origin currencies we computed, so H-a is least relevant for China.

### 3.4 UK visa outcomes

China is the lowest-risk nationality in the Home Office data. Refusals were 0.1% in the year to June 2026 (86 refused against 86,368 issued), and master's grants were 48,688 in 2025, down 23% from the 2023 peak of 63,070 (section 8) [data, author calculation]. Germany hosted 38,687 Chinese students in winter 2023/24 (DAAD/DZHW 2025) [data].

### 3.5 Return outcomes

China has a large returnee flow with falling pay expectations. Zhaopin's 2025 returnee report, as relayed by Caixin, says overseas students seeking jobs in China rose 5% in 2025 to an eight-year high (Caixin Global, 4 February 2026, https://www.caixinglobal.com/2026-02-04/chinas-overseas-graduates-return-in-record-numbers-as-job-market-shifts-102411269.html; the rest of the article is paywalled) [practitioner consensus, secondary]. Other search summaries of the same report say median salary expectations fell and over 80% hold a master's or above; we did not open the primary Zhaopin report (Claims to verify). LBS places 18% of its MiM class in Asian jobs (LBS MiM report 2025) [employer-stated].

### 3.6 Pitfalls: degree verification and online study

- **A foreign degree must be authenticated by the Ministry of Education's CSCSE for most returning uses** (the CSCSE service hall offers "学历认证" credential authentication under its return-to-China services; https://www.cscse.edu.cn, read 2026-10-01) [data]. Reports of the Centre's policy say degrees earned by online study after the end of zero-COVID would not be recognised, except for Ukraine and Russia (summary of CSCSE notice hosted by the Australian Department of Education, https://www.education.gov.au/ko/node/12097, not opened; search summary only) [practitioner consensus, secondary]. Pre-experience programmes are full-time on campus, so this matters mainly for part-time, hybrid or "online first term" formats. Check any programme that lets you study partly from China.
- **Agents.** No Chinese-specific UK or EU regulator warning on agents was found; see H-c.

## 4. Nigeria

### 4.1 Degree structure and grade conversion

- **Nigerian degrees follow the UK honours labels, so the question is the institution and the CGPA scale.** LSE requires "a bachelor's degree from a highly regarded institution with an overall grade of 2:1 or a GPA of 4.0/5, or 3.3/4"; it welcomed 25 graduate students from Nigeria in 2025 (LSE Nigeria page, 2026, https://www.lse.ac.uk/study-at-lse/international-students/nigeria) [data]. Imperial Business School asks for "First or Upper Second Class honours from a recognised university" (Imperial Business School international entry requirements, 2026) [data]. A 3.5/5 CGPA is a 2:1 on many Nigerian scales but is below LSE's 4.0/5 reading, so convert with your university's own published class boundaries and compare with the school's wording, not with the Nigerian label.
- Nigeria sent 112,416 internationally mobile students abroad in 2022, the eighth-largest origin worldwide (UNESCO data as reported in DAAD/DZHW, Wissenschaft weltoffen kompakt 2025, table of origin countries) [data; 2022, possibly stale].

### 4.2 Tests, language and the Chevening trap

- Nigerian applicants usually need an English test unless the school waives it for English-medium degrees; no school page read here gave a Nigeria-specific rule, so see getting-in/admissions.md.
- **Chevening is not open to most pre-experience applicants.** Chevening requires "at least two years' work experience, acquired after completing your undergraduate degree, equivalent to 2,800 hours", and a commitment to "returning to your home country … for at least two years after your scholarship ends" (Chevening eligibility criteria, https://www.chevening.org/scholarships/who-can-apply/eligibility/, read 2026-10-01) [data]. A 22-year-old finishing a bachelor's cannot qualify, which is why a Nigerian student aiming at a pre-experience MiM or MSc finance straight from university has few funded UK routes. Press reports counted 39 Chevening scholars and one fellow from Nigeria for 2025, from several thousand applicants (Nairametrics and ICIR, 2025, not opened beyond snippets) [anecdotal / secondary]. The Gemini lead on PTDF (Nigeria's oil-sector scholarship fund) could not be verified in this session.

### 4.3 Foreign exchange: the rule changed on 1 June 2026

- The Central Bank of Nigeria's Foreign Exchange Manual, 4th edition, effective 1 June 2026, sets tuition remittances for undergraduate and postgraduate study at a maximum of USD 25,000 per semester (previously USD 15,000), through authorised dealer banks. Maintenance billed separately is capped at USD 5,000 per quarter and sent directly to the student. Required documents: evidence of admission, fee schedule, passport biodata page, student ID for returning students and the first-degree certificate for postgraduates (Nairametrics, 4 June 2026, https://nairametrics.com/2026/06/04/cbn-raises-tuition-fee-remittance-limit-to-25000-for-nigerians-studying-abroad/, reporting the CBN manual; we did not open the CBN manual itself) [practitioner consensus, secondary]. The report does not say which exchange-rate window applies.
- The Gemini lead gave the same USD 25,000 / USD 5,000 figures and was right on those numbers. Its other claims (a "60 to 120 day" Form A backlog, the trade-system URL) are unverified.
- **Naira path.** NGN per USD (official, annual average): 192 in 2015, 307 in 2019, 646 in 2023, 1,479 in 2024, 1,518 in 2025. NGN per GBP: 294 in 2015, 392 in 2019, 1,999 in 2025: a fixed sterling price cost 6.8 times as many naira in 2025 as in 2015 and 5.1 times as many as in 2019 (World Bank PA.NUS.FCRF, updated 13 July 2026, author calculation; section 9) [data, author calculation].

### 4.4 UK visa outcomes: the most volatile origin

Master's grants fell from 51,634 (2022) to 15,321 (2024) and rebounded to 25,239 (2025). Refusals then rose from 4.0% in Q3 2025 to 17.0%, 22.6% and 24.9% in the following three quarters; the year to June 2026 is 11.6% (section 8). A Nigerian applicant who commits to a UK one-year MSc in 2027 is exposed to a visa decision risk that the school's offer does not remove. The visas file covers the Graduate route and dependants rules; the cause of the 2022-24 fall (the ban on dependants for taught postgraduates from January 2024) is documented there and was not re-researched.

Continental options for Nigerians are growing from a small base: Campus France's 2025 key figures, as summarised in search results, put Nigerian students in France at 1,181 in 2023-24, up 81% in five years (unverified, snippet only) [anecdotal].

### 4.5 Return outcomes and pitfalls

- **We found no official Nigerian graduate pay statistics suitable for a pre-experience master's.** LBS's MiM 2025 report shows 5% of the class in Africa/Middle East jobs at a mean base of £48,360 (small n, mixed region) [employer-stated]. Treat it as non-informative for Nigeria.
- **Naira risk cuts both ways.** A naira-earning graduate repaying a sterling or dollar loan faces the 6.8-fold depreciation above. A sterling-earning graduate who sends money home gains from it.
- **Agents.** No Nigeria-specific UK regulator warning on agents was found. UKCISA's scam page (3 February 2026) warns of impersonation calls (see section 2.6). See H-c.

## 5. Turkey

### 5.1 Degree structure and grade conversion

- **Turkish bachelor's (lisans) degrees are four years.** LSE asks for "a minimum cumulative GPA of 3.3/4" for a 2:1 equivalent and "3.6/4" for first-class; it welcomed 77 graduate students from Turkey in 2025 (LSE Turkey page, 2026, https://www.lse.ac.uk/study-at-lse/international-students/turkey) [data]. Imperial Business School sets "a minimum overall GPA of 3/4 to 3.25/4 depending on the university's ranking" (Imperial, 2026) [data]. So Imperial's tiered threshold can be lower than LSE's flat one: a 3.2/4 from a well-ranked Turkish university may clear Imperial but not LSE.
- Germany hosted 18,084 Turkish students in winter semester 2023/24, the third-largest origin group (DAAD/DZHW 2025) [data].

### 5.2 Tests, language and funding

- Tests and language follow the school rules (getting-in/admissions.md). We found no Turkey-specific waiver on an official page.
- **YLSY, the Ministry of National Education's overseas postgraduate scholarship, is aimed at staff for universities and public bodies.** Secondary descriptions say it supplies "the expert personnel needs of universities and public institutions", with a historical quota of 358 (200 for universities, 158 for public bodies) in one year (search summaries of ÖSYM/MEB documents, not opened; https://www.osym.gov.tr/Eklenti/3663,2010ylsyklvzpdf.pdf is a 2010 ÖSYM guide) [anecdotal, possibly stale]. It is not a route for a self-chosen private-sector business master's, and it carries a service obligation. The Gemini lead names Tübitak and Jean Monnet; neither was verified here.
- **Lira path.** TRY per GBP: 4.16 in 2015, 7.24 in 2019, 20.4 in 2022, 51.95 in 2025. A fixed sterling price cost 12.5 times as many lira in 2025 as in 2015, and 7.2 times as many as in 2019 (World Bank PA.NUS.FCRF, updated 13 July 2026, author calculation; section 9) [data, author calculation]. Any lira-denominated loan or saving is, over this window, a bet against a 12-fold depreciation. The Gemini lead's suggestion to hold euro or dollar deposits before departure follows from that arithmetic, but the lead's "over 80% loss" and loan-rate figures were not checked.

### 5.3 UK visa outcomes

Turkey is a small, low-risk origin: master's grants were 1,826 in 2025 (2023 peak 1,942), and year-to-June-2026 refusals were 1.3% (73 refused against 5,763 issued), with a spike of 6.5% in Q4 2025 (section 8) [data, author calculation].

### 5.4 Return outcomes and recognition

- **YÖK equivalence (denklik) matters for the public sector and regulated professions, not for most private employers.** The Council of Higher Education amended its regulation on recognition of foreign diplomas on 15 March 2024 (Official Gazette). Graduates of institutions ranked in the top 400 in at least three of the rankings YÖK accepts can obtain direct equivalence, and are exempt from the language-document rule (CNN Turk and Cumhuriyet reporting of the 2024 rule, https://www.cnnturk.com/turkiye/son-dakika-yokten-denklik-karari-2101400, read 2026-10-01; text of the regulation not opened) [practitioner consensus, secondary]. A Gemini lead adds a thesis-translation requirement for outside-top-400 degrees; unverified. We found no official page confirming that master's degrees from top-400 schools are also covered without extra review, so check YÖK's own portal for a master's.
- No official returnee pay data was found. LBS lists Turkey among 31 job locations for its MiM 2025 class but gives no Turkey salary (LBS MiM report 2025) [employer-stated].

### 5.5 Pitfalls

Currency, not paperwork, is the main Turkish risk (section 9). The 2024 YÖK change also rewards schools that rank in global top-400 tables, which favours LSE, Imperial and similar over mid-ranked programmes if public-sector work is a fallback.

## 6. United Kingdom (as a non-EU origin)

For an EU school, a UK passport holder is a third-country national since 1 January 2021. Everything below is about a British applicant going to the continent (or the US/Switzerland), not about EU students going to the UK (see places/visas-and-work-rights.md and money/costs-and-funding.md).

- **Degrees and grades.** A three-year UK honours degree is a full first degree: HEC accepts "at least a three-year bachelor's degree" (getting-in/admissions.md, from HEC 2026). Schools read UK classes directly (2:1 and first); the conversion burden that Italian, Indian or Chinese applicants carry does not apply. We did not research each EU school's UK-specific page.
- **Visas for a stay over 90 days are needed almost everywhere, with one German exception.** For Germany, the German Embassy in London says British citizens "may apply to the local immigration office (Ausländerbehörde) for their residence permit after arrival in Germany and without having obtained a visa prior to travelling", within 90 days, and "may only take up employment once you have been issued a residence permit explicitly authorising the employment"; applying for a visa before travel permits work "from the first day of visa validity" (German Embassy London, https://uk.diplo.de/uk-en/university-studies/2449178, read 2026-10-01) [data]. For France, a long-stay visa and the Études en France procedure apply (Campus France UK summary, https://campusfrance.org/en/node/303688, search summary, not opened) [practitioner consensus]. Switzerland has a separate quota for UK nationals of 3,500 (2,100 B and 1,400 L), only 17% used by September 2025 (Vischer, quoted in places/visas-and-work-rights.md) [practitioner consensus]. So Switzerland is the one case where a UK passport is plausibly better than an Indian or Nigerian one.
- **Fees.** UK nationals now pay non-EU fees at public universities. France's public-university fee for non-EU students was EUR 3,879 for a master's in 2024/25 against EUR 250 for EU students (Ministry of Higher Education, https://www.etudiant.gouv.fr/en/tuition-fees-france-1489, 2024/25, may be stale; the page does not name UK nationals) [data]. UK-specific classification is stated by Campus France ("differentiated tuition fees ... like students from other non-EU countries", search summary) [practitioner consensus]. Business schools with one price for all nationalities (HEC, ESSEC, Bocconi, IE and similar) are unaffected. For the Netherlands, a Dutch-based commercial site dated 20 October 2020 says UK nationals pay as other non-EU students (studyinholland.co.uk, owned by A Star Future Ltd, not a government site) [anecdotal]; check the school's fee page for the current category.
- **Funding.** The UK Postgraduate Master's Loan (up to GBP 13,206 from August 2026) is for study in the UK, so it does not help a British student at HEC or Bocconi (see money/costs-and-funding.md). Bocconi's partner-bank loans are open to Italian, EU and UK citizens (Bocconi 2026-27 loans page, via money/costs-and-funding.md) [data]. The UK's Turing Scheme does not fund full degrees abroad; that Gemini lead was not verified and is not used.
- **Work rights after the master's.** None of the continental job-search permits is automatic for a UK passport. The same post-study routes as for other non-EU graduates apply (Germany up to 18 months, Netherlands orientation year, France one year; see places/visas-and-work-rights.md).
- **UK demand for UK degrees from outside.** UK-origin applicants face no visa risk for the UK, which is why LSE, Imperial and LBS accept them on UK-class grades. For a British student, a UK one-year MSc is the lowest-friction option, which reverses the visa-fragility argument made for H-b below.

## 7. United States (as a non-EU origin)

- **Degrees and grades.** LSE asks US applicants for "a GPA of 3.3 on a 4 point scale" for a 2:1 equivalent and "3.7/4" for first-class, and received 852 graduate students from the US in 2025 (LSE United States page, 2026, https://www.lse.ac.uk/study-at-lse/international-students/united-states-of-america) [data]. Imperial Business School ties the requirement to the university's selectivity ranking: "3/4 to 3.6/4 depending on the institution" (Imperial, 2026) [data]. Four-year US bachelor's degrees are not an issue for European schools.
- **Federal loans changed on 1 July 2026.** Federal Direct Loans can be used at Title IV-eligible foreign schools, but the school must be on the Department of Education's participating list, the programme must be at least a year long, and it cannot be delivered by distance education in whole or in part; students are not eligible for Pell or other grants (FSA Partner Connect, foreign school FAQ for students, https://fsapartners.ed.gov/knowledge-center/faqs/foreign-school-frequently-asked-questions-students, read 2026-10-01) [data]. For new graduate borrowers from 1 July 2026, Grad PLUS is eliminated and the annual Unsubsidised limit is USD 20,500 with a USD 100,000 aggregate and a USD 257,500 lifetime cap; students already enrolled in a programme with a loan before 1 July 2026 keep an interim exception (FSA Frequently Asked Questions, Loan Limits, 20 May 2026, https://fsapartners.ed.gov/sites/default/files/2026-05/FrequentlyAskedQuestionsLoanLimits.pdf) [data]. For a one-year LBS or LSE programme costing more than USD 60,000 all-in, federal loans now cover roughly one third. The Gemini lead said Grad PLUS covers "up to 100%" of cost of attendance; that is outdated for anyone starting after 1 July 2026 (see Corrections).
- **Which schools are eligible?** The FSA page says a list exists, searchable by country; we did not search it school by school. Bocconi says it participates in US Direct Loans (money/costs-and-funding.md). Treat any other school as unconfirmed until you see it on the list.
- **UK visa outcomes.** Master's-level grants to US nationals reached 7,597 in 2025 (up 14% on 2022) and refusals were 0.3% in the year to June 2026 (section 8) [data, author calculation]. Americans are the least visa-risky nationality in the Home Office table.
- **Continental stay rights.** US citizens follow the standard third-country rules (places/visas-and-work-rights.md). Germany lets some nationalities apply for a residence permit after entry without a prior visa (the §41 AufenthV provision); we verified it for UK nationals only (German Embassy London page above), so check the US list on the German mission's page.
- **Tax and loan-repayment claims are unverified.** The Gemini lead says the Foreign Earned Income Exclusion lets an expatriate report zero adjusted gross income and thereby owe nothing under income-driven repayment. IDR plans (SAVE in particular) were in legal and legislative flux in 2025-26, and we did not verify the current plan rules; do not rely on this.
- **Return outcomes.** LBS places only 4% of its MiM class from North America and ~1% of jobs in North America (LBS MiM report 2025) [employer-stated]; US-origin students who want US jobs should read places/beyond-europe.md.

### 8.3 The 2027 squeeze on post-study time

The Graduate visa falls from 24 to 18 months for applications from 1 January 2027, and the sponsored-work salary floors are £41,700 / £33,400 for new entrants (see places/visas-and-work-rights.md; not re-researched). The proposed international student levy is £925 per student per year from 1 August 2028, with an allowance of 220 students per provider (ICEF/Wonkhe reporting of the DfE technical consultation outcome, 2026; https://wonkhe.com/wonk-corner/international-fee-levy-confirmed/) [practitioner consensus, secondary]. It falls on providers and may be passed on in fees; none of the schools on the Admetia list has said how.

## 9. Currency depreciation worked example (author calculation)

**Question.** If the sterling price of a UK master's stayed fixed, how much more would it have cost in home currency over time?

**Method.** Official exchange rate, local currency units per US dollar, period average, from the World Bank indicator PA.NUS.FCRF (World Bank Data API, series updated 13 July 2026, https://api.worldbank.org/v2/country/IND/indicator/PA.NUS.FCRF, and the same indicator for TUR, NGA, CHN, BRA, GBR; data to 2025) [data]. The home-currency price of GBP is the home rate per USD divided by the GBP rate per USD. We applied it to a fixed price of GBP 41,000, the LSE MSc Management (12-month) fee for 2025/26 (LSE Table of Fees 2025/26, October 2025, https://info.lse.ac.uk/staff/divisions/Planning-Division/Assets/Documents/Table-of-Fees-2025-26-Oct25.pdf) [data]. Using one fixed sterling price isolates the exchange-rate effect. It ignores home inflation, wage growth, fee rises and the cost of living.

| Home currency | per GBP, 2015 | 2019 | 2022 | 2025 | GBP 41,000 in 2015 | in 2025 | Multiple 2015 to 2025 | Compound rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Indian rupee | 98.0 | 89.9 | 96.9 | 114.8 | INR 4.02m | INR 4.71m | 1.17x | 1.6% a year |
| Chinese yuan | 9.51 | 8.82 | 8.30 | 9.47 | CNY 390,000 | CNY 388,000 | 1.00x | 0.0% |
| Brazilian real | 5.08 | 5.03 | 6.37 | 7.36 | BRL 208,000 | BRL 302,000 | 1.45x | 3.8% |
| Nigerian naira | 294 | 392 | 525 | 1,999 | NGN 12.1m | NGN 82.0m | 6.8x | 21.1% |
| Turkish lira | 4.16 | 7.24 | 20.4 | 51.95 | TRY 170,000 | TRY 2.13m | 12.5x | 28.7% |

(World Bank annual averages; author calculation, rounded.) Annual averages blur the steps: Nigeria's average rate went from 646 per USD in 2023 to 1,479 in 2024, so most of the naira move happened in a single year.

**Reading.**
- For the naira and the lira the fixed-price cost multiplied 6.8 and 12.5 times. A fee increase of LSE's size (GBP 41,000 to GBP 42,900 between 2025/26 and 2026/27, +4.6%, LSE Table of Fees 2025/26 and 2026/27 as cited in money/costs-and-funding.md) [data] cannot compete with that. For these two origins, depreciation is the dominant driver of cost, not the tuition level.
- For India the sterling price effect is 17% over ten years and 28% from 2019, comparable with plausible cumulative fee rises. We could not retrieve a verified LSE fee for 2015 or 2019 (older fee tables returned 404), so we cannot split the Indian rupee-cost change between fees and exchange rate. H-a is therefore not supported for India on this evidence.
- For China the exchange rate contributed nothing over ten years; for Brazil it contributed 45%.
- Sterling itself weakened against the dollar over the period (GBP 0.655 to GBP 0.759 per USD, i.e. USD 1.53 to USD 1.32 per pound), which cushioned dollar-linked costs; the table is per pound.
- Not tested: wage-side effects. A student whose family income is in the depreciating currency has lost purchasing power; a graduate who earns in sterling or euros has not. The exposure that matters is the currency of the loan versus the currency of the future salary (money/costs-and-funding.md makes the same point for loans).

## Hypotheses tested

**H-a (assigned). "Home-currency depreciation, not tuition level, is the main driver of study-abroad affordability for Indian, Turkish and Nigerian students."**
- Verdict: **supported for Nigeria and Turkey; not supported for India.**
- Holds when: the household's income and savings are in the depreciating currency, and the fee is quoted in a hard currency. The fixed-price GBP 41,000 programme cost 6.8 times as many naira and 12.5 times as many lira in 2025 as in 2015 (section 9) [data, author calculation].
- Fails when: the currency is stable against sterling (China 1.00x, India 1.17x), where a fee rise or a lender's rate matters as much. For India we could not split fees from FX, because no verified 2015 or 2019 LSE fee was retrievable.
- Also fails when the family has foreign-currency savings (diaspora income, dollar deposits); the table assumes none.
- Evidence: World Bank PA.NUS.FCRF, updated 13 July 2026 [data]; LSE Table of Fees 2025/26 [data].

**H-b (assigned). "A one-year UK MSc is the most visa-fragile option after 2027 for these origins."**
- Verdict: **partly supported.**
- Supported by: the Graduate visa cut from 24 to 18 months from 1 January 2027 and the sponsored-work floors of GBP 33,400 / 41,700 (places/visas-and-work-rights.md; not re-researched), and by the refusal split: Nigerians 17-25% per quarter and Pakistanis 34-41% since Q4 2025, Indians 7-9% (section 8.2) [data, author calculation].
- Not supported by: the UK's 18-month window is still longer than the Dutch and French 12-month permits (places/visas-and-work-rights.md). For Chinese, American, Turkish and Gulf nationals refusal rates are 0.1-1.5%. The fragility is nationality-specific, not route-specific.
- Fails when: you choose a continental programme with the same nationality problem (for example a Nigerian applicant to a school that also needs a long-stay visa from a consulate with long queues; we did not research consular wait times).
- Caveat: the Home Office tables give no refusal split by sponsor or course level, so we cannot say whether LSE or Imperial applicants face the all-sponsor average.

**H-c (assigned). "Agent and consultancy intermediaries add cost without improving admission odds."**
- Verdict: **insufficient evidence.** We found no regulator study comparing agent-assisted and direct applicants. The one UK regulator-adjacent page we read, UKCISA's scams page (updated 3 February 2026), warns about impostors posing as the Home Office, an agent or UKCISA demanding money, not about agent quality (https://www.ukcisa.org.uk/student-advice/life-in-the-uk/frauds-and-scams/) [data]. ICEF's reading is that schools are tightening because refusal rates above 5% threaten their sponsor licences (ICEF Monitor, 27 May 2026) [practitioner consensus]; that is a reason for stricter CAS issuance, not evidence about agents. No UK or EU authority statement that agents are fraudulent was found, and none is asserted here.

**H-d (own). "The same transcript clears one school and fails another, so grade conversion should be checked school by school, not by country."**
- Verdict: **supported.**
- Evidence: Indian 7.6/10 clears LSE's 2:1 equivalent but Imperial's mainstream bar is 8.0/10 or 70% (7.5/10 at elite institutions). Chinese 86% clears LSE only if the university is "highly regarded" (85%) and Imperial only if a Project 211 university. Turkish GPA 3.2/4 may clear Imperial (3-3.25 by ranking) but not LSE (3.3) (LSE and Imperial pages, 2026) [data].
- Fails when: you are far above both thresholds, or applying to schools that read transcripts holistically (no official evidence either way).

**H-e (own). "Funded UK routes are largely closed to pre-experience applicants from developing-country origins."**
- Verdict: **supported for Chevening; insufficient for school-level awards.** Chevening requires two years (2,800 hours) of post-bachelor's work (Chevening eligibility, read 2026-10-01) [data]. PM-Vidyalaxmi cannot be used abroad (PIB, 29 July 2026) [data]. School scholarships exist (money/costs-and-funding.md) but we did not compute success rates by nationality.

**H-f (own). "Foreign-exchange rules in India and China are paperwork, not bars; Nigeria's is a real cap."**
- Verdict: **partly supported.** India: USD 250,000 a year under the LRS with studies abroad a listed purpose (RBI FAQ) [data]. China: USD 50,000 is a convenience quota and larger amounts need supporting documents (SAFE reply, 2024; tuition specifics from press) [data, secondary]. Nigeria: tuition is capped at USD 25,000 per semester and maintenance at USD 5,000 per quarter from 1 June 2026 (Nairametrics reporting the CBN manual) [practitioner consensus, secondary]. A GBP 41,000 fee is about USD 54,000 at the 2025 average rate (author calculation), and how a bank applies a "per semester" cap to a 12-month programme billed in one invoice is not documented in what we read.
- Fails when: banks apply the rules stricter than the text; that is anecdotal and not tested.

**H-g (own). "US federal loans are no longer a realistic way for an American to fund a one-year European master's."**
- Verdict: **partly supported.** From 1 July 2026 new graduate borrowers lose Grad PLUS and face USD 20,500 a year, so a USD 60,000+ programme is about one-third covered, and only at schools on the Title IV list (FSA FAQ, 20 May 2026; FSA foreign-school FAQ) [data]. Fails for students already enrolled in a graduate programme with a loan before 1 July 2026 (interim exception).

## Common mistakes and myths

- **"A three-year Indian degree can't get into a UK master's."** LSE and Imperial both accept three-year Indian bachelor's degrees (LSE India page; Imperial "three to four-year") [data]. The risk is the grade, not the length. The doubt belongs to some US programmes (see getting-in/admissions.md).
- **"Chinese students need 85% everywhere."** LSE's 85% applies only to a "highly regarded institution"; everyone else needs 90% (LSE China page) [data].
- **"The Chinese USD 50,000 limit is the most you can send."** It is a convenience quota; larger sums need documents (SAFE reply, 2024) [data].
- **"Indian collateral-free education loans cover a UK master's."** PM-Vidyalaxmi is for 1,425 designated Indian institutions only (PIB, 29 July 2026) [data].
- **"Chevening is for fresh graduates."** It needs two years' work experience (Chevening) [data].
- **"UK schools refuse by school quality."** The Home Office data show refusals concentrated by nationality: 0.1% for China, 41% for Pakistan in Q4 2025 (section 8.2) [data, author calculation].
- **"The US government will lend whatever the school costs."** Not for new graduate borrowers after 1 July 2026 (FSA, 20 May 2026) [data].
- **"A UK degree makes me a home student in the EU."** UK nationals are third-country nationals for visas and, at public universities, fees (German Embassy London; Campus France) [data / practitioner consensus].
- **"Check one source for grade conversion."** Schools publish different thresholds for the same country (H-d).

## Decision rules

1. If you are Nigerian or Pakistani and your UK offer depends on a Student visa, treat the visa as a separate risk (Nigeria: one in five to one in four decisions refused in the last three quarters; Pakistan: one in three), and apply to at least one continental programme in parallel (section 8.2 refusal rates; Home Office, author calculation).
2. If you are Indian, plan for a refusal risk of roughly 7-9% in the latest three quarters, not the 2% of mid-2025, and do not cut the visa timeline short (section 8.2).
3. If you are Chinese, American, Turkish or Saudi, visa refusal risk is under about 3% in the data; choose on programme fit, not visa fear.
4. If your home currency has depreciated more than 5x against sterling in ten years (naira, lira), prefer a loan or savings in a hard currency and a programme with a short, front-loaded cost; avoid home-currency debt (section 9, author calculation).
5. If your transcript is within one band of a threshold, check LSE and Imperial separately: Indian 7.6/10 clears LSE but not Imperial's mainstream 8.0/10; a Chinese 86% needs a Project 211 or "highly regarded" university; a Turkish 3.2/4 depends on university ranking at Imperial (H-d).
6. If you are Indian and want state-supported loans, remember PM-Vidyalaxmi covers Indian institutions only; for a foreign master's compare bank loans (collateral usually needed above INR 7.5 lakh, unverified) with family funding, and ask for the loan to come from a notified institution to get nil TCS (RBI FAQ; Business Standard, Feb 2025).
7. If you are Chinese and want to send more than USD 50,000, assemble the passport, visa, admission letter and tuition invoice before the bank visit (SAFE principle; press detail).
8. If you are Nigerian, budget tuition under the CBN's USD 25,000 per semester and maintenance under USD 5,000 per quarter, and start Form A at least several months before the fee deadline (timing is unverified; Nairametrics 4 June 2026).
9. If you have under two years of work experience, do not plan on Chevening (Chevening eligibility).
10. If you are American and need loans abroad, check the school against the FSA participating list and assume USD 20,500 a year for new borrowers starting after 1 July 2026 (FSA FAQ, 20 May 2026).
11. If you are British and want the lowest visa friction, a UK programme is easiest; for Germany use the §41 route (apply for a permit after arrival) only if you accept you cannot work until it is issued, otherwise apply for a visa before travel (German Embassy London).
12. If you plan to return to Turkey for the public sector, pick a school likely to be top-400 in at least three YÖK-accepted rankings, and check master's rules on YÖK's portal (YÖK 2024 reporting).
13. If you plan to return to China and the programme is hybrid or online in any part, confirm CSCSE recognition before enrolling (CSCSE service page; policy via secondary summary).

## Corrections to the Gemini leads

1. **China "SAFE USD 50,000 annual cap."** Wrong framing. SAFE describes USD 50,000 as an annual convenience quota ("small amounts convenient, large amounts regulated"), with larger sums allowed on documents (SAFE reply, 2024) [data].
2. **US "Direct Unsubsidised USD 20,500 plus Grad PLUS up to cost of attendance."** Outdated for new graduate borrowers after 1 July 2026: Grad PLUS is eliminated, the aggregate is USD 100,000 (FSA FAQ, 20 May 2026) [data].
3. **UK dependants ban effects: "Nigeria over 70% down, India and China under 15%."** Nigeria's master's grants fell 70% from 2022 to 2024 (51,634 to 15,321), but India's fell 38% over the same period (115,445 to 71,766) and 26% from 2023 to 2024. China fell 5% from 2023 to 2024 (Home Office, author calculation) [data]. So the Nigerian figure is about right, the Indian "under 15%" is wrong, and a single cause is not established by these tables.
4. **"Imperial and UCL maintain internal bands that are not public."** For Imperial Business School this is wrong: it publishes an 85% Project 211 requirement for China, India thresholds of 70% / 8.0 (7.5 elite) and Turkey / US tiered GPAs (Imperial page, 2026) [data]. UCL was not checked.
5. **Manchester "80% for 985/211".** Not checked; LSE's published China rule is 85/90% (2:1) and 88/93% (first) [data]. The lead's tiering structure is directionally plausible, the numbers are unverified.
6. **Turkish lira ">80% loss against EUR 2022-2026."** World Bank annual averages show TRY per EUR 17.4 in 2022 and 44.6 in 2025, a 61% loss of euro value; over 2015-2025 the loss is 93% (author calculation). The ">80% since 2022" figure may reflect 2026 rates we did not read.
7. **Nigeria CBN USD 25,000 per semester and USD 5,000 per quarter, effective 1 June 2026.** Confirmed via press reports of the CBN FX Manual (Nairametrics, 4 June 2026). The "60-120 day backlog" and URL claims are unverified.

## Numbers to treat with care

- **All Home Office figures in section 8 are author aggregations** of quarterly dataset rows. Course-level data from Q1 2025 are provisional; refusal rates exclude withdrawn and lapsed cases and cover all course levels, not just master's. Q1 figures are seasonal (low volumes), so quarter-to-quarter rates for China, Turkey, Brazil and the US are noisy.
- **Refusal "per quarter" rates for Nigeria and Pakistan** are on thousands of cases but depend on how many applicants were screened out by sponsors before applying (issuance of CAS), which is not observed.
- **World Bank annual averages** hide intra-year moves; the 2023-24 naira step is understated. They are official period averages, not the rate a bank gave you.
- **LBS salary and location data** are self-reported by graduates and based on small subsamples for Asia and Africa/Middle East; 15% South Asia is a class share, not a placement rate.
- **CBN, YÖK, TCS and CSCSE rules** come from press or secondary summaries; the primary texts were not read. Treat them as "likely, confirm with your bank/ministry".
- **Imperial's page** is a general business-school page; individual programmes can ask for more.
- **Germany 58,800 Indians (2024/25)** is from a search snippet.
- **PM-Vidyalaxmi** facts are from a Ministry factsheet dated 29 July 2026 and should be current; the IBA loan caps are not verified.
