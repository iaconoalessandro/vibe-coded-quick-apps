# Costs and funding

## Bottom line

1. **For an EU citizen, tuition spans roughly two orders of magnitude for programmes in the same league.** CBS and SSE charge EU students nothing, Mannheim about €194 a semester, RSM €2,771 for the year, while HEC's MiM is €58,970 and LBS's MiM £52,950 (official fee pages, 2026–27 and 2027–28) [data]. Once living costs are added, the gap in total cost shrinks to roughly 2–4×, because rent and food do not get cheaper at a free school.
2. **UK business MSc fees are flat: EU students pay exactly what everyone else pays.** LSE, Imperial, Oxford MFE and Cambridge MFin all list one fee for home and overseas students (LSE Table of Fees 2026/27; Imperial 2026-27 fee table) [data]. The post-2021 loss of "home fee" status therefore matters little for these programmes. What EU students now pay on top is the visa (£558) and the Immigration Health Surcharge (£776 a year) (GOV.UK, 2026) [data].
3. **Bocconi is mid-priced and has the strongest aid system for Italians.** The MSc ordinary fee is €18,550 for year one (2026-27). Italian and Bocconi applicants can get need-based waivers of 20%, 60% or 100% (Bocconi4Access), plus the regional ISU/DSU grant, and top applicants of any nationality get full-tuition merit awards without a separate application (Bocconi fees and funding pages, 2026) [data].
4. **Non-EU students face the sharpest jumps at public schools, not private ones.** CBS goes from €0 to €16,000 a year, SSE from €0 to SEK 360,000 per programme, RSM from €2,771 to €28,000, TUM from €0 to €4,000–6,000 a semester (official pages, 2026) [data]. French private schools add only €2,500–7,800 in total for non-EU students (HEC, ESCP, EDHEC, ESSEC fee pages) [data].
5. **Merit money is front-loaded to early rounds.** Bocconi says the majority of merit awards go in the first rounds; Oxford's MFE only considers Stage 1 and 2 applicants for scholarships; ESSEC files Eiffel nominations only for first-round applicants (school pages, 2025–26) [data]. Applying late costs money as well as admission odds.
6. **Scholarship "negotiation" has no official support in what we could read.** Bocconi calls its committee's decisions on amounts "final and unquestionable", and, per money/scholarships.md (2026-10-01), school pages restrict combining awards from the *same school*, not external scholarships, whose own funders set the rules (the earlier wording "Esade and Bocconi forbid stacking" was overstated) [data]. Round 3e (2 Oct 2026) added the schools' own wording: Bocconi, "The decision is final and cannot be appealed"; HEC Paris MiM, "All scholarship results are final and cannot be reconsidered"; IE, the Financial Aid Committee "cannot revise your application unless you are able to demonstrate significant and unexpected changes to your financial situation" (school pages, read 2 Oct 2026) [data]. No school page I read invites matching a competing offer, and no survey or school publishes how often a request succeeds. Evidence that private schools match competing offers exists only as anecdote and is listed under Claims to verify.
7. **Loan access depends on passport more than on school, and the fallback is expensive.** Bocconi's bank loans are open to Italian, EU and UK citizens only; France's state-guaranteed student loan (up to €15,000 via EDHEC's description) is for French and EEA citizens; the UK Master's Loan (£13,206 for courses from August 2026) is open to EU nationals only with settled or pre-settled status (school pages; GOV.UK, 2026) [data]. MPOWER lends only for US and Canadian schools. Prodigy's own representative example is 13.26% APR on USD 40,000, with USD 114,661 repaid in total over 15 years (Prodigy and MPOWER sites, 2026) [data].
8. **Deposits are large, early and non-refundable.** LBS asks for £8,000 within about six weeks of an offer, ESSEC €6,000 and HEC €5,000 within two weeks, Imperial £6,500 (school pages, 2026) [data]. A student holding two offers can lose €5–9k by keeping the second option open.
9. **Living costs in London are about 1.5–2× those in Lyon, Madrid or Turin.** ESCP's own estimates run £1,450–2,300 a month in London against €900–1,450 in Madrid and Turin and €1,000–1,700 in Berlin (ESCP MiM page, 2026) [data]. For a two-year programme, city choice can move the total by €15–25k.

What this does to the cost examples: Milan's average room is about €225 a month above Turin's (€704 against €479), so ESCP's Turin total of €900 to €1,450 is a floor for Milan, and a Milan budget of roughly €1,125 to €1,675 a month (Turin range plus the rent gap, author calculation, food and transport not repriced) is a better planning range. Germany's national average (€876 a month, DAAD) understates Munich and Frankfurt, where a model flat alone costs €730 to €840.

**UK deposits and advance rent.** For years many agents asked international students without a UK guarantor to pay six or twelve months of rent up front. For private tenancies in England that is no longer lawful: from 1 May 2026 under the Renters' Rights Act a landlord cannot ask for or accept rent before the tenancy agreement is signed, and after signing can require at most one month in advance, with council fines of up to £5,000 (GOV.UK Renters' Rights Act guidance and local-authority summaries, 2026) [data]. Landlords may now ask instead for a UK guarantor or a paid guarantor service, so budget for a guarantor fee and the deposit; ask the university accommodation office whether it offers a guarantee scheme. University halls and tenancies outside England follow their own rules.

### Tax relief on the cost of the master's

- **Italy: 19% deduction on university fees (art. 15, c. 1, lett. e, TUIR).** Fees paid to Italian state universities are deductible in full at 19%. For non-state and, per the Revenue Agency's practice, foreign universities, the deductible amount is capped each year by a MUR decree by area of residence; for post-graduate courses the 2026 caps are reported as €4,100 (North), €3,100 (Centre) and €3,050 (South and islands), so the tax saving is at most about €780 a year (secondary summaries of the 2026 decree; not read at source) [practitioner consensus]. It only helps someone with Italian taxable income: a parent can claim it for a dependent child. The €741 a year quoted in an external audit corresponds to an older €3,900 cap.
- **Germany: a master's after a first degree is deductible in full as work-related expenses (Werbungskosten), and an unused loss is carried forward (Verlustvortrag).** Tuition, travel, materials and some living costs of a second degree can be declared even with no income, creating a loss that reduces tax in the first years of work in Germany; a first degree is limited to €6,000 a year as special expenses with no carry-forward (German tax-guide summaries, 4 Oct 2026) [practitioner consensus]. It needs a German tax return for each study year, usually within four to seven years, and only pays off if you later earn taxable income in Germany. No source for the size of the typical refund was read.

## 4. Negotiating scholarships

What official sources say, which is the only part we could verify:
- Bocconi's merit regulation says the committee's "decisions about numbers, amounts and recipients is final and unquestionable" (Bocconi Graduate Merit Awards 2026-27) [data]. There is no appeal route in the text.
- Bocconi awards are tied to the round and programme of admission, and are lost if you defer to another round unless a new award is granted (same source) [data]. You cannot "shop" the award to a later round.
- Esade Excellence Awards and need-based scholarships are "not compatible with other prizes or awards granted by Esade" (Esade, 2026) [data]. Bocconi revokes a merit award if a larger Bocconi benefit is accepted [data].
- Several schools decide scholarships at the moment of admission with "no additional application" (ESSEC, Bocconi, LBS) [data]. That design leaves little formal room for later negotiation.
- Round 3e (read 2 Oct 2026) [data: school policy]: Bocconi's MSc results page says the Merit Award decision "is final and cannot be appealed" (https://www.unibocconi.it/en/applying-bocconi/master-science-and-ma-programs/application-and-admissions/results-and-enrollment-ay-2026-27). HEC's MiM FAQ says "All scholarship results are final and cannot be reconsidered" and that HEC scholarships cannot be combined (https://www.hec.edu/en/master-s-programs/master-management/faq). IE's master's FAQ says the committee can revise an award only for "significant and unexpected changes" in the student's finances, with the programme start date as the deadline (https://www.ie.edu/financial-aid/masters/frequently-asked-questions/). ESCP says a scholarship "is not guaranteed" and that students not eligible for need-based awards may request a partial exemption, awarded "if possible, based on available budget" (https://campuslife.escp.eu/admitted/scholarships-mim-master-in-management/). LBS says admitted candidates are automatically considered and awards are "typically around £20,000" (https://www.london.edu/masters-degrees/financial-aid/search-for-funding/london-business-school-merit-scholarship); it has no appeal wording on that page. Esade and LUISS wording could not be read.

What we could not verify: that private schools routinely raise awards when shown a competing offer. No success-rate data exist in what I could reach: the GMAC Prospective Students Survey 2026 has no negotiation item, and Poets&Quants (Nov 2014, MBA, no school named) only says "a good number of business schools are willing to negotiate" without a statistic [anecdotal]. This is a common claim on applicant forums and in admissions-consultant content, but we have no school statement or data confirming it for pre-experience master's (it is more documented for MBAs). It sits in Claims to verify and should be presented to users as "sometimes possible, never guaranteed". A polite, evidence-based request (a written competing offer, a change in family finances) costs nothing; strategic bluffing risks nothing formally but should not be counted in a budget.

### How the Consap-guaranteed loan works, and what it really costs

The Fondo per il credito ai giovani (often called Fondo Studio) is a state guarantee, not a loan. Consap, the Ministry of Economy's company that runs it, checks your eligibility; you then take the loan from one of the banks that have signed up, on that bank's terms. The guarantee lets the bank lend without a guarantor, but it does not make the loan cheap. A worked case, from the information sheet of the Bolzano savings bank (Cassa di Risparmio di Bolzano, "Prestito Studio Spark", in force from 1 July 2026) [data]:

- **Who.** Students aged 18 to 40 with 75/100 at school or 100/110 in the previous degree. Each annual tranche needs proof of enrolment, paid fees and, after the first year, at least half the exams passed. If you miss a requirement, disbursement stops and repayment starts on the original schedule.
- **How much.** €3,000 to €50,000 for courses in Italy, up to €70,000 for courses abroad recognised by the Ministry of University (MUR), paid in annual tranches of at most €15,000 over up to four years.
- **When you repay.** After the last tranche there is a 36-month pre-amortisation period, extended by 24 months if you start a laurea magistrale within a year of the triennale; then 3 to 10 years of monthly instalments. The sheet does not say how interest accruing during the grace period is settled, so ask whether it is paid during that period, added to the debt or financed out of the loan itself.
- **Rate.** Fixed for the whole life of the loan: the 10-year IRS swap rate plus 1.00 percentage point. The bank's own example: IRS 2.55% + 1.00% = TAN 4.05%, **TAEG 4.30%** on €50,000 over 17 years in total. The rate is fixed on the day you sign, so it moves with market rates at that date.
- **Fees.** No arrangement fee and no early-repayment charge. The instalment collection fee is €2 if paid from an account at the same bank and **€10 if paid from another bank or in cash**, which over 120 instalments is €240 against €1,200 and in practice pushes you to open, and pay for, an account there. Late payment carries 14.75% (six-month Euribor plus 12 points, within the usury cap).
- **Total cost, author calculation on the bank's example.** €50,000 repaid over 10 years at 4.05% is about €507 a month and about €10,900 of interest. If interest is also paid during the three-year grace period, add about €6,100, so roughly €17,000 of interest on €50,000, before account fees.

**Owner's experience and practitioner reports [anecdotal].** Across the banks that offer the Consap guarantee, effective rates cluster around 4% to 5%; some charge per-instalment or withdrawal fees that do not show in the headline TAN; and in at least one case the interest due in the first 24 months was paid out of the loan itself, so part of the money borrowed went straight back to the bank. None of this is visible until you read each bank's information sheet.

**Checklist before signing any study loan.**
1. Compare **TAEG**, not TAN, and ask for the TAEG on your actual amount and schedule, not the brochure example.
2. Ask how grace-period interest is handled (paid monthly, capitalised, or deducted from the tranches) and how much of the money you will actually receive.
3. Ask for the instalment fee from an external account and the yearly cost of any account the bank expects you to open; add both to the total.
4. Check the per-tranche conditions (exams passed, deadlines): missing one stops the money mid-degree but not the repayment.
5. Check whether the rate is fixed or variable, the late-payment rate, and that early repayment is free (it is at Sparkasse).
6. Distinguish true merit loans with no guarantor (Intesa "per Merito", Consap-guaranteed loans) from ordinary personal loans marketed for study (BNL Futuriamo), which can require guarantors.
7. Remember the currency: these are euro loans, which is an advantage if you will earn in euros (see the currency note below).

Two non-obvious points from the loan data. First, MPOWER is irrelevant for European schools, since it lends only for US and Canadian programmes (MPOWER, 2026) [data]. Second, Prodigy's representative example implies repaying almost 2.9× the principal over 15 years, including fees and a 30-month period of USD 100 repayments (Prodigy, 2026) [data]. Paying it off faster cuts this sharply, but the example shows why a subsidised bank loan or a family loan is worth exhausting first.

Currency risk (analysis, not a sourced fact): a loan in pounds or dollars taken by a student who plans to work in the euro area converts the exchange rate into a repayment risk. On a £40,000 loan, a 10% fall in sterling against the euro is a €4–5k gain to the borrower, and a 10% rise is the same-size loss, before interest. The risk is two-sided and cannot be forecast, but it is additional to interest-rate risk. Borrowing in the currency you expect to earn in (a euro loan from Intesa or a French bank for a Paris or Milan career) removes it. A UK-bound Italian who expects a London job for several years has a natural hedge and can reasonably borrow in sterling.

**UK IHS worked through (our calculation from GOV.UK rules):** a 12-month MSc usually carries a visa a few months longer than the course. If the visa is 16 months, the IHS is one full year (£776) plus a half-year for the remaining 4 months (£388), so £1,164; add the £558 visa fee and an EU student pays about £1,700 before arriving (GOV.UK, 2026) [data for rates; visa length is an assumption to check on the CAS].

**Internships and gap years can be income, not cost.** ESCP says "most students get paid €1,200 to €2,900 per month" during internships, and markets sandwich years as a way to "help finance tuition fees" (ESCP MiM page, 2026) [data]. In France, apprenticeships (alternance) let a company pay tuition and a salary: ESSEC opens them only on the Flexible Track, and emlyon to French-speaking students in the final year (ESSEC and emlyon pages, 2026) [data]. For a French-speaking EU student this is the single biggest lever on net cost at a French school. See getting-in/recruiting-calendar.md for timing.

**Alternance, with the numbers.** An apprentice is paid a share of the French minimum wage (SMIC) set by age and year of contract. Aged 21 to 25 the legal minimum is 53% of the SMIC in year one (about €990 a month), 61% in year two (about €1,139) and 78% in year three (about €1,456); from 26 it is 100%. Pay above these floors is negotiated, so the €1,100 to €1,800 range quoted in some summaries is plausible only for later years or generous employers, not a guarantee. For contracts starting from 1 March 2025 the salary is exempt from income tax up to the annual SMIC amount (service-public.gouv.fr, apprenticeship contract page, read 4 Oct 2026) [data]. The training cost is paid by the employer and its skills operator rather than by the student, which is why the tuition can be effectively free; for contracts made after 1 July 2025 the employer must also make a small contribution to training costs, a reform whose amount was not checked. Limits: you must first find an employer, the contract runs alongside a work rhythm that may not suit every programme, and ESSEC opens it only on the Flexible Track and emlyon only to French speakers in the final year (section 1).

**Werkstudent in Germany, with the rules.** A student enrolled at a German university who works up to 20 hours a week during the lecture period is exempt from health, long-term care and unemployment insurance contributions but still pays into pension insurance; the exemption lapses if the job becomes the main activity, although the weekly cap can be exceeded in the official lecture-free periods (AOK employer guidance, 2026) [data]. Pay depends on the employer and city and no source for a typical monthly figure was read, so none is stated. Combined with Mannheim's €194 semester fee, it is the main reason a German public master's can cost little in total, but the cost still includes rent (Munich €840 for a model flat) and the job competes with study time.

## Hypotheses tested

**H1. For EU students, a top German/Nordic/Dutch public MSc is 10–30× cheaper than a top private school.**
Verdict: supported for tuition, not supported for total cost.
Holds: on tuition alone the ratio is larger than 30×: Mannheim's four semester fees (€776) against HEC (€58,970) is about 76×; RSM (€2,771) against LBS (£52,950) about 20×; CBS and SSE are free (official pages) [data].
Fails: once living costs enter, example D (≈ €24.6k) against example C (≈ €92k) is under 4×, and against Bocconi (≈ €62–74k) about 2.5–3×. "Comparable outcomes" is outside this file (see money/salaries-and-roi.md and getting-in/employer-pipelines.md).

**H2. Scholarship offers at private schools are negotiable more often than students think.**
Verdict: insufficient evidence; official sources point the other way.
Holds: possibly for schools that decide awards case by case after admission; no verified example found.
Fails: Bocconi states decisions are "final and unquestionable" and ties awards to the round; Esade and Bocconi restrict combining awards from the same school; external funders set their own rules (corrected 2026-10-01; see money/scholarships.md) [data].

**H3. Cost of living is the larger cost for two-year programmes in expensive cities.**
Verdict: partly supported.
Holds: at public or low-fee schools (Mannheim, TUM, CBS, SSE, HSG, RSM for EU students) living costs are 70–95% of the total. At Bocconi, 22 months of living costs are roughly equal to tuition.
Fails: at HEC, ESSEC Flexible and IE-type fees, tuition still exceeds living costs even in Paris (C: €59k tuition vs €33k living) [data from school estimates; shares are our calculation].

**H4. Loans in a foreign currency add material risk for students planning to return home.**
Verdict: supported as analysis; no primary data on outcomes. Prodigy's representative example is in US dollars (USD 40,000 at 13.26% APR, 15 years), so an EU borrower carries dollar risk for up to 15 years (Prodigy, 2026) [data].
Holds: whenever loan currency and expected salary currency differ. The exposure is two-sided and scales with the loan size.
Fails: when the student expects to work in the loan's currency long enough to repay (natural hedge), or borrows in euros from Italian or French banks, which are open to EU citizens (Bocconi, EDHEC pages) [data].

**H5 (own). Non-EU students pay a much smaller premium at French private schools than at northern European publics.**
Verdict: supported.
Evidence: French surcharges are €2,500 (HEC total), €3,700/yr (ESCP), €6,550 total (EDHEC) and €7,800 total (ESSEC Flexible); ESSEC Intensive differs by €100. Publics go from €0 to €16,000/yr (CBS), €0 to SEK 360,000 (SSE), €2,771 to €28,000 (RSM) (official pages, 2026) [data]. So for non-EU students, the private/public cost gap is much narrower than for EU students.

**H6 (own). Brexit made UK business MSc programmes more expensive for EU students mainly through visa and health charges, not tuition.**
Verdict: supported for the programmes checked.
Evidence: LSE, Imperial, Oxford MFE and Cambridge MFin have a single fee for all; LSE's home/overseas split survives only in non-business programmes (LSE, Imperial fee tables) [data]. The EU-specific added cost is the £558 visa plus IHS (about £1,700 on a 16-month visa) [data; our calculation].

**H7 (own). Merit money is front-loaded, so applying early is a financial decision.**
Verdict: supported.
Evidence: Bocconi allocates the majority of awards in the first rounds; Oxford MFE considers only Stage 1–2 applicants; ESSEC submits Eiffel nominations only for first-round applicants; Imperial runs round-based scholarship deadlines (school pages, 2025–26) [data].

## Common mistakes and myths

- **"As an EU student I'll pay home fees in the UK."** Business MSc fees at LSE and Imperial are identical for all, so this was never a saving here; what changed is the visa and IHS (LSE, Imperial, GOV.UK) [data].
- **"Bocconi fees depend on family income."** For 2026-27 there is one ordinary fee; income matters only if you apply for Bocconi4Access or the ISU grant (Bocconi, 2026) [data]. Students who don't apply pay full price.
- **"Switzerland is cheap because it's public, and EU students get the local rate."** At HSG EU students pay the foreign-national rate, CHF 3,557.50 a semester, and HSG recommends CHF 25–30k a year for living (HSG, 2026) [data].
- **Budgeting with the visa floor.** The UK and German proof-of-funds figures are legal minima. School estimates for London (£1,450–2,300) and Paris (€1,100–1,750) sit well above them (ESCP) [data].
- **Treating the deposit as refundable.** LBS, HEC, ESSEC, Imperial and emlyon deposits are non-refundable (school pages) [data].
- **Assuming a merit award follows you if you defer.** At Bocconi it does not, unless re-awarded (Bocconi, 2026) [data].
- **Ignoring the apprenticeship route in France.** It is often the cheapest way through a Grande École for French speakers (ESSEC, emlyon) [data], but is restricted by track and language.

## Decision rules

1. If you are an EU citizen and cost is a binding constraint, shortlist at least one of Mannheim, TUM, CBS, SSE or RSM (because tuition there is €0–2,771 against €40–60k at private schools).
2. If you are Italian and considering Bocconi, submit the Bocconi4Access and ISU applications even if you think you won't qualify (because the waivers are 20/60/100% and are not granted without an application).
3. If you want a merit award, apply in the first round the school offers (because Bocconi, Oxford MFE and ESSEC's Eiffel nominations favour or require early rounds).
4. If you hold offers from two schools with deposits, compute the deposit you would forfeit before paying the second (because LBS £8,000, ESSEC €6,000 and HEC €5,000 are non-refundable).
5. If you are EU and choosing between UK one-year MSc programmes, compare tuition directly and add about £1,700 for visa and IHS to each (because UK fees are flat and the visa/IHS charges apply to EU students too).
6. If you are non-EU and comparing a French Grande École with a Dutch or Nordic public MSc, compare total costs, not "public vs private" labels (because the non-EU fee at RSM or CBS narrows the gap to French schools a lot).
7. If you are French-speaking and admitted to ESSEC or emlyon, evaluate the apprenticeship option before borrowing (because a company can cover tuition and pay a salary).
8. If you will borrow and expect to work in the euro area, prefer a euro-denominated loan (Intesa via Bocconi, French state-guaranteed loan) over a GBP/USD loan (because it removes currency risk).
9. If you are an EU national without UK settled or pre-settled status, do not plan on the UK Master's Loan (because eligibility requires EU Settlement Scheme status).
10. If you are non-EU and planning on Bocconi, do not count on a Bocconi partner bank loan (because Bocconi states none is available to non-EU/UK citizens).
11. If you need a private loan as a non-EU student at a European school, compare Prodigy's total repayable against a family loan or ISA before signing (because Prodigy's own example repays USD 114,661 on USD 40,000, and MPOWER does not cover European schools).
12. If a two-year programme is in London or Paris, budget living costs at the school's mid-point, not the visa floor (because London mid-point ≈ £1,900/month vs the £1,529 legal floor).
13. If a private-school award is under about 25% of tuition and you have a competing offer, ask for a review politely with documents only where the school allows it (Bocconi and HEC state in writing that results are final; IE reconsiders only for significant, unexpected changes in your finances; ESCP has a need-based exception request), and budget as if the answer is no (because the only published average we found, Esade's, is 26%, and no school we read commits to matching; round 3e).
14. If you are choosing between a one-year and a two-year programme at similar fees, add 11 months of living costs to the two-year option before comparing (because living costs are €11–25k a year in the cities covered).
