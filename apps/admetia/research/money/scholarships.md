# Scholarships and funding for business master's students

## Bottom line

1. **The famous scholarships mostly do not fit an Italian pre-experience reader.** Chevening requires 2,800 hours of work after the bachelor's (about two years); the ERP business scholarship at DAAD is limited to 12 CIS and neighbouring countries; Swiss Government Excellence funds researchers and art master's only; Gates Cambridge excludes the MFin and MBA; SI, Aga Khan, Mastercard Foundation, IYT, MAECI and the NL Scholarship are for non-EU or developing-country students (official pages, 2026) [data].
2. **For Italians the real levers are three:** automatic school merit awards (Bocconi full waiver; HEC up to €15,000; ESSEC €10,000; Esade averaging about 26%), low public fees, and the regional right-to-study grants (national ceiling ISEE €28,339.88, minimum grants €2,890 to €7,171 for 2026/27) (school pages and MUR decrees, 2026) [data].
3. **Two flagship schemes are open to Italians and are worth a look:** Fulbright Italy (Italian citizens with an Italian degree, up to $50,000 for year one, deadline Monday 30 November 2026 at 23:59) and Erasmus Mundus Joint Masters (€1,400 a month plus waived tuition, nothing excludes EU nationals, but business programmes are few) (fulbright.it, read 2 Oct 2026; European Commission, 2026) [data].
4. **Return clauses are real for two schemes.** Chevening requires two years in your home country and makes you repay everything if you breach it (Chevening T&Cs, Aug 2026, clause 13, read by the lead) [data]. Its FAQ says "No, Chevening Scholars cannot apply for a Graduate route visa", and clause 13(d) of the T&Cs says "You must not apply to switch to any other visa category or extend your current visa on completion of your studies" (both re-read on chevening.org on 2 Oct 2026). A Fulbright J-1 normally carries the US two-year home-residency rule, with waivers "very rarely" granted (Berkeley International Office) [practitioner consensus]. DAAD, Eiffel, CBS, SI and school awards showed no such clause on the pages read.
5. **"Esade and Bocconi forbid stacking" is overstated.** Their texts restrict combining awards from the same school; neither page read bans external scholarships. External funders set their own rules (Chevening, MAECI IYT and Einaudi do restrict) (pages above) [data].
6. **Timing is the hidden filter.** Chevening closes 6 Oct 2026 (11:00 UTC); Fulbright on 30 Nov 2026; DAAD, Erasmus Mundus, SI, CBS and Eiffel between October and January; LSE need-based funding in January to April; Bocconi gives most merit awards in the first rounds. Applying late costs money as well as admission odds [data].
7. **Loans follow the passport.** Intesa "per Merito" through Bocconi: up to €20,000 at EURIRS plus 1.30%; the UK Master's Loan (£13,206) needs settled or pre-settled status, so it is closed to most Italians; the Erasmus+ Master Loan ended with the 2015 to 2022 scheme; Prodigy's own example is 13.26% APR in dollars [data].
8. **Expected value:** automatic awards cost nothing, so always take them into account when choosing between offers. For competitive external awards the break-even is p = application cost / award value (author calculation); with no reliable success rates published for most schemes, apply to one or two big awards only if the award is large and the material is reusable.


## 1. Government and supranational schemes

Read on official pages between 30 Sept and 1 Oct 2026 (WebFetch). Where only a university or embassy page republishing the call was readable, that is said.

### 1.1 Chevening (UK government) [data]
- **Who:** citizens of Chevening-eligible countries. Italy is in the country list on chevening.org and has its own application page open for 2027/28 (Chevening, Oct 2026, https://www.chevening.org/scholarship/italy/) [data]. The page gives no Italy-specific award count.
- **The catch for pre-experience readers:** applicants need "at least two years' work experience, acquired after completing your undergraduate degree, equivalent to 2,800 hours", and must have finished the undergraduate degree at least two years before the deadline (Chevening eligibility page, Oct 2026, https://www.chevening.org/scholarships/who-can-apply/eligibility/) [data]. Internships, part-time and voluntary work count towards the hours (work-experience page, https://www.chevening.org/scholarships/who-can-apply/work-experience/) [data]. A final-year student or a graduate of under two years is therefore not eligible this cycle. It is a scheme for people who have worked, closer to the MBA and experienced-master's reader than the MiM reader.
- **Offers:** applicants must apply to three eligible UK courses, and the eligibility page (now at https://www.chevening.org/resource-hub/guidance/eligibility/) says an unconditional offer from at least one is needed "by the deadline listed on the application timeline". The 2027/28 timeline gives 4 Aug to 6 Oct 2026 (11:00 UTC), interviews March to April 2027, results from mid-June 2027, and the unconditional offer to be uploaded by 8 July 2027 (https://www.chevening.org/scholarships/application-timeline/) [data]. The two pages are consistent: the "deadline" in the eligibility text is the offer deadline on the timeline page (re-read 2 Oct 2026).
- **Value:** a full award covers tuition, economy flights, a monthly stipend (inside or outside London), arrival and departure allowances and the visa fee for the scholar only (Chevening Terms and Conditions, Aug 2026, https://www.chevening.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Terms-Conditions-Scholarships_-August-2026-1.pdf) [data]. MBA tuition is capped at £22,000. Universities are expected to cover at least 20% of tuition.
- **Obligations:** two-year return home (section 7). **Stacking:** no other UK government funding; the FCDO "reserves the right to reduce a Chevening award or withdraw a scholarship" where scholars have "other significant awards, bursaries, prizes or earned income" (same T&Cs) [data].
- **Dependants:** not funded or visa-endorsed (same T&Cs) [data].

### 1.2 Fulbright Italy (Italian citizens to the US) [data, republished by university offices]
- **Who:** Italian citizens with an Italian degree (at least a bachelor's from an Italian institution). The "Concorso Self Placed in All Disciplines" covers Master's or PhD study in the US for 2027-28. The bachelor's (laurea triennale) is the entry requirement and "deve essere conseguita entro il 30/04/2027", so a final-year Italian student can apply; no work experience is required; admission to a US university is not needed to apply, but a list of target universities is, and the applicant must apply to US universities independently (Fulbright Italy, https://www.fulbright.it/borse-studio-cittadini-italiani/studio/fulbright-all-disciplines/, read in full 2 Oct 2026) [data]. Exclusions include dual Italian-US citizens, anyone with a US degree and anyone who lived in the US on a study or work visa for five of the last six years. The page also says the Commission looks for a "desire to give back to your home country upon returning". Earlier summary: University of Naples research office, June 2026, https://www.ricerca.unina.it/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/fulbright_27-28_studenti-e-dottor.pdf [secondary].
- **Value:** "borse di studio fino a $50,000" for the first academic year only ("finanziano esclusivamente il primo anno accademico"), 9 months (1 academic year, 2 semesters), plus EUR 1,100 towards travel between Italy and the US, health insurance funded by the US Department of State and J-1 visa sponsorship. **Deadline: Monday 30 November 2026, 23:59**; applications are made on the IIE platform (Fulbright Italy page above, read 2 Oct 2026) [data]. Timeline on the page: first-stage review December-January, interviews from early February, finalists must send proof of admission and of the bachelor's by 30 April, final selection by the Fulbright Board by May-June.
- **Reality check:** $50,000 is below the cost of most US business master's. The MIT, Princeton, Duke, WashU, Vanderbilt and Michigan programmes the calculator scores are priced above this (see money/costs-and-funding.md). Expect it to be a partial award. The grant is for one year only, so a 2-year US master's needs other money for year two. **Top-up for finalists:** the Fulbright page says the Zegna Foundation's "Ermenegildo Zegna Founder's Scholarship" offers up to EUR 65,000 according to financial need, and a Banca Sella honour loan can add up to a further EUR 65,000, for a maximum of EUR 130,000 on top of the $50,000 (Fulbright Italy page above) [data; the Zegna and Banca Sella terms themselves were not read].
- **Obligation:** J-1 visa; US-government-funded J-1 exchange visitors are normally subject to the two-year home-country physical presence rule (section 7).

### 1.3 DAAD (Germany) [data]
- **ERP Study Scholarships for graduates of economics and business administration** (Master's, 18 to 24 months, €992/month plus €460/year study allowance, insurance, travel allowance on request): open only to nationals of a list of countries: Armenia, Azerbaijan, Belarus, Georgia, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Moldova, Serbia, Tajikistan, Turkmenistan, Ukraine, Uzbekistan. The page says: "If your status and/or country is not included in the list, you cannot apply" and the page showed "Currently you cannot apply" outside the call window. Applications open from 1 June (DAAD scholarship database, https://www2.daad.de/deutschland/stipendium/datenbank/en/21148-scholarship-database/?detail=50015171, read 1 Oct 2026) [data]. A Mastersportal listing (secondary, unverified) gave a 17 Nov 2026 deadline. Italian readers are **not** eligible.
- **DAAD Study Scholarships, Master's, all disciplines:** €992/month, €460/year, insurance, optional travel allowance, 10 to 24 months, for a full Master's in Germany or one year of a home-university Master's abroad; "funding is not possible for a Master's degree programme that has already begun" (DAAD database page, https://www2.daad.de/deutschland/stipendium/datenbank/en/21148-scholarship-database.pdf?detail=50026200) [data]. The page summary did not show a country list; DAAD's overview says Master's scholarships exist "in particular, for persons from industrial countries" (https://www.daad.de/en/studying-in-germany/scholarships/daad-scholarships/) [data]. Whether Italians are on the eligible list and the 2027 deadline: not confirmed (Claims to verify 2).
- **Development-oriented DAAD programmes (EPOS, Helmut Schmidt, Sub-Saharan In-Country):** for applicants from developing and emerging countries, so not for Italians. EPOS targets development-related fields and requires professional experience (DAAD overview and EPOS page, 2026) [data].
- **Germany's own cost context:** public German master's programmes charge nearly no tuition for EU students, so a DAAD award matters mainly for living costs (see money/costs-and-funding.md).

### 1.4 Swiss Government Excellence Scholarships [data]
- **Not a route into a business master's.** The 2027-28 round (applications from 20 Aug 2026) is for early-career researchers who "have already completed their Master's degree" (research fellowships, PhD, postdoc), plus art scholarships for "an initial Master's degree in an artistic field". CHF 2,450 a month, age limit 35, open to 183 countries (Swiss State Secretariat for Education, Research and Innovation, Aug 2026, https://www.sbfi.admin.ch/sbfi/en/home/education/scholarships-and-grants/swiss-government-excellence-scholarships.html) [data]. An MSc at St. Gallen would not qualify.

### 1.5 Eiffel (France) [data]
- **Mechanism:** the student cannot apply. "Only applications submitted by French higher education institutions are accepted", and applications sent directly by students are ineligible (Campus France, 2026, https://www.campusfrance.org/en/france-excellence-eiffel-scholarship-program) [data]. Master's applicants must be "up to 29 years old". The 2026 call closed on 8 January 2026 with results from 30 March 2026, so the 2027 call should follow a similar January shape, but no 2027 date was published when read (Campus France call page, https://www.campusfrance.org/en/actu/call-for-france-excellence-eiffel-applications-2026) [data].
- **Fields:** seven priority fields including Economics/Management (same page) [data].
- **Value:** €1,200 a month at Master's level appears in a Campus France search summary but was not on the pages I could read (unverified, snippet only).
- **Who:** French citizens are excluded. The pages read do not say whether EU nationals are excluded. Treat it as aimed at "future foreign decision-makers" and, in practice, non-EU students; whether an Italian can be nominated is unconfirmed (Claims to verify 3). ESSEC files Eiffel nominations only for first-round applicants (see money/costs-and-funding.md).

### 1.6 Erasmus+ (European Commission) [data]
- **Erasmus Mundus Joint Masters (EMJM):** "students from all over the world" may apply, directly to each consortium, "between October and January for courses starting the following academic year" (Erasmus+ students page, https://erasmus-plus.ec.europa.eu/opportunities/individuals/students/erasmus-mundus-joint-masters) [data]. Scholarship: "1 400 Euros/month x DS" for the full programme (12, 18 or 24 months); funded projects "cannot charge tuition fees or other mandatory costs" to scholarship holders; no more than 10% of scholarships to one nationality; nothing excludes EU nationals (Erasmus+ Programme Guide, Erasmus Mundus action, https://erasmus-plus.ec.europa.eu/programme-guide/part-b/key-action-2/erasmus-mundus-action) [data]. 37 new EMJMs were selected in the latest call and open to students in autumn 2026 for September 2027 starts, taking the catalogue past 170 (Erasmus+ news, https://erasmus-plus.ec.europa.eu/whats-new/news/27-new-joint-masters-join-the-erasmus-mundus-catalogue) [data]. EMJMs are academic, three-institution, 1 to 2 year degrees. I could not confirm which are business-relevant. They are not a substitute for MiM or MSc Finance at the schools the calculator scores, and a business-relevant EMJM is rare compared with engineering, humanities and science programmes (Claims to verify 4).
- **Erasmus+ Master Loan:** closed; see section 4.

### 1.7 Italy: MAECI "Invest Your Talent in Italy" and Italian-government scholarships [data, university and embassy copies]
- **IYT:** for foreign students from 23 named countries (Argentina, Armenia, Azerbaijan, Bangladesh, Brazil, Chile, Colombia, Egypt, Ethiopia, Ghana, India, Indonesia, Iran, Kazakhstan, Mexico, Mongolia, Paraguay, Peru, China, Tunisia, Turkey, Uruguay, Vietnam). €10,800 a year plus tuition exemption (except about €156 in regional fees), renewable with 30 ECTS, mandatory internship at an Italian company, born after 1 January 2000, B2+ in English and Italian, cannot be combined with other government scholarships. Deadline for 2026-27 was 11 May 2026 (University of Pisa IYT page, https://www.unipi.it/en/education/registration/enrolment-and-registration/enrolment-for-international-students/invest-your-talent-in-italy-scholarships; also https://en.unimib.it/node/4096) [data, secondary]. Not open to Italians.
- **MAECI Italian Government Scholarships:** 9-month awards (1 Nov 2026 to 31 July 2027) for foreign students and Italian citizens residing abroad (IRE) for Master's, AFAM, PhD and research; 2026-27 deadline 26 March 2026 on the Study in Italy portal; "covers only attendance at courses held in Italy" (Italian Embassy Panama copy of the call, https://ambpanama.esteri.it/en/news/dall_ambasciata/2026/03/bando-per-lassegnazione-di-borse-di-studio-offerte-dal-governo-italiano-a-studenti-stranieri-e-italiani-residenti-allestero-ire-per-lanno-accademico-2026-2027/) [data, secondary]. Monthly amount not on the page read. Not for Italians living in Italy.

### 1.8 Regional right-to-study grants (DSU) for Italy-based study [data]
- **National parameters for 2026/27.** The Ministry (MUR) fixed the maximum ISEE at €28,339.88 and the maximum ISPE at €61,608.48 (Decreto Direttoriale 176 of 10 Feb 2026, https://www.mur.gov.it/sites/default/files/2026-03/Decreto%20Direttoriale%20n.%20176%20del%2010-02-2026.pdf), and the minimum grant amounts at €7,171.11 (fuori sede), €4,190.71 (pendolare) and €2,890.16 (in sede) (Decreto Direttoriale 175 of 10 Feb 2026, https://www.mur.gov.it/sites/default/files/2026-03/Decreto%20Direttoriale%20n.%20175%20del%2010-02-2026.pdf) [data]. Both are a 1.4% ISTAT uprating of the previous year (the 2025/26 ISEE ceiling quoted inside the decree is €27,948.60).
- **Regions and universities run the grants.** The national figures are a ceiling on income and a floor on amounts. Each region (ER.GO, EDISU, DSU Toscana, LazioDiSCo, Lombardy via the universities) sets its own merit requirements (credits earned), budget and cut-offs. Whether every "idoneo" gets paid depends on the regional budget; I did not find a primary source that quantifies the "eligible but not funded" gap, so treat it as a risk, not a number (Claims to verify 5).
- **Bocconi's own version (ISU Bocconi).** Bocconi's economic thresholds follow Lombardy Region's decree, not the national ceiling: for 2026-27 the ISEE limit was €26,887.93 and the ISPE limit €58,452.06, and the 2027-28 figures "will be communicated after Lombardy Region's decree, expected between June and July 2027" (Bocconi ISU requirements page, https://www.unibocconi.it/it/entrare-bocconi/corsi-di-laurea-triennale-e-giurisprudenza/agevolazioni/requisiti-economici-isu-bocconi-aa-2027-28) [data]. The page read concerns bachelor's and law; Bocconi's master's page is in money/costs-and-funding.md. The point for a calculator: a family just under the national cap may still be over Bocconi's.
- **Who:** Italian, EU and non-EU students all use the same system if they document their family income (for non-EU: ISEE parificato). For Italians this is the largest scheme by number of recipients, but it is need-based and conditioned on credits. It is not a scholarship you "win" by writing an essay.

### 1.9 Netherlands, Sweden, Denmark [data]
- **Netherlands:** the Orange Knowledge Programme (Nuffic) ended in December 2024 (Nuffic news and programme pages, https://www.nuffic.nl/en/international/impact-and-results-of-the-orange-knowledge-programme) [data]; the Gemini lead listing it as an option was outdated. What remains is the **NL Scholarship** (formerly Holland Scholarship): a one-off €5,000 in the first year for non-EEA nationals starting a full-time bachelor's or master's at a participating institution (11 research universities, 23 universities of applied sciences), applied for through each institution with its own deadline (Study in NL, 2026, https://www.studyinnl.org/finances/nl-scholarship) [data]. Italians (EEA) are excluded. RSM's own awards are in section 3.
- **Sweden:** the Swedish Institute Scholarships for Global Professionals cover tuition, SEK 12,000/month and a SEK 15,000 travel grant for master's in Sweden, but are for citizens of a country list of developing economies, require work and leadership experience, and exclude holders of Swedish or EU/EEA citizenship; the 2026/27 call ran University Admissions 16 Oct 2025 to 15 Jan 2026, scholarship portal in February (SI page, fetched via curl 1 Oct 2026, https://si.se/en/apply/scholarships/swedish-institute-scholarships-for-global-professionals/) [data]. Stockholm School of Economics is "part of separate intakes and admissions processes" and does not get the standard application number (same page), so check SSE directly. Not for Italians.
- **Denmark:** EU/EEA citizens pay no tuition. For non-EU/EEA/Swiss applicants, CBS charges €8,000 per semester (DKK 60,000) and offers about 25 scholarships a year: tuition waiver plus DKK 8,000 a month for up to 22 months, selected on motivation and academic record, deadline 15 January 23:59 CET (15 October for the winter round) (CBS, 2026, https://www.cbs.dk/en/study/graduate/admission/non-eueea-citizens) [data]. This is a school scheme, so non-EU only.

### 3.1 Two claims in money/costs-and-funding.md that need softening
1. **"Esade and Bocconi forbid stacking awards."** The wording on both pages is narrower. Bocconi's merit regulation says only one benefit "among those offered by Bocconi University itself" may apply per academic year, and revokes the merit award if a larger Bocconi benefit is accepted (Bocconi, 2026-27, above; search summary of the regulation) [data]. Esade's wording is "not compatible with other prizes or awards granted by Esade". Neither page read says an **external** scholarship (Fulbright, DAAD, a regional DSU grant, a foundation) is forbidden. For external awards, the funder's own rules decide. Chevening reduces or withdraws awards if you have other significant awards or earned income (T&Cs above); MAECI IYT cannot combine with other government scholarships or Erasmus+; Einaudi is "incompatible with other forms of financing"; the NL Scholarship is silent in the text read. HEC says Foundation awards cannot be combined with each other, but can be combined with external scholarships such as Eiffel (search summary of a different HEC page; the page I fetched was silent on this, so treat as unverified).
2. **Eiffel value and age.** money/costs-and-funding.md quotes €1,181/month and "aged 30 or under" from ESSEC's page. Campus France's 2026 page I read says "up to 29 years old at master's level"; the €1,200 figure appeared only in a search summary. The discrepancy is most likely a year-to-year change, but the Campus France page governs, so check it for the 2027 call.

### 3.2 What school awards actually look like for a European reader
- Merit awards are automatic, decided with the admission offer, and mostly capped at a fraction of tuition. The disclosed numbers cluster at €10,000 to €15,000 (ESSEC €10,000, HEC up to €15,000, Esade average about €10,000 on a €39,000 fee per money/costs-and-funding.md) [data]. Bocconi is the exception on size, with a full waiver, but the number of awards is not published on the page read.
- **Share funded is rarely published.** Bocconi's homepage says one student in three benefits from funding (money/costs-and-funding.md [data]); Oxford Saïd reports over a third of the 2021-22 MBA class (search summary, unverified); Cambridge MFin's cohort funding mix gives 15% from scholarships. ESCP's "around 400 scholarships a year" appeared only in a search summary and is unverified. HEC, LBS, LSE publish no share.
- **Non-EU students get more merit money in some places and Europeans in others.** ESCP's merit awards exclude Europeans; HEC's and Bocconi's include them; CBS, MAECI, SI and the NL Scholarship are non-EU only. Check the "who is eligible" column before comparing sizes.

## 6. Success rates and expected-value reasoning

**What is published.**
- **Chevening:** about 1,500 awards a year worldwide (gov.uk news page for 2025/26, via search summary, unverified wording). Applicant numbers appeared only on secondary sites (about 60,000, giving roughly 2.5%) and could not be confirmed on a Chevening page; the Chevening selection FAQ I fetched states no numbers (https://www.chevening.org/faqs/how-are-chevening-scholars-selected/). Use "low single digit percent, worldwide, not Italy-specific" (unverified, secondary).
- **Erasmus Mundus:** each project can offer at most 60 scholarships plus up to 30 for target regions (EACEA summary via search, unverified wording); the Commission publishes nationality counts of past scholarship holders (India 2,199, Brazil 1,544, China 1,505, Mexico 1,357, Pakistan 1,236 over 2004 to 2023), not application-to-award ratios (https://erasmus-plus.ec.europa.eu/resources-and-tools/factsheets-statistics-evaluations/statistics/erasmus-mundus-20) [data]. The top five nationalities are all non-European, and a 10% nationality cap per project applies.
- **Fulbright Italy, DAAD, Eiffel, LSE GSS:** no application or success counts found on the pages read. LSE gives a budget (£3 million) and a range (£5,000 to £20,000), which implies at most a few hundred awards a year (author calculation: £3m divided by £5k to £20k gives 150 to 600 awards).
- **School awards:** share funded is published rarely (Bocconi homepage: one in three students benefits from funding [money/costs-and-funding.md, data]; Cambridge MFin 2026/27: scholarships 15% of the funding mix).

**Expected value (author calculation, not sourced).** Applying is worth it when p x V > C, where p is the chance of success, V the award value and C the cost of the application (hours x what your time is worth, plus fees). Break-even p = C / V.
- Zero-effort school awards (Bocconi, HEC, ESSEC, LBS: automatic with admission): C is about 0, so always take them; the real choice is which admission offers you take.
- A research-style application costing 20 hours at an assumed €15/hour (C = €300) against a €40,000 award breaks even at p = 0.75%; against a €5,000 award at 6%. At low-single-digit odds, one or two serious external applications are worth it if the award is above about €15,000 and the application can be reused across schemes (a statement of purpose and references). The 20-hour and €15 figures are assumptions.
- A **fixed-value partial award** such as Fulbright's $50,000 helps most if your destination costs exceed it by little. A scheme that obliges you to leave your home market for two years (Chevening) has a hidden cost that belongs in C (section 7).
- **Correlation matters.** Most government awards need a strong GPA, leadership story and a recommendation set. Ten applications built from the same material have correlated outcomes; the second to fourth add the most, after that returns drop (author reasoning, no data).

**H-c verdict:** the "X%" threshold depends on your own C and V; the algebra above gives a way to state it, but I found no published success-rate data good enough to put a universal X on it. See Hypotheses.

## 8. Hidden conditions: tax, visa funds, stacking

- **Tax, Italy.** The Revenue Agency's student guide and rulings (search summary, unverified wording) indicate that scholarships are in general taxable income unless an express rule exempts them, and that regional right-to-study grants under Law 390/1991 and university postgraduate scholarships have an express IRPEF exemption (Agenzia delle Entrate, https://www.agenziaentrate.gov.it/portale/documents/20143/233527/Guida+disciplina+irpef_guide_pratiche_DR_studenti_e_fisco_versione_febbraio2014.pdf; note the guide is from February 2014 and a 2025 change is mentioned in the summary) [data, not read in full]. I did not read tax rules for foreign scholarships paid to an Italian resident, nor for the UK, France or Germany. Treat tax on foreign awards as unchecked; ask the funder and an adviser (Claims to verify 8).
- **Tax, school discounts.** Tuition reductions (HEC, ESSEC, Bocconi waivers) are reductions in fees, not cash paid to you; I found no source that treats them as income, but I did not look for one.
- **Visa financial requirement.** UK student visa: £1,529 a month for up to 9 months in London and £1,171 outside; funds held for 28 consecutive days; if you rely on a loan or financial sponsorship you provide evidence from the lender or sponsor (GOV.UK, https://www.gov.uk/student-visa/money, 1 Oct 2026) [data]. A search summary of the caseworker guidance says Chevening and similar government schemes count as official financial sponsors, which lifts the 28-day test; I did not read that guidance itself (Claims to verify 9). For a full-ride award the practical point is to obtain the funder's letter early; see places/visas-and-work-rights.md for the other countries.
- **Stacking.** Reported in section 3.1: Bocconi and Esade restrict combining awards from the same school; external-funder rules differ (Chevening can reduce an award for other "significant awards"; MAECI IYT bars other government scholarships and Erasmus+; Einaudi bars other financing; HEC reportedly allows external ones, unverified). The money/costs-and-funding.md line that "Esade and Bocconi forbid stacking" is true only of their own awards.
- **Clawback and duty to inform.** Chevening T&Cs require telling the Secretariat about other funding and allow a £100 penalty for missed administrative steps; the FCDO reserves the right to reduce an award where you have "earned income" (T&Cs above) [data]. Part-time work income can therefore interact with the award.

## Hypotheses tested

**H-a. "For EU students most big-name scholarships are closed, so school merit awards and public-school fees are the main levers."** Verdict: **supported, with one addition.** Holds for: Chevening (experience), DAAD ERP, Swiss, IYT, MAECI, SI, NL, CBS, Aga Khan, Mastercard (section 5 matrix). Fails: Fulbright Italy and Erasmus Mundus are open to Italians, and the regional DSU/ISU grants are a third lever for lower-income Italians. Evidence: official pages above [data].

**H-b. "Return obligations make several flagship scholarships incompatible with a post-study work plan."** Verdict: **partly supported (two schemes, not several).** Holds for Chevening (explicit two-year return, no visa switch, repayment) [data] and for Fulbright J-1 (two-year home-residency rule for government-funded J-1; university international offices) [practitioner consensus]. Fails for: DAAD, Eiffel, Erasmus Mundus, CBS, SI and school awards, where no return clause appeared on the pages read; IYT actually requires an internship in Italy. Conditions: it matters only if your target job market is the scholarship country or a visa-dependent third country.

**H-c. "Applying has positive expected value only when the award exceeds X% of costs."** Verdict: **insufficient evidence for a universal X; the algebra holds.** The break-even is p = C / V (author calculation). With the assumed 20 hours at €15 an hour, a €40,000 award breaks even near 0.75%, a €5,000 award near 6%. Published success rates (Chevening about 2.5%, secondary and unverified) would pass the first and fail the second. No official application or award counts were found for Fulbright Italy, DAAD, Eiffel or most Erasmus Mundus programmes. [author calculation; Chevening rate anecdotal/secondary]

**H-d (mine). "The best-known scholarships are for people with work experience, so the pre-experience reader is mostly excluded."** Verdict: **supported.** Chevening needs 2,800 hours after the bachelor's and two years since graduating; SI needs "demonstrated work experience" and leadership; EPOS needs professional experience; Oxford MBA and MBA-linked awards need two years of work (Chevening, SI, Rhodes pages) [data]. Fails for: Fulbright self-placed (bachelor's suffices), Erasmus Mundus, school awards, DSU. A reader with a gap year or internships can reach Chevening's hours through part-time and internship work counted by the page.

**H-e (mine). "Most money for Europeans is bundled with the admission decision rather than applied for separately."** Verdict: **supported.** Bocconi: "a separate application will not be required"; HEC: "No" application, all admitted students considered; LBS: automatically considered; ESSEC decides with admission (money/costs-and-funding.md) [data]. Fails for: LSE GSS and ESCP need-based aid, which have separate forms, and for external schemes. Practical meaning: scholarship planning is mostly admissions planning (which schools, which rounds).

**H-f (mine). "Cheap loans are tied to the passport and the school, so the choice is smaller than it looks."** Verdict: **supported.** Intesa via Bocconi: citizens of Italy, EU, UK only per Bocconi, capped at €20,000; UK Master's Loan: EU nationals only with settled or pre-settled status; Prodigy is open broadly but is priced in dollars at a 13.26% APR example [data].

**H-g (mine). "Tuition-waiver awards are more valuable in Italy and Germany only if you are in the need-based tier, because public fees already sit low."** Verdict: **insufficient evidence in this file.** I did not read the public-university fee tables here (see money/costs-and-funding.md). Treat as a question for the calculator, not a finding.


## Common mistakes and myths

- **"Chevening is for any student."** It needs 2,800 hours of post-bachelor's work and two years since graduating; many final-year students find this out at the end of the application (Chevening eligibility page) [data].
- **"Chevening or Fulbright let me work there afterwards."** Chevening explicitly bars the Graduate route and visa switching; a Fulbright J-1 normally comes with a two-year home-residency rule (Chevening FAQ; Berkeley International Office) [data; practitioner consensus].
- **"Esade and Bocconi forbid external scholarships."** What the pages read restrict is combining awards from the same school (section 3.1) [data].
- **"The Erasmus+ Master Loan can fund my master's."** The scheme ran 2015 to 2022 and has been replaced by a guarantee pilot run through banks (European Commission) [data].
- **"The DAAD ERP scholarship is open to EU students."** It is for 12 listed countries; the page says if your country is not listed "you cannot apply" (DAAD) [data].
- **"The Swiss Government scholarship funds a St. Gallen MSc."** The 2027-28 round covers researchers and art master's only (SERI) [data].
- **"If I'm under the national ISEE cap, I'll get the DSU grant."** The national figure is a ceiling; Bocconi's own 2026-27 ISEE limit was €26,887.93, below the national €28,339.88, and regional budgets decide who is paid (MUR; Bocconi) [data].
- **"Merit scholarships exist everywhere for Europeans."** ESCP's merit awards are for non-European students; Europeans there can seek need-based aid only (ESCP, July 2025) [data].
- **"Scholarship application is free."** Early deposits can be lost: LBS asks £2,000 within about four weeks and £6,000 within about six weeks of an offer, non-refundable (LBS) [data].


## Decision rules

1. If you are an Italian pre-experience applicant, then count Chevening out for this cycle unless you already have 2,800 hours of work counted by Chevening's rules (because it requires them and two years since graduating).
2. If you will hold an Italian bachelor's and want a US business master's, then apply to Fulbright Italy self-placed in November, but budget for years beyond the first (because $50,000 covers year one only).
3. If you plan to start your career in the UK or the US and the scholarship is Chevening or a Fulbright J-1, then price the two-year return or home-residency clause as a cost (because the Graduate route and H/L status changes are blocked).
4. If your family income is low (ISEE under about €26,900 for Bocconi, up to €28,339.88 for the national DSU ceiling), then apply for DSU/ISU grants and check the university-specific threshold (because Bocconi's limit is lower than the national ceiling and regions fund by budget).
5. If you are comparing offers from HEC, Bocconi, ESSEC, LBS or Esade, then compare net tuition after the automatic award, and apply in the first round (because merit awards are decided with admission and Bocconi allocates most in early rounds).
6. If you are European and considering ESCP, then do not expect a merit award (because ESCP's merit awards are for non-European students; Europeans get need-based aid).
7. If you are non-EU and considering Denmark, then apply to CBS by 15 January for its scholarship (because tuition is €8,000 per semester and about 25 scholarships a year cover it).
8. If you need a loan for Bocconi and are Italian, EU or UK, then apply to Intesa "per Merito" (up to €20,000, EURIRS 10y + 1.30%) before Prodigy (because the bank loan is cheaper and in euros).
9. If you are an EU national without UK settled or pre-settled status, then do not plan on the £13,206 Master's Loan (because eligibility requires it).
10. If an external award has a stacking or "no other funding" clause (Chevening, IYT, Einaudi), then read it before accepting a school award (because accepting both can lead to a reduction or loss).
11. If the deadline falls before the offer date (Chevening, Fulbright), then apply on the basis of your application, not the outcome, but keep a paid deposit decision for after the result (because LBS-type deposits are due within weeks of an offer).
12. If you are submitting more than one big external application, then reuse the core essay and references and cap yourself at 2 to 4 (author reasoning, correlated outcomes, no data).
13. If you rely on a scholarship for a student visa, then get the funder's letter early (because the UK requires evidence from the sponsor and a 28-day funds test applies otherwise).
