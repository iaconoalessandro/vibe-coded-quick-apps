# Salaries and ROI

Exchange rates used throughout: ECB reference rates of 29 Sept 2026, €1 = CHF 0.9461 = £0.85718 = US$1.1355 = SEK 11.321 = DKK 7.4754 (ECB, 2026, https://www.ecb.europa.eu/stats/eurofxref/eurofxref-daily.xml) [data]. PPP conversions use World Bank 2025 PPP factors (local currency per international $): CHF 0.931, GBP 0.677, EUR France 0.678, Germany 0.710, Italy 0.610, Spain 0.571, Netherlands 0.747, SEK 8.55, DKK 6.12 (World Bank indicator PA.NUS.PPP, 2025, https://api.worldbank.org/v2/country/CHE;GBR;FRA;DEU;ITA;ESP;NLD;SWE;DNK;ARE;USA/indicator/PA.NUS.PPP?format=json&date=2023:2025) [data].

## Bottom line

1. **Switzerland pays the most at the start, by a wide margin, and the gap survives tax and rent.** The median Swiss university master's graduate in economics/business earns CHF 87,100 gross one year after graduating (BFS, 2024) [data]. The average LBS MiM graduate working in the UK earns £44,675 base (LBS, 2024) [data]. That is roughly €92k against €52k gross, about €74k against €42k net, and about €3,500 against €900 a month left after a central one-bed rent (author calculation, see section 5). The Swiss figure is before mandatory health insurance, which the BAG puts at CHF 640 a month for an adult in the city of Zurich (premium region 1; CHF 586 is the canton-wide average) and CHF 459 for a 19–25-year-old (average standard premium 2026, franchise CHF 300, with accident cover), so about €2,800–3,000 a month after it.
2. **The Swiss premium is mostly a location effect, not a degree effect.** Swiss applied-sciences (FH) bachelor's graduates earn CHF 80,300, almost the same as all Swiss university master's graduates at CHF 80,900 (BFS, 2024) [data]. The typical Swiss economics master's graduate out-earns the average LBS MiM graduate in London. That points to Swiss wage levels, not school prestige.
3. **London starting pay is low relative to London rent unless you land IB, MBB or top tech.** On a £45k starting salary, net pay is about £2,990 a month. The ONS average London rent is £2,332 (ONS, Aug 2026) [data]. London's payoff is the career ladder and bonus pools, not the first year.
4. **FT "salary three years after" figures are not starting salaries and should not be read as typical outcomes.** They are sector-weighted means from alumni who respond, three years out, in PPP dollars. LBS MiM shows $119,990 PPP in the FT 2026 table, while its own report shows a £44,541 mean starting base (FT 2026; LBS 2024) [data].
5. **On FT data, St Gallen alumni still lead at year three.** HSG's weighted salary is $150,490 PPP against LBS at $119,990, HEC at $137,760 and Bocconi at $109,250 (FT MiM 2026) [data]. So the idea that Swiss graduates fall behind London by year three is not supported. Whether London overtakes later is not shown by the data I could fetch: UK-domiciled business master's graduates reach a median of £53,400 at ten years (LEO, 2012/13 cohort, tax year 2023-24), and the Swiss wage survey's business-professional median for ages 30–49 is about CHF 116,500 (BFS LSE 2024), but the two series are not like-for-like (section 4).
6. **Tax regimes change rankings at the same gross salary.** At €60k gross, a single person keeps about 84% in Zurich, 79% in London, 71% in Paris, 74% in Amsterdam (88% with the 30% facility), 70% in Madrid, and about 62% in both Munich and Milan. In Dubai the figure is 100% (author calculations from official and PwC schedules) [data inputs; author calculation].
7. **Expat tax breaks mainly help higher earners, and they have eligibility traps.** Spain's Beckham regime (24% flat) is worth slightly less than the normal regime below about €65k (about €0.5k a year less at €60k), then about €4.8k a year at €90k and about €10.5k at €120k. The Dutch 30% facility is worth about €8.5k a year at €60k. However, it requires having lived more than 150 km from the Dutch border for over 16 of the 24 months before the job starts, which a one-year Dutch master's can break (Belastingdienst, 2026) [data]. It is also being phased down (government.nl, 2026) [data].
8. **The gender gap starts on day one in France.** CGE shows men from business schools on €42,583 against €39,662 for women, a 7.4% gap. Including bonuses it grows to about 12% (€49,612 against €43,611) (CGE via Diplomeo and MiM in Europe, 2025) [data]. The 2026 wave shows 7.2% (CGE via L'Express Education, Aug 2026) [data].
9. **For a Bocconi versus Mannheim choice, where you will work matters more than the school.** If you work in Italy, Italian pay is low (AlmaLaurea national second-level graduates: €1,495 net a month at one year, €1,903 at five years) [data]. Bocconi's FT PPP salary is also below Mannheim's ($109k against $119k). But 52.8% of employed Bocconi MSc International Management graduates work abroad one year out (Bocconi, a.y. 2022–23) [data].
10. **ROI is dominated by where you work in years 1–5, not by tuition.** A €30k difference in tuition is repaid in one to two years by the gap in after-rent savings between Zurich and London starter jobs. Choose a programme for the labour market it opens, then check the cost.

## 4. Long-term pay growth

- **Switzerland (BFS, class of 2018 at five years).** 72% of UH master's and doctoral graduates changed jobs within five years. Of those who changed job, 27–31% were promoted; across all higher-education graduates 23% were promoted (12% within the same employer, 11% at a new one). Promotion raised standardised real gross pay by 13–18% against people who changed neither position nor employer, and an employer change without promotion by about 6% (BFS press release, 1 Sept 2025, https://www.admin.ch/de/newnsb/a-36akeiIpQUL0J3HdNMn) [data]. This is modest, step-wise growth. It fits the idea of a high Swiss starting level with slower relative growth, but the release gives no five-year level by field.
- **Italy (AlmaLaurea 2026).** Second-level graduates go from €1,495 net/month at one year to €1,903 at five years, +27% nominal. The figures come from different cohorts, so this is not the same people tracked over time (AlmaLaurea via UniVdA, 2026) [data].
- **France (CGE 2026).** Business-school graduates report salary progression of 7.6% after two years (L'Express Education, 2026) [data].
- **FT three-year versus starting salary (author calculation, indicative only).** HSG's FT figure back-converts to about CHF 140k, against programme starting averages of CHF 92–98k, a multiple of about 1.45–1.5×. LBS's FT figure back-converts to about £81k against a £44.7k UK start, about 1.8×. **London grows faster in percentage terms but from a much lower base, and is still below HSG in PPP at year three.** Caveats: different cohorts, FT respondents are not a random sample, the FT weights by sector, and alumni work in many countries.
- **London top end.** M&I's associate bases of £115–135k with 80–100% bonuses [anecdotal] show where London beats Switzerland. These outcomes apply to the minority who stay in IB or PE, not to the median graduate.
- **Ten-year series (new, round 3c).** UK-domiciled level-7 business and management graduates at all UK providers: median annualised earnings £35,100 at one year, £41,400 at three, £48,300 at five and £53,400 at ten (upper quartile £80,900 at ten; DfE LEO release 2023-24, 25 June 2026, author extraction from the underlying CSV; different cohorts; the same 2012/13 cohort went from £40,200 at five years to £53,400 at ten) [data]. Swiss wage structure survey 2024, business and administration professionals (ISCO 24), standardised monthly gross median: CHF 6,706 for ages up to 29, CHF 9,707 for 30–49, CHF 11,215 for 50 and over (Zurich region CHF 6,966, 10,714 and 12,526) (BFS LSE via the BFS PxWeb API, table px-x-0304010000_205) [data]. The proportional rise from the youngest to the middle age band is 1.45 (Switzerland) and 1.54 (Zurich), similar to the UK's 1.52 from year one to year ten. The two series differ in population (jobs, not graduates), pay definition and time axis (age band, not years since graduation), so they show direction, not a like-for-like gap.
- **Swiss pay scales.** I found no primary source measuring how compressed Swiss or German pay scales are compared with London or the US beyond the age-band series above. See Claims to verify.

## 5. Net pay, rent and what is left

### 5a. Method
- **General information only, not tax advice.** Rates and thresholds are the published 2026 schedules as read on the dates given; individual outcomes depend on permits, residence history, pension plans, deductions and municipality, and rules change. Check an official calculator or an adviser before relying on any figure.
- All figures are **author calculations** for a single person, no children, no church tax, full year.
- **Zurich (city):** AHV/IV/EO 5.3%; ALV 1.1% up to CHF 148,200; occupational pension at 3.5% of coordinated salary (BVG minimum employee share for ages 25–34, an assumption); 3% professional-expense deduction and the insurance-premium deduction as in PwC's sample. Zurich basic tariff × (0.95 cantonal + 1.19 city) and the 2026 federal tariff (PwC Switzerland, reviewed July 2026) [practitioner consensus, tax-firm summary]. **Correction (round 3c, 2 Oct 2026):** an earlier edit of this file replaced a 95% cantonal rate with 98%, which is the 2024–25 rate; the Zurich Kantonsrat cut it to 95% for 2026 and 2027 on 15 Dec 2025 (zh.ch budget page: "ab 2026 mit einem Steuerfuss von 95%"; https://www.zh.ch/de/steuern-finanzen/kantonsfinanzen/budget-und-finanzplanung/konsolidierter-entwicklungs-und-finanzplan-2027-2030/finanzentwicklung.html) [data]; the city rate is 119% (kantonsvergleich.ch, secondary). The table in 5b was calculated with the higher cantonal rate and has not been recomputed; the correct 95% would raise Zurich net by very roughly €50 at €60k, €100 at €90k and €200 at €120k (author estimate), so the Zurich column is slightly conservative and the percentages are unchanged at the rounding shown. My method reproduces PwC's worked CHF 200k example to within about 1%. **It excludes mandatory health insurance**, paid separately: the BAG's average standard premium for 2026 (franchise CHF 300, with accident cover) is CHF 640.1 a month for adults from 26 and CHF 458.5 for 19–25-year-olds in premium region 1, which consists solely of the city of Zurich (CHF 585.8 and CHF 418.0 are the canton-wide averages; region 2 is CHF 583.5 and 420.3) (BAG, regional average premiums 2026, https://www.bag.admin.ch/dam/de/sd-web/ZHSq3s8mYbWS/regionale%20monatliche%20durchschnittliche%20Pr%C3%A4mie_PG2026.pdf; region 1 = city of Zurich per SVA Zürich) [data]. The BAG announced on 29 Sept 2026 that the national mean premium rises 5.0% for 2027 to CHF 412 a month (https://www.priminfo.admin.ch/downloads/medienmitteilung_2027_DE.pdf) [data]; a Zurich region-1 adult figure of CHF 677.00 for 2027 appeared only in a search summary and is unverified; a higher franchise or a managed-care model lowers it, by an amount not verified here. EU graduates on a B permit earning under CHF 120k are taxed at source: the Kanton Zürich 2026 tariff A0 (single, no children, no church tax; official PDF, valid from 1 Jan 2026, https://www.zh.ch/content/dam/zhweb/bilder-dokumente/themen/steuern-finanzen/steuern/quellensteuer/quellensteuertarif/2026/p208840_QuellensteuerTarif_A_ohne_KS_ENT1.pdf) takes 8.06% of gross in the CHF 7,251–7,300 monthly band (CHF 87,100 a year is CHF 7,258 a month), about CHF 7,020 [data], and staff whose gross reaches CHF 120,000 a year move to a compulsory subsequent ordinary assessment (Kanton Zürich Merkblatt on source taxation, via search summary), which is lower than the ordinary-assessment calculation used here, so source-taxed staff keep roughly CHF 1,500–2,400 a year more at that salary before the non-occupational accident premium (author calculation from the official tariff table).
- **London:** Personal allowance £12,570 tapering above £100k; 20%/40%/45% bands; employee NI 8% between £12,570 and £50,270, then 2% (GOV.UK, 2026/27) [data]. Pension auto-enrolment and student loans excluded.
- **Paris:** Linear interpolation of PwC's France table. At €50k, social contributions are €9,198 and single-person income tax €4,120. At €75k they are €13,610 and €9,679; at €100k, €18,021 and €15,238 (PwC France, reviewed Apr 2026) [practitioner consensus].
- **Munich:** 2026 employee social contributions: pension 9.3% and unemployment 1.3% up to €101,400; health 7.3% plus half the 2.9% average additional rate, and care 1.8% plus 0.6% childless surcharge, both up to €69,750 (PwC Germany, 2026) [practitioner consensus]. Income tax uses the 2025 §32a formula, which I checked against PwC's worked example to within €2. The 2026 tariff is slightly lower, so the German figures are slightly pessimistic.
- **Amsterdam:** 2026 Box 1 rates of 35.75%, 37.56% and 49.5% (PwC NL) [practitioner consensus]. 2026 general and labour tax credits from Belastingdienst tables [data]. Employee pension and the mandatory health premium are excluded, so Dutch net is overstated by a few thousand euros.

## 6. ROI method

**Formula to use in the calculator.** A cleaner version of the GMAC/FT approach:

ROI over N years = Σ over t = 1..N of (net pay after the master's_t − rent_t) − Σ(counterfactual net pay_t − rent_t) − (tuition + living costs during the programme + forgone net pay during the programme).

Counterfactual = what the same person earns with a bachelor's only, in the market they would realistically enter. Rules for the inputs:
1. **Use starting salary from the school's own report for the country you will work in** (e.g. the LBS UK line, not the headline). Use national statistics (BFS, CGE, AlmaLaurea) as a floor.
2. **Convert gross to net with section 5 and subtract rent.** Do not compare gross figures across countries.
3. **Grow pay with market-specific rates only if sourced.** Switzerland: +13–18% in real terms for a promotion against staying put; 23% of all graduates, and 27–31% of UH master's and doctorate graduates who changed job, were promoted within five years (BFS 2025). Italy: +27% nominal from year 1 to 5 (AlmaLaurea). Otherwise use the FT year-3 figure as an optimistic ceiling, not a mean.
4. **Discount** at a real rate of 3–5% and cap the horizon at 5–10 years. Beyond that, career choices dominate programme choice.

**Illustration (author calculation, orders of magnitude only).** Compare a Swiss-track graduate (median CHF 87,100) with a London-track graduate (LBS UK mean £44,675). The after-rent monthly residual differs by about €2,600, or about €31k a year. Over three years that is roughly €90k before any bonus or promotion differences. This is larger than most tuition gaps between a Swiss public university and a London school (fee levels are in money/costs-and-funding.md). For an EU citizen who can work in Switzerland, **the labour market is the ROI driver and tuition is second-order.**

**Published ROI studies.** The FT's value-for-money rank is the only cross-country ROI measure I could fetch (section 2b). GMAC, Poets&Quants and ifo/IW studies could not be retrieved (see Claims to verify).

## Hypotheses tested

### H1 (assigned): "A public Swiss university can lead to higher starting pay than a London business school, but slower long-term career growth."
**Verdict: partly supported.** The starting-pay half is strongly supported. The slower-growth half is only supported in percentage terms up to year three, and the evidence is insufficient beyond that.

- **Starting pay, gross.** Median Swiss economics master's graduate: CHF 87,100 ≈ €92k (BFS 2024) [data]. HSG programme averages: CHF 92–98k (HSG pages) [data]. LBS MiM UK mean: £44,675 ≈ €52k (LBS 2024) [data]. The Swiss figure is about 75–85% higher.
- **PPP.** CHF 87,100 is about $93.6k PPP against about $66k PPP for LBS UK (my conversion; LBS's own figure is $68,728 international dollars). That is still about 40% higher.
- **Net.** About €74k against €42k.
- **After rent.** About €3,500 against €900 a month.
- **Cross-check.** CGE data (class of 2024, business-school graduates working abroad, mean gross excluding bonus) shows €84,202 in Switzerland against €73,191 in the UK; the class of 2025 shows €87,240 against €69,090 (CGE reports of June 2025 and June 2026, page 45) [data]. The group includes foreign nationals returning home after studying in France.
- **Long-term trajectory.** By year three, FT 2026 still puts HSG ($150k PPP) above LBS ($120k), Imperial ($89k) and Warwick ($74k) [data]. HEC Lausanne ($103k) and ZHAW ($103k) sit between Imperial and LBS. London's growth multiple from start is larger (about 1.8× against 1.45–1.5×, indicative only). Swiss five-year growth is step-wise: +13–18% (real) for a promotion, with 23% of all graduates promoted, and 27–31% of those who changed job (BFS 2025) [data]. Ten-year series now exist on each side but are not like-for-like (section 4): UK-domiciled business master's median £53,400 at ten years (LEO), and a Swiss business-professional median that is 1.45 times higher at ages 30–49 than up to 29 (BFS LSE 2024), against 1.52 for the UK from year one to year ten. The data show no sign that Swiss proportional growth is slower, so "slower long-term growth" is not supported, and not refuted either, beyond year five.
- **Who can access Swiss jobs.**
  - EU/EFTA citizens have free movement (details in places/visas-and-work-rights.md).
  - For non-EU citizens, SEM says third-country nationals may only work in Switzerland if highly qualified, "essentially" with a degree "as well as a number of years of professional work experience", and subject to quotas (SEM, 2026, https://www.sem.admin.ch/sem/en/home/themen/arbeit/nicht-eu_efta-angehoerige.html) [data]. **For a non-EU fresh graduate, H1 largely fails**, except via the Swiss-graduate route (to verify).
  - Language: HSG's pipeline events (Banking Days, Industry Days) exist on campus (HSG MGM page) [data]. How often Swiss domestic roles require German or French could not be verified (Claims to verify).
- **When it holds:** EU citizen; willing to work in Switzerland; targets consulting, banking, insurance, corporate strategy or industry in Switzerland; speaks German for Zurich or French for Geneva/Lausanne, or targets English-speaking global firms.
- **When it fails:**
  - You want London IB/PE, where analyst total pay of "£100K to £150K" [anecdotal] plus a faster ladder can beat Swiss corporate pay.
  - You are non-EU.
  - You plan to work outside Switzerland, where the "Swiss premium" disappears because it is a location effect (see H7).
  - You compare an average Swiss university with LBS on *global* mobility rather than pay.

### H2: "The big-city premium disappears after rent."
**Verdict: partly supported.**
- **Where it holds:** London and Amsterdam without the 30% facility. At the same €60k gross they leave the least after a central one-bed (≈€1,360–1,370/month), below Madrid, Paris, Munich and Milan (author calc.; Numbeo) [data inputs + anecdotal rent]. At *actual* London graduate pay (≈£44.7k), only about €900 a month is left.
- **Where it fails:** Zurich and Dubai. Local graduate pay (Zurich) or zero tax (Dubai) more than covers the rent. It also fails for the IB/MBB minority in London, whose bonuses are not in the base figures.

### H3: "The FT 'salary after three years' figure overstates what a typical graduate earns."
**Verdict: supported on mechanism; the size of the bias is not quantified.**
- The FT figure is a sector-weighted mean, in PPP dollars, three years out, from alumni who respond. LBS: FT $119,990 PPP ≈ £81k, against a £44,541 mean starting base (LBS 2024) [data].
- French schools show FT figures of $97k–$138k, while the CGE mean start for all business-school graduates is €41k [data]. Part of the gap is genuine growth. Part is selection (top schools, alumni who respond) and PPP conversion.
- **It fails** as a critique for HSG, where school-reported starting salaries (CHF 92–98k) are already about two-thirds of the FT year-3 PPP-equivalent figure.

### H4: "Milan starting pay makes a Bocconi MSc a worse ROI than a Mannheim MSc for the same student."
**Verdict: partly supported.**
- **Where it holds:** if the student stays in Italy. Italian second-level graduates average €1,495 net/month at one year (AlmaLaurea 2026) [data]. At the same gross, Milan and Munich tax take is similar (both about 62% kept at €60k) and central rent is similar (€1,523 against €1,500) (author calc.; Numbeo). FT 2026 puts Mannheim above Bocconi ($118,678 against $109,250 PPP; value for money 21st against 52nd) [data]. Mannheim is a public university with low fees (see money/costs-and-funding.md).
- **Where it fails:** Bocconi is used as an international launchpad. 52.8% of employed MSc International Management graduates work abroad at one year (Bocconi) [data]. Bocconi's FT salary increase (57.9%) is double Mannheim's (27.9%). Mannheim also requires German for most German corporate roles (to verify).

### H5: "Expat tax regimes (Beckham, 30% ruling) mostly benefit high earners, not typical graduates."
**Verdict: supported.**
- **Beckham:** below about €65k gross, the 24% flat rate is worth slightly less than the regular Madrid regime (≈€41.7k against €42.2k net at €60k; break-even at about €64,500). At €90k it saves about €4.8k and at €120k about €10.5k (author calc. from art. 63 and 93 LIRPF, Madrid's own scale and the 2026 social-security rates) [data inputs].
- **Dutch 30% facility:**
  - It is worth about €8.5k/year at €60k and €20k at €120k (author calc.).
  - The under-30 master's salary threshold is only €36,497 (2026), so it is accessible to graduates (Belastingdienst, 2026) [data].
  - **Trap:** you must have lived more than 150 km from the Dutch border for over 16 of the 24 months before starting work (Belastingdienst) [data]. On my reading of that rule, a student who spent 12+ months in the Netherlands on a master's (e.g. RSM) and then starts work is likely to fail it. Verify with a tax adviser.
  - It has been phased down since 2024 (30% for 20 months, 20% for 20, 10% for 20), with a planned partial reversal from 2027 not yet final (government.nl, 2026) [data]. The Belastingdienst page still describes "up to 30%", so the two official pages frame it differently.

### H6: "The graduate gender gap is driven by bonuses and sector, not base pay for the same role."
**Verdict: partly supported (France only).** The base gap is 7.4% and grows to about 12% including bonuses (CGE 2025) [data]. It is larger in business schools than in engineering schools. Role-level data to test "same job, same pay" was not available.

### H7: "The Swiss premium is a location premium, not a degree premium."
**Verdict: supported.** FH bachelor's graduates earn CHF 80,300 against CHF 80,900 for UH master's (BFS 2024) [data]. The economics field premium is only about 8% above the UH average. ZHAW, an applied-sciences university, matches HEC Lausanne on FT weighted salary (both about $103k PPP) [data]. The implication is that a non-Swiss master's graduate who then works in Switzerland captures most of the premium. So "study in Switzerland" and "work in Switzerland" are separate decisions (see H1 access conditions).

## Common mistakes and myths

1. **Comparing gross salaries across countries.** At €90k gross, a Zurich earner keeps about 81% and a Munich earner about 59% (author calc., section 5) [data inputs].
2. **Reading the FT weighted salary as a starting salary.** It is a three-year, PPP, sector-weighted mean of respondents (see H3) [data].
3. **Using a school's headline average instead of the country line.** The LBS headline includes salaries from £6,638 upward across 29 countries. Use the UK line (£44,675) if you will work in London (LBS 2024) [data].
4. **Treating "salary increase %" as career growth.** It is measured against pre-programme pay, which for pre-experience students is often an internship. Low-baseline countries (Italy, Portugal) look better and high-baseline ones (Germany, Denmark) look worse (FT 2026) [data].
5. **Assuming the 30% facility is automatic for anyone who studied in the Netherlands.** The 150 km/16-of-24-months rule can exclude people who studied there (Belastingdienst) [data].
6. **Assuming Beckham makes Madrid a tax haven for starters.** Below about €65k it gives no benefit (H5).
7. **Forgetting Swiss health insurance.** It is not deducted from payslips and must be budgeted separately (amount to verify).
8. **Assuming London base pay is where IB money is.** At LBS, finance's base is below consulting's, and the difference is in "other compensation" (LBS 2024) [data].

## Decision rules

1. If you are an EU citizen, open to working in Switzerland, and targeting consulting, banking or corporate roles, prefer a Swiss public university (HSG, UZH, Bern, Lausanne) over a London one-year MiM on ROI. Swiss starting pay is about 75–85% higher gross and leaves about €2,500 more per month after rent (BFS; LBS; section 5).
2. If you are non-EU and have no work experience, do not count on the Swiss starting-pay premium. SEM admits third-country workers mainly with a degree plus several years of experience, within quotas (SEM 2026).
3. If your target is London IB/PE or global MBB and you are admitted to LBS/HEC-level programmes, weight the career ladder and bonus pools over year-1 net pay. London base pay alone is below Zurich's.
4. If you plan to work in Italy after graduating, discount Bocconi's FT figures toward AlmaLaurea levels (€1,495 net/month at year one nationally). Compare on the share of graduates placed abroad instead.
5. If two programmes differ by less than about €30k in total cost but lead to different labour markets, choose on the labour market. One year of after-rent savings difference (Zurich against London) exceeds that (section 6).
6. If you will earn under about €65k in Spain, ignore the Beckham regime in your calculations (it is worth slightly less than the normal regime). At €90k to €120k, include it (about €5–10.5k a year).
7. If you are considering a job in the Netherlands, check the 30% facility's 150 km/16-of-24-months test *before* choosing a Dutch master's. Studying there may disqualify you, and the facility is worth about €8.5k/year at €60k.
8. If you compare schools using the FT, compare weighted salary within the same country of work. Treat value-for-money rank as the ROI proxy, where Swiss public universities (ZHAW 3rd, HSG 12th, HEC Lausanne 19th) beat London schools (73rd–94th).
9. If you are choosing a city for your first job at a fixed salary offer, rank by "net minus rent". At €60k: Dubai > Paris ≈ Madrid ≈ Amsterdam with 30% > Munich ≈ Milan ≈ Zurich (before Swiss health insurance; after it Zurich falls below London) > London ≈ Amsterdam without the ruling.
10. If you are a woman targeting French business-school outcomes, benchmark offers on total pay including bonus, not base. The gap roughly doubles once bonuses are counted (CGE 2025).
11. If a programme reports only a headline average, ask for the median, the reporting rate and the country breakdown. LBS provides these under the CSEA standard, and HSG's programme pages do not state their method.
