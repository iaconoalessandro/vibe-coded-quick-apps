# Career branches: index

Researched 9 October 2026 for the Admetia career-orientation site. Thirteen branch reports explain, for students and early-career readers, which areas, roles and sectors each branch opens, what the work is like day to day, what it pays, and how to get in. Every report uses the same six-section template and the same rating scales, so roles can be compared across branches. All figures are dated and approximate; check the source lists before publishing a number.

Files: `reports/<branch>.md` (one per branch); Finance, Computer Science and Artificial Intelligence were researched in parts (`reports/<branch>-part-N.md`) and assembled into the single branch file. `reports/italy-pay-addendum.md` fills Italian pay gaps across all branches. `progress.md` is the run log.

**No fourteenth branch was added.** The two branches newcomers ask about most that the brief did not list are **Sales & Business Development** and **Human Resources**. Rather than spend a sub-agent on a separate report, sales and BD were added as a full role family in Marketing (3.8: SDR/BDR → account executive, key account management, with OTE pay), and HR as a full role family in Management (3.4). Sales is the larger entry door for business graduates and is the one to promote to its own report if the site needs it.

## 2. Shared scales and conventions

- **Stress 1-5:** 1 = low, predictable deadlines; 2 = occasional crunch; 3 = regular deadlines and steady pressure; 4 = frequent high-stakes pressure driven by clients, markets or incidents; 5 = sustained extreme pressure (e.g. junior M&A on live deals, trading through volatile markets).
- **People/communication 1-5:** 1 = mostly solo work; 3 = regular team and stakeholder communication; 5 = the job is mainly relationships, persuasion and client work.
- **Quantitative/technical 1-5:** 1 = basic numeracy; 3 = solid spreadsheets, modelling or statistics, some scripting; 5 = advanced maths, statistics or programming is the core of the job.
- **Entry difficulty 1-5:** 1 = many openings, open to most graduates; 3 = competitive, needs a relevant internship or demonstrable skills; 5 = extremely selective (low single-digit acceptance rates, target schools or elite credentials).
- **Background fit:** strong (standard feeder) / possible (hired regularly with extra preparation) / stretch (rare, needs a deliberate route).
- **Tier lists:** Tier 1/2/3 or the industry's own labels (bulge bracket / elite boutique / middle market, MBB / tier-2, Big 4 / mid-tier). Every tier list carries the line "Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact."
- **Pay:** local currency, with base or total stated, city, year and source; no currency conversion, because rates move. "No reliable data found" is used instead of guesses.
