Artificial intelligence careers are about building, studying and governing systems that learn from data, from classic machine-learning models to the large language models (LLMs) behind chat assistants and agents. Most jobs are engineering jobs: training and deploying models, building products on top of foundation models, and running the GPU infrastructure that serves them. A smaller set are research, safety and evaluation roles at frontier labs, universities and AI safety institutes, and a growing set are customer-facing, product and governance roles that turn AI into deployed, compliant products. Pay at the frontier is extreme for a small elite, while the entry-level market is tough and crowded, and much AI data-labelling work is low-paid and precarious; the reports below separate these realities rather than averaging them.

Scope notes: researched in two parts (building AI systems; research, frontier and governance roles). General software engineering, data science and data analytics have their own reports. Part 2 drew most of its pay-band evidence from published US job postings, many of them Anthropic's because that employer publishes ranges; treat those figures as one employer's data points, not as the market, and see the gaps list in ../index.md.
