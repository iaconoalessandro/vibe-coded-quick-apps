### (a) Ratings by background (whole branch)

| Background | Overall fit | One-line reason |
|---|---|---|
| Management | stretch | Fits AI product management, consulting and governance; engineering, research and safety research are out of reach without a technical degree. |
| Logistics & Supply Chain | stretch | Domain knowledge helps in forecasting and enterprise deployments, but every core role needs programming and ML. |
| Finance | stretch | Realistic doors are AI consulting, model-risk and governance roles in banks, and finance-domain deployment work; engineering needs a reskill. |
| Accounting | stretch | Governance, compliance and audit of AI systems are plausible; technical roles need a deliberate reskill. |
| Marketing | stretch | Some AI product roles and AI data work; research and engineering are far away. |
| Data Analytics | possible | SQL, Python and data skills transfer to AI engineering, MLOps, evaluations support and AI PM; ML theory and systems are the gap. |
| Economics | possible | Statistics and econometrics are a good base for ML and for AI policy and economics research; software engineering must be added. |
| Computer Science | strong | The default route into engineering, research engineering, forward-deployed and evaluation roles; research science usually adds a PhD. |
| Cybersecurity | possible | Strong for AI red teaming, security evaluations and secure AI infrastructure; ML knowledge must be added for most other roles. |
| Data Science | strong | Modelling skills transfer directly to ML engineering, evaluations and research; production engineering is the usual gap. |
| Artificial Intelligence | strong | The direct match for every role family, though everyone with this degree competes for the same scarce entry seats. |
