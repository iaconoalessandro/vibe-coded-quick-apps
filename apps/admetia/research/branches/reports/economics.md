## 1. What this branch is

This branch covers jobs where you are hired as an economist, or specifically for economic analysis: you use economic theory and statistics (called *econometrics*) to answer questions such as "will inflation fall?", "would this merger raise prices?", "did this policy work?" or "how should a marketplace set prices?". The work happens in six main settings: central banks, international organisations (IMF, World Bank, OECD), governments and statistical offices, economic consultancies that advise companies and courts, technology companies, and think tanks or universities. Pay ranges from a French-tax-exempt young-associate stipend to US tech packages above USD 200,000, but the single biggest divider is the degree: many of the best-known economist seats (central-bank research, IMF Economist Program, big-tech economist, university professor) require a PhD, while analyst seats in government, consulting, think tanks and as research assistants are open to bachelor's and master's graduates. The work is mostly writing, coding and arguing with data, not forecasting headlines; hours are lighter than in banking or management consulting except in litigation consulting.

Scope notes: global, with the US (Washington DC), UK, Italy, Frankfurt/Brussels/Paris/Basel and Hong Kong/Singapore/Tokyo covered; Gulf institutions and the Bank of Japan have almost no verifiable entry data and are flagged. Finance-sector macro roles, data science and management consulting are cross-referenced only. Ratings (stress, people, quant, difficulty) are my judgement from the evidence and the shared scales, not survey results. Hours are mostly practitioner-consensus ranges, not measured; no systematic hours survey for economists was found. Several employer pages (IMF, OECD, World Bank PDFs, AEA memo PDF) could not be read directly and are marked.

## 2. Map of areas and sectors

| Sub-area | Who employs economists | How it is funded / makes money | Typical entry degree |
|---|---|---|---|
| Central banks and monetary institutions | Fed Board and 12 Reserve Banks, ECB and euro-area national banks (Bank of Italy, Bundesbank, Banque de France), Bank of England, BIS, MAS, HKMA, BoJ, Gulf central banks | Seigniorage and interest income on assets; the central bank is not profit-driven | Bachelor's/master's for RA and analyst; PhD for research economist |
| International organisations (IOs) | IMF, World Bank Group, OECD, regional banks (EIB, EBRD, ADB), UN agencies | Member-government contributions; development banks also earn on lending | Master's (mid-career) / PhD (IMF Economist Program); bachelor's for IMF Research Analyst and OECD Young Associates |
| Government economists and statistical offices | UK Government Economic Service (GES), US BEA, BLS, CBO, Treasury; Italy's MEF and ISTAT; EU Commission (DG ECFIN) | Tax-funded civil-service budgets | Bachelor's/master's |
| Economic consulting | Compass Lexecon, CRA, Analysis Group, Cornerstone Research, NERA, Brattle (US); Oxera, Frontier Economics, RBB Economics, CEPA (Europe/UK) | Clients pay hourly or project fees (see below) | Bachelor's/master's analysts; PhD associates |
| Financial-sector economists | Bank and asset-manager macro/strategy teams | Banks/asset managers fund the team as research or marketing support | Master's; PhD for senior roles (cross-ref Finance branch) |
| Think tanks and policy research | Brookings, PIIE, Bruegel, IFS, Resolution Foundation, CEPR, CEPS | Donors, foundations, memberships, commissioned research | Master's; PhD for senior research roles |
| Tech-sector economists | Amazon, Uber, Microsoft, Airbnb, Meta, Google, Netflix | Company revenue; economists sit inside product, marketplace or policy teams | PhD (strongly preferred) |
| Academia and pre-docs | Universities and research institutes; pre-docs are one- to two-year research-assistant (RA) posts with professors | Tuition, public research grants, endowments | PhD for faculty; bachelor's/master's for pre-docs |

**Central banks and monetary institutions.** A central bank runs monetary policy (interest rates, balance sheet), often supervises banks, and employs hundreds of economists. Three profiles coexist. *Research economists* (nearly always PhDs) write academic working papers and publish in journals, and also advise on policy. *Policy or analyst economists* prepare forecasts, briefing notes for the governor or rate-setting committee and internal analysis, usually on a master's or a PhD. *Supervisory economists* assess banks' risk and capital, work that overlaps with finance and risk. The Fed Board's own page says nearly all its economists work in the divisions of Financial Stability, International Finance, Monetary Affairs and Research & Statistics, with others in Supervision and Regulation and Consumer and Community Affairs, and that about 150 research assistants support them (Federal Reserve Board, accessed October 2026). The ECB lists four economist graduate posts for 2026, against 15 generalist posts (prior library research, accessed October 2026 and ECB page). The BIS in Basel, described as the "central bank for central banks", employs economists in Basel plus Hong Kong and Mexico City offices (BIS careers, accessed October 2026).

**International organisations.** The IMF monitors member economies (the annual "Article IV consultation" is a country review you may draft a part of), lends in crises and publishes flagship forecasts. The World Bank Group lends and advises on development, and economists cover poverty, trade, public finance and sectors from health to energy. The OECD (Paris) is a think tank for rich-country governments: it produces country surveys, statistics and policy reviews. Regional development banks (EIB in Luxembourg, EBRD in London, ADB in Manila) combine lending with economic research. UN agencies and the UN Secretariat employ economists in development and statistics roles, but their young-professional exam is closed to some nationalities (see 3.2).

**Government economists and statistical offices.** UK GES economists sit in ministries (Treasury, Defra, Transport) and write the economic cases that justify spending decisions. US federal economists work at BLS (labour statistics), BEA (GDP and national accounts), the Congressional Budget Office (CBO; scores the budget effects of bills), Treasury and many agencies. Italian equivalents are the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MEF) and the national statistics institute ISTAT. The European Commission's DG ECFIN (Directorate-General for Economic and Financial Affairs) writes the EU's economic forecasts; it hires via EPSO, the EU's central recruitment office. BLS counts 18,600 US economists in 2025; 26% work for the federal government, 22% in management/scientific/technical consulting, 14% for state government, 9% local government and 6% in scientific research and development (BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook, May 2025 data).

**Economic consulting.** These firms are hired by companies, law firms and regulators for competition (antitrust, mergers), litigation (damages in commercial disputes, class actions, intellectual property), and regulation (utilities, telecoms, energy). Practice varies by region: US firms (Compass Lexecon, CRA, Analysis Group, Cornerstone Research, NERA, Brattle) are driven by litigation and class-action work; European/UK firms (Oxera, Frontier Economics, RBB Economics, CEPA) are more competition, regulation and public-policy focused (grouping from the prior library report and firm graduate pages; "tier" labels are perception). Firms bill clients by the hour or project, with partners and academic "affiliates" acting as *expert witnesses*, specialists who give opinion evidence in court or before regulators. Juniors do the data and modelling behind those reports.

**Financial-sector economists.** Bank and asset-manager economists publish forecasts for clients and advise portfolio managers. Teams are small, mostly masters/PhD holders; pay is above public-sector pay but the job is closer to markets than to research. Covered in the Finance branch; here only compared in section 4.

**Think tanks and policy research.** Brookings, the Peterson Institute for International Economics (PIIE) in Washington, Bruegel in Brussels, the Institute for Fiscal Studies (IFS) and Resolution Foundation in London, CEPR and CEPS across Europe write papers and briefings that shape policy debates. Funding is from donors, governments and subscriptions, so pay is lower and job security is weaker.

**Tech-sector economists.** Amazon, Uber, Airbnb and Microsoft employ PhD economists to price products, design marketplaces and auctions, measure advertising effectiveness (called *incrementality*) and run experiments. Amazon's job posts mention causal inference and structural demand models for Prime Video, marketing measurement and stores economics (Amazon.jobs listings, 2026). Uber states that it wants economists to be economists, not generic data scientists (Uber PhD-graduate postings, 2024-26). This overlaps with the Data Science branch.

**Academia and pre-docs.** A PhD takes about 5-6 years in the US, 3-4 in much of Europe (longer in the UK at top schools). Graduates enter the "job market": a once-a-year process where candidates present a *job market paper* (their best single research paper) and are interviewed for tenure-track posts (see 3.7). *Pre-docs* are one- to two-year research assistant posts after a bachelor's or master's, used to prepare for a PhD. The US pre-doc market has grown rapidly; I found no systematic placement statistics.

## 3. Role families

### 3.1 Central bank economist (research and policy tracks)

- **What you actually do**: On the research track, you write working papers (pre-publication drafts) on topics such as inflation, labour markets or bank lending, estimate models in Stata, R, Python or MATLAB, present at internal seminars and external conferences and try to publish in journals; the Fed Board states its economists present at conferences, publish in peer-reviewed journals and circulate working papers (Fed Board, October 2026). On the policy track, you maintain forecasting models (for example a nowcast, an estimate of today's GDP or inflation from the latest data) and write briefing notes for the policy committee (FOMC at the Fed, Governing Council at the ECB, MPC at the Bank of England). The ECB graduate economist works on analytical and policy work, improves analytical tools, prepares groundwork for policy decisions and communications and represents the ECB externally (ECB graduate page, 2026). Research assistants (RAs) at the Fed prepare data for memos, run literature searches, draft limited-complexity analytical memos and handle data requests (Fed RA page, October 2026). Supervisory economists review banks' risk models and stress tests.
- **A typical day and week**: Illustrative composite, not a measured schedule. Monday: update the data pipeline and nowcast in R or Python; Tuesday: draft two pages for the governor's briefing; Wednesday: seminar where a colleague's working paper is critiqued; Thursday: coding the model or regressions; Friday: meeting with the policy division. In the weeks before a rate decision, forecast rounds and briefing deadlines tighten. Researchers have weeks of protected time for papers.
- **Hours, stress and lifestyle**: Typical 40-50 hours, peak 55-60 around forecast rounds and policy meetings (author judgement from practitioner descriptions; no survey found). Stress 2/5 for research, 3/5 for policy or crisis periods. Flexibility is good: most central banks offer hybrid work, and travel is occasional (conferences, committee meetings). Fed DC jobs are in-person in Washington (Fed Board, October 2026).
- **Human vs. quantitative profile**: People/communication: 3/5. Quantitative/technical: 4/5. Research economists spend most time alone on code and writing; policy economists must turn technical results into short memos for non-economists, so writing matters as much as modelling.
- **Compensation (approximate)**: *US*: Fed Board does not publish role pay on the pages I read; its 2025 FR salary structure runs from FR-16 (USD 36,400-44,400) up to FR-31 (up to USD 295,200), with no mapping to RA or PhD-economist jobs on the page (Fed Board salary page, 2025). A 2026 San Francisco Fed (a regional Reserve Bank) listing shows USD 80,000 for an economic research associate (Built In listing, 2026, snippet-level); a university career page cites about USD 60,000 for Fed RAs from Glassdoor (undated, unverified). *Euro area*: ECB Economist Graduate Programme, band E/F step 1, EUR 4,982 net per month plus benefits (about EUR 59,800 net per year over 12 months; ECB page, 2026 cycle). *UK*: Bank of England graduate programme GBP 33,525 (Leeds, 2026 listing, TargetJobs) and GBP 36,500 in London/32,750 Leeds for 2025 (Prospects); the Economist stream salary was not separated; the Bank's PhD Research Programme was advertised at GBP 60,000 but was not recruiting for 2025/26 (BoE research careers page, snippet-level). *Italy*: Bank of Italy Esperto (graduate-level) starting pay is reported at EUR 50,126 gross per year (money.it, 18 December 2025; secondary and unverified; the official notice does not state pay). *Asia*: HKMA Graduate Economist, HK$ 49,000-50,000+ fixed monthly pay plus lump-sum on completion (HKMA vacancy and programme page, 2026; currency symbol shown as "$"). ~5 years and senior: no reliable data found for any central bank; Fed senior pay can reach the upper FR grades, but role mapping is not published.
- **Career path**: RA/analyst (0-2 years, often leaving for a PhD) -> economist (PhD, year 0-5) -> senior economist (5-10) -> principal economist/adviser -> division head/director. At the ECB the graduate programme lasts two years with an option for a third (ECB page). Bank of Italy is a permanent civil-service-style career entered by competitive exam (concorso).
- **Exit opportunities**: After 2-3 years (RA): PhD programmes (the Fed says over half of RAs enter graduate programmes, mainly economics or finance PhDs), government or consulting. After 7-10 years (economist): IMF/World Bank, bank chief-economist roles, academia, economic consulting, tech.
- **Tier list of employers**: *Tier 1 (research-heavy, global policy influence)*: Federal Reserve Board and New York Fed, ECB, Bank of England, BIS. *Tier 2*: other Reserve Banks, Bank of Italy, Bundesbank, Banque de France, Bank of Japan, MAS, HKMA, Bank of Canada. *Tier 3*: smaller or emerging-market central banks and financial supervisors, where analytical depth, publishing culture and PhD intensity vary widely. Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact. Gulf central banks and sovereign entities: no reliable data found on entry routes or pay.
- **How to enter**: *Degrees*: PhD for research economist (ECB graduate economist requires a master's plus PhD in economics completed in the window, EU nationality, C1 English; Fed economist requires a PhD or dissertation in progress); bachelor's or master's for RA/analyst (Fed RA: bachelor's or master's in economics, statistics, maths, finance, data science or similar, US citizenship required); master's for many policy-analyst roles. *Programmes and calendar*: Fed RA applications Aug 17-Oct 15 per the Fed page, interviews Oct-Nov and Feb-May, rolling offers, start in June/July, two-year term. ECB Economist Graduate Programme 2026: application closed 6 January 2026, up to four posts, two-year fixed term from 1 September, written exercise, presentation, interview; the generalist graduate programme (19 places in total) is a separate track for master's graduates (library research, ECB page). BIS Graduate Programme: contract up to 11 months, salary net of Swiss income tax, advertised in spring; PhD fellowship in late summer (BIS pages). HKMA Graduate Economist: two-year programme, postgraduate degree in economics, finance or similar, 2026 deadline 9 January 2026, essay (about 300 words), aptitude test, written test, interview. Bank of Italy: Esperto economics profile December 2025 notice, 45 places, master's at 105/110 or above in listed classes (LM-77, LM-56, LM-16, LM-52, LM-62), 8,307 applications (about 185 per place), 60-question preselection, top 500 to written, top 400 to oral including English; separate PhD research fellowships for economists (six fellows, 12 months renewable once, October 2025 call). *Interview formats*: technical presentation of research, written exercise, policy memo. *Skills*: econometrics (time series, panel, causal inference), Stata/R/Python/MATLAB, clear writing. *Common mistakes*: applying with a master's to PhD-only research seats; missing the narrow window; ignoring nationality rules (EU-only at ECB; US-citizen only at the Fed RA). *Alternative routes*: RA jobs at the Fed or central bank, then PhD; for Italians, a concorso is the route and grade floors are hard. Entry difficulty: 4/5 (RA/analyst) to 5/5 (research PhD seats at tier 1).
- **Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for**: Policy impact is often indirect; senior decisions are made by committees. Research seats are PhD-gated, and the best ones are scarce (ECB: four economist posts in 2026). Pay is below consulting and tech, and in Italy it is tied to public scales. Public-exam routes (Bank of Italy) filter on grades and test performance more than on skills. Not a good fit if you want fast pay growth, client-facing work, or are not willing to commit to years of technical writing.

### 3.2 Economist at an international organisation (IMF/World Bank/OECD/development banks)

- **What you actually do**: IMF country economists draft parts of Article IV reports, a yearly assessment of a member country's economy, and estimate fiscal, monetary and external-sector scenarios; the IMF Economist Program rotates PhDs through country surveillance and programmes, policy analysis and research for flagship publications (IMF EP page, October 2026). World Bank economists write country economic memoranda, evaluate project impact and advise governments on reforms. OECD economists draft country surveys and statistical series. Tools: Stata, R, Python, spreadsheets and macro models; deliverables are reports, briefing notes and missions where you meet ministry officials.
- **A typical day and week**: Illustrative composite. Weeks at HQ: data work, drafting a chapter and internal review meetings; mission weeks (one to two weeks, several times a year) mean travel to the member country for meetings with the finance ministry and central bank. A deadline-heavy "report week" can run late.
- **Hours, stress and lifestyle**: Typical 40-50 hours, peaks 55-60 during missions and report deadlines (author judgement). Stress 3/5. Travel is substantial for IMF/World Bank (several missions a year; the IMF programme specifically includes surveillance missions) and lower at the OECD. Remote/hybrid is common but HQ-based. IO staff often live abroad on expatriate benefits.
- **Human vs. quantitative profile**: People/communication: 4/5. Quantitative/technical: 4/5. You must negotiate with ministry officials and convey politically sensitive messages, not only run models.
- **Compensation (approximate)**: *IMF*: the most recent public salary table I could retrieve is dated 1 May 2017: entry-level PhD economists sit at grade A11 (USD 95,090-142,610), economists at A12-A13 (A12 USD 108,670-163,000; A13 USD 124,620-186,950); these are old and current pay is higher (IMF Annual Report Web Table 3.4, 2017; 2025 table not retrievable). IMF salaries are generally not subject to national income tax and carry an internal staff assessment; I could not confirm current detail (unverified, general knowledge). *World Bank*: net-of-tax scales; US headquarters, effective 1 July 2023: GF USD 101,700-189,000 (midpoint 145,300), GG USD 131,900-245,000 (midpoint 188,500), GH USD 177,600-330,000 (World Bank Group net salary scale, 2023). FY27 scales exist but the PDF was unreadable (World Bank FY27 scales, 2026). Economists enter around GF (YPP is GF-level). *OECD*: Young Associates EUR 3,886 per month exempt from French income tax, plus leaving allowance around EUR 21,000 (OECD YAP page via Wayback capture of 1 September 2026, library research); senior OECD economist pay: no reliable data found. *EU institutions*: AD5 basic EUR 6,152.64 per month from 1 July 2025 (Council doc 16220/25, library research); net about EUR 4,859 with no allowances and EUR 5,870 with expatriation allowance (AMLA worked example, a Frankfurt EU agency; Brussels may differ by a few per cent). *EIB*: graduate programme at the minimum of grade level 3; EUR 56,125 reported by a search snippet (unverified). Italy: Italian JPO (Junior Professional Officer, state-funded) pay is set by the host organisation: no reliable data found. ~5 years and senior: IMF grades A12-A13 (2017 bands above) for economists and A14+ for division chiefs.
- **Career path**: Research analyst or intern (0-2 years) -> Economist (PhD entrant, IMF Economist Program, 3 years) -> Senior Economist (5-8) -> Division/unit chief or advisor (10-15) -> Director. World Bank YPP: graduate degree plus 2-6 years' experience, two-year programme with three 8-month rotations, then a role at least a further 5 years on completion (World Bank YPP page, October 2026, via library research).
- **Exit opportunities**: After 2-3 years: PhD, ministries, central banks, consulting. After 7-10 years: senior posts in governments (finance ministry, central bank), regional banks, private-sector chief economist roles, academia.
- **Tier list of employers**: *Tier 1*: IMF, World Bank, OECD (policy influence, high selectivity). *Tier 2*: regional development banks (EIB, EBRD, ADB, IDB, AfDB), BIS, specialised UN bodies (UNCTAD, UNDP economics units). *Tier 3*: smaller UN agencies, NGOs and consultancies working on IO projects. Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact.
- **How to enter**: *Degrees*: PhD for IMF Economist Program (recent PhD or within one year of completing, member-country national, under 34 when joining, apply by 4 December 2026 for September 2027 start; three-year programme with two 18-month assignments); bachelor's for IMF Research Analyst Program (recent bachelor's, GPA at least 3.5/4.0, two-year contract in Washington, apply by 30 December 2026) and OECD Young Associates (bachelor's only, no master's or PhD allowed, OECD-member nationality, GPA 3.0/4.0, 8-12 participants a year, applications Nov-Dec); master's plus 2-6 years for World Bank YPP (2027 cohort window was 1-30 September 2026, which has closed; start September 2027). UN YPP: bachelor's accepted, age 32 or younger, but only nationals of listed countries; Italy was not on the 2026 list (careers.un.org, October 2026). Italians can use the state-funded JPO programme (45 Italian places in 2026, born 1 January 1995 or later, master's; deadline was 5 May 2026). *Interview formats*: IMF EP has a preliminary interview, paper submission with three references, panel interview and written assessment; offers in February. *Skills*: macro and public finance, econometrics, writing, languages. *Common mistakes*: assuming a master's can enter the Economist Program (it cannot); missing narrow annual windows; ignoring nationality rules. *Alternative routes*: IMF RAP, OECD internships (about EUR 1,000 per month), EU Blue Book traineeships, development-bank graduate programmes (EIB GRAD, under two years since graduation), national secondments. Entry difficulty: 5/5 (Economist Program, YPP; no published acceptance rates).
- **Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for**: Extremely selective with narrow windows and nationality gates; IO pay is high but the cost of living in Washington, Paris or Luxembourg takes a chunk; slow, consensus-based work; heavy travel; promotion is slow past mid-grades; UN hiring was down (external recruitment to geographical posts was only 104 in 2024, from the library report). Not a good fit if you dislike bureaucracy or cannot relocate.

### 3.3 Government economist / official statistician

- **What you actually do**: UK GES economists produce cost-benefit analysis for spending proposals, evaluate programmes, advise ministers on policy trade-offs and write the economic sections of business cases. US BLS/BEA statisticians produce the monthly jobs/CPI and national accounts data, with large-scale data processing, seasonal adjustment and revisions; CBO analysts model the budget impact of bills; Treasury economists work on tax and international policy. In Italy, ISTAT researchers and technologists build national statistics; MEF staff cover public finance and tax analysis. Tools are Excel, Stata/R, SQL, increasingly Python.
- **A typical day and week**: Illustrative composite. Morning: refresh data, check outliers in a series; midday: team meeting on a ministerial request; afternoon: write a two-page options note; weekly submission for a minister's deadline. Statistical offices follow a rigid release calendar (publication day is fixed and errors are public).
- **Hours, stress and lifestyle**: Typical 37-45 hours, peaks 50-55 for ministerial deadlines, budget rounds or release days (author judgement). Stress 2/5, rising to 3/5 in spending reviews. Stable, hybrid-friendly, low travel. 
- **Human vs. quantitative profile**: People/communication: 3/5. Quantitative/technical: 3/5. Policy economists must explain trade-offs to non-economists; official statisticians skew toward 4/5 quantitative.
- **Compensation (approximate)**: *UK*: GES Assistant Economist GBP 34,123 per year (2026 listing, JobsGoPublic aggregator); GBP 31,500 for the 2025 scheme; the Civil Service Fast Stream starts at GBP 31,554 (Civil Service Careers, 2 October 2026, library research). After training GBP 45,000-55,000 (Planit, undated, indicative). Grade 7 national minimum GBP 52,120 or 56,375 depending on the Defra snapshot; a commentary places Treasury/Cabinet Office G7 roles at the higher end of GBP 55,000-75,000 (secondary, unverified). *US*: BLS median for all economists USD 124,720 (May 2025); federal government median USD 144,680; state government USD 78,080; consulting USD 141,010 (BLS OOH, 2026). GS-9 (a typical master's entry grade) in Washington, DC 2026: about USD 70,623 step 1; GS-11 USD 85,447 step 1, USD 111,087 step 10; GS-12 USD 102,415-133,142 (OPM 2026 DC table via third-party reproduction; CBO uses its own scale, not checked). *Italy*: Bank of Italy Esperto about EUR 50,126 gross (secondary, unverified); MEF and ISTAT pay: no reliable data found. *EU*: AD5 about EUR 4,859-5,870 net per month (above). 
- **Career path**: GES: Assistant Economist (0-3 years, promoted to Economic Adviser within about 3 years per a 2022-23 GES outreach deck, salary GBP 45k+ ) -> Grade 7 Senior Economic Adviser (5-8 years) -> Grade 6 Deputy Director (10+). US: GS-7/9 economist -> GS-11/12 (3-5 years) -> GS-13/14 supervisory. Italy: permanent civil servant by concorso, progression by seniority and further exams.
- **Exit opportunities**: After 2-3 years: PhD, regulator, consulting, central bank. After 7-10 years: consulting, think tanks, international organisations, bank economist roles, senior civil service.
- **Tier list of employers**: *Tier 1*: HM Treasury, US Treasury, CBO, EU DG ECFIN and similar central policy shops that publish influential numbers. *Tier 2*: spending departments (UK Defra, DfT, DWP), BLS and BEA, ISTAT, MEF, national ministries. *Tier 3*: local-government and state-level units, where scope and pay are narrower (BLS: state government median USD 78,080, local USD 100,370, 2025). Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact.
- **How to enter**: *Degrees*: UK GES requires a first or 2:1 in economics, a joint degree with at least half economics, or a postgraduate economics degree; micro and macro required; no visa sponsorship; Civil Service nationality rules apply; 50 hours a year of economics CPD (continuing professional development) plus 50 hours of personal skills (gov.uk, 2026). The 2026 window was 3-27 February 2026; the next round is likely early 2027 (gov.uk, closed page). US federal: bachelor's possible at GS-5/7, master's typical at GS-9; USAJOBS and the Pathways programme; citizenship usually required. Italy: concorsi via the InPA portal (a state recruitment platform with SPID identity); ISTAT's 2026 call offered 54 researcher and 46 technologist posts plus 95 technical collaborators (closed 22-23 January 2026; sources disagree: one outlet reports 100 posts), EU/Italian citizenship required; MEF's concorso closed 27 January 2026 with 485 funzionari posts (secondary, unverified) and a 40-question, 60-minute multiple-choice test. EU: EPSO competitions; the generalist AD5 competition EPSO/AD/427/26 drew 174,727 applicants for 1,490 places, 45% of them Italian, with reasoning tests from November 2026 (EPSO via library research); DG ECFIN economists are typically recruited through EPSO specialist competitions (unverified). *Interview formats*: online tests (numerical, verbal, situational), written exercises, panel interviews; Italian concorsi use written exams, preselection and orals. *Skills*: cost-benefit analysis, Excel, Stata/R, writing short briefs. *Common mistakes*: missing the nationality rules; applying to the UK Fast Stream as an EU graduate without settled status (not possible without right to work). *Alternative routes*: statistical offices as a back door (data skills valued), contract-agent database (CAST) for EU institutions, graduate internships. Entry difficulty: 3/5 (UK/US), 4/5 for oversubscribed EU and Italian competitions.
- **Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for**: Pay growth is slow; entry in Italy and the EU is a lottery-like exam with huge applicant pools; political direction constrains your conclusions; US federal roles are exposed to budget and hiring-freeze cycles (general observation, not sourced). Not a good fit if you want rapid pay or autonomy over research questions.

### 3.4 Economic consultant (competition, litigation, regulation)

- **What you actually do**: You build the empirical analysis behind an expert witness's report. In competition cases you estimate whether a merger would raise prices (using *merger simulation*, a model of how rivals react after a merger) or whether a cartel caused overcharges; in litigation you calculate damages; in regulation you analyse costs and returns of utilities. CRA's analyst posting lists researching economic and industry issues, building valuation models, running regressions in Excel, Stata, R, Python or SQL, summarising reports, depositions and academic literature, and quality-checking expert reports, tables and charts (CRA posting, 2026). Juniors do data cleaning and coding; PhD associates design the econometric strategy and draft expert reports.
- **A typical day and week**: Illustrative composite. Monday: clean a dataset of transactions received from the client's lawyers; Tuesday-Wednesday: run regressions and sensitivity checks; Thursday: summarise findings in a slide deck for the case team; Friday: review a draft expert report. The weeks before a filing deadline or hearing are intense, with weekend work.
- **Hours, stress and lifestyle**: Typical 45-55 hours, peak 65-80 before filings or trials (library research reports 45-60 hours with spikes to 75, unverified practitioner figure). Stress 4/5: client and legal deadlines drive crunch. Travel is moderate (client sites, depositions). CRA expects 3-4 office days a week (CRA posting, 2026).
- **Human vs. quantitative profile**: People/communication: 3/5. Quantitative/technical: 4/5. Strong coding and clear writing for lawyers; client interaction grows with seniority.
- **Compensation (approximate)**: *US analyst (bachelor's/master's), base*: CRA USD 92,500-100,000 (2027 graduates, posted 2026); Analysis Group Analyst USD 95,000-100,000 (Analysis Group pay page via search summary, undated); Cornerstone Research Analyst USD 112,500 base plus starting bonus (University of Florida career hub listing, recruiting from August 2026). *US PhD hires*: NERA Consultant (new PhD) USD 220,000-235,000 base (Built In posting, undated); Analysis Group Associate USD 180,000-240,000; Cornerstone Associate (Menlo Park) USD 195,000-245,000 (May 2026 posting). Glassdoor-based guide: median total pay for US economic consultants about USD 170,000 (2026, all levels, aggregator). *UK/Europe*: analyst GBP 48,000-62,000 plus 10-25% bonus; senior analyst GBP 65,000-80,000; PhD associate GBP 85,000-110,000 (library research; secondary and unverified - Frontier, Oxera and RBB do not publish salaries on the pages I found; Frontier lists pay as "competitive"). *Italy*: no reliable data found. *Asia/Gulf*: no reliable data found.
- **Career path**: Analyst/research analyst (0-3 years, often a two- to three-year programme) -> Senior Analyst or Associate (PhD entrants start here) -> Manager/Senior Consultant (4-7 years) -> Vice President/Principal -> Partner/Affiliate expert (10+ years, usually a PhD and academic profile). Partner pay is uncapped and tied to origination of cases; data not available.
- **Exit opportunities**: After 2-3 years: top economics PhD programmes (a major route), competition authorities (UK CMA, DG COMP), law, corporate strategy. After 7-10 years: big-tech antitrust and regulatory teams, regulators, hedge funds, academia.
- **Tier list of employers**: *Tier 1*: the global pure-plays often cited by practitioners: Compass Lexecon, CRA, Cornerstone Research, Analysis Group, NERA, Brattle. *Tier 2*: European/UK specialists: Oxera, Frontier Economics, RBB Economics, CEPA; big-four and mid-sized practices (economic modelling and valuation). *Tier 3*: smaller boutiques and in-house economics units at law firms. Differences: tier 1 get the big antitrust and class-action cases and have academic affiliates; European firms lean to regulation and policy. Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact.
- **How to enter**: *Degrees*: bachelor's or master's for analyst (CRA recruits December 2026 or summer 2027 graduates with a quantitative degree; honours thesis or research apprenticeship highly desired); PhD for associate. In Europe, a master's with strong econometrics (MSc/MPhil in Economics from a research-intensive university) is the standard credential; MBA or MiM without graduate econometrics are screened out (library research, unverified). *Calendar*: US analyst recruiting runs August-autumn for the following summer (CRA review from 17 August 2026, rolling; Cornerstone began 3 August 2026); PhD hires recruit around the AEA job market in January. *Interview formats*: resume screen, technical econometrics questions, a case or written test with data, behavioural interviews. *Skills*: Stata/R/Python/SQL, applied micro and industrial organisation (IO, the study of competition among firms), writing for lawyers. *Common mistakes*: generic consulting prep instead of econometrics prep; no coding sample or thesis; late applications. *Alternative routes*: research assistantships with professors, statistical office or regulator internships, master's thesis with data work. Entry difficulty: 4/5 (analyst at tier 1 firms), 4/5 for PhD associate (competitive).
- **Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for**: Billable-hour pressure and crunch before filings; the work is advocacy-adjacent (analysis is tailored to the client's side); junior work can be repetitive data cleaning; pay for analysts is lower than banking. Not a good fit if you dislike deadlines, working for a side, or want to do academic-style open research.

### 3.5 Tech-sector economist

- **What you actually do**: Design and test pricing, incentives and marketplace rules; estimate demand with *structural models* (models derived from economic theory) and causal effects from experiments (A/B tests) and observational data. Amazon listings describe causal inference for product selection, cost-to-serve and delivery speed, structural demand models for Prime Video investments, incrementality measurement for ads and labour-market analysis (Amazon.jobs, 2026). Uber's economists design pricing, incentives and subscription structures, run marketplace experiments and ML-driven optimisation; its policy-economics applied scientist role covers driver and courier policy (Uber postings, 2024-26). Airbnb's senior policy economists support regulation, housing, tourism and tax analysis (Airbnb posting, 2026). You write code in Python/R/SQL and present to product managers.
- **A typical day and week**: Illustrative composite. Review overnight experiment results; refine an estimator; two meetings with product managers; write a one-page memo with a recommendation; weekly team read-out with engineers and data scientists.
- **Hours, stress and lifestyle**: Typical 40-50 hours, peaks 55-60 around launches and quarterly planning (author judgement). Stress 3/5. Flexible, usually hybrid; limited travel. Layoff and reorganisation risk is real in tech (general observation, not sourced).
- **Human vs. quantitative profile**: People/communication: 4/5. Quantitative/technical: 5/5. You must sell a model to product managers and engineers, and coding is expected.
- **Compensation (approximate)**: *US (Amazon, Levels.fyi, updated 9 October 2026, 16 submissions)*: L4 median total compensation about USD 211,921; L5 about USD 295,812; overall median pay mix USD 147,000 base, USD 56,000 stock, USD 44,750 bonus. A snapshot of 11 August 2026 gave L4 USD 218,600, L5 USD 295,800 (the two vary, small sample). *Uber*: policy-economics applied scientist base USD 131,000-138,000 in San Francisco (Uber posting, undated). *Airbnb*: Senior Policy Economist USD 179,000-210,000; staff-level USD 194,000-240,000 (aggregator listings, approximate). INOMICS found the PhD premium among economists was 96% in 2023 (and about 50% in 2022) (INOMICS Salary Report). UK/Europe: no reliable data found. Asia: no reliable data found.
- **Career path**: Economist/Scientist (PhD entry, L4) -> Senior Economist (L5, about 3-5 years) -> Principal/Tech Lead (L6, 8-12 years) -> Director/Chief Economist. Levels vary by company.
- **Exit opportunities**: After 2-3 years: other tech, consulting, central banks, tenure-track academia. After 7-10 years: chief economist, product/strategy leadership, startup founding, antitrust expert roles.
- **Tier list of employers**: *Tier 1*: Amazon (the largest economist team), Uber, Airbnb, Microsoft, Meta, Google, Netflix - perception based on size of economist teams and publication culture. *Tier 2*: Lyft, Spotify, eBay, Etsy, Booking, Zalando, fintechs. *Tier 3*: startups with one economist. Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact.
- **How to enter**: *Degrees*: PhD (economics or similar) is the main route; Amazon lists a PhD in economics or equivalent as basic qualification for its marketing economists; Uber's marketing applied scientist role accepts PhD, MS or bachelor's with years of industry experience; Airbnb counts a PhD as roughly two years' experience for its senior role. *Calendar*: PhD candidates apply through the AEA job market (Uber's "AEA/ASSA economists" postings), fall-winter, with interviews in January; experienced hires apply all year. *Interview formats*: job-talk-style presentation of your research, coding screen (SQL/Python), causal-inference and experimentation questions, behavioural interviews. *Skills*: causal inference, experimentation, structural estimation, Python/SQL, ability to communicate to non-economists. *Common mistakes*: pure-theory PhD with no applied data skills; weak coding. *Alternative routes*: master's plus industry data science, then move into economist track (rare and role-dependent), pre-doc plus PhD. Entry difficulty: 5/5 (PhD from a strong programme practically required for the best seats).
- **Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for**: Many tech economists are used as senior data scientists; impact depends on product priorities; publishing is constrained. Compensation depends heavily on stock, which moves with the share price. Not a good fit if you want policy influence or academic freedom.

### 3.6 Think-tank / policy researcher

- **What you actually do**: Write policy briefs and papers, build data analysis for reports, present at events and brief journalists or officials. At the Resolution Foundation or IFS, analysts model tax and benefit changes and publish distribution analyses; at Bruegel, research assistants support economists on energy, climate or macro with data and drafting (job postings 2025-26).
- **A typical day and week**: Illustrative composite. Morning: refresh data on household incomes; midday: draft a chart pack and two-page note; afternoon: meeting about a media briefing; end of week: proof a report before launch.
- **Hours, stress and lifestyle**: Typical 40-50 hours, peaks 50-60 around budget days and publication launches (author judgement). Stress 3/5 due to short news cycles. Hybrid, limited travel except conferences.
- **Human vs. quantitative profile**: People/communication: 4/5. Quantitative/technical: 3/5. Writing for non-specialists and handling media matter as much as statistics.
- **Compensation (approximate)**: *UK*: Resolution Foundation (London), April 2026: Economist/Research and Policy Analyst GBP 45,170-56,990; Senior Economist GBP 56,990-70,300; Principal GBP 70,300-82,900. IFS Research Economist (doctorate required), September 2026 start: GBP 65,600; Senior Research Economist GBP 73,400-90,900 (IFS advert via search summary, 2026). Older IFS advert: new graduates about GBP 40,000 (undated). *US*: Brookings Economic Studies research assistant USD 58,000, two-year appointment, about 20 RAs a year, unionised (Brookings posting, 2026); PIIE research analyst USD 63,000-66,000 (PIIE posting, undated). *Brussels*: Bruegel research assistant one-year renewable contract, master's required, pay not stated; a third-party guide gives EUR 2,200-2,800 gross per month (unverified). New Economics Foundation Brussels economist EUR 47,946 (year unclear). *Italy*: no reliable think-tank pay data found. *Asia/Gulf*: no reliable data found.
- **Career path**: Research assistant/analyst (0-2 years) -> Economist/Research fellow (3-5) -> Senior/Principal (5-8) -> Director/Fellow. Many leave to PhDs or government.
- **Exit opportunities**: After 2-3 years: PhD, government, consulting. After 7-10 years: senior civil service, international organisations, politics/advisory, media, academia.
- **Tier list of employers**: *Tier 1*: Brookings, PIIE, Resolution Foundation, IFS, Bruegel, CEPR. *Tier 2*: CEPS, Centre for European Reform, NEF, CER, sector think tanks. *Tier 3*: advocacy-driven or small organisations with limited research funding. Italian think tanks: I could not verify a roster; no reliable data. Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact.
- **How to enter**: *Degrees*: master's for RA at Bruegel; bachelor's or master's at Brookings; doctorate for IFS research economist; PhD for Bruegel economist (2025 posting). *Calendar*: rolling, advertised on organisation websites and INOMICS. *Interview formats*: writing sample, data exercise, interview. *Skills*: Stata/R, charts, concise writing. *Common mistakes*: academic writing style, no portfolio. *Alternative routes*: internships, RAs, government analyst posts. Entry difficulty: 3/5.
- **Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for**: Low pay and fixed-term contracts (Bruegel postings: one-year renewable); funding dependence on donors; limited progression ladders. Not a good fit if you need financial stability early.

### 3.7 Academic economist and pre-doctoral research assistant

- **What you actually do**: *Academic economist*: research (write and publish papers), teach, supervise students, referee papers and apply for grants. *Pre-doc*: a research assistant who cleans data, runs regressions in Stata/R/Python, reads literature and sometimes co-authors; the work is a one- to two-year apprenticeship before a PhD application.
- **A typical day and week**: Faculty (illustrative composite): two days of teaching/office hours, two days of research, seminar on one afternoon, refereeing and admin. Pre-doc: coding for the principal investigator, weekly meeting, reading papers.
- **Hours, stress and lifestyle**: Faculty 45-60 hours with flexible scheduling; peaks at job-market season, grant deadlines and tenure review. Stress 3/5 for pre-docs; 4/5 for the PhD job market and tenure track (author judgement; EJMR-style anecdotes not used). Travel: conferences. Pre-docs 40-50 hours.
- **Human vs. quantitative profile**: People/communication: 3/5. Quantitative/technical: 5/5. Research requires technical depth and writing; teaching and collaboration require communication.
- **Compensation (approximate)**: *Faculty*: AEA Universal Academic Questionnaire reports average starting salary for assistant professors at PhD-granting institutions at USD 154,288 in 2024-25; another table shows USD 157,017 (AEA Papers and Proceedings 2025, 115:724-727; the two figures differ and the basis, nine- vs twelve-month, is unclear). Payscale's generic estimate is lower (about USD 74,863, not comparable). *Pre-docs (2026 postings)*: Harvard (Stantcheva) USD 50,000-55,000; USC USD 42,436 plus benefits; London Business School GBP 43,250, up to two years; LSE STICERD GBP 36,513-41,565 (rising to 44,468); an older Harvard/Yale/HBS fellowship offered USD 66,000. *PhD stipends*: Italian borsa about EUR 18,433 gross in 2026/27 at Brescia (university board decision, March 2026); Bocconi PhD EUR 27,000 year 1, EUR 23,000 later (library research); UKRI GBP 21,805 from 1 October 2026 (library research); LBS GBP 32,154 tax-free (library research). Italy faculty pay: no reliable data found. Asia/Gulf faculty pay: no reliable data found.
- **Career path**: Pre-doc (1-2 years) -> PhD (US 5-6 years; Europe 3-5; library notes Bocconi 4 years) -> Assistant professor (tenure track, 6-7 years to tenure; US) -> Associate professor with tenure -> Full professor. In the UK, lecturer -> senior lecturer -> reader/professor. Italy: PhD -> postdoc/RTD-A/RTD-B -> associate professor via national qualification and local competition (system details: unverified in my sources).
- **Exit opportunities**: After a PhD or 2-3 years on the market: industry (tech, consulting), central banks, IOs. ProMarket analysis: around two-thirds of PhDs took academic jobs in 2009, now roughly half, while tech rose from 1% of placements in the 2010s to 12% in the 2020s (ProMarket, accessed October 2026). After tenure: policy roles, central banks, consulting affiliate work.
- **Tier list of employers**: Departments are conventionally ranked by research output (practitioner perception): *Tier 1*: MIT, Harvard, Chicago, Princeton, Stanford, Yale, Berkeley, LSE, Oxford, TSE, PSE and similar. *Tier 2*: other top-50 research universities. *Tier 3*: teaching-oriented colleges and universities. Pre-doc tiers: Fed/NBER/Harvard/Chicago-affiliated programmes versus smaller departments. Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact.
- **How to enter**: *Degrees*: PhD required for faculty; bachelor's or master's for pre-docs (Fed RA, IMF RAP, think-tank RA also count as pre-doc-like). *Calendar of the academic job market (typical practice; AEA says interviews will occur online in 2026)*: candidate profiles and listings on JOE (Job Openings for Economists, the AEA's listings site) open in the late summer; applications are due in the autumn; first-round interviews historically ran at the January ASSA meetings (now online), followed by campus visits ("flyouts") in January-March and offers in spring. Hiring conditions: JOE listings in the second half of 2024 were down 4.6% from 2023 and 22.8% from 2022; the first half of 2025 was down 14.4% from 2024 and 39% from 2023 (AEA Committee on the Job Market, July 2025). The December 2025 department-chair survey attributed weakness to US university shocks (endowment tax, grant cuts, indirect-cost reductions, visa restrictions); I could not read the hiring count from the PDF. *Interview formats*: job market paper presentation (job talk), research conversations with faculty, teaching statement. *Skills*: econometrics, one's subfield, coding, writing. *Common mistakes*: starting a PhD without research experience or pre-doc; weak letters; no job market paper. *Alternative routes*: pre-docs (Harvard, USC, LBS, LSE and others; NBER predoc job announcement 2026), master's with thesis, central bank RAs. I found no systematic placement rate for pre-docs. Entry difficulty: 5/5 for top-programme tenure-track seats; 3/5 for pre-docs.
- **Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for**: 5-6+ years of stipend-level pay; a weak 2025-26 job market (listings down 39% vs 2023 by mid-2025); US-visa uncertainty; tenure review stress; pre-docs are low-paid and temporary. Not a good fit if you need income soon, dislike open-ended research, or want applied impact quickly.

## 4. Banks vs. other employer types

Where the same role (an economist) exists in different kinds of organisation, the trade-offs differ. Ratings are my judgement; pay is from the sources above.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Central bank | 40-50 (55-60) | 2-3 | Moderate; ECB EUR 4,982 net/month at entry; Italy about EUR 50k gross (secondary) | Academic and hierarchical; publishing valued | High for researchers, lower for policy economists |

**Commentary.** Tax treatment matters: IMF, World Bank, OECD and EU salaries are set net of national tax or tax-exempt in the host country, so a headline of EUR 6,153 basic for AD5 shrinks to EUR 4,859-5,870 net (AMLA example), while OECD's EUR 3,886 is already tax-exempt. Compare net with net when you compare to consulting. In the US, the key divide is public versus private: BLS shows a federal median of USD 144,680, similar to consulting at USD 141,010 (May 2025), although these are medians for all experience levels. **Regional differences.** In the US, a master's can enter federal agencies and consulting, but research seats at the Fed and the best tech jobs need a PhD; pre-docs are a US/UK norm. In Europe, central-bank and supervisory careers often start through national competitions or graduate programmes (ECB, Bank of Italy) and a permanent contract is common; Italy's *concorso* is a public exam system with preselection tests, written and oral exams and grade floors (Bank of Italy: 105/110), where mobility between public bodies is limited. In Brussels (EU institutions through EPSO), Frankfurt (ECB and EU agencies) and Washington (IMF, World Bank, Fed), expatriate benefits and tax-free salaries improve take-home pay, though Washington's cost of living is high. In Asia, Hong Kong's HKMA runs a graduate economist scheme (two years, postgraduate degree, HK$49,000-50,000+ a month), while MAS advertises senior roles requiring a PhD or research master's; the Bank of Japan's and Gulf entry routes are not verified. Japanese hiring generally follows the spring new-graduate cycle (general; not verified for BoJ).
