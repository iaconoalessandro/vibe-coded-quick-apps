# Marketing (including sales and business development)

*Italian pay: where this report says "no reliable data found" for Italy, see [italy-pay-addendum.md](italy-pay-addendum.md) for recruiter-guide, contract (CCNL) and posting figures. Shared rating scales and the cross-branch comparison are in [../index.md](../index.md).*

## 1. What this branch is

Marketing is the work of deciding what a company sells, to whom, at what price and message, and then getting customers to notice, choose and come back. It runs from big-picture jobs (owning a brand's strategy and profit) to very hands-on ones (buying ads, writing emails, running a loyalty programme, interviewing consumers). Sales and business development sit next door: marketing creates demand, sales turns it into signed deals, and for business graduates sales is one of the largest entry doors, because many employers hire hundreds of juniors a year into it. The branch is open to almost any degree, but the entry routes, pay and working conditions differ sharply by employer type (an FMCG multinational, a luxury house, a software company, an ad agency) and by country.

Scope notes: (1) Global scope; the best-sourced figures are UK, Italy (Michael Page Italy 2026 salary study) and US sales (Bridge Group), with Hong Kong and Singapore from Morgan McKinley 2026; Tokyo, Dubai and Paris luxury pay are thin or weak-sourced and flagged as such. (2) Today is 9 October 2026; figures are dated, and where only a secondary source or a salary simulator was available it is marked "weak" or "unverified". (3) No reliable entry-level US marketing pay source could be opened (search allowance ran out; Glassdoor, Levels.fyi and NACE pages were not readable), so US entry pay is shown as "no reliable data" except for sales and BLS medians. (4) Marketing analytics, product management and consulting are covered in other branch reports; they are only cross-referenced here. (5) Hours for FMCG brand management and content roles rest on practitioner consensus, not statistics, and are labelled as such. (6) Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact.

## 2. Map of areas and sectors

**Cross-cutting context, 2024-2026 (verified where stated).**
- **Agency consolidation.** Omnicom completed its takeover of Interpublic (IPG) on 26 November 2025 (Campaign, The Drum). Omnicom then announced about 4,000 job cuts and the retirement of the DDB, FCB and MullenLowe brands during the first half of 2026, folding creative work into TBWA, McCann and BBDO; Omnicom's combined headcount was reported heading from about 128,000 (end 2024) to about 105,000 and annual savings were guided above $750m (Campaign, Storyboard18, Social Samosa; final cut count not confirmed by a 2026 source). WPP reported first-half 2026 revenue less pass-through costs down 4.7% like-for-like (Adweek, City AM, 6 August 2026), under its "Elevate28" plan targeting £500m of savings by end 2028 and a return to growth in 2027; further cuts in the "mid-to-high hundreds" up to about 1,000 jobs were reported for H2 2026, and CEO Cindy Rose declined to give numbers. WPP headcount sources conflict (104,083 per City AM, 97,388 per another source), probably different definitions. Publicis moved the other way: H1 2026 organic growth +4.7%, full-year guidance raised to +4.5-5%, margin 17.5% (Publicis release, 16 July 2026).
- **Generative AI in content and ad production.** Meta said (WSJ, June 2025) it aims to let advertisers create and target whole ads from a product image and a budget by end-2026; whether this has fully launched I could not confirm. Marketing Week's 2025 Career & Salary Survey (3,500+ UK marketers) found 75.8% see AI expertise as a major skills gap and 54.9% say they are asked to do more with less and without proportionate pay. Michael Page Italy reports 61% of Italian digital professionals and 30% of sales and marketing professionals already use generative AI (2026 study). A US payroll study finds a 19% relative employment decline for 22-25-year-olds in AI-exposed occupations (Stanford Digital Economy Lab, revised August 2026; US all-occupation evidence, not marketing-specific).
- **Retail media** (ads sold on retailers' own sites and apps, e.g. Amazon, Walmart, Tesco): WARC forecasts $200.4bn worldwide in 2026 and $223.4bn in 2027 (+11.5%), about 15% of all ad spend, but with growth slowing; Amazon took about 78% of US retail media spend in 2025 (Walrus Intelligence via WARC coverage, August 2026). Europe is growing in single digits.
- **Privacy and cookies.** Google kept third-party cookies in Chrome (April 2025 decision) and on 17 October 2025 announced the retirement of most Privacy Sandbox technologies. Safari and Firefox already block them. Net effect: measurement relies on first-party data, consented customer data, modelling and experiments rather than one replacement for the cookie.

**Brand management (FMCG/consumer goods, luxury).** A brand manager owns a brand's strategy, budget and results: pricing and promotions, product launches, advertising briefs, and the sales forecast agreed with the sales team. FMCG (fast-moving consumer goods: food, drink, cosmetics, cleaning) employers such as P&G, Unilever, L'Oreal and Nestle are called "marketing academies" because they train people systematically. Employers: consumer-goods multinationals, luxury houses (LVMH, Kering, Hermes, Chanel, Richemont), consumer brands in pharma and automotive. Measured on market share, sales volume, brand health, profit.

**Digital and performance marketing.** Paid search (SEM; Google Ads), paid social (Meta, TikTok, LinkedIn), SEO (free search ranking), programmatic (automated ad buying), e-commerce and marketplace advertising (Amazon, retail media). "Performance" means paid by results: measured on CPC (cost per click), CPA (cost per acquisition) and ROAS (return on ad spend, revenue divided by ad cost). Employers: e-commerce firms, retailers, apps, fintechs, performance and media agencies. Michael Page Italy lists E-commerce Manager, CRM Manager, Head of Growth, Digital Analytics Manager and Digital Marketing Manager as the five most requested digital roles in Italy for 2026.

**Content, social media and influencer marketing.** Creating and distributing posts, video, newsletters, blogs and creator partnerships, and managing community replies. Employers: brands, publishers, social/creative agencies, influencer agencies, software firms (B2B content). Measured on reach, engagement, traffic, leads. Most exposed to generative AI at entry level.

**Product marketing (tech/B2B).** Product marketing managers (PMMs) sit between product, sales and marketing: they decide positioning (who the product is for and why it beats rivals), run launches, write messaging and sales enablement material (decks, battlecards that help sales reps win). Employers: SaaS and tech firms (from Google and Microsoft to Series B start-ups). Measured on pipeline, win rate, adoption and launch results.

**CRM and lifecycle marketing.** CRM (customer relationship management) here means marketing to existing customers: email, push messages, loyalty programmes, automation journeys (welcome, abandoned basket, win-back) built in tools like Braze, Salesforce Marketing Cloud, Klaviyo or HubSpot. Employers: retailers, e-commerce, fintech, subscription apps, luxury (clienteling), airlines. Measured on retention, repeat purchase, customer lifetime value, email revenue.

**Market research and consumer insights.** Collecting and interpreting data on what people think and buy: surveys, focus groups, panel and till data, brand trackers. Agencies: Ipsos, Kantar (Kantar Worldpanel and Kantar Media operate separately), NielsenIQ, Circana, YouGov, GfK (now part of NIQ); in-house insight teams at FMCG and retailers. Agencies bill clients per project and for subscribed data; in-house teams are measured on decisions informed.

**Communications and PR (brief).** Corporate communications, press relations, internal comms, investor relations and public affairs. Employers: PR agencies (Edelman, FleishmanHillard, Brunswick-type financial PR, Havas/Publicis/WPP-owned firms), in-house comms teams, institutions. BLS projects only 2% growth for US PR specialists, 2025-35, with a $74,750 median (May 2025).

**Advertising and media agencies.** Creative agencies develop the idea and the ads; media agencies plan and buy the space. Agencies earn fees (retainers or project fees), commissions on media bought and increasingly performance bonuses; staff are measured on billable utilisation and client retention. The five big holding companies are WPP, Publicis, Omnicom (now including IPG), Dentsu and Havas; independents (Wieden+Kennedy, Mother, Armando Testa in Italy, many smaller shops) compete on creative reputation. Pitching (competing for a client's account against other agencies) drives workloads.

**Sales and business development.** B2B SaaS: SDR/BDR (sales/business development representative, who finds and qualifies leads) then account executive (AE, who closes deals), with a quota (annual sales target). FMCG: key account management (managing the relationship with a retailer chain such as Carrefour or Esselunga). Luxury retail: clienteling (building personal relationships with high-spending clients). Pharma: medical sales representatives visiting doctors, regulated and degree-heavy. BLS: US wholesale and manufacturing sales reps median $76,460 (technical/scientific $104,920), employment flat at 0% growth 2025-35 but about 123,400 openings a year from turnover.

## 3. Role families

### 3.1 Brand manager

**What you actually do**
An assistant brand manager (ABM) supports a brand manager, who supports a senior brand manager, who reports to a marketing director. You write briefs for ad agencies, build the annual brand plan and budget, track weekly sales and market share data (from NielsenIQ or Circana), plan promotions with sales, and manage launches with R&D, supply chain and finance. In practice: you review a 10-week sales trend, see a promotion lost money, and rewrite next quarter's plan. Deliverables: brand plan, creative brief, launch timeline, P&L (profit and loss) forecast, post-campaign review. Tools: Excel/PowerPoint, retailer data portals, agency briefs. The "mini-CEO" label is half true: you own the brand's numbers but depend on sales, supply chain and the retailer for execution.

**A typical day and week**
Mornings: sales and share data, email from the sales team about a retailer's request, a call with the agency about a script. Midday: cross-functional meeting (supply chain flags a shortage, finance questions the discount). Afternoon: write or review a brief or deck. Rhythm: monthly business reviews and budget cycles; quarterly plan presentations to the marketing director. Frequent shoots and launches add travel bursts.

**Hours, stress and lifestyle**
Typical 40-50 hours; peaks 55-60 around launches, annual planning and agency shoots (practitioner estimate, unverified; no statistics found). BLS says marketing managers often work over 40 hours with stress near deadlines. Stress 3/5. Hybrid is normal at multinationals. International mobility is a condition of the fast track: P&G describes reassignments every 2-3 years (P&G Germany graduate ad, June 2026).

**Human vs. quantitative profile**
People/communication: 4/5. Quantitative/technical: 3/5. You persuade sales, agencies and finance constantly, and you are expected to read market-share, price-elasticity and ROI data without a data team.

**Compensation (approximate)**
- Entry: UK £35,000 on Unilever UFLP plus bonus and a benefits envelope worth 25% of pensionable pay (Unilever, 2026 intake) and £35,000 at L'Oreal UK (advert text, 2026); Germany €67,500 plus bonus at P&G (P&G ad, 16 June 2026). Italy: Michael Page Italy 2026 shows Brand Manager at €40-50k for under 5 years' experience (bonus 0-5%), so entry sits below that range; pharma brand manager €35-55k (bonus 15-20%). Hong Kong: Brand Manager HK$35-45k per month (Morgan McKinley, 2026). US: no reliable entry data found.
- ~5 years: Italy €50-65k (Michael Page Italy 2026); UK brand manager London lower to upper quartile £51k-£75k, median £58k; UK national median £50.8k (Ashdown Group 2026 guide, which skews to recruiter-placed roles); Singapore consumer-goods brand manager about S$120k for 5-10 years' experience (Morgan McKinley 2026). Paris luxury: weak data, €32-42k for a junior luxury product manager (simulator and career article, anecdotal).
- Senior: Italy marketing director €75-85k (<5 years in the role) to over €100k (Michael Page Italy 2026); US marketing managers median $166,790 and advertising/promotions managers $133,660 (BLS, May 2025); UK head of marketing median £100k London (Ashdown 2026).

**Career path**
ABM (0-2 years) to brand manager (2-4) to senior brand manager (4-7) to marketing director/ category lead (7-12) to general manager or CMO (12+). Typical promotion every 2-3 years at the academies (P&G's reassignment cycle is the verified proxy; other timelines unverified). A sales or customer-development rotation and one international move are usual for general-management tracks.

**Exit opportunities**
After 2-3 years: another FMCG or luxury firm, retail e-commerce, start-up growth roles, consulting (a minority). After 7-10 years: marketing director elsewhere, general manager, CMO at a mid-sized firm, founder of a consumer brand, or move into strategy. FMCG brand names are strong CV currency.

**Tier list of employers**
- Tier 1 ("marketing academies" by reputation): P&G, Unilever, L'Oreal, Nestle, Mars, Coca-Cola, PepsiCo, Mondelez, Diageo, Reckitt. Tier 1 luxury: LVMH, Kering, Hermes, Chanel, Richemont, Prada. Hardest to enter; best brand training.
- Tier 2: Ferrero, Henkel, Colgate-Palmolive, Beiersdorf, Danone, Kraft Heinz, Estee Lauder, Barilla, Lavazza, Campari; mid-sized luxury and premium brands.
- Tier 3: regional or mid-sized consumer firms, private label teams at retailers, smaller brands; faster ownership, less formal training.
Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact.

**How to enter**
- Degrees: employers state no required subject. Unilever UK accepts degrees "across all disciplines", P&G Germany asks for a Bachelor's or Master's with strong results, LVMH SPRING asks for a Master's and no prior luxury experience (employer pages, 2025-26).
- Programmes (verified): Unilever Future Leaders Programme (UK, starts September, four-stage online/digital interview/discovery centre process), L'Oreal Marketing Management Trainee (London, 18 months, three rotations, up to 40 graduates), P&G graduate brand roles (hired straight into a brand role in Germany), LVMH SPRING Future Leaders (3 years, three 10-month missions). Experience windows are tight: Unilever 24 months post-graduation, LVMH three years.
- Calendar: Unilever applications typically open Q4 (autumn) with rolling close; L'Oreal rolling. Assessments: cognitive and personality tests, digital interview, case and assessment centre. L'Oreal Brandstorm (240,000 participants in 2025; about 500 first roles a year, roughly 0.2%) is practice and a recruiter touchpoint, not a route.
- Skills: Excel, a worked P&L model, data reading, a structured launch case; internships in marketing or sales. Certifications (CIM, Google, Meta) are not required by the employers reviewed.
- Competition: UK retail/FMCG/tourism received 290 applications per graduate vacancy in 2025, versus 140 across all sectors (ISE 2025; applications, not applicants).
- Mistakes: gap years that burn the experience window; assuming English is enough on the continent (P&G Germany needs solid German); applying to two programmes.
- Alternative routes: agency or sales first (P&G and Unilever value sales rotations), internships at second-tier FMCG, start-up consumer brands, retailer category/trade marketing into brand roles.
Entry difficulty: 5/5.

**Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for**
Slow, process-heavy and political; much time goes to decks and alignment; ownership is real but limited by sales and supply chain; layoffs happen (P&G announced 7,000 cuts in June 2025; Unilever 7,500 in March 2024 - via secondary summaries). Mobility demands can cost you a relationship or city. Not for people who dislike spreadsheets, bureaucracy or being relocated, or who want fast autonomy (a start-up fits better).

### 3.2 Digital / performance marketing specialist

**What you actually do**
You run paid campaigns and optimise them with data. Example: you rebalance a €40k weekly Meta and Google budget towards the campaigns with the best ROAS (return on ad spend), pause weak ads, test new creatives and landing pages, and write the Monday performance report. Sub-specialisms: SEM/PPC (pay-per-click) specialist, paid social specialist, SEO specialist, programmatic specialist, marketplace advertising (Amazon), CRO (conversion rate optimisation). Tools: Google Ads, Meta Ads Manager, Google Analytics 4, Looker Studio, tag managers, Excel/SQL for reporting. Deliverables: campaign setups, test results, weekly and monthly dashboards, budget recommendations.

**A typical day and week**
Check spend and cost-per-acquisition overnight, adjust bids and budgets, brief designers on new ad variants, review an A/B test, answer client or internal questions on why results moved. Weekly: report and planning; monthly: budget re-forecast. Big sales events (Black Friday, Prime Day, January sales) bring long days.

**Hours, stress and lifestyle**
Typical 37-40 hours (Prospects, November 2024), peaks 50 hours in peak trading seasons or at agencies (practitioner estimate). Stress 3/5: money is spent live, so mistakes (a budget typo) cost real euros, and agencies carry client pressure. Hybrid is common: 64% of Italian digital professionals work hybrid (Michael Page Italy 2026). Travel is minimal.

**Human vs. quantitative profile**
People/communication: 2/5. Quantitative/technical: 4/5. Mostly screen-based analysis, with communication limited to clients, designers and stakeholders.

**Compensation (approximate)**
- Entry: UK digital marketing assistant £23,500-£25,000 (Prospects, November 2024); Italy SEO specialist €25-35k, SEM/biddable media specialist €30-40k, ad trafficker €23-33k (Michael Page Italy 2026, bands for under 5 years' experience). US: no reliable entry data found.
- ~5 years: UK digital marketing manager median £65k London, £57k national (Ashdown 2026; UK Prospects says "experienced" £27-30k at officer level, so the two sources describe different levels); Italy digital performance manager €50-60k (5-10 years), digital marketing manager €45-65k (Michael Page Italy 2026); Singapore digital manager S$100k at 5-10 years; Hong Kong digital marketing manager HK$40-68k per month (Morgan McKinley 2026).
- Senior: UK digital marketing manager £40-60k (Prospects) to head of digital; Italy head of digital €65-80k (>10 years, bonus 10-20%); Singapore digital marketing director S$180-280k (Morgan McKinley 2026).

**Career path**
Executive/specialist (0-2 years) to senior specialist/manager (3-5) to head of performance/growth or digital director (6-10) to CMO or founder. Specialisation (retail media, marketplace advertising) is rewarded; Michael Page Italy says digital roles are heading towards "hyper-specialisation".

**Exit opportunities**
After 2-3 years: in-house growth or e-commerce teams, product or CRM roles, marketing analytics (see data analytics branch). After 7-10 years: head of growth, e-commerce director, agency leadership, consulting in digital, or founder.

**Tier list of employers**
- Tier 1: in-house growth teams at scale-ups and tech (Google, Meta, Booking, Spotify-type firms), top performance/data agencies in Publicis (Epsilon, Publicis Media) and WPP Media; Amazon Ads.
- Tier 2: large e-commerce and retail (Zalando, Amazon sellers, fast fashion), mid-size agencies, fintechs.
- Tier 3: small agencies and SMEs; broad scope, thin training, high turnover.
Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact.

**How to enter**
- Degrees: any; marketing, business, economics, statistics or computing help. Prospects calls the field "open to all graduates", most graduate schemes want a 2:1.
- Entry: agency graduate schemes (autumn in the UK), apprenticeships (UK Digital Marketer apprenticeship), direct applications; internships carry weight.
- Certifications: Google Ads and Google Analytics certificates, Meta Blueprint, HubSpot: free or cheap and useful to prove hands-on familiarity; they rarely decide a hire (practitioner view; Prospects lists CIM and IDM diplomas).
- Skills and portfolio: run a real small ad budget (a friend's shop or a local charity), document the test, build a dashboard in Looker Studio; learn SQL.
- Mistakes: collecting certificates without a live campaign; only reporting clicks, not results.
- Alternative routes: sales or customer service to growth marketing; media agency to in-house.
Entry difficulty: 2/5.

**Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for**
Platform automation (Performance Max, Advantage+) and AI take over trafficking and basic optimisation, shrinking junior task lists; tools and rules change constantly; results are judged weekly. Not for people who dislike numbers or want long strategy cycles.

### 3.3 Content, social and community marketer

**What you actually do**
You plan and produce content: social posts and short video, newsletters, blog articles, case studies, creator partnerships; you reply to comments and DMs, track engagement and report monthly. Examples: a content calendar for TikTok and Instagram, briefing influencers, repurposing a webinar into five posts, community moderation. Tools: Canva/Adobe, CapCut, scheduling tools (Hootsuite, Later), analytics dashboards, AI writing/image tools, a CMS. Deliverables: calendar, posts, short videos, monthly performance report.

**A typical day and week**
Morning: check overnight comments and trends, post scheduled content; midday: shoot or edit; afternoon: brief creators, update the calendar, review analytics. Weekly: content meeting and report. Events, launches and crises (a negative viral post) break the rhythm, and social is a 7-day medium.

**Hours, stress and lifestyle**
Typical 38-45 hours; peaks 50+ around launches and crises (practitioner estimate; Prospects gives 37-40 hours for digital roles). Weekend or evening monitoring is common. Stress 3/5: public visibility, negative comments, short deadlines. Remote/hybrid is common.

**Human vs. quantitative profile**
People/communication: 3/5. Quantitative/technical: 2/5. Writing, taste and speed matter more than statistics, but you must read engagement data.

**Compensation (approximate)**
- Entry: Italy social media manager €23-30k, digital PR and influencer marketing €23-30k, marketing & communication specialist €23-30k (Michael Page Italy 2026, <5 years' experience). UK marketing assistants £25-27k, marketing executives £29-34k (Prospects, November 2025). No reliable US entry data found.
- ~5 years: Italy €30-38k (social media manager, 5-10 years); UK content manager £42k median national, £48k London (Ashdown 2026); Hong Kong content manager HK$35-52k per month (Morgan McKinley 2026).
- Senior: Italy €38-45k (social media manager, >10 years), digital PR €45-50k; UK content manager upper quartile £52.6-60k (Ashdown 2026); Singapore head of content S$130-220k (Morgan McKinley 2026, senior bands). Pay ceilings are lower than for performance or product marketing.

**Career path**
Content/social executive (0-2 years) to content or social manager (3-5) to head of content/brand communications (6-10) to director. Moves into brand management, product marketing (B2B content), or creator-economy roles are common.

**Exit opportunities**
After 2-3 years: performance marketing, PR/communications, brand teams, agency account roles. After 7-10 years: head of content, brand director, editor, founder or freelance creator.

**Tier list of employers**
- Tier 1: major consumer brands with large social teams (Nike, Red Bull, LVMH Maisons), top social/creative agencies, strong B2B content shops at software firms.
- Tier 2: mid-size brands, publishers, influencer agencies.
- Tier 3: small companies where one person does everything; flexible but low-paid.
Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact.

**How to enter**
- Degrees: communications, marketing, journalism, languages, design; portfolio matters more than the degree.
- Entry: internships, agency junior roles, apprenticeship routes, freelance and student-society accounts.
- Certifications: Meta Blueprint, HubSpot content certificates, Google Analytics - optional proof; CIM for UK career builders.
- Portfolio: a channel you grew (with numbers), 3-5 short videos, a content calendar with results, writing samples; show AI tools used with judgement.
- Mistakes: showing follower counts without explaining what you did; copying AI output without editing; ignoring analytics.
- Alternative routes: customer support, community moderation, journalism, freelance.
Entry difficulty: 3/5 (many openings but the portfolio is the filter, and AI is cutting first-draft work).

**Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for**
Low entry pay (Italy €23-30k), always-on pressure, public criticism, thin career ladders at small firms, and high exposure to AI-generated content. Not for people who dislike public exposure, irregular hours or ambiguous metrics.

### 3.4 Product marketing manager (tech/B2B)

**What you actually do**
You decide how a product is described and sold. Example: before a new feature ships you interview customers, write the positioning, a launch plan, the pricing page copy, a sales deck and a battlecard comparing it with competitors, and train sales reps before launch day. Tools: customer interviews, win/loss analysis, product analytics, CRM (Salesforce/HubSpot), presentation and writing tools. Deliverables: positioning, messaging, launch plan, sales enablement, customer case studies.

**A typical day and week**
Sync with product managers, review a demo, write a messaging document, join sales calls to learn objections, brief designers on launch assets. Launch weeks are intense; between launches work is research and enablement. For product management itself see the product management branch; for marketing analytics see the data analytics branch.

**Hours, stress and lifestyle**
Typical 40-50 hours; peaks 55-60 at launches and quarter-end (practitioner estimate; no statistics found). Stress 3/5. Hybrid/remote common in tech; travel for sales kick-offs and customer events.

**Human vs. quantitative profile**
People/communication: 4/5. Quantitative/technical: 3/5. You need technical fluency to talk to engineers and customers, plus enough data skill to measure launch impact.

**Compensation (approximate)**
- Entry: PMM is rarely a first job; "associate" PMM roles exist at big tech. No reliable entry data found for the US, UK or Italy; Hong Kong PMM HK$35-45k per month (Morgan McKinley 2026, all levels in one band). Michael Page Italy: Product Manager (FMCG/industrial context) €50-55k for under 5 years and product specialist €28-32k (not the tech PMM role).
- ~5 years / senior: no reliable data found for the US or UK from sources I could open. The Ashdown 2026 guide does not list product marketing. Treat any figure from salary sites as weak.

**Career path**
Associate PMM or marketing generalist (0-2 years) to PMM (2-5) to senior PMM (5-8) to PMM lead / director of product marketing (8-12) to head of marketing or product leader. Typical timelines are practitioner estimates, unverified.

**Exit opportunities**
After 2-3 years: product management, growth marketing, sales enablement, customer success. After 7-10 years: VP marketing, head of product, chief of staff, start-up founder.

**Tier list of employers**
- Tier 1: Google, Microsoft, Amazon (AWS), Salesforce, Adobe, Stripe-type scale-ups (by perception and brand).
- Tier 2: established SaaS (HubSpot, Atlassian, Datadog-type) and well-funded Series C+ companies.
- Tier 3: early-stage start-ups (broad scope, equity upside, instability).
Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact.

**How to enter**
- Degrees: business, marketing, computer science, engineering; technical literacy is the differentiator. Imperial's MSc Strategic Marketing class of 2024 sent 18% of graduates into tech (Imperial employment report).
- Typical routes: marketing internships at tech, SDR/sales to PMM, product analyst or customer-success to PMM, content marketing at a SaaS firm, or MBA/master's hire into rotational programmes.
- Calendar: US big-tech internships recruit the preceding autumn; many PMM roles are posted year-round.
- Assessments: written messaging exercises, a launch plan case, a presentation.
- Certifications: Pragmatic Institute, Product Marketing Alliance certificates are optional signals.
- Portfolio: a positioning teardown of a real product, a mock launch plan, a customer-interview summary.
- Mistakes: writing feature lists instead of customer problems; skipping sales exposure.
- Alternative routes: from sales (SDR to PMM is a recognised pivot), customer success, or consulting.
Entry difficulty: 4/5.

**Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for**
Hard to enter directly after graduation; influence without authority (product and sales own the decisions); tech layoffs hit marketing teams; impact is hard to attribute. Not for people who want direct control, or who dislike technical products.

### 3.5 CRM / lifecycle marketing specialist

**What you actually do**
You manage the customer's journey after the first purchase: welcome series, abandoned-basket emails, win-back flows, loyalty tiers, app notifications, segmentation. Example: you build a segment of customers inactive for 90 days, send a tested offer, and report revenue and unsubscribe rate. Tools: Braze, Salesforce Marketing Cloud, Klaviyo, HubSpot, SQL, customer data platforms, A/B testing. Deliverables: automated journeys, campaign calendars, segment definitions, retention dashboards. In luxury, "clienteling" extends this to store staff using client data.

**A typical day and week**
Build or QA an email flow, pull a segment with SQL or a BI tool, check deliverability and results, write the monthly retention report, meet with e-commerce and brand teams about the promotional calendar. Calendar-driven peaks: Black Friday, January sales, Christmas.

**Hours, stress and lifestyle**
Typical 37-42 hours; peaks 50 hours in peak trading weeks (practitioner estimate). Stress 2/5: errors (wrong audience, broken link) are embarrassing but usually fixable, and work is calendar-driven. Hybrid common. No travel.

**Human vs. quantitative profile**
People/communication: 2/5. Quantitative/technical: 4/5. Segmentation, testing and SQL matter; you work with data and tools more than with people.

**Compensation (approximate)**
- Entry: Italy marketing automation specialist €25-35k; CRM manager €35-40k (<5 years; Michael Page Italy 2026). UK CRM analyst: London lower quartile £35k, median £48k (Ashdown 2026; not strictly entry). US: no reliable entry data found.
- ~5 years: Italy CRM manager €40-55k (5-10 years), in retail/GDO €45-60k; UK CRM manager £60k London median, £52.6k national (Ashdown 2026); Hong Kong CRM manager HK$48-68k per month (Morgan McKinley 2026).
- Senior: Italy CRM manager €55-70k (>10 years); Hong Kong CRM director HK$68-90k per month; UK upper quartile CRM manager £75k London (Ashdown 2026).

**Career path**
CRM executive/assistant (0-2 years) to CRM specialist/manager (3-5) to head of CRM / retention (6-10) to director of customer or marketing. Often merges with growth, e-commerce and data roles.

**Exit opportunities**
After 2-3 years: performance marketing, e-commerce, analytics, product growth. After 7-10 years: head of customer/retention, e-commerce director, CMO in direct-to-consumer firms, martech consulting.

**Tier list of employers**
- Tier 1: scaled consumer tech and subscription firms (Spotify, Booking, Uber-type apps), leading fintechs, large retailers with strong data (Zalando, Amazon).
- Tier 2: retailers, airlines, luxury groups, banks' marketing teams, CRM agencies.
- Tier 3: small e-commerce and SMEs (a CRM generalist doing everything).
Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact.

**How to enter**
- Degrees: marketing, business, economics, statistics, computing; data comfort matters.
- Entry: junior CRM/email roles at retailers or fintechs, agency CRM teams, internships; graduate schemes at retailers (Tesco-type loyalty teams in the UK).
- Certifications: HubSpot, Salesforce Marketing Cloud, Braze and Klaviyo certifications are cheap, concrete and recognised by practitioners (impact on hiring: moderate, weak evidence).
- Skills/portfolio: build a mock lifecycle plan with segments, triggers, KPIs; SQL; run a newsletter with measured open/click rates.
- Mistakes: focusing on design not retention economics; ignoring GDPR consent rules.
- Alternative routes: customer service, e-commerce operations, analytics.
Entry difficulty: 3/5 (open to numerate graduates; relevant tool experience is the filter).

**Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for**
Repetitive build/QA work, pressure to push promotions, depends on data quality and consent rules (GDPR, ePrivacy); automation and AI-generated content compress junior tasks. Not for people who want creative variety or lots of face-to-face interaction.

### 3.6 Market research / consumer insights analyst

**What you actually do**
You find out what consumers think and do and turn it into a recommendation. At an agency: design a survey, check fieldwork, clean data, run crosstabs, write the slides. In-house at an FMCG firm: commission and interpret studies, track brand health, and present insights to brand teams. Qualitative researchers run interviews and focus groups; quantitative researchers work with survey and panel data. Tools: survey platforms, Excel, SPSS/R/Python, PowerPoint, dashboards. Deliverables: questionnaire, data tables, insight deck.

**A typical day and week**
Draft a questionnaire, review fieldwork progress with a field agency, analyse results, write findings, talk to the client about what they mean. Peaks before client presentations and tracker deliveries.

**Hours, stress and lifestyle**
Typical 37-42 hours (Prospects, March 2026: 9-5 for in-house and quantitative researchers, with evenings and weekends common for qualitative researchers). Peaks 45-50 at deadlines. Stress 2/5. Hybrid common; travel for fieldwork and client meetings varies.

**Human vs. quantitative profile**
People/communication: 3/5. Quantitative/technical: 4/5. Strong stats and clear storytelling both matter; senior roles are more about client advice.

**Compensation (approximate)**
- Entry: UK around £22,000 (Prospects, March 2026); Ipsos graduate research executive London £28,000 (job-board listing, deadline 7 August 2026, weak source); Payscale UK entry average £22,965 (2026, weak). Italy: marketing analyst €30-38k (<5 years) and product specialist €28-32k (Michael Page Italy 2026). US: BLS market research analysts and marketing specialists median $78,760 (May 2025); entry data not found.
- ~5 years: UK experienced about £27k to over £38k (Prospects, March 2026); Italy marketing analyst €38-50k (5-10 years).
- Senior: UK £42k to over £61k (Prospects); Italy marketing analyst over €50k (>10 years); US median is $78,760 with 7% projected growth to 2035 (BLS), above average.

**Career path**
Research assistant/executive (0-2 years) to research manager (3-5) to associate director/director (6-10) to head of insights or client lead. Usually a 12-24 month graduate programme (Ipsos "Generation Ipsos" is 12 months; Kantar runs 12-24-month schemes and about 100 UK graduates, apprentices and placements a year).

**Exit opportunities**
After 2-3 years: in-house insights, brand/marketing, data analytics, consulting. After 7-10 years: head of insights, strategy/planning, CRM/analytics director, consultancy partner.

**Tier list of employers**
- Tier 1: Ipsos, Kantar, NielsenIQ, Circana; insights teams at P&G, Unilever, L'Oreal.
- Tier 2: YouGov, Savanta, specialist and boutique agencies, retailer insights teams (Tesco-type Dunnhumby-style analytics).
- Tier 3: smaller research shops and field agencies; narrower scope.
Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact.

**How to enter**
- Degrees: numerical subjects (statistics, economics, maths, business) for quantitative; psychology, sociology, anthropology for qualitative (Prospects, March 2026).
- Graduate programmes (verified): Ipsos Generation Ipsos; Kantar graduate schemes (autumn adverts, spring assessments, September starts); NielsenIQ graduate client roles.
- Certifications: MRS Advanced Certificate (some large firms build it into schemes), MRS Foundation course.
- Skills: Excel, SPSS/R/Python, survey design, clean slide writing.
- Mistakes: being able to run tests but not explain "so what"; ignoring sampling.
- Alternative routes: field agency operations or data processing, apprenticeship (Level 4 Market Research Executive), or move from marketing/analytics.
Entry difficulty: 2/5.

**Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for**
Low entry pay in the UK; reporting can be repetitive; AI is automating desk research and tabulation (the Stanford signal on young workers in AI-exposed jobs applies by inference); the career ladder is long. Not for people who want to make the final business decisions early, or who dislike statistics and detail.

### 3.7 Agency roles (account management, strategy/planning, media planning and buying)

**What you actually do**
- *Account management:* you are the client's day-to-day contact and the agency's internal project driver: brief the creatives, manage budgets and timelines, protect the relationship.
- *Strategy/planning:* you turn consumer and market insights into the idea behind the brief. Rare at entry; most planners start in research, account roles or analytics.
- *Media planning and buying:* planners decide where a campaign runs (TV, social, search, outdoor); buyers negotiate and book space. Example: planning how to split a €1m launch across YouTube, Instagram, TV and out-of-home, then reporting reach and cost per outcome.
Tools: PowerPoint/Excel, planning and ad-serving tools (e.g. Nielsen/Comscore data, DV360), CRMs, project management software.

**A typical day and week**
Account manager: client call, internal status meeting, chase approvals, update the timeline, deal with last-minute changes. Media planner: update plans, pull performance data, answer client questions on delivery, negotiate with media owners. Pitch weeks and campaign launches mean evenings.

**Hours, stress and lifestyle**
Typical 40-50 hours (Prospects, January 2026: advertising account roles are normally 9-6 with longer hours around deadlines, launches and pitches; weekend work is rare). Peaks 55-65 in pitches (practitioner estimate). Stress 4/5: client demands and pitch deadlines. Hybrid common; client and shoot travel. Marketing Week 2025: 54.9% of marketers are asked to do more with less.

**Human vs. quantitative profile**
People/communication: 5/5 for account roles; 3/5 for media planners. Quantitative/technical: 3/5 overall (account roles about 2, media planning about 4). Account roles are relationship work; media planning is numbers-heavy.

**Compensation (approximate)**
- Entry: UK graduate trainee £24,000-£25,000, some £20,000 (Prospects, January 2026); junior media planner £26,000 (Prospects, March 2026). Italy: digital media planner €25-35k, ad trafficker €23-33k (Michael Page Italy 2026, <5 years). Hong Kong agency account manager HK$36-50k per month (Morgan McKinley 2026; mid-level). US: no reliable entry data found; BLS reports a $133,660 median for advertising and promotions managers (May 2025), a senior figure.
- ~5 years: UK account manager £35,000-£53,000 (Prospects, January 2026) and media planner with 3-5 years £37,000-£46,000 (Prospects, March 2026); Singapore regional account manager S$120k at 5-10 years (Morgan McKinley 2026).
- Senior: UK account director £56,000-£100,000 (Prospects, January 2026); Singapore account director S$200-350k (regional/global, Morgan McKinley 2026).
Pay is lower than FMCG at entry (about 30-45% below Unilever/L'Oreal in the UK) but rises quickly with promotion.

**Career path**
Account executive/media assistant (0-2 years) to account manager/media planner (2-4) to senior account manager/group planner (4-7) to account director/associate director (7-10) to client services director/managing partner. Strategy: planner to senior planner to strategy director.

**Exit opportunities**
After 2-3 years: client-side marketing (often into digital, brand or CRM), another agency, media owner (Google, Meta, publishers), consulting. After 7-10 years: marketing director client-side, head of agency, consultancy partner, founder.

**Tier list of employers**
- Tier 1: holding-company flagships and creative networks (WPP: Ogilvy, VML, WPP Media; Publicis: Leo Burnett, Publicis Media, Epsilon; Omnicom: BBDO, TBWA, McCann, OMD; Dentsu; Havas) and prestige independents (Wieden+Kennedy, Mother, Armando Testa in Italy).
- Tier 2: mid-size networks, strong digital and performance shops, large PR/communications agencies.
- Tier 3: small local agencies; flexible, lower pay and thin training.
Industry notes: the holding-company tier is under restructuring (Omnicom-IPG, WPP Elevate28); Publicis is growing. Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact.

**How to enter**
- Degrees: any; business, communications, advertising, English, psychology, statistics (Prospects). UK apprenticeships (Level 3 Advertising and Media Executive).
- Graduate schemes: larger agencies, applications usually open in the autumn (Prospects, January 2026); smaller agencies recruit year-round.
- Certifications: IPA Foundation Certificate (UK; the recognised agency signal), CIM; Google/Meta certificates for digital media roles.
- Skills/portfolio: a campaign critique, a media plan with a justified budget split, the ability to write a clear brief.
- Mistakes: ignoring paid media numbers; underestimating hours.
- Alternative routes: junior roles at independents, media owner sales, client-side marketing then agency.
Entry difficulty: 3/5.

**Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for**
Low entry pay, long hours around pitches, client-driven stress, and job insecurity: holding-company layoffs (Omnicom 4,000 cuts; WPP H1 2026 revenue down 4.7%) and AI in production and reporting. Not for people who dislike client pressure, last-minute changes or low early pay.

### 3.8 Sales and business development (SDR/BDR to account executive; key account manager)

**What you actually do**
- *SDR/BDR (software):* find potential customers, send emails, call and message them, and book meetings for account executives. Example: 40-60 outbound touches a day, aiming for a handful of meetings a week (activity targets vary by company).
- *Account executive (AE):* run demos, negotiate, and close deals against an annual quota (target). Pipeline reviews every week.
- *Key account manager (FMCG):* manage a retailer relationship: negotiate promotions and listings, forecast volumes, handle conflicts, jointly plan with brand teams.
- *Luxury retail/clienteling and pharma sales (brief):* luxury sales advisors build lasting client relationships; pharma sales representatives (Italy: "informatore scientifico del farmaco") visit doctors; both are regulated or high-training roles.
Tools: CRM (Salesforce, HubSpot), sales engagement software, LinkedIn Sales Navigator, call recording, increasingly AI assistants.

**A typical day and week**
SDR: prospecting blocks, calls and sequences, CRM updates, daily activity reviews. AE: demos, proposal writing, follow-ups, internal approvals. KAM: retailer meetings, promotion planning, monthly performance reviews. Month-end and quarter-end are pressure peaks.

**Hours, stress and lifestyle**
Typical 40-50 hours; peaks 55-60 at quarter-end (BLS: many sales reps work more than 40 hours; quotas add stress; Prospects: sales hours are "generally long" with early starts and late finishes). Stress 4/5: pay depends on quota. Travel varies from none (inside sales) to heavy (field and key account). Hybrid common in SaaS.

**Human vs. quantitative profile**
People/communication: 5/5. Quantitative/technical: 2/5. Persuasion, resilience and discipline dominate; you need pipeline math and basic product knowledge, not advanced statistics.

**Compensation (approximate)**
- Entry (SDR): US median SDR OTE $80K (about $55K base, $25K variable, 68:32 split), 60% of SDRs at quota (Bridge Group, February 2025, 351 B2B companies). A secondary source citing RepVue shows median base about $60K and OTE about $85K (unverified). UK sales executive basic £25,000-£28,000 (Prospects, September 2025). Italy: inside sales €25-35k with bonus 10-20%; field sales €30-35k; sales account €35-40k (Michael Page Italy 2026, <5 years); medical sales rep €30-40k with 15-20% bonus. Hong Kong BD manager HK$30-55k per month (Morgan McKinley 2026).
- ~5 years (AE/KAM): US median AE OTE $200K (up from $190K in 2024), median quota $960K (4.6 times OTE), 48% of reps hit quota, ramp time 6.2 months, average experience at hire 3.7 years (Bridge Group AE report, 23 June 2026, 158 companies). RepVue medians via a secondary source (unverified): SMB AE about $130K OTE, mid-market $175K, enterprise $265K. Italy key account manager €55-70k (5-10 years, bonus 10-20%); pharma key account manager €65-90k (bonus 15-20%). Singapore key account manager S$95k at 5-10 years; Hong Kong key account manager (sales) HK$35-50k per month.
- Senior: Italy national account manager €75-85k, sales director €85-100k (bonus 25-35%), commercial director over €95k; UK senior sales over £100k with commission (Prospects, September 2025); Singapore head of sales/BD director S$160-300k.
Commission structure (SaaS): about 50/50 base/variable for AEs; median commission about 11.5% of annual contract value at quota (Bridge Group 2024 AE report via secondary summary, not re-verified).

**Career path**
SDR (12-24 months; average SDR tenure 1.9 years) to AE (SMB) to mid-market/enterprise AE (3-6 years) to sales manager/director (6-10) to VP sales / chief revenue officer. SDR promotions are scarcer: promotions were 16% of SDR attrition in 2024, down from 34% in 2020 (Bridge Group 2025). FMCG: sales rep/assistant KAM to KAM to national account manager to commercial/sales director.

**Exit opportunities**
After 2-3 years: AE, customer success, product marketing, sales operations, recruitment, marketing. After 7-10 years: sales director, VP, founder, general manager, or partnership/strategy leadership.

**Tier list of employers**
- Tier 1 (by brand and training): Salesforce, Oracle, SAP, Microsoft, Google Cloud, HubSpot-type scale-ups; FMCG commercial rotations at P&G, Unilever, Nestle; luxury maisons for clienteling.
- Tier 2: mid-size SaaS, staffing, telecom, medical device, B2B industrial.
- Tier 3: commission-heavy, high-churn, low-base start-ups and call-centre-style outbound shops.
Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact.

**How to enter**
- Degrees: any; business, marketing or economics help, technical degrees for technical/scientific sales (BLS: technical/scientific reps typically need a bachelor's). Pharma sales often prefers life-science degrees.
- Entry: SaaS SDR programmes at large vendors; FMCG sales graduate schemes (also parts of Unilever UFLP and P&G tracks); retail management trainee programmes; UK Level 4/6 sales apprenticeships.
- Calendar: SDR hiring is rolling and frequent; FMCG schemes follow the autumn cycle.
- Assessments: role-plays, cold-call exercises, personality tests, a final manager interview.
- Certifications: Salesforce Administrator/Trailhead and HubSpot sales certificates are optional proof; few firms require them.
- Skills: resilience, written outreach, listening, CRM familiarity.
- Mistakes: ignoring commission plan details and quota realism; accepting a role with a low base and high quota.
- Alternative routes: customer support or retail to SDR; SDR to marketing or product marketing.
Entry difficulty: 2/5 for SDR/inside sales; 4/5 for FMCG graduate sales schemes and KAM jobs at top firms.

**Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for**
Variable pay and rejection; a 48% attainment rate for AEs and 60% for SDRs means many people miss targets (Bridge Group); SDR roles are the most exposed to AI automation (AI SDRs were first measured at 1% of respondents in 2025, a trend to watch). Not for people who need stable income, dislike targets or cold outreach, or want little interpersonal conflict.

### 3.9 PR and corporate communications officer

**What you actually do.** You manage how an organisation is described by people it does not control: journalists, investors, employees, politicians, the public. The US Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) lists the core tasks: write press releases and prepare information for the media, answer media requests, draft speeches and arrange interviews for executives, and track public opinion on social media (Occupational Outlook Handbook, May 2025 data). Prospects (August 2026) adds planning campaigns, running events, managing web and social content, researching new business and handling a crisis. In a consultancy you serve several clients; in-house you serve one employer. Media relations is no longer the whole job: in the CIPR State of the Profession survey (2,016 UK responses, April 2024) copywriting and editing was the most common activity, then stakeholder relations, media relations and crisis management, and the most junior grade most often reported stakeholder relations, media relations and events. Tools: media databases and monitoring services, a content calendar, slides.

**A typical day and week.** Morning: scan overnight coverage and social media and send a summary. Then draft a release or statement and clear it with legal and the business, pitch journalists by phone and email, update the client or the management team, and log the coverage. Weekly in a consultancy: client status calls, a new-business pitch, a report. Launches, results days and press events bring evenings. This sequence is my reading of the Prospects and BLS task lists, not an observed diary.

**Hours, stress and lifestyle.**
- Hours: 38-40 a week (National Careers Service) or 9am to 5.30pm (Prospects, August 2026), longer around client deadlines, press launches, crises and pitches. 37% of UK practitioners said their hours had risen in the previous 12 months (CIPR 2024). The only weekly figure found is for senior US staff: 49 hours on average (PRCA US Census, 112 respondents, late 2023). Peaks 50+ (practitioner estimate).
- Stress: 3/5; 4/5 in a crisis or in a consultancy during pitches. No direct measure found. 21% of CIPR respondents put practitioners' mental health among the profession's main challenges (2024); in the PRCA UK Census 2025, 58% of women and 80% of men rated their mental health at work as good or very good.
- Lifestyle: 67% of UK practitioners work hybrid and 17% fully on site (CIPR 2024); FGS Global wants graduates in the London office full time at first. Travel to media and client events; you are on call when news breaks.

**Human vs. quantitative profile.** People/communication: 5/5. Quantitative/technical: 1/5 to 2/5. The entry test is writing and persuading; numbers appear in coverage reports and budgets. Financial and transaction communications (the Brunswick and FGS Global kind of work) requires reading company results and sits nearer 3/5. Measurement is a weak point of the trade: 37% of UK respondents still use advertising value equivalents (PRCA 2025).

**Compensation (approximate).**
- UK entry: consultancy account executives averaged £28,000 in consumer PR and £29,000 in corporate and B2B technology PR, range £25,000-£33,000 (Latte recruiter survey of 440+ PR staff, 89% in London, published by PRmoment, August 2025). Prospects gives typical starting salaries of about £25,500 (August 2026). The National Careers Service starter figure of £22,000 is below the legal minimum for a full-time adult, about £24,800 a year (my arithmetic: £12.71 an hour from 1 April 2026, GOV.UK, for 37.5 hours), so treat it as out of date. The CIPR average of £36,940 for interns, trainees, assistants and executives (2024) covers everyone holding those grades, not starters. FGS Global publishes no figure for its graduate programme.
- UK later (Latte 2025 survey averages): senior account executive £32,000-£33,000, account manager £36,000-£39,000, account director £51,000-£54,000, director £75,000-£89,000; in-house PR or communications manager £60,000, with a very wide range. CIPR 2024 averages for full-time practitioners: £53,052 overall (down from £56,340 in 2022), consultancy £59,718, in-house private sector £54,117, directors and partners £91,828.
- US: public relations specialists had a median of $74,750 in May 2025; the lowest tenth earned under $44,110 and the highest tenth over $135,150; advertising and PR firms $77,480; public relations managers $136,470 (BLS; official figures for the whole occupation). Entry-level pay: no reliable data found.
- Italy (Michael Page Italy, Studi di retribuzione 2026, gross annual RAL): digital PR and influencer marketing €23,000-€30,000 under 5 years' experience and €30,000-€45,000 at 5-10 years; communication and PR manager in fashion and luxury €45,000-€55,000 under 5 years, over €70,000 after 10, bonus 10-20%; head of communication at an insurer €50,000-€70,000 rising to over €90,000. Graduate starting pay: no reliable data found.

**Career path.** Assistant or junior account executive (0-2 years) → account executive or PR officer (1-3) → senior account executive or account manager (3-5) → account director or communications manager (5-10) → director, partner or head of communications. Prospects (August 2026) gives one to two years at the first grade and a further two to three to manager level.

**Exit opportunities.**
- After 2-3 years: in-house communications, public affairs, internal communications, content and social roles, marketing.
- After 7-10 years: head or director of communications, investor relations, public affairs lead, or your own consultancy (independent practitioners averaged £65,971 in the CIPR 2024 survey).

**Tier list of employers.**
- Tier 1: global consultancies (Edelman, Weber Shandwick, Burson, FleishmanHillard), the financial and corporate advisers (Brunswick, FGS Global, Teneo), and the communications teams of listed companies and major institutions.
- Tier 2: mid-sized and specialist agencies, including the Milan corporate firms (for example Barabino & Partners, Community, Comin & Partners, SEC Newgate Italia); public bodies, universities, charities.
- Tier 3: small agencies and single-person in-house roles.
- Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact. Firm names are from general knowledge; the PRovoke agency ranking could not be opened.

**How to enter.**
- Degrees: no set subject. Prospects lists business, communication and media, English, journalism, marketing, politics and PR as helpful. FGS Global states no subject or grade requirement. 35% of UK respondents hold a master's (PRCA 2025), but no scheme read requires one.
- Programmes: FGS Global UK Graduate Programme, London, with three pathways (core consulting, digital and creative, UK public affairs). For the 2026 intake applications closed on 15 January 2026, interviews ran from 2 February to 6 March, the assessment day was 24 April and work began on 8 September. Stage one is a presentation task, a form and a CV, reviewed anonymously; graduates become Associates after six months of training. Most other entry is direct into assistant or account executive jobs, all year.
- Qualifications: CIPR Foundation Certificate for beginners and Professional PR Certificate for graduates (National Careers Service); UK Level 4 PR and Communications Assistant apprenticeship (Prospects). BLS says certification is not typically required.
- Languages: FGS Global requires English of native standard. Writing in the working language is the entry test, which makes this one of the least portable roles for someone working in a second language.
- Experience: Prospects calls work experience essential: placements, internships, student media, volunteering for a charity.
- Common mistakes: no writing samples; confusing PR with advertising; applying to financial PR without following company news.
- Non-target routes: student journalism, an agency internship, a marketing-assistant job, the apprenticeship.
- Entry difficulty: 3/5.

**Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for.** Starting pay is modest (London account executives at £28,000-£29,000 against £35,000 on the FMCG schemes in 3.1). Average income fell between 2022 and 2024 while hours rose (CIPR 2024), and the PRCA 2025 census reports a 17% gender pay gap. Results depend on journalists you do not control, and you are on call when news breaks. Practitioners rank AI as the profession's main challenge (38%, CIPR 2024), and BLS projects US employment growth of only 2% from 2025 to 2035. Poor fit: people who dislike writing to order, being on call, or defending a position they did not choose.

### 3.10 E-commerce manager

**What you actually do.** You run an online shop, or a brand's sales on other companies' sites, as a business line with a sales target. The National Careers Service lists the tasks: plan how to raise online sales, develop the retail platform, analyse and report on website performance, work with marketing on campaigns, manage budgets, watch competitors, and keep the site within data-protection rules. Tesco's online graduate programme names the working disciplines: online trading, the digital customer experience, online operations and fulfilment, website and app performance, customer-journey optimisation, digital marketing, and data analytics and reporting (Tesco Ireland advert, 2027 intake). There are two variants: your own site (direct to consumer), and marketplaces or retailers' sites, where the work is listings, prices, stock availability and paid placement; Michael Page Italy lists "Marketplace Advertising Specialist" as a job of its own (2026). You sit between marketing, which brings the traffic, and operations, which ships the order. Tools: the e-commerce platform, web analytics, spreadsheets, marketplace dashboards.

**A typical day and week.** Morning trading check: yesterday's sales, traffic, conversion rate and out-of-stock lines against plan. Then fix what the numbers show: a listing with the wrong price or image, a checkout fault with the developers, a best-seller out of stock with the supply team. Set up next week's promotion and brief marketing. A weekly trading meeting with the commercial director; the year is built around sale events. (My reading of the task lists above; no diary source found.)

**Hours, stress and lifestyle.**
- Hours: 40-45 a week, with a working pattern given as days, nights and weekends as customers demand (National Careers Service). Peaks about 55 around sale periods (practitioner estimate; no survey found).
- Stress: 3/5. A daily sales number is visible to everyone. No measured data found.
- Lifestyle: 64% of digital professionals in Italy work hybrid (Michael Page Italy 2026), and Tesco's head-office roles are hybrid. Tesco's UK online scheme begins with 12 weeks in a store (Prospects, April 2026). Peak trading in late November and December limits holidays (practitioner knowledge).

**Human vs. quantitative profile.** People/communication: 3/5. Quantitative/technical: 3/5. Every description read puts analysis at the centre (website performance, conversion, reporting), and Michael Page Italy says the digital market wants analytical, data-driven profiles, so the first years are nearer 4/5 on numbers. The people side grows with seniority, when you negotiate with marketplaces, agencies and the supply team.

**Compensation (approximate).**
- UK: £25,000 starter to £60,000 experienced (National Careers Service; a careers-profile range, undated). No UK graduate-scheme salary specific to e-commerce was found; for scale, Tesco's UK product scheme (buying and merchandising) pays £33,000 plus a bonus of up to 10% for the 2027 intake.
- Ireland: €35,000 on Tesco's 18-month Online Graduate Programme in Dublin, starting 6 September 2027 (advertised starting salary).
- Italy (Michael Page Italy 2026, gross annual RAL): e-commerce specialist €25,000-€35,000 under 5 years' experience in grocery and specialist retail (bonus 5-10%) and €25,000-€30,000 in fashion and luxury; marketplace advertising specialist €30,000-€40,000; e-commerce manager €35,000-€45,000 under 5 years, €45,000-€60,000 at 5-10 years, €60,000-€80,000 beyond, bonus 10-20%; head of digital €65,000-€80,000 after 10 years.
- US: no reliable data found. BLS has no separate e-commerce occupation. Employers projected an average starting salary of $66,994 for 2026 marketing graduates in general (NACE Winter 2026 survey, 150 employers, base pay only), which is not specific to this role.

**Career path.** E-commerce assistant or specialist (0-3 years) → e-commerce or marketplace manager (3-6) → head of e-commerce or omnichannel manager (6-10) → digital or commercial director. No source gives promotion timing; the years are my reading of Michael Page Italy's experience bands, where omnichannel manager appears only from 5 years. The National Careers Service names sales director or chief executive as the senior steps.

**Exit opportunities.**
- After 2-3 years: performance marketing, CRM, category management or buying, marketplace account roles at brands, product management at retail-technology firms.
- After 7-10 years: head of digital, sales director or general management, e-commerce consultant, your own online business (the last three are listed by the National Careers Service).

**Tier list of employers.**
- Tier 1: marketplaces and online-only retailers (Amazon, Zalando, eBay, ASOS, Ocado) and large retailers with their own online operations (Tesco, Sainsbury's, John Lewis, Marks & Spencer, Next), plus the direct-to-consumer teams of consumer-goods and fashion groups. Most UK names are from the Prospects employer list (April 2026).
- Tier 2: mid-sized brands, and agencies that run shops and marketplace accounts for clients.
- Tier 3: small online retailers, where one person does everything. Italy had about 87,000 active e-commerce firms in 2026, 4.4% fewer than in 2025 (Osservatorio eCommerce B2c Netcomm-Politecnico di Milano, 19 May 2026, reported by PagamentiDigitali).
- Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact.

**How to enter.**
- Degrees: any. Prospects (April 2026) lists marketing, retail management, logistics, computing and buying degrees, says some retailers accept any discipline, and reports that a 2:1 is preferred and a 2:2 widely accepted.
- Programmes: retailer graduate schemes with an online stream at Asda, Morrisons, Sainsbury's and Tesco (Prospects); most start on the shop floor before head-office roles. Tesco Ireland's online programme has two placements, one outside your own discipline, and closes on 31 October 2026 for a September 2027 start. Most entry is direct, as assistant or specialist.
- Demand: "E-commerce Manager / Head of E-commerce" heads Michael Page Italy's five most requested digital roles for 2026. Italians' online purchases of products reached €42.6 billion in 2026 (+6%), 11.5% of retail spending (Netcomm-Politecnico di Milano); 28.3% of UK retail sales were online in February 2026 (Retail Gazette, cited by Prospects).
- Skills: web analytics, basic SEO, spreadsheets, commercial awareness (Prospects). The National Careers Service direct route asks for experience in digital marketing, web analytics and SEO, or project management.
- Common mistakes: presenting only social-media experience; no evidence of working with sales numbers.
- Non-target routes: run a small online shop or marketplace account yourself; move across from merchandising, buying, customer service or digital marketing; UK degree apprenticeships such as Digital Marketer or Retail Leadership (National Careers Service).
- Entry difficulty: 2-3/5.

**Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for.** You answer for a sales number that depends on stock, price and traffic you only partly control, and trading never closes: the official profile's working pattern includes nights and weekends. The market is maturing: Italian online sales grow about 6% a year and the number of active sellers is falling. Only 47% of digital professionals in Italy are satisfied with their pay and 54% are considering a move within 12 months (Michael Page Italy 2026). Poor fit: people who want purely creative work, or who dislike a daily scoreboard.

### 3.11 Category and trade marketing manager

**What you actually do.** At a consumer-goods company you plan how the product range is sold through retailers. Two jobs share this section. In category management you use sales and shopper data to advise a retailer on which products to stock, where they sit on the shelf, at what price and with which promotions, for the whole category. In trade marketing you turn brand plans into what happens in the retailer's stores and site: promotions, displays, leaflets, and the budget for them. On the retailer's side the matching job is buyer or category manager, who takes the decision. Tesco's commercial graduate programme lists the content: buying and category management; space, range and merchandising planning; pricing, promotions and profitability analysis; supplier relationships; customer insight (Tesco Ireland advert, 2027 intake). P&G's UK sales graduates manage one retailer's account from the first day and build its business plan from analytics and shopper insights (advert, 2027 intake). BLS defines wholesale and retail buyers as people who buy goods for resale; "category manager" is also a procurement title for buying a company's own supplies, a different job, so read the advert (the second point is general knowledge). Tools: retailer data portals, market and shopper panels (NielsenIQ, Circana, Kantar), spreadsheets, slides.

**A typical day and week.** Monday: pull last week's sales and market share by retailer and work out what moved and why (price, promotion, distribution, a competitor's launch). Midweek: build the range-review or promotion-evaluation deck with the account manager and brief marketing and supply on next month's promotion. Then the meeting with the retailer's buyer. The year runs on the retailers' range reviews, the annual joint business plan and seasonal peaks. This is my reading of the adverts above; no diary source was found.

**Hours, stress and lifestyle.**
- Hours: no survey found for this role. Typical 40-45, peaks 50-55 before range reviews and annual negotiations (practitioner estimate). The nearest official profile, retail buyer, says hours often run beyond 9 to 5, with early starts and late finishes at Christmas, summer sales and new-season launches (Prospects, September 2025); BLS says most buyers work full time and some more than 40 hours.
- Stress: 3/5. Deadlines follow the retailer's calendar. No measured data found.
- Lifestyle: hybrid for 51% of sales and marketing professionals in Italy and 43% in retail (Michael Page Italy 2026); Tesco asks for 60% of the week on site, and the Michael Page UK advert was five days on site. Travel to retailers' head offices; P&G requires a driving licence from the first day.

**Human vs. quantitative profile.** People/communication: 4/5. Quantitative/technical: 3/5. You persuade a buyer who can say no, and the argument is built from data. The analyst years are more numerical than the title suggests, nearer 4/5 (market data, promotion returns, profitability); the manager years are more negotiation.

**Compensation (approximate).**
- UK entry (advertised graduate salaries): P&G Sales Graduate Role £45,000 plus bonus, pension and shares (2027 intake; advert read on Built In London in October 2026); Unilever Future Leaders Programme, customer development stream, £35,000 plus bonus and a benefits allowance worth 25% of pensionable pay (2026 intake); Tesco product scheme (buying and merchandising) £33,000 plus a bonus of up to 10% (2027 intake). Tesco Ireland commercial programme: €35,000 (2027 intake).
- UK later: junior category manager or category manager at a branded food firm £35,000-£55,000 (Michael Page UK, single advert, 2026); category manager typically £45,000-£65,000 base (Coburg Banks hiring guide, July 2026; recruiter, method not stated, weak). Retail side: junior buyer £25,000-£35,000, buyer £30,000-£45,000, senior buyer £45,000-£65,000, head of buying £70,000-£90,000 or more (Prospects, September 2025).
- Italy (Michael Page Italy 2026, gross annual RAL): category manager in grocery and specialist retail €35,000-€45,000 under 5 years, €45,000-€65,000 at 5-10 years, over €70,000 beyond, bonus 10-15%; trade marketing manager €50,000-€55,000 under 5 years in consumer goods (€40,000-€50,000 in retail, €30,000-€40,000 in wine), rising to over €65,000; key account manager €45,000-€55,000 to over €70,000, bonus 10-20%; national account manager €65,000-€75,000 to over €85,000, bonus 15-25%. Nearest analyst bands: marketing analyst €30,000-€38,000, retail marketing analyst €28,000-€35,000. Graduate-scheme pay: no reliable data found.
- US: entry pay: no reliable data found. Official medians for the retailer-side occupation: buyers and purchasing agents $77,710, lowest tenth under $48,380; purchasing managers $148,080 (BLS, May 2025; whole occupation, all industries).

**Career path.** Category analyst or sales graduate (0-2 years) → category executive, junior category manager or account manager (2-4) → category manager, trade marketing manager or key account manager (4-7) → national account manager or head of category (7-10) → commercial or sales director. Prospects gives two to three years per step in buying; other timings are my estimate. People move sideways between category, trade marketing and account roles.

**Exit opportunities.**
- After 2-3 years: account manager, brand management, buying at a retailer, shopper insights, e-commerce or retail media.
- After 7-10 years: commercial or sales director (in Italy €85,000-€95,000 rising to over €130,000, bonus 25-35%), category director at a retailer (€80,000-€100,000 rising to over €150,000; both Michael Page Italy 2026), general management.

**Tier list of employers.**
- Tier 1: commercial graduate entry at the large consumer-goods groups (P&G, Unilever, Nestle, Mondelez, Mars, PepsiCo, L'Oreal; in Italy Ferrero, Barilla, Lavazza) and the head offices of the largest grocers (Tesco, Sainsbury's; in Italy Esselunga, Coop, Conad).
- Tier 2: mid-sized food, drink and household manufacturers; discounters and specialist chains; the data firms that supply category teams (NielsenIQ, Circana, Kantar).
- Tier 3: small suppliers where one person covers sales, trade marketing and category.
- Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact.

**How to enter.**
- Degrees: Unilever accepts all disciplines for customer development; P&G names no subject and asks for leadership evidence, a driving licence and the right to work in the UK; Prospects says retail graduate programmes usually ask for a 2:2 or better. Business, economics or a numerate degree fits the analyst work.
- Programmes and calendar: P&G UK Sales Graduate Role: apply by 30 October 2026, online tests by 9 November, interviews until December, a two-day assessment in Harrogate on 14-15 December, start from January 2027; no rotations, one retailer account from the start. Unilever Future Leaders Programme: opens around the fourth quarter with a rolling close, starts in September, four stages; you choose one function and only your first application counts. Tesco UK product scheme and Tesco Ireland commercial programme: apply by 31 October 2026 for 6 September 2027.
- Italy: adverts usually say sales, trade marketing or customer development; national account manager is among the five most requested sales and marketing roles (Michael Page Italy 2026). No Italian scheme page with dates or pay could be opened.
- Skills: spreadsheets to a high standard, reading market-share and promotion data, a structured recommendation in slides.
- Common mistakes: applying to "marketing" and not knowing this commercial side exists; weak numeracy; no driving licence where field sales comes first.
- Non-target routes: sales support or field sales, shopper insights at a data firm, or a retailer's buying office, then across.
- Entry difficulty: 3/5 for direct analyst jobs; the Tier 1 schemes are as selective as brand management (see 3.1).

**Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for.** The retailer decides and you persuade; a delisting or a lost promotion slot can undo a year's plan. The work repeats around review cycles and is largely confined to consumer goods and retail. It is head-office work in specific places, sometimes five days on site, sometimes after a spell in field sales. Italian titles say "manager" early, but the analyst bands start around €30,000. Poor fit: people who want creative campaign work, who dislike negotiating, or who avoid spreadsheets.

### 3.12 Events and sponsorship manager

**What you actually do.** You plan and deliver events (conferences, trade fairs, product launches, festivals, sports hospitality), or you manage sponsorship: selling a rights holder's packages to brands, or running a brand's sponsorships so that it receives what it paid for. Prospects (November 2024) lists the event work: agree the brief and a detailed proposal (timeline, venue, suppliers, legal duties, staffing, budget); negotiate with venues and contractors; manage catering, travel and accommodation; promote the event with sales and marketing; brief staff and solve problems on the day; meet insurance and health-and-safety requirements; oversee dismantling and write the evaluation. The National Careers Service adds selling exhibition space and arranging sponsorship. A conference producer at an organiser researches topics, builds the programme, recruits speakers and supports sponsor sales (Informa advert, Boston, 2025). Sponsorship is a large market: €34.45 billion in Europe in 2025, 72% of it in sport; Italy €2.01 billion (Nielsen Sports for the European Sponsorship Association, March 2026). Tools: budget spreadsheets, registration and ticketing systems, supplier contracts, floor plans.

**A typical day and week.** Between events: supplier calls, site visits, budget updates and client or sponsor meetings in office hours. In the final fortnight: daily checks on registrations, schedules and deliveries. On site: the build, long shifts while the event runs, the breakdown, then invoices to reconcile and a report to write. BLS notes that planners often run several events at once and review the bills afterwards.

**Hours, stress and lifestyle.**
- Hours: 37-39 a week on paper, with evening, weekend and bank-holiday work described as common (National Careers Service). BLS: most planners work full time, many more than 40 hours, with extra hours as an event approaches and weekends during it (2025). Typical 37-45; peaks 60+ in event weeks (practitioner estimate; no survey figure found).
- Stress: 3/5, 4/5 in event weeks. BLS describes the work as fast paced and demanding, with many deadlines and several events at once. No measured data found: the industry salary survey that covers mental health and work-life balance (You Search & Select, over 1,300 respondents) sits behind a form and was not read.
- Lifestyle: frequent travel, nights away and physically active work (National Careers Service); a driving licence is usually required (Prospects). In office weeks two to three office days are standard (Live Recruitment, March 2026). Seasonal peaks.

**Human vs. quantitative profile.** People/communication: 5/5. Quantitative/technical: 2/5. Clients, suppliers, venues, sponsors and temporary staff all need managing at once. The numbers are budgets, quotes and attendance, plus audience and media-value figures in sponsorship.

**Compensation (approximate).**
- UK: £23,000 starter to £40,000 experienced (National Careers Service). Prospects (November 2024) gives £18,000-£22,000 at entry and £22,000-£26,000 with experience, but those figures are now below the legal minimum for a full-time adult, about £24,800 a year (my arithmetic: £12.71 an hour from 1 April 2026, GOV.UK, for 37.5 hours). Expect entry pay at or just above that floor. Event managers about £28,000 to over £40,000 (Prospects). Specialist roles pay more: conference producers £50,000-£65,000 or more, senior producers £60,000-£80,000 or more (Live Recruitment, March 2026; recruiter, method not stated, weak).
- US: meeting, convention and event planners had a median of $61,160 in May 2025; the lowest tenth earned under $36,830 and the highest tenth over $101,700 (BLS; whole occupation). One Informa advert for a conference producer in Boston with 1-3 years' experience offered $70,000-$75,000 (2025; single advert).
- Italy (Michael Page Italy 2026, gross annual RAL): communication and event specialist in fashion and luxury €30,000-€35,000 under 5 years' experience and €35,000-€50,000 at 5-10 years, bonus 0-10%; hotel event manager €30,000-€40,000 under 5 years, €40,000-€50,000 at 5-10 years, over €50,000 beyond, bonus 15-25%. Agencies and trade-fair organisers: no reliable data found.
- Sponsorship roles, any country: no reliable data found.

**Career path.** Events assistant or coordinator (0-2 years) → events executive or project manager (2-5) → events manager, producer or account manager (5-8) → senior manager, account director, or head of events or sponsorship. Prospects describes the same ladder (assistant, team leader, manager, senior manager, director) and says progress usually means changing employer or going freelance; the years are my estimate.

**Exit opportunities.**
- After 2-3 years: brand and partnership marketing, PR, venue or hospitality management, charity fundraising, sales at an organiser.
- After 7-10 years: head of events or partnerships, agency director, freelance work or your own agency (about 10% of US planners are self-employed; BLS, 2025).

**Tier list of employers.**
- Tier 1: exhibition and conference organisers (Informa, RX, Clarion; in Italy Fiera Milano and Italian Exhibition Group), sports and entertainment agencies (IMG, Wasserman, Octagon, Sportfive), and the events and sponsorship teams of large brands and rights holders (leagues, clubs, federations, festivals).
- Tier 2: venues, hotels, conference centres, universities, charities, local authorities, PR agencies with events teams (employer types from Prospects).
- Tier 3: small agencies and freelance work.
- Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact. Firm names are from general knowledge.

**How to enter.**
- Degrees: none required. Prospects says employers weigh skills and experience above the subject and lists events, hospitality, tourism, marketing and sport management as useful. BLS: a bachelor's degree is typical in the US, and event or hospitality study can bring more responsibility earlier.
- Experience: Prospects calls paid or voluntary experience essential: student-society or charity events, volunteering at large events, hotel, sales or customer-service jobs, placements.
- Routes: assistant jobs all year; the UK Level 3 Event Assistant apprenticeship. No large graduate scheme with published pay was found. Vacancy sources: the Association of Event Organisers, Campaign Jobs, specialist recruiters (Prospects).
- Qualifications: voluntary. In sponsorship the European Sponsorship Association runs an Introduction to Sponsorship course, a Sponsorship Sales Certificate and a Diploma (2027 intake open), and a jobs board. BLS notes voluntary certifications for meeting planners.
- Common mistakes: applying for the glamour; no evidence of handling a budget or a supplier.
- Non-target routes: venue or hotel operations, an agency assistant job, exhibition or sponsorship sales at an organiser.
- Entry difficulty: 3/5; Prospects calls the field competitive.

**Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for.** UK entry pay sits at the legal minimum, the hours are unsocial exactly when friends are free, and one bad day can undo months of work. Progress often means changing employer, and freelance income is less predictable (Prospects). The work is physical and involves time away from home. Poor fit: people who need routine or predictable hours, or who want early pay growth.

**Commentary.**
- **Pay:** FMCG schemes pay far more at entry than agencies in the UK; Germany pays more than Italy for the same FMCG name. Italy's agency and digital entry bands (€23-35k) are well below UK corporate schemes in nominal terms, and Italian bands for <5 years' experience (brand manager €40-50k) hide a lower true entry level. Sales has the highest ceiling: US AE median OTE $200k (Bridge Group 2026), Italian national account manager €75-85k.
- **Hours:** agencies and start-ups have the longest peaks; CRM and market research the most predictable. Italian "hybrid" is common: 64% in digital, 51% in sales and marketing (Michael Page Italy 2026).
- **Region:** US versus Europe - the US pays more at senior levels (BLS marketing manager median $166,790) and in tech sales; Europe pays less but with shorter hours and more vacation (practitioner view). Italy: luxury and FMCG are concentrated in Milan, with a strong North-South pay gap (Northern regions about 15% higher, JobPricing via Michael Page coverage). Paris is the luxury HQ; French internship minimum is €4.50 per hour above two months and the Lombardy minimum for an extracurricular internship is €500 gross per month under 2018 rules (check for updates). Asia: Hong Kong brand manager HK$35-45k per month, Singapore consumer-goods brand manager S$120k at 5-10 years (Morgan McKinley 2026); Dubai data is weak (job-board estimates AED 15-25k per month for assistant brand manager/marketing executive, method not stated; AED 25-50k for brand roles at regional groups); Tokyo: no reliable data found.
