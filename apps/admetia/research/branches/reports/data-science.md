## 1. What this branch is

Data science is the work of using statistics, programming and domain knowledge to answer questions and make predictions from data, so that someone can decide something: ship a feature, approve a loan, stock a warehouse, continue a clinical trial. The title "data scientist" now covers three quite different jobs: (1) analytics or product data science, where you run experiments and explain what changed and why; (2) machine-learning (ML) or modelling data science, where you build predictive models such as forecasts, recommendations, fraud scores; and (3) research or applied science, a mostly PhD-track job inventing new methods. Beside these sit older, more specialised professions that are data science in everything but name: operations research (optimisation), statistics and biostatistics (clinical trials, official statistics), and the modelling teams of banks and insurers. In October 2026 the market is two-speed: the US Bureau of Labor Statistics projects data scientist employment to grow 35% from 2025 to 2035 and a median pay of US$120,230 (May 2025), yet generalist "data scientist" postings and entry-level hiring have weakened as work shifts to ML/AI engineers and as LLM-based tools automate routine analysis. The first job is the hard part; pay for those who get in is high, especially in the US.

(2) Other branches cover data analysts/BI/analytics engineering, AI/ML engineering and AI research, software and data engineering, and quant finance; they appear here only as cross-references (see the boundary table in 2.0). ## 2. Map of areas and sectors

### 2.0 Where data science ends and its neighbours begin

Students confuse these because job titles overlap. A rule of thumb: ask what the main deliverable is.

| Neighbour branch | Main deliverable | How it differs from data science |
|---|---|---|
| Data analytics (analysts, BI, analytics engineering) | Dashboards, reports, clean metric tables | Describes what happened using SQL and BI tools; less modelling, less statistics theory. Many data scientists start here. Covered in the Data Analytics branch. |
| AI/ML engineering and AI research | A model running reliably in production, or a new model architecture | Software-engineering heavy (APIs, pipelines, deployment, LLM applications). A data scientist prototypes; an ML engineer ships and operates it. Covered in the AI branch. |
| Software and data engineering | Code, systems, data pipelines and warehouses | Builds the platform the data scientist uses. Covered in the Computer Science branch. |
| Quant finance (quant researchers, traders, quant developers) | Trading and pricing models in markets | Same maths, but the client is the market and pay and selectivity are in a different league. Credit-risk modelling at a bank is data science; derivatives pricing is quant. |
| Actuarial | Reserves, premiums and capital for regulated insurance, signed off under professional exams | Legally accountable and exam-gated; data scientists in insurance build predictive models but do not sign reserving opinions. |

### 2.1 What "data scientist" means in 2026, and how the title has split

The title was popularised around 2012 as "the sexiest job" combining statistics, coding and business sense. By 2026 it has fragmented. Large companies now run separate ladders: for example Levels.fyi lists Meta data scientist levels IC3-IC8 with a different ladder for machine learning engineers, and a career-advice source describes the split as product/analytics data science, ML data science and analytics data science, noting the clean split only holds at companies with enough headcount to specialise (resumegeni and codingshuttle pieces, 2026; low-quality sources, treat as description not evidence). In practice:

- **Analytics / product data science**: experiments, metrics, causal inference (methods for working out whether X caused Y rather than merely correlating with it). Closest to a product manager. Little model building.
- **ML / modelling data science**: forecasting, recommendation, pricing, churn, fraud. Python, feature engineering (turning raw data into inputs a model can use), model evaluation. Increasingly overlaps with ML engineering.
- **Research / applied science**: new methods, papers, often a PhD.

**Effect of LLMs and AutoML (automated model building).** The evidence is mixed and partly vendor-sourced:
- BLS still projects 35% growth for data scientists, 2025-35, 24,800 openings a year, median pay US$120,230, and 275,600 jobs in 2025 (BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook, accessed 9 Oct 2026). BLS projections are produced from historical trends and may not capture a sudden AI shock.
- Indeed Hiring Lab data, as relayed by Interview Query and Pin (secondary, late 2025): data scientist postings were up about 15% over three years while tech postings overall were more than 30% below pre-pandemic levels as of late October 2025; roughly 45% of Indeed data and analytics postings contained AI-related terms by end-2025. I could not open the original Indeed report.
- A vendor dataset (Interview Query, 2026, weak) reports interview activity for data scientist roles falling from 1,763 sessions a month in September 2025 to 772 in June 2026 (-56%) while ML Engineer and AI Engineer demand rose. A Refonte Learning post claims generalist data scientist listings fell about 25% year on year on LinkedIn with AI engineer postings up over 70% (unverified, could not confirm against LinkedIn).
- Stanford's "Canaries in the Coal Mine" paper (Brynjolfsson, Chandar and Chen, August 2025), using ADP payroll data, found employment of 22-25 year-olds in the most AI-exposed occupations fell about 13% relative to less exposed ones, while older workers in the same jobs were stable. Software developers and customer service were the headline examples; data science was not isolated in what I could verify. Authors call it consistent with, not proof of, AI effects.
- Meta, July 2026: Alex Schultz, previously CMO and head of Analytics (which includes data science), became Meta's first chief data officer, and he cited an internal Analytics Agent as Meta's most widely used AI agent (The Star / Spokesman-Review, 1-2 July 2026). It signals that routine analytics is being automated at the largest employers.

Net reading: tools like AutoML and LLM coding assistants have made writing a basic model or query cheap, so entry hiring has narrowed toward people who can frame a business problem, design experiments and judge model output. Employers' demand is shifting to ML/AI engineering. Demand for statistically rigorous people (causal inference, experimentation, regulated modelling) looks sturdier than demand for "can fit a random forest".

### 2.2 Product and experimentation data science

- **Definition**: Using A/B tests (randomised experiments comparing a new version of a product with the current one), metric design and causal inference to guide product decisions.
- **In practice**: you define success metrics for a feature, size a two-week test on checkout, write the SQL for conversion lift, check for pitfalls (novelty effects, network effects, multiple comparisons), and present a go/no-go to the product team. Also: diagnosing metric drops, forecasting growth, building north-star metrics.
- **Employers**: tech and consumer platforms (Meta, Google, Netflix, Airbnb, Uber, DoorDash, Stripe, Pinterest, Snap among the top payers on Levels.fyi, 2026; in Europe Booking.com, Spotify, Zalando, Delivery Hero, Wolt, Revolut, Monzo; in Asia Grab, Rakuten, PayPay), plus e-commerce, fintech, gaming and media.

### 2.3 Machine-learning / modelling data science

- **Definition**: Building predictive or optimisation models that feed a product or an operational decision: demand forecasting, recommendation, dynamic pricing, customer churn, fraud detection, predictive maintenance.
- **In practice**: framing the target variable, assembling features from messy tables, training and validating models, handing them to engineers for deployment (MLOps), monitoring drift. Increasingly includes LLM-based features (classification, extraction, search).
- **Employers**: big tech and scale-ups; retail and e-commerce; telecoms; energy and utilities (Enel is the highest-paying Italian employer on Levels.fyi's Italian data scientist page, 2026, small sample); manufacturing; media.

### 2.4 Decision science and operations research

- **Definition**: Operations research (OR) uses mathematical optimisation (finding the best allocation under constraints), simulation and queueing theory to improve operations; "decision science" is the business-facing label for the same family plus behavioural and econometric methods.
- **In practice**: route and schedule optimisation, inventory and network design, workforce rostering, airline pricing and revenue management (how many seats to sell at which price), warehouse and delivery simulation, energy dispatch. Tools: solvers (Gurobi, CPLEX, OR-Tools), Python, simulation packages, sometimes AMPL/Julia.
- **Employers**: logistics and e-commerce operators, airlines, retailers, utilities and grid operators, manufacturers, defence and government analysts, and software vendors who sell planning engines. BLS says operations research analysts work mostly in finance and insurance (26%), professional/technical services (22%), management of companies (9%), federal government (6%) and manufacturing (4%) (BLS, May 2025). I did not verify specific employers' OR teams in this round (e.g. Amazon's supply-chain optimisation group); examples named in role section 3.3 are general industry knowledge.

### 2.5 Statistics and biostatistics

- **Definition**: Designing studies and analysing data with rigorous inference. Biostatistics applies this to health: clinical trials, epidemiology, public health. Official statistics produces national numbers (inflation, employment, census).
- **In practice**: writing statistical analysis plans for a trial, sample-size calculation, randomisation, analysing endpoints, interacting with regulators; or producing survey estimates and quality-controlling national statistics. Tools: R and SAS dominate pharma, with CDISC data standards (SDTM/ADaM) as the industry format.
- **Employers**: pharma and biotech (e.g. GSK has a 2026 statistics/biostatistics graduate programme in Siena, Italy, requiring an MSc with a biostatistics focus, R assessed, and fluent English and Italian), contract research organisations (CROs, firms that run trials for drugmakers: IQVIA, Parexel and others), regulators, hospitals and universities, and national statistics offices: ISTAT (Italy), ONS (UK), Eurostat (EU). ISTAT opened a 195-post permanent recruitment in 2026 (54 researchers, 46 technologists, 95 technical collaborators); researcher/technologist applications closed 22 January 2026 (ISTAT/press, accessed Oct 2026). BLS: statisticians are most often employed by the federal government (16%), R&D in life/physical/engineering sciences (15%) and healthcare (8%).

### 2.6 Data science in financial services and insurance

- **Definition**: Credit scoring (predicting the chance a borrower defaults), fraud detection, customer analytics, insurance pricing and claims, anti-money-laundering models, plus model validation.
- **In practice**: models are regulated and must be explainable and documented; the work includes model risk governance, bias testing and sign-off by independent validators. Credit-risk modelling (probability of default, IFRS 9 provisioning) is a mature specialism. In insurance, predictive pricing models sit next to the actuarial function: Solvency UK/II requires the actuarial function to opine on underwriting policy, so pricing is where actuaries and data scientists collide (Novarica 2016 warned of friction; vendor Akur8 argues for hybrid "actuarial data scientists"; low-quality sources).
- **Employers**: retail and commercial banks, card issuers (Capital One), fintechs (Monzo, Revolut), insurers (Admiral, Aviva, Allianz, Generali, AXA). BLS: insurance carriers employ 10% and credit intermediation 6% of US data scientists.
- **Versus actuaries and quants**: actuaries are exam-qualified professionals responsible for reserves and capital; quants price and trade in markets (see Finance, Quant branch). Bank data scientists mostly model customers and risk, not markets.

### 2.7 Research and applied science

- **Definition**: "Applied scientist" (Amazon's title; similar titles elsewhere: research scientist, scientist) builds new or heavily customised models and often publishes. Economist and "economic data scientist" roles in tech use econometrics (statistics for economic data, esp. causal) to study pricing, marketplaces and advertising. Cross-reference Economics.
- **In practice**: reading papers, prototyping models, running offline and online evaluations, writing internal or external papers, sometimes shipping production code.
- **Employers**: Amazon, Google, Meta, Microsoft, Apple, large marketplaces and platforms, research labs; AI labs sit in the AI branch. Amazon's US applied scientist pay is US$247k (L4) to US$413k (L6) in total compensation (Levels.fyi, 9 Oct 2026, 107 submissions).

### 2.8 Data science consulting

- **Definition**: Building analytics and AI solutions for client companies, as a practice inside a consultancy (McKinsey QuantumBlack, BCG X), a Big Four or IT-services analytics team (Deloitte, Accenture, Capgemini), or a specialist boutique (Reply and Bip in Italy).
- **In practice**: 3-6 month client projects, mixing modelling with stakeholder management; heavy emphasis on PowerPoint and getting the client to adopt the model.
- **Employers**: strategy consultancies, Big Four, IT integrators, boutiques. Clients are banks, energy companies, retailers, public bodies.

## 3. Role families

### 3.1 Product / experimentation data scientist

- **What you actually do**: You sit inside a product team (checkout, search, ads, growth) and own the numbers behind decisions. Concrete tasks: define the success metric and guardrail metrics for a feature; design and size an A/B test (power, sample size, test duration); write SQL against event tables to compute conversion lift and confidence intervals; investigate why a metric dropped 3% on Tuesday; build a causal estimate when you cannot randomise (difference-in-differences, matching, instrumental variables); forecast user growth; present a recommendation ("ship, don't ship, or run it longer") to a product manager and engineers. Tools: SQL (daily), Python or R, a notebook environment, an experimentation platform, dashboards. Deliverables: experiment readouts, metric definitions, analysis memos, occasionally a model.
- **A typical day and week**: Mornings: check experiment dashboards and metric alerts; answer ad-hoc questions in chat. Midday: write SQL and Python for an analysis; a 30-minute sync with the product manager and designer. Afternoon: write up a readout. Weekly: planning meeting, experiment review, a deeper project (e.g. redefining "active user"). Roughly 30-40% of time is meetings and communication (consensus).
- **Hours, stress and lifestyle**: Typical 40-50 hours a week, peaks around launches or quarter planning of 55-60 (consensus; no survey found). Stress 3/5: steady deadlines, pressure when a launch decision hangs on your readout, but rarely overnight crises. Flexible hybrid is common in tech; fully remote less so since 2023 (consensus). No travel beyond offsites. In the US the big employers pay for the flexibility with at-will culture and periodic layoffs (consensus).
- **Human vs. quantitative profile**: People/communication: 4/5. Quantitative/technical: 3/5. Your statistics must be correct, but the scarce skill is persuading a non-technical team to act on a result, so Meta's interview rubric reportedly values product thinking over complicated maths (Glassdoor candidate report, anecdotal).
- **Compensation (approximate)**:
  - US (Levels.fyi, 9 Oct 2026, total compensation = base + stock + bonus): Google data scientist L3 US$196k, L4 US$281k, L5 (senior) US$380k; Meta IC3 US$153k, IC4 US$240k, IC5 US$375k, IC6 US$486k (median all levels US$320k); all-employer US median US$180k (n=3,376), 25th percentile US$134k, 75th US$250k, San Francisco Bay Area median US$250k, NYC US$190-200k. Self-reported and skewed to big tech; BLS median for all data scientists is US$120,230 (May 2025), 10th percentile US$67,240, 90th US$199,130.
  - UK: London all-levels median GBP 91,096 (Levels.fyi, 9 Oct 2026, n=326; Meta ~GBP 144.7k and JPMorgan ~GBP 143.8k at the top); 25th percentile GBP 65.7k. No reliable entry-level figure for big-tech product data science found; Morgan McKinley's 2026 guide gives GBP 85-100k as an average London range for data scientists (all levels).
  - Continental Europe: Germany median EUR 77,643 (Levels.fyi, 9 Oct 2026, n=207); Berlin EUR 112,951; Delivery Hero EUR 108k, Wolt EUR 107k, Zalando EUR 102k. Italy: median EUR 46,145 (n=23, all levels), Milan EUR 56,058 (small sample); Glassdoor (Feb 2026, 214 salaries) junior data scientist in Italy about EUR 29,000 gross (EUR 26-33k); Indeed Italy average EUR 34,617 (Aug 2026, 160 salaries). Pay for pure product data science in Italy: no reliable data found (the few such roles are at multinationals' Milan offices or Italian scale-ups).
  - Asia hubs: Singapore median SGD 120,084 (Levels.fyi, n=128), Grab ~SGD 160k; Tokyo median JPY 8.76m (n=16; PayPay ~JPY 18.4m); Hong Kong median HKD 803k (n=12); Dubai median AED 437,560 (n=20). The last three are very thin samples.
- **Career path**: Data scientist (0-3 years; Google L3/Meta IC3) → data scientist II/senior (3-6 years; L4-L5/IC4-IC5) → staff/lead, or manager of data science (7-10 years). Titles drift: "product analyst" or "analytics" at smaller firms.
- **Exit opportunities**: After 2-3 years: ML/AI roles at the same firm, product management, growth/strategy at start-ups, other tech employers. After 7-10 years: head of analytics/data science, product management leadership, chief-of-staff or strategy roles, startup founder, consulting. Meta's Alex Schultz is a recent example of a growth/analytics leader being promoted to chief data officer (July 2026).
- **Tier list of employers**: Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact.
  - Tier 1: Meta, Google, Netflix, Airbnb, Uber, Stripe, DoorDash, Pinterest, Snap. Highest total compensation (Netflix US$500k, DoorDash and Stripe US$450k median on Levels.fyi), most rigorous experimentation cultures and tooling, but the hardest interviews and the thinnest entry intake.
  - Tier 2: Microsoft, Amazon, Spotify, Booking.com, Zalando, Delivery Hero, Wolt, Revolut, Monzo, Grab, Rakuten. Strong analytics, pay 30-50% below Tier 1 in the US, closer in Europe, broader scope.
  - Tier 3: retailers, telecoms, media, marketplaces and fintechs outside the top group; agencies. Smaller teams, less mature experimentation, more dashboard work, lower pay, more generalist learning.
- **How to enter**:
  - Degrees and backgrounds: Bachelor's in statistics, mathematics, economics, computer science, engineering or a quantitative social science; a master's is common at the top firms but not required. Economists with causal-inference training are a classic feeder. Bachelor's is BLS's typical entry requirement for data scientists; some employers want a master's.
  - Internships and graduate programmes: US tech internships (summer, applications opened in the preceding autumn). Monzo's Associate Data Scientist internship (London, STEM/CS degree graduating 2028, SQL and Looker; employer listing, 30 Sep 2026) is an example of European early-stage hiring. Recruiting calendars: US autumn (Aug-Oct) for the following summer; UK graduate schemes open Aug-Sep and many close Sept-Dec (consensus, consistent with the banking examples in 3.5).
  - Interviews: for Meta, a recruiter screen, a technical phone screen on SQL and probability, then a 4-5 round virtual onsite covering SQL (window functions, rolling averages), product sense (define metrics and guardrails for a fictional app), experimentation (sample size, power, novelty and network effects), statistics intuition and behavioural questions (Interview Query and Glassdoor candidate reports, 2025-26; anecdotal). Take-homes are common at smaller firms.
  - Certifications: do not matter here; a portfolio does. Skills and portfolio: strong SQL, Python (pandas, statsmodels), hypothesis testing, causal inference basics (Mostly Harmless Econometrics-style methods), a project with a real experiment or natural experiment (e.g. a public dataset where you estimate the effect of a policy or a price change).
  - Common mistakes: overweighting machine learning (product roles test SQL and judgement), ignoring business context, reporting p-values without effect sizes, poor communication.
  - Alternative routes: analyst → data scientist after 1-3 years inside the same company; business or marketing graduates can reach product analytics via strong SQL, a statistics minor and a reference project; non-target schools rely on projects and smaller firms.
  - Entry difficulty: 4/5 at Tier 1 and 2 (hundreds of applicants per opening, consensus; 2025-26 junior freeze), 3/5 elsewhere.
- **Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for**: Entry intake has contracted as AI tools absorb routine analysis; the role at some firms has become a dashboard-and-ad-hoc-queries job with limited influence; experiments often produce flat results and stakeholders may ignore them; US big-tech layoffs happen. Not a good fit if you want to build models all day (that is ML data science or ML engineering) or you dislike negotiating with product managers.

### 3.2 Machine-learning / modeling data scientist

- **What you actually do**: You turn a business prediction problem into a working model. Examples: forecasting demand for 50,000 products per store so replenishment can order stock; ranking items on a home page; predicting which customers will cancel and testing which retention offer works; scoring transactions for fraud. Tasks: gather and clean data, feature engineering, train and compare models (gradient-boosted trees such as XGBoost or LightGBM remain the workhorse for tabular data; deep learning for text/images), validate offline and online, document, hand over to engineers or deploy yourself, and monitor for model drift (performance decay as the world changes). Increasingly: prototype LLM-based features (classifying support tickets, extracting fields from documents) and evaluate them. Tools: Python, SQL, scikit-learn, PyTorch, Spark, cloud platforms, experiment trackers, Git.
- **A typical day and week**: Morning stand-up with the ML engineer and product owner; debugging data quality; training runs and code review; mid-week presentation of model results to the business owner; periodic retraining and model-monitoring reviews. About half the time is data work, not modelling (consensus).
- **Hours, stress and lifestyle**: 40-50 typical, 55-60 peak around launches or incidents (consensus). Stress 3/5; on-call is uncommon unless the model is customer-facing (consensus). Hybrid is common; project cycles of 1-3 months.
- **Human vs. quantitative profile**: People/communication: 3/5. Quantitative/technical: 4/5. You must be genuinely fluent in statistics and code; explaining model limits to stakeholders is also needed, but less than in product roles.
- **Compensation (approximate)**:
  - US: BLS median for data scientists US$120,230 (May 2025); P10 US$67,240; P90 US$199,130. Levels.fyi top-company data above apply when the job is at big tech. Amazon applied scientist (the research-leaning cousin) L4 US$247k (Levels.fyi, 9 Oct 2026). Non-tech employers (insurers, retailers) pay far less than the Levels.fyi medians suggest; BLS is the better guide for the average market.
  - UK: banks' data science graduate schemes advertise GBP 42,000 (NatWest, 12-month data science programme, 2027 intake) and GBP 48,500 (Lloyds Banking Group Data Science & AI scheme, 2 years); Capital One UK GBP 46,500-55,500 plus GBP 5,000 joining bonus on its technology programme (employer listings, September 2026). Mid-career London: GBP 60-90k (Pocketwise, a general site, weak); senior GBP 70-100k nationally.
  - Italy: junior roughly EUR 29-42k gross (Glassdoor Feb 2026 EUR 26-33k; NettoCalc Milan EUR 42,000 for 0-3 years, a weaker aggregator); Generali Italia listing for a junior innovation and data analytics role EUR 36,000 (undated). Mid-career Italy and senior: no reliable data found beyond Levels.fyi's Italy 75th percentile EUR 60k and 90th EUR 74k (all levels, n=23). Hays Italy's 2026 guide gives EUR 56,360 as the average RAL across all professions surveyed, not data science.
  - Switzerland (for reference): Zurich senior data scientist median CHF 150,748 (n=16); all-levels CHF 116,413 (Levels.fyi, 9 Oct 2026). Singapore: median SGD 120k (all levels); Skillup (2026, MyCareersFuture data from March 2024) gives SGD 60k at 0-2 years, 105k at 2-5, 140k at 5-8, 180k at 8+ (base).
- **Career path**: Junior/associate data scientist (0-2 years) → data scientist (2-5) → senior (5-8) → staff/principal or lead (8+). Many people move to "ML engineer" or "AI engineer" at the mid-level because that title currently carries more demand.
- **Exit opportunities**: After 2-3 years: ML engineer, AI engineer, data scientist at a larger employer, product analytics, quant research (with strong maths, see Finance). After 7-10 years: lead/head of data science, ML engineering management, AI product, consulting, founder.
- **Tier list of employers**: Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact.
  - Tier 1: big tech ML organisations and top ML-driven scale-ups (Google, Meta, Netflix, Stripe, Spotify, Zalando, Booking.com). Large data, mature MLOps, ML-engineer colleagues; very selective.
  - Tier 2: banks and insurers with real analytics functions (Capital One, JPMorgan, Lloyds, NatWest, Admiral, Allianz), retailers with data platforms, telecoms, energy majors (Enel, Eni), larger Italian and European corporates. Real problems and good training, slower tooling, more governance.
  - Tier 3: consultancies' delivery teams, mid-market firms and IT-services outsourcers where "data scientist" can mean reporting and light modelling.
- **How to enter**:
  - Degrees: bachelor's in computer science, statistics, mathematics, physics, engineering or economics; a master's in data science, statistics, machine learning or an applied field is the common path and is expected for most European roles. A PhD is not needed for most modelling roles. UK banks typically require a 2:1 in a STEM subject (NatWest). Lloyds states no minimum degree classification but expects demonstrable technical skills and does not sponsor visas.
  - Programmes: NatWest Graduate Trainee Data Science (12 months, GBP 42,000, 2027 intake); Lloyds Data Science and AI graduate scheme (2 years, 2 year-long placements incl. fraud and credit risk); Capital One UK technology programme; UK bank intakes typically run September to January with applications closing in autumn, so 2027 intake rounds are running now. Admiral's analytics/data science scheme (Cardiff): two 25-month programmes, 2026 intake deadline 26 Oct 2025 (listing).
  - Interviews: online assessment (logic, maths), SQL and Python coding test, then case or technical interviews on statistics and model evaluation; take-home projects are common at start-ups. Italian consultancies describe an online test, SQL/Python technical interview and optional case (Glassdoor candidate reports on Accenture and Reply, anecdotal).
  - Certifications: cloud ones (AWS, Azure, Google) help only slightly; kaggle rankings and projects matter more. Skills: Python, SQL, scikit-learn and gradient boosting, validation and leakage, basic cloud and Git, communication. Portfolio: an end-to-end project with deployment (even a small API), a forecasting or fraud project on public data, a GitHub repo with clean code.
  - Common mistakes: modelling without checking the data, leakage, optimising accuracy instead of the business metric, building huge deep-learning models where a simple baseline works.
  - Alternative routes: analyst → data scientist; software engineers moving into ML; non-target schools can use competitions, open-source contributions and internships at mid-sized firms.
  - Entry difficulty: 4/5.
- **Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for**: Much of the day is data cleaning, and many models never reach production; the title is being replaced by ML/AI engineer, which asks for software skills; entry hiring has tightened (see 2.1). Not a good fit if you dislike coding or messy data, or want research freedom (that is 3.6).

### 3.3 Decision scientist / operations research analyst

- **What you actually do**: You build mathematical models that tell a business what to do. Concrete examples: decide which warehouse serves which customers, design delivery routes for 500 vans, schedule crews and aircraft, set airline fares and seat limits per flight (revenue management), size safety stock, simulate a new hospital layout, optimise power dispatch. Method: formulate the problem (objective, decision variables, constraints), solve with a solver or heuristic, test against simulation and historical data, deliver a tool or recommendation. Tools: Python, a modelling library or solver (Gurobi, CPLEX, OR-Tools, Pyomo), simulation software, SQL, sometimes Julia/AMPL; Excel remains common at the business end. Deliverables: optimisation engines, what-if tools, business cases quantifying savings.
- **A typical day and week**: Mix of modelling (50%) and stakeholder work (clarifying constraints with planners, validating that the model "feels right" to the people who run operations). Weekly cadence of progress reviews; occasional site visits to warehouses or hangars.
- **Hours, stress and lifestyle**: 38-45 typical, 50-55 peak at go-live or planning season (consensus). Stress 2/5 (occasional crunch at go-live or when an operational crisis hits). Mostly desk work, light travel to sites. Hybrid common. A BLS note: most operations research analysts work full time.
- **Human vs. quantitative profile**: People/communication: 3/5. Quantitative/technical: 5/5 at research-heavy teams; 4/5 for analyst roles. Mathematical modelling is the core, but the model only matters if planners trust it.
- **Compensation (approximate)**:
  - US: BLS operations research analysts median US$88,940 (May 2025); P10 US$57,060; P90 US$159,910. Growth 12% 2025-35, about 7,500 openings a year. Entry roughly US$55-75k typical outside tech (state career portals show about US$48k for entry-level; older and weak). Amazon-type optimisation scientist roles in big tech fall in the applied-scientist band (3.6); not verified for OR-specific titles.
  - UK: a careers-profile figure of GBP 25-29k starting and GBP 40-80k+ for experienced operational researchers (undated, probably older); UK Government Operational Research Service (GORS) sandwich-placement advert Nov 2025 GBP 22,699-31,324 pro rata; Fast Stream operational researchers start around GBP 27k (older figure). ITJobsWatch "decision scientist" median GBP 82,500 (6 months to 10 May 2025, only 2 salaries quoted; anecdotal).
  - Italy: no reliable data found. Continental Europe and Asia: no reliable data found.
- **Career path**: OR analyst/associate (0-3) → senior analyst/scientist (3-7) → lead/principal scientist, optimisation architect or planning manager (7-12). Public-sector route: GORS analyst → senior → head of profession.
- **Exit opportunities**: After 2-3 years: supply-chain planning and analytics, data science, consulting (operations), software vendors of planning tools. After 7-10 years: head of supply-chain analytics, product owner of an optimisation product, consulting partner track, operations leadership.
- **Tier list of employers**: Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact. Employer names below are general industry knowledge, not verified in this round.
  - Tier 1: large logistics/e-commerce optimisation groups, major airlines' revenue-management and network-planning teams, planning-software vendors (Gurobi, Kinaxis, Blue Yonder, o9, FICO), and defence/government research bodies. Deepest OR work and best pay; few entry posts.
  - Tier 2: manufacturers, retailers, utilities and grid operators (Enel and Terna in Italy), banks' operations units, consultancies' supply-chain practices.
  - Tier 3: general operations analyst roles where "analyst" means Excel reporting.
- **How to enter**:
  - Degrees: industrial/management engineering, mathematics, applied mathematics, operations research, computer science, physics; a master's (OR, industrial engineering, applied maths) is the usual route for modelling roles, and BLS says a bachelor's is the minimum with some employers preferring a master's. Italy is strong in the field through engineering faculties (e.g. gestionale/industrial engineering) - consensus; I did not verify programme details.
  - Programmes and calendars: UK government GORS via Fast Stream (applications open autumn); corporate supply-chain graduate programmes follow the usual autumn-to-spring cycle.
  - Interviews: optimisation modelling case (formulate a vehicle routing or scheduling problem on a whiteboard), probability and statistics, coding (Python), behavioural.
  - Certifications: INFORMS Certified Analytics Professional (CAP) now has three tiers: CAP-Essentials (early career, no eligibility requirements), CAP-Pro, and CAP-Expert (requires an advanced degree with 2 years of analytics experience, or bachelor's with 4, or 8 years' experience). They are recognised in the US more than in Europe; they help rather than decide.
  - Skills and portfolio: linear/integer programming, simulation, solver use, a project such as a delivery-route optimiser or a staff-rostering model on open data.
  - Common mistakes: building elegant models without involving the planners; ignoring data quality; not quantifying business value.
  - Alternative routes: logistics or supply-chain graduates add a quantitative master's or a strong Python/solver portfolio; data analysts can move via planning teams.
  - Entry difficulty: 3/5 (smaller field, specific skills, but fewer applicants than product data science).
- **Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for**: Niche job market (a small number of employers use real optimisation); many roles are labelled "analyst" and become reporting; adoption by planners can be slow; pay is lower than product/ML data science outside big tech. Not a good fit if you dislike formal mathematics or want fast-moving product work.

### 3.4 Statistician / biostatistician

- **What you actually do**: In pharma or at a CRO (contract research organisation, which runs clinical trials for drug companies), you are the person who makes sure a trial's conclusions are statistically valid. Tasks: write the statistical analysis plan (the pre-agreed recipe for analysing the trial), calculate sample size, specify the randomisation, review the case report forms, analyse efficacy and safety endpoints, handle missing data, produce tables and figures for the clinical study report, and answer regulators' questions. Statistical programmers (a close neighbour role) write the SAS or R code to produce datasets in CDISC standards (SDTM/ADaM) and the outputs. In official statistics (ISTAT, ONS, Eurostat) you design surveys, weight and estimate, check quality and publish national figures such as inflation or unemployment. In public health, you analyse cohort and registry data. Tools: SAS (still dominant in regulated pharma), R, Python growing; survival analysis, mixed models, Bayesian methods.
- **A typical day and week**: Mix of programming and analysis (40%), documentation and review (30%), meetings with clinicians and data managers (20%), and waiting for data (10%). Workload peaks around database lock and submission deadlines; calmer in between (consensus). In national statistics offices the rhythm follows publication calendars.
- **Hours, stress and lifestyle**: 38-45 typical; 50+ during database lock or regulatory submission (consensus). Stress 2/5 (occasional crunch around deadlines; accuracy pressure because errors affect regulatory decisions). Remote or hybrid is widespread, particularly at CROs (consensus). Little travel. Public sector hours are 36-40 with strong job security in Italy and the UK (consensus).
- **Human vs. quantitative profile**: People/communication: 3/5. Quantitative/technical: 5/5. Statistical correctness is the job; you also need to explain trade-offs to physicians and regulators.
- **Compensation (approximate)**:
  - US: BLS statisticians median US$105,650 (May 2025); P10 US$64,000; P90 US$174,050; highest-paying industry computer systems design (median US$167,180), federal government US$132,620, R&D in life/physical/engineering sciences US$130,590, healthcare US$100,900. Levels.fyi lists Biostatistician as a top-paying data-scientist-adjacent title at US$143,000 median (9 Oct 2026; sample not disclosed). Entry-level biostatistician: no reliable US figure found in this round.
  - UK: Payscale (2026) average biostatistician in London GBP 49,132 (range GBP 33-58k), early-career London GBP 33,971; advertised CRO statistician roles GBP 45-55k (experienced late-phase and senior roles; undated listings); ITJobsWatch biostatistics median GBP 80,000 (6 months to 25 July 2026, 6 salaries; anecdotal). Government: ONS Higher Statistical Officer GBP 35,144 (advert closing 6 January 2026); GBP 30-35k (advert closing September 2025); ONS statistician about GBP 39.4k (2024 advert); senior statistician about GBP 43.0k; Fast Stream Statistician starts around GBP 28-31k (older figures).
  - Italy: no reliable data found for biostatistician pay. ISTAT hires through public competitions (concorsi) with a nationally set pay scale; 2026 competition for 54 researchers and 46 technologists (third level, master's degree required; a doctorate or documented research experience counted favourably) closed 22 January 2026 (ISTAT, accessed Oct 2026); pay in the call text not retrieved. GSK Siena's 2026 statistics/biostatistics graduate programme: pay not stated.
  - Continental Europe elsewhere and Asia: no reliable data found.
- **Career path**: Junior/associate biostatistician or statistical programmer (0-3 years) → biostatistician / senior (3-6) → principal / associate director (7-10 at CROs; IQVIA's associate-director postings ask for a master's with seven years' CRO/pharma experience, or a doctorate with five) → director/VP biostatistics. Academic/public-health track: research statistician → lecturer. Official-statistics track: statistical officer → statistician → senior statistician.
- **Exit opportunities**: After 2-3 years: another CRO or pharma company, regulatory agencies, health-data science, real-world evidence, statistical programming → data engineering, clinical data science. After 7-10 years: head of biostatistics, clinical development leadership, HEOR or market-access modelling (cross-reference Healthcare/Pharma), consultancy, regulator.
- **Tier list of employers**: Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact.
  - Tier 1: large pharma (Roche, Novartis, AstraZeneca, GSK, Novo Nordisk, Sanofi, Pfizer, MSD, J&J, Lilly, Bayer) and top biotech; regulators (FDA, EMA) and elite academic trial units (e.g. the UK's MRC/university units). Better pay and scientific influence, hardest to enter (Roche and AXA are the top Zurich payers on Levels.fyi data scientist pages, 2026).
  - Tier 2: large CROs (IQVIA, Parexel, Syneos, ICON, Thermo Fisher PPD, Fortrea) and mid-size pharma (Italian examples: Chiesi, Menarini, Zambon; names from general knowledge, not verified for biostatistics). Large entry intake, structured training, more repetitive workload and billable-hour pressure.
  - Tier 3: small CROs, hospitals and general statistical consultancies; public-sector offices (ISTAT, ONS) sit apart: lower pay, high stability, good work-life balance, meaningful but less commercial work.
- **How to enter**:
  - Degrees: a master's in statistics or biostatistics is the standard ticket (BLS: statisticians typically need a master's, some entry jobs accept a bachelor's; IQVIA's UK graduate postings (2021-22) accepted a BSc or MSc with six months of experience). Italy: Laurea Magistrale in Biostatistica (Milano-Bicocca, taught in Italian, admission interview) or Statistical Methods and Applications (Sapienza, in English), or Statistics and Data Science at Florence with a Biostatistics curriculum (university pages, accessed Oct 2026). GSK Siena's 2026 programme requires an MSc with a strong biostatistics or medical statistics focus, R assessed, and fluent English and Italian. Mathematics, physics, engineering and economics graduates enter with a statistics master's.
  - Programmes and calendar: pharma graduate programmes recruit on a rolling or annual autumn basis (GSK is rolling); CRO graduate roles hire continuously; the UK Government Statistical Service Fast Stream applies a 3.5-year scheme and requires a 2:1 in a numerate subject with taught statistics (GOV.UK/Civil Service). ISTAT hires only through formal concorsi, announced irregularly; applications via the InPA portal, which requires a digital identity (SPID, CIE or CNS).
  - Interviews: statistics theory (confidence intervals, hypothesis tests, survival analysis, regression), SAS or R coding test, case about trial design (how many patients? what endpoint?), behavioural. Public-sector roles use written exams or the concorso format.
  - Certifications: Chartered Statistician (Royal Statistical Society) and the ASA's PStat are valued by employers, particularly in the UK and US, but not required to enter (general knowledge, not verified this round). SAS certifications help marginally.
  - Skills and portfolio: R and SAS, CDISC basics, clinical trial concepts, survival analysis; a project analysing open trial data.
  - Common mistakes: applying with machine learning projects only and no inference background; weak SAS/R; ignoring regulatory context.
  - Alternative routes: a maths or biology bachelor's plus a one-year statistics MSc; statistical programmer → biostatistician; data analysts in healthcare moving via a part-time master's.
  - Entry difficulty: 3/5 (master's required, but CROs hire many people).
- **Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for**: Work is regulated and procedural; innovation is slower; SAS and rigid documentation can feel old-fashioned; CRO workload spikes and billable pressure; pay in Europe is moderate. Not a good fit if you want fast-moving product environments or if you dislike formal documentation and statistics theory.

### 3.5 Data scientist in banking and insurance (credit, fraud, pricing)

- **What you actually do**: You build and maintain models that decide money questions: probability of default for loan applicants (credit scoring), the expected loss reserve under accounting standard IFRS 9, transaction fraud scores, customer lifetime value, claims-severity and pricing models for car or home insurance, anti-money-laundering alert ranking. Banks usually separate model development (the data scientist) from independent model validation and from model risk oversight; every model needs documentation, back-testing and explainability for regulators. Tools: Python, SQL, SAS (still common in credit risk), gradient boosting, logistic regression and scorecards, cloud platforms. Deliverables: model code and documentation, monitoring reports, committee presentations.
- **A typical day and week**: Model build and testing (40%), documentation and governance (25%), data checks (20%), stakeholder meetings with risk and business owners (15%). Regulatory deadlines and audit findings cause bursts of documentation work.
- **Hours, stress and lifestyle**: 38-45 typical, 50 peak near regulatory deadlines (consensus). Stress 3/5: steady deadlines, accountability for model failures, but far from investment-banking hours. Hybrid is standard in UK and European banks, fully on-site expectations rising in US banks (consensus). Little travel.
- **Human vs. quantitative profile**: People/communication: 3/5. Quantitative/technical: 4/5. Explaining a model to a risk committee and validators counts as much as its accuracy.
- **Compensation (approximate)**:
  - US: US$150,000 base for Barclays' 2027 Quantitative Analytics graduate programme in New York (a quant-risk role; listing); generic data scientist data from BLS (US$120,230 median; insurance carriers and credit intermediation employ 16% of US data scientists). Entry-level for a credit-risk data scientist outside quant roles: no reliable figure found.
  - UK: NatWest Risk Modelling graduate trainee GBP 37,500 (2027 intake; older cohorts GBP 33.5-35k), NatWest Data Science graduate trainee GBP 42,000, Lloyds Data Science and AI GBP 48,500, Capital One UK GBP 46,500-55,500 plus GBP 5,000 joining bonus (technology and strategy analyst graduate programmes, intake Sept 2027), Aviva Data and Analytics pathway GBP 33,200 (closed). Levels.fyi: NatWest data scientist median total GBP 48.8k (April 2026), Lloyds GBP 48.5k; JPMorgan London data scientists average ~GBP 144.9k (all levels, thin).
  - Italy: Generali Italia junior innovation and data analytics role EUR 36,000 (undated listing); UniCredit's Munich trainee programme advertises from EUR 59,761 gross (a German salary, from a different trainee listing); Levels.fyi Italy top payers Enel EUR 56.8k and Prima EUR 56.1k, Generali EUR 35.8k (all levels, small samples). Intesa Sanpaolo's data science offering found this round was internship-level (audit data science internship, May 2026).
  - Elsewhere: Singapore bank data scientists (DBS, others) senior roughly SGD 12-16k a month per Skillup (2026, weak); GovTech median SGD 117,421 (Levels.fyi, Feb 2026). Insurers in Zurich: AXA CHF 123,942 (Levels.fyi, thin).
- **Career path**: Graduate/associate modeller (0-2) → data scientist / model developer (2-5) → senior / VP (5-9) → head of credit analytics, chief data scientist or model risk head (10+). Insurance: pricing analyst → pricing data scientist → head of pricing; actuarial qualification opens another ladder.
- **Exit opportunities**: After 2-3 years: fintech or big-tech risk teams, consulting (risk analytics), model validation, credit strategy, or other lenders. After 7-10 years: chief risk officer track, head of data science, fintech leadership.
- **Tier list of employers**: Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact.
  - Tier 1: data-driven lenders and large global banks with significant modelling teams (Capital One, JPMorgan, Barclays, HSBC), and leading insurers/pricing houses (Admiral, Allianz, AXA, Zurich, Aviva). Strong tooling, best pay inside finance.
  - Tier 2: high-street and national banks (NatWest, Lloyds, Intesa Sanpaolo, UniCredit) and Italian insurers (Generali, UnipolSai). Large intakes, rotational programmes, stronger governance burden.
  - Tier 3: regional banks, smaller insurers, and outsourced analytics providers; smaller teams and less modern stack.
- **How to enter**:
  - Degrees: STEM (maths, statistics, physics, engineering, computer science) or economics with quantitative focus; NatWest requires a 2:1 in STEM; Admiral lists maths, physics, engineering, chemistry or biology; a master's is common but not required for UK graduate schemes. For Italy a laurea magistrale in statistics, economics, mathematics or data science is typical.
  - Programmes and calendar: UK schemes open August-October for next September, with early deadlines (Capital One closed 6 Sept 2026; Admiral's 2026 intake closed 26 Oct 2025; Aviva's pathway has closed; Allianz Ireland's data analytics programme closes 11 Oct 2026). Italian banks and insurers use a mix of graduate programmes (Intesa Sanpaolo SLAM, a 24-month international programme; Generali's international programme) and internships/stage; UniCredit Group runs a 12-month rotational trainee programme with a 2026 "data scientist for agentic AI and banking" track in Munich.
  - Interviews: online tests (logic, numeracy), coding (SQL/Python) tests, case study on risk or pricing, technical interview on regression and classification metrics (AUC, Gini, KS), behavioural.
  - Certifications: not required; for insurance, actuarial exams (UK Institute and Faculty of Actuaries, Italian Albo) are a different path; FRM/PRM are sometimes seen in risk functions (general knowledge).
  - Skills and portfolio: logistic regression and gradient boosting, scorecard development, calibration, explainability (SHAP), fairness testing, SQL; a credit-scoring project using public data with proper validation.
  - Common mistakes: ignoring interpretability and regulation; treating bank data as clean; overselling deep learning.
  - Alternative routes: analysts in risk or finance with a coding upgrade; actuarial trainees adding machine learning; from the Lloyds scheme, which does not require a particular degree classification.
  - Entry difficulty: 3/5.
- **Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for**: Heavy governance and documentation slow delivery; legacy systems; models limited to explainable techniques; pay at the entry level is much lower than big tech. Not a good fit if you want rapid experimentation or cutting-edge ML, or if you dislike regulation and process.

### 3.6 Applied scientist / research data scientist (PhD-track)

- **What you actually do**: You tackle open-ended problems where off-the-shelf methods are not enough, and you aim for a measurable product or business impact. Typical Amazon-style applied scientist work: improving search ranking with a new deep-learning approach, building a better demand-forecasting model, training or fine-tuning language models for a shopping assistant, or designing a causal model of how a price change affects demand. Economists in tech do similar work with econometrics: for example estimating how advertising affects sales, or designing an auction. Tasks: literature review, prototyping in Python and PyTorch, offline evaluation, then collaborating with software engineers to put the model into production, plus writing papers or internal research memos. Deliverables: models and methods, experiments, papers, patents.
- **A typical day and week**: Reading papers and running training jobs (40%), writing code and analysis (30%), meetings with engineers and product partners (20%), writing (10%). Project cycles of 3-9 months (consensus). Heavy GPU/compute scheduling; waiting for runs.
- **Hours, stress and lifestyle**: 40-50 typical, 55-60 around paper deadlines or launches (consensus). Stress 3/5: high expectations to deliver impact, performance reviews, uncertainty about whether research will work. Hybrid. Conference travel (NeurIPS, ICML, KDD, EC) is a perk.
- **Human vs. quantitative profile**: People/communication: 2/5. Quantitative/technical: 5/5. Deep technical competence is the core; you still need to convince engineers to adopt your method.
- **Compensation (approximate)**:
  - US: Amazon applied scientist L4 US$247k, L5 US$323k, L6 US$413k, L7 US$653k total compensation (Levels.fyi, 9 Oct 2026, 107 submissions; median all levels US$335k). Another Levels.fyi snapshot (18 Aug 2026) shows L5 US$324k and L6 US$407k. Stock makes up a large share at senior levels. Google data scientist L3 US$196k, L4 US$281k, L5 US$380k (not research scientists; research scientist ladders were not retrieved).
  - UK: no reliable data found for applied scientist roles (London figures on Levels.fyi are all-levels data scientist data: median GBP 91k). UK skilled-worker route: the going rate for SOC 2433 (statistical data scientists) is GBP 55,100 (GOV.UK, updated April 2026, from the project's earlier research); new entrants can be paid 70% of the going rate if above the general floor.
  - Continental Europe: Amazon's Berlin applied scientist posting (employer-stated, 16 June 2026, from project research) asks for a PhD or a master's plus four years' experience; pay not stated. Italy: no reliable data found for applied scientist pay. Asia: Tokyo's Sakana AI JPY 21.1m and PayPay JPY 18.4m for data scientists (Levels.fyi, 9 Oct 2026, thin); Singapore Grab SGD 160k.
- **Career path**: Applied scientist I/II (0-4 years after PhD or MSc plus experience; Amazon L4/L5) → senior applied scientist (L6, ~5-9 years) → principal scientist (L7) or research manager (10+ years). Economists follow a similar scientist ladder.
- **Exit opportunities**: After 2-3 years: research scientist at other labs, ML engineering, startups (founding technical), AI labs (see the AI branch). After 7-10 years: principal scientist, head of science, academia (professor), startup CTO or founder, quant research (with strong maths).
- **Tier list of employers**: Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact.
  - Tier 1: frontier research groups at Google (Research/DeepMind), Meta, Microsoft Research, Amazon, Apple, and elite AI labs (see AI branch). Highest pay, strongest peers, hardest entry.
  - Tier 2: product-focused science teams at Booking.com, Spotify, Zalando, Uber, Airbnb, Netflix, Adyen and marketplaces; corporate research labs (Siemens, Bosch, IBM, SAP); central banks' and policy institutions' economists.
  - Tier 3: applied research in banks, pharma and consulting; smaller labs. Less publication freedom, more business constraints.
- **How to enter**:
  - Degrees: PhD (or an excellent master's with research output) in computer science, statistics, machine learning, economics, operations research or physics. Amazon's mid-level applied scientist posting asks for a PhD, or a master's with four or more years of CS/ML experience, and 3+ years of model-building experience; many postings also list publications at top conferences or patents (Amazon postings, accessed Oct 2026; Berlin posting 16 June 2026). Google's research scientist internships require current enrolment in a master's or PhD with research experience preferred (Google listings, 2026). IMC London lists a graduate Machine Learning Researcher role requiring an MSc, PhD preferred (employer listing, Oct 2026).
  - Programmes and calendar: research internships for PhD students (Google, Microsoft, Meta, Amazon) recruit in autumn for the next summer; full-time applied scientist hiring is continuous; new-grad research scientist posts are rare.
  - Interviews: a research talk, ML fundamentals and coding, statistics, and a system or experimental design discussion; economists face theory and applied microeconomics.
  - Certifications: do not matter.
  - Skills and portfolio: publications at top venues, open-source code, strong mathematics, PyTorch, ability to ship.
  - Common mistakes: expecting a pure research job (business impact is the metric); weak engineering; confusing a coursework master's with research credentials. A one- or two-year master's without publications is a weak route to this role (prior project research notes an effective near-zero hit rate for research-scientist roles from such programmes, a claim I treat as unverified).
  - Alternative routes: start as a data scientist or ML engineer and move into science after a part-time PhD, or via research-oriented master's theses and publications; economics PhDs enter via tech economist roles.
  - Entry difficulty: 5/5.
- **Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for**: Needs 4-6 years of PhD training at lower pay; few openings; pressure to turn research into products; the best research roles are moving into frontier AI labs. Not a good fit if you want a business-facing career quickly or do not enjoy academic-style research.

### 3.7 Data science consultant

- **What you actually do**: You deliver analytics and AI projects for clients. Typical engagement: a retailer wants to forecast demand, a bank wants a churn model, an energy company wants predictive maintenance. You scope the problem with the client, access and clean their data, prototype a model, build a proof of concept, and present results to executives, ideally with a path to production. Roles range from hands-on data scientist inside a strategy consultancy's AI unit (McKinsey QuantumBlack, BCG X) to analytics consultants in the Big Four and IT integrators (Deloitte, Accenture, Capgemini, Reply, Bip). Tools: Python, SQL, cloud platforms (Azure, AWS, GCP), Power BI/Tableau, PowerPoint.
- **A typical day and week**: Client-site or video calls in the morning, data work and modelling mid-day, slides and weekly steering meeting with the client, plus staffing and proposal activities. Mostly 3-6 month projects, with client changes common. Postings for McKinsey QuantumBlack ask for willingness to travel and to attend meetings outside traditional business hours (McKinsey job listings, 2026).
- **Hours, stress and lifestyle**: 45-55 typical, 60+ before steering committees (consensus). A former McKinsey consultant said data scientists there work slightly fewer hours than generalist consultants, unless on site with the client (PrepLounge forum answer, 2022, anecdotal). Stress 4/5: client deadlines, shifting scope, "up or out" or utilisation pressure at strategy firms; IT-consulting body shops run at 3/5 (consensus). Travel: variable; a BCG Hong Kong posting lists travel based on client needs; a McKinsey Lisbon posting lists willingness to travel. Hybrid with client-site days.
- **Human vs. quantitative profile**: People/communication: 4/5. Quantitative/technical: 4/5. You need both: clients buy the story, not the model.
- **Compensation (approximate)**:
  - US: BCG data scientist ladder on Levels.fyi US$166k (L1) to US$265k (L5) total compensation (updated 7 June 2025, small sample); no reliable figure for QuantumBlack or Big Four entry-level data scientists found.
  - UK: no reliable data found for data science consultant pay at strategy firms; Big Four and integrators typically pay graduate-level salaries comparable to other consulting entry roles (not verified here).
  - Italy: no firm-specific figures found. Deloitte Italy junior consultants about EUR 30k gross per an aggregator (not data-science-specific, weak); Glassdoor (Feb 2026, 214 salaries) junior data scientist Italy about EUR 29k; NettoCalc Milan junior EUR 42k. Accenture, Deloitte, Reply and BIP post junior data scientist roles in Milan without stating pay.
  - Asia: no reliable data found.
- **Career path**: Analyst/associate data scientist (0-2) → consultant / data scientist II (2-4) → senior consultant / engagement manager (4-8) → principal, associate partner, partner (8-14 at strategy firms; different at IT firms). At QuantumBlack-type units, ladders are Data Scientist I/II, Senior Data Scientist, then Principal/Associate Partner (McKinsey listings use "Data Scientist I", "Data Scientist II", "Senior Data Scientist I").
- **Exit opportunities**: After 2-3 years: in-house data science at a client; product or strategy roles; ML engineering; PhD. After 7-10 years: head of analytics or Chief Data Officer, partner, startup leadership.
- **Tier list of employers**: Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact.
  - Tier 1: McKinsey (QuantumBlack), BCG (BCG X), Bain (advanced analytics). Prestige, very strong colleagues, high pay and the hardest entry; hours are long.
  - Tier 2: Deloitte, PwC, EY, KPMG, Accenture, Capgemini and specialist data and AI firms. Large intakes, more varied quality, more delivery-style work.
  - Tier 3: Italian and European boutiques and integrators such as Reply and Bip (which says it recruits graduates through those with two years' experience), small agencies and outsourcers. Rapid learning, long hours for pay, little prestige.
- **How to enter**:
  - Degrees: STEM bachelor's or master's (computer science, statistics, maths, engineering, physics); QuantumBlack postings list a bachelor's, master's or PhD in computer science, machine learning, applied statistics, mathematics, engineering or AI, and ask for statistical sampling and machine-learning fluency. BCG X postings vary: a data science internship exists, and other posts ask for 2-4 years' experience.
  - Programmes and calendar: strategy-firm recruiting is autumn-driven; IT consultancies hire continuously. Accenture Milan's process: online assessment (logic, maths), technical test on SQL and Python, optional case study; Reply Milan: SQL and Python questions then reasoning (Glassdoor reports, anecdotal).
  - Interviews: case interviews (business problem structuring), coding tests, machine learning questions and a "tell us about a project" discussion.
  - Certifications: cloud certificates (Azure, AWS, Google) are viewed positively at Big Four and integrators; not at strategy firms.
  - Skills and portfolio: a portfolio of client-style projects, strong slides, structured problem solving, English fluency.
  - Common mistakes: building models without stakeholder buy-in; ignoring the "so what"; accepting a job at an IT body shop expecting data science work.
  - Alternative routes: analyst-to-consultant moves; PhD graduates enter strategy firms' expert tracks; company switchers from industry.
  - Entry difficulty: 4/5 at Tier 1; 3/5 elsewhere.
- **Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for**: Long hours and travel; the project-based work means shallow involvement in the final product; many projects stop at proofs of concept; pay at Tier 3 firms is low for the workload. Not a good fit if you want depth in one domain or predictable hours.

| Consulting firm (QuantumBlack, BCG X, Big Four) | 45-55 | 4 | High at Tier 1 (BCG ladder US$166k L1, 2025), moderate at integrators | Client-driven, up-or-out pressure, travel | Low-medium: client sets agenda |
| Pharma / CRO (Roche, GSK, IQVIA) | 38-45 | 2 | Moderate: UK CRO adverts GBP 45-55k for experienced statisticians; US BLS median US$105,650 | Regulated, careful, procedural; stable | Medium: protocol dictated by regulation |
| Retail / consumer goods | 40-45 | 2-3 | Moderate to low: BLS median applies; retail pay below tech | Practical, commercial; data maturity varies | Medium |
| Startup | 45-60 | 4 | Variable: cash lower, equity upside (Stripe/DoorDash medians US$450k on Levels.fyi are late-stage, not typical) | Fast, broad scope, no mentors, high risk | Highest |
| Public sector / statistics office (ONS, ISTAT, GORS) | 36-40 | 1-2 | Lowest: ONS HSO GBP 35,144 (adverts to Jan 2026); ISTAT scale not retrieved | Stable, mission-led, slow | Medium; policy-driven |

Commentary:
- **Regional pay gap.** US big tech pays 2-3 times European equivalents in total compensation (Meta IC3 US$153k, Google L3 US$196k vs. Germany all-levels median EUR 77.6k and Italy EUR 46k all-levels, small sample). Within Europe, London and Zurich lead (London median GBP 91k, Zurich CHF 116k), Germany follows with Berlin scale-ups paying more than the national median (Berlin EUR 113k), and Italy trails: junior Italian data scientist pay is about EUR 29-42k gross (aggregators), with Milan higher than the rest of Italy. The gap partly reflects Levels.fyi sample bias to big tech, which makes Europe look better than it is for the typical local employer.
- **Asia/Middle East hubs.** Singapore (median SGD 120k, n=128) is the strongest local market; Grab pays about SGD 160k; Tokyo's median is JPY 8.76m (n=16) but a few firms (PayPay, Sakana AI) pay JPY 18-21m; Hong Kong (HKD 803k, n=12) and Dubai (AED 437.6k, n=20) have very thin data and wide uncertainty. Banks and government-linked agencies (GovTech SGD 117k) are major employers in Singapore. Dubai and Gulf roles are growing around government AI programmes (not verified here).
- **Hours.** Where hours differ the most is consulting and startups (45-60) vs. public sector and pharma (36-45). Banks sit in the middle but with regulation-driven spikes. Evidence base: consensus and anecdotes; no systematic survey.
- **Italy specifically.** Fewer product-led tech employers; most data science jobs are in banks (Intesa, UniCredit), insurers (Generali), energy (Enel, Eni), consultancies (Accenture, Deloitte, Reply, Bip) and public bodies (ISTAT). Salaries are quoted in RAL (gross annual); 13th/14th-month payments should be checked in an offer. Many strong Italian graduates move to London, Zurich or Amsterdam after 2-3 years.
- **Autonomy and AI**: Employers with large experimentation cultures give more freedom; regulated environments give less. LLM tools reduce routine coding everywhere but entry-level roles are most exposed.

## 5. Which backgrounds fit this branch

### (a) Fit by background

| Background | Fit | One-line reason |
|---|---|---|
| Management | Stretch | Little statistics or coding; possible via product/analytics roles only after a quantitative master's or strong self-study. |
| Logistics & Supply Chain | Possible | Natural route into operations research and forecasting if you add optimisation, Python and statistics. |
| Finance | Possible | Credit, risk and insurance modelling are open to finance graduates with strong statistics and coding; markets quant is a different branch. |
| Accounting | Stretch | Audit analytics and fraud detection use data, but the core training is not statistical; need a bridge master's. |
| Marketing | Stretch | Marketing analytics and experimentation are reachable, but technical depth is the barrier. |
| Data Analytics | Strong | The standard stepping stone: SQL, dashboards, business context; extra statistics and modelling needed. |
| Economics | Strong | Econometrics and causal inference are directly valued in product data science, consulting and tech economist roles. |
| Computer Science | Strong | Coding and algorithms are the foundation; add statistics for product and biostatistics roles. |
| Cybersecurity | Possible | Anomaly detection and fraud models are adjacent; statistics and ML modelling must be added. |
| Data Science | Strong | Direct fit for all seven role families, with biostatistics needing formal statistics depth. |
| Artificial Intelligence | Strong | Direct fit for ML modelling and applied science; product and biostatistics need more classical statistics. |
