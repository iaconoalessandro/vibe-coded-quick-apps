# Artificial Intelligence, part 2: Research, frontier and governance roles

## 1. What this branch is

This part of the AI branch covers the jobs that decide what AI systems can do and how they are allowed to be used. These are the people who invent and train the models (research scientists and research engineers), the people who try to make them safe and measure their dangers (alignment, interpretability, evaluations and red teaming), the people who put them inside customers' businesses (forward-deployed engineers, solutions architects, AI consultants), the people who write the rules and manage the legal and ethical risk (governance, policy, responsible-AI and compliance staff), and the product managers who decide what gets built. Part 1 of the branch covers the more conventional engineering jobs (ML engineers, LLM application builders, MLOps, computer vision and robotics, data and annotation work).

Two things shape everything below. First, the top end of this field is small, very well paid and extremely selective: a few dozen organizations in a handful of cities (San Francisco Bay Area, London, Zurich, Paris, New York, Seattle) employ most frontier researchers, and the Stanford AI Index 2026 says industry produced more than 90% of notable frontier models in 2025. Second, the "AI governance" side is the opposite shape: it is a young, fast-growing, mostly non-technical-or-semi-technical profession driven by regulation (the EU AI Act, Italy's national AI law), with many more entry points and much lower pay.

Employer figures come from public job-board feeds read on 9 October 2026 (OpenAI, Anthropic, xAI, Scale AI, Databricks, Cohere, Palantir, UK AISI) and, for Google DeepMind and Meta, from third-party mirrors of their postings (marked "mirror"; possibly expired, so signals only). (2) Posted ranges cover different seniority levels and mostly state base salary; equity and bonus come on top at most employers, so ranges across employers are indicative, not like-for-like. (6) Part 1 and the general software, data science, cybersecurity and PM branches are cross-referenced only briefly.

## 2. Map of areas and sectors

**AI research (the "science" side).** Definition: producing new methods or knowledge about how models learn and behave, for example a new training recipe for reasoning or a theory of why a method works. At a frontier lab (a company building the most capable general-purpose models, such as Anthropic, Google DeepMind, Meta's Superintelligence Labs and FAIR, Mistral, OpenAI, xAI) you run experiments on models from small to huge, track results and write internal documents; at a university you also teach, write grants and supervise students. Employers: frontier labs; big-tech research groups (Microsoft Research, NVIDIA, Apple, Amazon); AI companies (Cohere, Databricks, Scale AI, Sakana AI in Tokyo); universities and institutes. In Europe the main academic network is ELLIS (a pan-European network of AI research units); in Italy the main public programme is FAIR (Future Artificial Intelligence Research), a nonprofit foundation funded through the national recovery plan (PNRR) with one hub, ten "spokes" and seven transversal projects (FAIR website, read October 2026), plus IIT, FBK, and universities such as Politecnico di Milano, Sapienza and Bocconi. Industry is where the compute is: the AI Index 2026 reports that new AI PhDs in the US and Canada rose 22% from 2022 to 2024 but the whole increase went to academic jobs, not industry.

**Research engineering.** Definition: building and running the software that lets researchers train and test models: distributed training code across thousands of GPUs, reinforcement-learning (RL) environments, data pipelines, experiment tracking, evaluation harnesses. In practice the line between "research engineer" and "research scientist" at frontier labs is blurry: several lab postings (OpenAI "Research Engineer / Research Scientist" combined titles, Anthropic RL research engineer roles, xAI and Cohere "Member of Technical Staff" titles) describe one shared job of designing experiments, RL tasks and reward signals and diagnosing why models improve, not just plumbing. Employers: the same labs, plus NVIDIA, Meta, Microsoft, Databricks, Scale AI and well-funded startups.

**AI safety, alignment, interpretability and evaluations.** Alignment research tries to make models do what their developers and users intend and avoid catastrophic behaviour. Interpretability tries to reverse-engineer what happens inside a model. Evaluations ("evals") measure what a model can do, including dangerous capabilities (cyber attacks, biological misuse); red teaming means adversarially attacking a model or product to find failures. Employers fall in four groups: labs' safety teams (Google DeepMind, OpenAI and Anthropic all post dedicated safety research roles in October 2026, as do Scale AI and xAI); government institutes (the UK AI Security Institute, AISI, formed November 2023 and renamed in February 2025, with more than 100 technical staff and a stated budget of about GBP 66 million per year; the US Center for AI Standards and Innovation, CAISI, at NIST, which publishes evaluations of foreign AI models, the latest dated 17 September 2026; the EU AI Office's AI Safety unit); nonprofits (METR, Apollo Research, Redwood Research); and academic groups. Scale note: 80,000 Hours estimated in 2022 only about 300 people (90% interval 76 to 536 full-time equivalents) worked on technical approaches to catastrophic AI risk; the field has grown but is still small and entry "can be very competitive" (page updated 17 September 2026).

**Forward-deployed engineering, AI solutions engineering and AI consulting.** Definition: customer-facing technical work that gets AI to function inside a specific organization. A forward-deployed engineer (FDE) is a software engineer who embeds with a customer, builds production systems on the vendor's platform and feeds lessons back to the product; Palantir pioneered the role (internally "Delta"), and the Pragmatic Engineer (12 August 2025) describes solutions architects as more advisory, FDEs as more hands-on and long-term, and consultants as one-off recommenders. Employers with open FDE or deployment-strategist roles in the October 2026 job feeds: Palantir, Databricks, OpenAI, Anthropic, Cohere, Scale AI and Sakana AI, across the US, London, Paris, Munich, Zurich, Madrid, Tokyo, Seoul, Singapore, Sydney and the Gulf (Anthropic also lists "Applied AI Architect" roles in Milan); plus cloud providers and consultancies (McKinsey/QuantumBlack, BCG X, Accenture, Deloitte and similar; I could not open a primary page for these, so role details are general knowledge, unverified).

**AI governance, policy, responsible AI and compliance.** Definition: writing, interpreting and implementing the rules and internal controls around AI, from drafting national policy to running a model-risk review at a bank, building an inventory of AI systems for EU AI Act compliance, or writing a lab's product-use policy. Employers: regulators and governments (the European Commission's AI Office, with more than 125 staff across six units on its page; national authorities; UK DSIT/AISI; US agencies); think tanks (GovAI and others); corporate responsible-AI and compliance teams (big tech, banks such as Citi); Big Four, consultancies and law firms (Andersen in Italy announced an AI governance and compliance department in June 2026, per Milano Finanza); standards bodies (CEN-CENELEC JTC 21, ISO/IEC SC 42; general knowledge). The IAPP-Credo AI AI Governance Profession Report (16 April 2025; 671 respondents) found governance sits in many places: privacy and legal/compliance each lead it in 22% of organizations, IT 17%, data governance 10%; about half of governance staff sit in ethics, compliance, privacy or legal teams.

The EU AI Act timeline as of October 2026, after the "AI Omnibus" simplification (political agreement 7 May 2026, in force 27 July 2026, per the European Commission's AI Act page): prohibited practices and AI literacy duties since 2 February 2025; general-purpose AI model duties since 2 August 2025; most of the Act applies from 2 August 2026; a new prohibition on nudification apps and child sexual abuse material from 2 December 2026; high-risk systems in areas such as biometrics, employment, education and migration delayed to 2 December 2027; high-risk AI embedded in regulated products to 2 August 2028. The AI Office enforces rules on general-purpose models; national authorities share enforcement from 2 August 2026. Italy also passed its own AI law (Law 132/2025, in force 10 October 2025); I could not read which authorities it designates (widely reported as AgID and ACN, unverified). artificialintelligenceact.eu (updated 31 August 2026) lists the same later dates.

**AI product management.** Definition: deciding what AI products to build and how to ship them when the technology is probabilistic. What is distinctive versus general PM (covered in another branch): outputs vary, so quality is defined through evals; researchers' timelines are uncertain; capabilities change every few months so roadmaps are short; cost per query is a first-order constraint; and safety, abuse and trust issues are part of the product. October 2026 postings show the spread: OpenAI lists PMs for API agents, core models, integrity, youth and multimodal safety; Scale AI "Senior AI Product Manager" roles including cybersecurity and finance agents; Databricks PMs for its AI platform plus an associate-PM new-graduate programme; Cohere PMs for agents and integrations; Anthropic "Product Manager, Claude" and specialised PMs. Employers: labs, big tech, AI startups, enterprise software.

## 3. Role families

### 3.1 AI research scientist (industry and academia)

**What you actually do:** You pick a research question (for example, how to make a model reason more reliably over long tasks), design experiments, run them on clusters, analyse results, and turn them into methods that go into the next model or into papers. Typical tools: PyTorch or JAX, internal training stacks, experiment trackers, Slurm or Kubernetes clusters. Deliverables: internal write-ups, ablation studies (removing components to see what matters), code that ships into training runs, and for academic and some industry roles, papers at NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR or ACL. Postings differ by subfield: Anthropic's interpretability scientist role is about reverse-engineering algorithms inside models; a Databricks principal scientist role is about scaling; Scale AI's research scientists cover post-training, evaluations and agents; OpenAI's researcher titles span alignment, safety training, reasoning and multimodal work. Academic scientists add grant writing, teaching and supervising PhD students.

**A typical day and week:** Industry: morning reading experiment results and dashboards from overnight runs; a few hours of coding or analysis; a team research meeting; a call with an engineer about a training bug; writing notes. A week often includes a research review where you present, plus chat-heavy collaboration on shared training runs. Academia: split between supervising students, writing papers and grants, teaching and committees; much less access to compute. Hours below are my estimates.

**Hours, stress and lifestyle:** Typical 45-55 hours a week at frontier labs, peaks of 60-80 around model releases and conference deadlines (estimate; no survey exists). Stress 3-4/5: no client deadlines but competitive pressure, release timelines and a publish-or-be-scooped dynamic. Academia: 45-60 hours, stress 3/5 (grant and tenure pressure). Most frontier-research postings I read name an office city (San Francisco, London, New York, Seattle, Zurich, Paris, Palo Alto); Anthropic's say staff should be in the office at least 25% of the time. Remote work is rare for frontier research. Travel: conferences.

**Human vs. quantitative profile:** People/communication: 2/5. Quantitative/technical: 5/5. You mostly work with a small team and communicate through write-ups and code review; the core of the job is mathematics, statistics and programming.

So entry for a PhD graduate at big tech is roughly $190,000-$430,000 total; about five years in, $430,000-$520,000; senior $650,000+. Equity at private companies is paper value.
- Employer postings, US base salary ranges (October 2026; levels differ, so do not read them as a ranking): OpenAI Research Scientist/Engineer (San Francisco) $250,000-$445,000 and named researcher roles (interpretability, safety training, multimodal safety) $380,000-$500,000; Anthropic Research Scientist, Interpretability $350,000-$850,000; xAI "Member of Technical Staff" $180,000-$440,000, up to $600,000 for post-training (the pay text names the company SpaceXAI; equity extra); Databricks Principal Research Scientist, Scaling $280,000-$350,000; Scale AI ML Research Scientist $165,600-$207,000, senior/staff agents roles $302,400-$378,000; Google DeepMind Research Scientist (US) $174,000-$252,000 and Frontier AI Research Scientist $147,000-$211,000 (mirrors of 2026 postings); Meta Superintelligence Labs AI Research Scientist $122,000-$181,000, technical-leadership role $219,000-$301,000 (mirrors, May 2026). **Career path:** Typical (industry): PhD (4-6 years) -> research scientist / member of technical staff (years 0-3) -> senior (years 3-7) -> staff/principal or research lead (years 7-12) -> director of a research area. Lab titles vary ("Member of Technical Staff" at several labs). Academic: PhD -> postdoc (2-4 years; one to three postings) -> assistant professor (tenure track, 5-7 years to tenure) -> associate/full professor. In Italy: dottorato -> assegno di ricerca / RTD-A -> RTD-B -> associate professor through national qualification (abilitazione); the path is long and slow (general knowledge, unverified in detail).

**Exit opportunities:** After 2-3 years: another lab, a startup (many researchers co-found companies), applied-research roles at big tech, or quantitative finance (hedge funds and trading firms hire ML researchers; general knowledge). After 7-10 years: research director, chief scientist or founder at an AI startup, professor, government advisor (AI safety institutes), venture capital technical partner.

**Tier list of employers (alphabetical within each tier; no ranking implied inside a tier):**
- Tier 1 (the frontier): Anthropic, Google DeepMind (offices in London, Bay Area, Zurich, Paris, Tokyo, Toronto, Montreal and others per its careers page), Meta (Superintelligence Labs, including the TBD Lab and FAIR group, reorganised since 2025), OpenAI, plus the strongest academic groups (Stanford, Berkeley, MIT, CMU, Oxford, Cambridge, ETH Zurich/EPFL, Mila/Toronto). Distinguishing feature: access to the largest compute budgets, top pay, and the right to work on the frontier.
- Tier 2: Amazon's AGI/science groups, Apple machine learning research, Cohere, Databricks, Microsoft Research, Mistral AI (Paris), NVIDIA Research, Scale AI, xAI, newer well-funded labs, and leading Chinese groups (ByteDance, Alibaba, DeepSeek; general knowledge). Strong research and good pay, but either less compute, less publishing freedom, or a narrower mission.
- Tier 3: corporate research groups at banks, pharma and industrials; national and regional institutes (IIT, FBK, FAIR network spokes, Fraunhofer, INRIA, and in Asia and the Gulf MBZUAI, TII, Sakana AI and similar; I could not verify their standing, so placement is a judgement); mid-ranked universities. Good for applied work, lower pay and compute.
- Reorganisations (for example the reported cut of about 600 roles at Meta's AI unit in October 2025) show that even Tier 1 job security is volatile.

Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact.

**How to enter:** A PhD is still the default for research scientist roles; DeepMind's careers page states that research scientists normally hold one. But several lab postings (Anthropic's interpretability and research engineer roles, for example) ask for a bachelor's degree or equivalent experience plus evidence of research ability, so the door has widened. The strongest signals: first-author papers at top venues, strong open-source or replication work, and a mentor's recommendation. Routes: a PhD at a top group (ideally with a lab internship: DeepMind runs a Student Researcher Program); external safety fellowships open to people from outside the field (Anthropic Fellows, MATS, the OpenAI Safety Fellowship and others, compared in 3.3); the ELLIS PhD programme in Europe (I could not verify details). In Italy: a master's in AI/data science/mathematics (Politecnico di Milano, Sapienza, Bocconi, Trento, Pisa, SNS) then a PhD in the FAIR network or abroad. Calendar: European PhD applications close roughly October-January, US ones in December; lab internships recruit mostly September-January (general pattern, unverified per lab). Interviews: at DeepMind, a recruiter call, two or three skills interviews, then leadership interviews (careers page); other labs test coding and research discussion (Anthropic's alignment posting says Python). Certifications do not matter. Common mistakes: applying with only coursework and no research output; chasing every trend instead of going deep; treating Kaggle as a research record. Alternative routes: physics, maths or neuroscience career changers; non-target-school candidates can win with replications, open-source work and fellowships; 80,000 Hours notes some people moved into safety research from other quantitative fields in under a year. Entry difficulty: 5/5.

**Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for:** The work is concentrated in a few cities and companies, hours can be punishing around launches, and pay is tied to equity in private companies whose value is uncertain. Most experiments fail, and projects get cancelled when strategy changes. Academia pays far less than industry and gives limited compute, and competition for posts is rising. In Europe the pay gap versus San Francisco is large (section 4). Not a fit for people who need predictable hours, want to work mainly with people and clients, will not relocate, or want guaranteed outcomes.

### 3.2 Research engineer

**What you actually do:** You make the research possible and fast. Typical work across labs: designing RL environments and coding tasks, building reward signals and verifiers, running training experiments on frontier models and diagnosing why models do or do not improve, and building the pipelines that let research iterate quickly. October 2026 examples: Anthropic's Research Engineer, Code RL and Interpretability roles; OpenAI Research Engineer roles in retrieval and search, frontier evals and environments, Codex and privacy; Scale AI's ML Research Engineer, ML Systems; xAI and Cohere "Member of Technical Staff" roles in training infrastructure, RL frameworks and inference. Other work: distributed training across GPU clusters, profiling and CUDA kernels, data platforms for RL, evaluation tooling.

**A typical day and week:** Debugging a failed training run or a slow job; reviewing a colleague's pull request; pairing with a researcher on a new experiment; writing infrastructure code; on some teams an on-call rotation (one Anthropic cybersecurity RL posting mentions a possible on-call rotation). A week mixes coding (60-70%), meetings with researchers, and diagnosing experiments.

**Hours, stress and lifestyle:** Typical 45-55 hours; peaks 60-70 near launches or when a big run breaks (estimate). Stress 3-4/5: expensive runs make failures costly and on-call can interrupt evenings. On-site or hybrid: postings I read name an office city (San Francisco, Palo Alto, New York, London, Paris, Toronto, Zurich). Travel: low.

**Human vs. quantitative profile:** People/communication: 3/5. Quantitative/technical: 5/5. Collaboration with researchers is constant, but the hard part is distributed systems, performance and ML engineering.

**Compensation (approximate):**
- US employer postings, base ranges (October 2026): OpenAI Research Engineer (San Francisco) $250,000-$445,000, Frontier Evals & Environments $295,000-$380,000, Retrieval & Search $293,000-$585,000, Codex $380,000-$500,000; Anthropic Code RL $500,000-$850,000, Interpretability $315,000-$560,000 (5-10+ years' experience), Applied AI Research Engineer $300,000-$400,000; Scale AI ML Research Engineer, ML Systems $189,600-$237,000, senior/staff $290,400-$363,000; Databricks Staff ML Engineer $190,000-$285,000; xAI Member of Technical Staff $180,000-$440,000; Cohere Lead MTS, Inference Infrastructure $235,000-$325,000. The spread is wide because titles map to different levels.
- London and Europe: Anthropic Research Engineer (RL) GBP 260,000-630,000; Cohere Senior ML Systems Engineer, London CA$250,000-535,000 (about US$175,000-$375,000, my conversion); OpenAI research roles in London and Paris: no pay printed.
- Levels.fyi (9 October 2026): research engineers across companies, median US$240,000 total (25th $115,000; 75th $369,000; 90th $660,000); Google research engineer shows one level, L5 at $620,000 (base $233,000). Anthropic software engineers: entry $367,000 total (base $250,000), senior $598,000, staff $1.13 million; OpenAI software engineer L2 $249,000, L3 $346,000, L5 $960,000; Palantir software engineer median $230,000 (711 submissions).
- Big tech entry: a new-graduate or junior ML engineer at Google/Meta earns roughly $190,000-$310,000 total (derived from the research scientist ladders above at L3/E3-E4 levels; this is an upper-ish proxy).
- Switzerland: research scientist median CHF 119,153 (proxy). Italy: software engineer median EUR 39,258; no data for research engineers. UAE software engineer median AED 337,547 (282 submissions; 90th AED 540,000; Levels.fyi). Mistral (Paris): software engineer median US$104,124 (small sample, Levels.fyi).

**Career path:** Software or ML engineer (0-3 years) -> research engineer (2-5) -> senior/staff research engineer (5-10) -> research engineering manager or technical lead. Many labs also convert strong research engineers into researchers; postings for "Staff+ Research Engineer", "Research Manager" and (at OpenAI) "Research Engineer / Research Scientist" shared titles show the ladder.

**Exit opportunities:** After 2-3 years: ML infrastructure roles at big tech, AI startups (often at higher equity), HPC/quant firms. After 7-10 years: head of infrastructure, founding engineer, CTO at an AI startup, or staff engineer at a cloud/GPU company.

**Tier list of employers (alphabetical within each tier):**
- Tier 1: Anthropic, Google DeepMind, Meta Superintelligence Labs, NVIDIA (for performance engineering), OpenAI. Distinguishing: frontier-scale clusters and the hardest infrastructure problems.
- Tier 2: Amazon, Apple, Cohere, Databricks/MosaicML-style training companies, Microsoft, Mistral, Scale AI, xAI, strong AI startups.
- Tier 3: research engineering at universities, institutes and corporate labs (smaller clusters), national supercomputing centres (for example CINECA in Italy; general knowledge).

Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact.

**How to enter:** A bachelor's or master's in computer science, maths or physics works; Anthropic's postings require a bachelor's or equivalent experience and say publications are not necessary (I did not verify the same wording elsewhere). PhD not required but helpful. The practical filter is evidence of strong engineering plus familiarity with LLMs: shipping projects with PyTorch/JAX, reproducing a paper, contributing to open-source training libraries (reimplementing a small transformer, writing a fast attention kernel). Experience is usually required: postings call for 5-10+ years (Anthropic Interpretability) or "depends on level"; entry-level research engineer roles exist mainly at big tech and through fellowships (see 3.3) and internships. Interviews: Python coding, ML-infrastructure system design, take-home or pair programming on experiments, research discussion. Certifications: not valued. Common mistakes: notebook-level ML projects, ignoring systems fundamentals (networking, GPUs), underestimating coding-interview preparation. Alternative routes: backend or HPC software engineers; cybersecurity engineers moving into RL environments for security (Anthropic's cyber RL postings); non-target-school candidates through open source. Entry difficulty: 4/5.

**Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for:** You are a support function for researchers: credit goes to the papers' authors and priorities can change quickly. On-call and launch crunches exist. Pay at the top is very high but concentrated in equity of private firms. Not a fit for people who want to set the research agenda, dislike infrastructure work, or cannot live in a few expensive cities. Not a fit for those wanting a standard regulated-industry career with predictable hours.

### 3.3 AI safety, alignment, interpretability and evaluations / red-teaming researcher

**What you actually do:** Alignment researchers run experiments on whether models behave as intended (October 2026 examples: Anthropic's London role testing safety techniques by training models to subvert them; OpenAI's "Researcher, Alignment CoT Monitorability" and "Researcher, Safety Oversight"; Scale AI's "Research Scientist, AI Controls and Monitoring"). Interpretability researchers analyse model internals (features and circuits). Evaluations researchers design tasks that measure capabilities and risks: METR's roles include "evaluation execution" and "task development engineer" with a roughly two-day paid work trial; Scale AI lists "Frontier Risk Evaluations" and OpenAI "Data Scientist, Preparedness". Red teamers attack products: Anthropic's Red Team Engineer, Safeguards role asks for penetration-testing and jailbreak experience and a public body of work; OpenAI lists a "Red Team Specialist - Cyber"; AISI has alignment, control and misuse red teams. Government evaluators (UK AISI, US CAISI) test models before and after release; AISI open-sources its evaluation tool Inspect.

**A typical day and week:** Running and analysing experiments; writing evaluation tasks and scoring rubrics; reviewing transcripts of model behaviour; discussing findings with policy or product teams; writing up a safety case or a blog post. In evaluations roles, deadlines come from model release dates: pre-deployment testing windows can be short. Red teamers spend time crafting attacks, building automation, and reporting.

**Hours, stress and lifestyle:** Typical 45-55 hours, peaks 60-70 before model releases (estimate). Stress 3-4/5: high-stakes releases and the sense that errors matter. Government institutes are closer to 40-45 hours with hybrid working (AISI offers hybrid, with offices in London and other UK government sites). METR and Apollo are mostly office-based in Berkeley, London or San Francisco; Apollo offers flexible hours and visa sponsorship.

**Human vs. quantitative profile:** People/communication: 3/5. Quantitative/technical: 4/5. Strong written communication is needed because the output is arguments and evidence; interpretability and theory roles are heavily mathematical; red teaming leans on security skills.

**Career path:** Fellowship or master's (0-1 year) -> junior researcher/engineer, research contributor (1-3 years) -> senior researcher, research lead (3-7 years) -> team lead or head of a safety area (7+). 80,000 Hours describes the same split: contributor versus research lead, with a PhD "practically the default" for leads. Government path: technical staff -> research scientist -> workstream lead (AISI's levels above).

**Exit opportunities:** After 2-3 years: mainstream ML research, safeguards or trust-and-safety roles at labs, AI security startups, government institutes. After 7-10 years: head of safety/evaluations at a lab or institute, founder of an evaluation or security startup, senior government advisor, policy leadership.

**Tier list of employers (alphabetical within each group):**
- Tier 1: Google DeepMind, OpenAI and Anthropic (lab safety teams); UK AI Security Institute and US CAISI (state evaluators with pre-release access to models: AISI has access agreements with Anthropic, Google and OpenAI); METR, Apollo Research and Redwood Research (specialist nonprofits).

- Tier 3: corporate red teams in banks and cybersecurity firms with an AI practice, small startups and consultancies.

Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact.

**How to enter:** Backgrounds: ML, software engineering, security, physics, maths. Several fellowships say a PhD, prior ML experience or papers are not required (Anthropic Fellows and Astra say no prior AI safety experience is needed; the OpenAI Safety Fellowship asks for no specific credentials but requires reference letters). Cohorts: Anthropic Fellows (4 months; apply by 18 October for January); MATS Winter 2027 applications closed in October 2026; Astra's have run late August to late September; Pivotal and LASR open rounds ahead of each cohort. Horizon covers policy-oriented tech talent in the US (3.5). Portfolio: replicate a safety paper, run evaluations with an open framework such as Inspect, write a public post analysing model behaviour, produce a red-team report, hold CVEs or bug-bounty credit. Interviews: Apollo's process is short application questions, a 30-minute screen, a 2-2.5 hour take-home, three 60-minute interviews and a leadership interview; METR uses a code screen, take-home, interviews and a roughly two-day paid work trial; UK AISI uses a CV, short written statement, skills assessment and 2-4 interviews, sponsors visas for technical roles, and requires civil-service nationality for non-technical roles. Certifications: none valued. Common mistakes: reading only the discourse and never producing empirical work; applying with no artefact; applying to one organization only. Alternative routes: security professionals (red teaming), physicists and mathematicians (interpretability and theory), lawyers and policy people into evaluation policy; fellowships open the door for non-target schools. Entry difficulty: 5/5.

**Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for:** The field is small and jobs are few; funding and strategy shift with politics (the UK institute was renamed from "Safety" to "Security" in February 2025). Nonprofit and government roles can be insecure or capped in pay (AISI's top published total is GBP 145,000); lab safety teams depend on company priorities. Reviewing harmful outputs (for enforcement and safeguards roles) can be distressing. Not a fit for people who need a standardised career ladder, who are mainly drawn by pay (non-lab jobs pay far less), or who dislike uncertainty about whether the work will matter.

### 3.4 Forward-deployed engineer / AI solutions engineer / AI consultant

**What you actually do:** You take a general-purpose model or data platform and make it solve a customer's real problem: production applications, agents and connectors (for example MCP servers, which let models use tools), evaluation sets, and reusable deployment patterns fed back to product teams. Examples: Anthropic's London FDE role embeds with enterprise customers with 25-50% travel, and its Applied AI Architect role is pre-sales; OpenAI lists FDE roles by industry (healthcare, legal, financial services) and for government and security customers; Palantir's FDE and Deployment Strategist roles run at customer sites including defence and government; Databricks separates FDEs (hands-on builds), Deployment Strategists and Delivery Solutions Architects (post-sales). Consultants at McKinsey/QuantumBlack or BCG X combine strategy with delivery (general knowledge, unverified for specific firms).

**A typical day and week:** On site or on video call with the client's engineers; scoping a use case with a business owner; building a prototype (Python, APIs, vector databases, evaluation sets); presenting to executives; writing up reusable patterns for the product team. Monday-Thursday at the client, Friday at base is a common rhythm at Palantir-style firms (practitioner commentary; unverified).

**Hours, stress and lifestyle:** Typical 45-55 hours; peaks of 60-70 during go-lives or customer escalations (estimate). Stress 4/5: client-driven deadlines, ambiguity, and responsibility for production systems. Travel: 25-50% (Anthropic FDE posting); Palantir expects about 25% on site and another employer cited up to 50% (Pragmatic Engineer, 12 August 2025). Hybrid; some customer sites are hard to reach (factories, air-gapped networks).

**Human vs. quantitative profile:** People/communication: 4/5. Quantitative/technical: 4/5. This is the role where you must both code and persuade; consulting-style roles lean to 5/5 for people and 3/5 technical.

**Compensation (approximate):**

**Career path:** Software engineer or consultant (0-3 years) -> FDE/solutions engineer (2-5) -> senior FDE or solutions architect (5-8) -> staff/principal, engineering manager or customer-engineering lead (8+). Postings across employers show the ladder: FDE, senior FDE, "Manager, Forward Deployed Engineering", "Staff FDE" and pre-sales programme leads.

**Exit opportunities:** After 2-3 years: product engineering, startup founding engineer, consulting, solutions-architect roles at cloud providers. After 7-10 years: head of customer engineering, chief technology officer at an AI startup, partner-track consulting, or founder (customer exposure is a classic startup apprenticeship).

**Tier list of employers (alphabetical within each tier):**
- Tier 1: Palantir (the role's originator) and frontier labs (Anthropic, Google Cloud/DeepMind customer engineering, OpenAI).
- Tier 2: AI-native startups (Cohere, Ramp, Lindy and others cited by the Pragmatic Engineer), cloud providers (AWS, Google Cloud, Microsoft), Databricks, Salesforce, Scale AI, Snowflake; consultancies' AI arms (QuantumBlack, BCG X, Accenture, Deloitte; general knowledge).
- Tier 3: systems integrators, regional consultancies and in-house AI enablement teams at banks and pharma.

Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact.

**How to enter:** The role is rarely a first job at a frontier lab: Anthropic's FDE postings want 4+ years (London) and 8+ (Paris), its Architect roles 5+. But Palantir and Databricks have earlier-career doors: Palantir's feed lists New Grad and Internship tracks for Forward Deployed Software Engineer and Deployment Strategist (New York, London, Chicago, Seoul, Washington DC, Paris and others; US new-grad FDE pay about $135,000-$145,000, estimated in the posting), and Databricks lists new-graduate product roles. Paths: software engineer who has built LLM applications (Part 1 branch) plus customer exposure; consultant who can code. A bachelor's or equivalent is enough per Anthropic; computer science, engineering, data or maths are common, but economics, management and finance graduates enter via consulting and sales engineering. Portfolio: two or three real applications on LLM APIs (agent with tool use, retrieval, evaluation set) and a short case study; cloud certifications help for solutions-architect roles at cloud firms (not at labs). Interviews: Python technical interview, customer scenario role-play, system design, executive communication. Language: several European roles require local fluency (Anthropic Milan Italian C1, Paris native French; OpenAI lists Spanish-speaking roles in London and Madrid), an opening for Italians and Francophones. Common mistakes: treating it as pure coding; promising capabilities models cannot deliver; no evidence of production experience. Alternative routes: management consultants and data scientists who upskill in coding; technical account managers. Entry difficulty: 3/5 at consulting and integrators; 4/5 at frontier labs and Palantir.

**Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for:** Travel and context-switching are real; the Pragmatic Engineer notes the role can feel like two jobs (customer delivery and platform engineering) and that expectations vary. You live with customers' messy data and politics, and your success depends on things outside your control. Consultancies add up-or-out pressure. Not a fit for people who want deep research time, dislike travel, or prefer a single focused technical problem. Not a fit if you cannot present to executives.

### 3.5 AI governance, policy and responsible-AI specialist

**What you actually do:** Four sub-jobs. (a) Public policy: drafting or analysing legislation, consultations, standards input and briefings; at the EU AI Office you support the Act's rules for general-purpose models (units for regulation and compliance, AI safety, and innovation policy). (b) Lab and company policy: OpenAI's "Model Policy Manager" roles, "Senior Product Policy Lead, Regulation" and "Research Program Manager, Governance"; Anthropic's Policy Design Manager (owns the usage policy, builds threat models) and Product Policy Manager; Google DeepMind's "Frontier Public Affairs Lead"; Cohere's public policy roles. (c) Corporate responsible AI / compliance: inventorying AI systems, risk-classifying them under the EU AI Act, writing governance policies, running impact assessments with legal, privacy, security and data teams; Citi's AI Business Risk and Control Director (October 2026) holds approval authority for AI use cases and aligns the framework to the EU AI Act and NIST AI RMF. (d) Think-tank or consulting research: reports, workshops, client advice.

**A typical day and week:** Reading regulation and draft guidance; drafting a policy paper or compliance checklist; meetings with legal, engineering and business teams; stakeholder calls with regulators or civil society; in the lab setting, reviewing a product launch against policy. Week-ending output: memo, briefing or risk assessment.

**Hours, stress and lifestyle:** Typical 40-50 hours in regulators, think tanks and corporate roles; 50-60 at labs and law/consulting firms; peaks of 60+ around regulation deadlines, consultations and legislative sessions (estimate). Stress 3/5 (4/5 at law firms, labs during incidents, and policy shops during legislative crunch). Hybrid is common outside labs. Travel: moderate (Brussels, DC, Geneva, summits).

**Human vs. quantitative profile:** People/communication: 5/5. Quantitative/technical: 2/5 (rising to 3/5 for technical policy and evaluation-oriented roles). The job is persuasion, writing and interpretation; technical policy roles require reading model evaluations and understanding ML basics.

**Compensation (approximate):**

Junior compliance pay at banks sits below lab bands, but senior risk-and-control jobs overlap with them.
- US fellowships and government: Horizon Fellowship base salaries for the 2027 cohort are $78,000 (junior), $130,000 (fellow) and $190,000+ (senior), plus a $17,000 a year benefits stipend (Horizon page); placements in executive-branch offices, Congress, national labs and think tanks. **Career path:** Policy analyst/assistant, compliance analyst, or trainee/fellow (0-2 years) -> senior analyst/manager (3-6) -> head of AI policy/governance, director (7-12) -> chief AI officer/responsible AI lead or senior official. In law: trainee/associate -> senior associate -> partner (10+). The IAPP report finds organizations usually build governance teams gradually, starting with existing staff and then hiring senior managers, and often hire managers with prior digital governance experience such as privacy.

**Exit opportunities:** After 2-3 years: privacy, compliance and risk roles; tech-policy jobs at platforms; consultancy; law firm. After 7-10 years: chief privacy/AI officer, head of policy at a lab or regulator, partner at a firm, senior official, NGO leadership.

**Tier list of employers (alphabetical within each tier):**
- Tier 1: Frontier-lab and big-tech frontier policy teams (Anthropic, Google DeepMind, OpenAI), the European Commission's AI Office, the UK AI Security Institute and DSIT, US White House/Commerce/CAISI, leading research centres (GovAI, and similar), top Brussels and Washington think tanks.
- Tier 2: Big-tech public policy and responsible-AI teams (Amazon, Google, Meta, Microsoft), national regulators and data-protection authorities (Italy's Garante, AgID, ACN; general knowledge), international organisations (OECD, UN, Council of Europe), law-firm AI practices, Big Four and consultancy AI-governance practices, global banks' responsible-AI leadership roles (Citi, for example).
- Tier 3: Corporate compliance and risk teams in banks, pharma and insurance; startups building governance tooling; national associations and standards committees.

Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact.

**How to enter:** Degrees: law, public policy, international relations, political science, economics, computer science (technical policy). Lab policy postings ask for a bachelor's or equivalent plus domain experience (Anthropic's do; Google's AI policy role wants 7 years of tech-policy work). 80,000 Hours says policy master's degrees are particularly useful in Washington and law degrees and relevant PhDs help; executive-office roles (OSTP, OMB, NSC) are among the hardest to obtain; it advises against starting in civil service without experience and lists the Presidential Innovation Fellowship, Horizon, TechCongress and AAAS fellowships. Horizon applications run roughly July to early September (2027 cohort deadline 30 August 2026; selection November-December). Other fellowships: Anthropic Fellows economics and policy track (4 months, rolling, next cohort January 2027, no visa sponsorship, US/UK/Canada work authorisation), Constellation Astra's strategy and governance track, and GovAI's summer fellowship (2026 applied track paid GBP 12,000 over three months in London, per an aggregator). Europe: EU Blue Book traineeship (five months; applications ahead of March and October starts), EPSO competitions (heavy competition), AI Office expression-of-interest calls (the 2026 call closed 8 September). Italy: the Garante, AgID, ACN and ministries via public competitions (concorsi), and compliance roles in banks, insurers and consultancies preparing for the AI Act. Certifications: IAPP's AIGP exam costs US$649 for members and $799 for non-members (100 questions, 2.75 hours; IAPP, October 2026); it is common in compliance postings and shows commitment but does not replace a degree or domain experience (my assessment). Portfolio: policy writing samples (a consultation response, an AI Act analysis for a sector), a clear explanation of how a model is evaluated, familiarity with the NIST AI Risk Management Framework and ISO/IEC 42001 (general knowledge). Interviews: written briefing note, panel interview, regulation case study. Common mistakes: relying on AIGP alone; staying too abstract; ignoring technical literacy; writing like a lawyer for a technical audience. Alternative routes: privacy/IP lawyers, engineers moving into technical policy, consultants, think-tank research assistantships. Entry difficulty: 4/5 for lab, regulator and top fellowship roles; 3/5 for corporate compliance and consulting.

**Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for:** Pay outside labs and consultancies is modest relative to technical AI roles (UK recruiter bands for managers are GBP 90,000-130,000, against six-figure dollar bases at labs). Roles depend on political cycles: the EU delayed high-risk obligations to December 2027 and August 2028, which shifts hiring plans, and the AI Office's 2027 contract-agent hiring depends on budget decisions. Policy teams at companies can be overruled by commercial priorities. Government processes are slow. Not a fit for people who want to build things, dislike ambiguity about whether their work changes outcomes, or dislike bureaucracy.

### 3.6 AI product manager

**What you actually do:** You define what an AI product should do and how to measure it: owning strategy, roadmap and delivery for a product area, turning model capabilities into usable experiences with metrics, experiments and research, and working with design, engineering, data science, research and marketing. Distinctive AI-PM work: defining evals that stand for product quality, deciding when a model is "good enough", managing cost per request, handling prompt and model changes, working with safety teams on misuse (OpenAI lists PMs for multimodal safety and integrity; Scale AI for cybersecurity and finance agents; Anthropic for Safeguards and Public Sector), and shaping what research should build next.

**A typical day and week:** User and data review; writing a product requirements document with eval criteria; syncing with engineers and researchers; reviewing launch readiness with safety teams; customer calls; prioritisation meetings. Weekly rhythm: launch planning, metrics reviews, and unscheduled escalations when model behaviour changes.

**Hours, stress and lifestyle:** Typical 45-55 hours, peaks 60-70 around launches (estimate). Stress 3-4/5: fast-changing technology, high visibility, and launch deadlines. Hybrid at big tech; frontier-lab and AI-startup roles are mostly on-site in San Francisco, New York or Seattle (Cohere's PM roles list Toronto). Travel: low to moderate.

**Human vs. quantitative profile:** People/communication: 5/5. Quantitative/technical: 3/5. You need to read experiment results and understand model limitations, but the main skill is alignment of people and judgment.

**Compensation (approximate):**
- Employer postings, US base ranges (October 2026): OpenAI Product Manager, API Agents $293,000-$325,000, Core Models $347,000-$490,000, Integrity $347,000-$385,000, Financial Services $401,000-$530,000; Anthropic Product Manager, Claude $385,000-$460,000 (8+ years preferred); Scale AI Senior AI Product Manager $205,600-$257,000, Staff PM, Agentic Platform $210,000-$345,000; Databricks Senior PM, Databricks AI $148,800-$215,250, Staff PM, Agentic AI Applications $172,200-$236,850; Cohere PM, Agent Harness (Toronto) $160,000-$320,000, currency as printed.
- Entry: Databricks Associate Product Manager, New Grad (2027 start; Bellevue, Mountain View, San Francisco) $133,000-$150,000; Levels.fyi entry-level total comp (9 October 2026) Meta E3 rotational PM $174,000 and Microsoft level 59-60 PM $168,000-$180,000; Google L4 PM $308,000.
- Levels.fyi (9 October 2026), US total comp: all employers median $230,000 (25th $169,000; 75th $330,000; 90th $450,000); Google median $402,000 (L4 $308,000, L5 $391,000, L6 $588,000); Meta E4 $265,000, E5 $418,000, E6 $674,000; Microsoft median $253,000; Anthropic $407,000; OpenAI $750,000; Palantir $180,000. No AI-specific PM series.
- UK, Italy, Switzerland and Asia/Gulf: no reliable data found; Mistral and London labs are likely to sit well below US levels (unverified).

**Career path:** Associate PM / technical program manager (0-3 years) -> product manager (2-5) -> senior PM (5-8) -> group PM or head of product (8-12) -> VP/Chief Product Officer. Also: engineers, data scientists and consultants lateral into AI PM after 3-6 years.

**Exit opportunities:** After 2-3 years: PM roles in other tech companies, startup product leads. After 7-10 years: head of product, founder, general manager, venture capital.

**Tier list of employers (alphabetical within each tier):**
- Tier 1: Frontier labs (Anthropic, Google DeepMind, OpenAI), Google and Microsoft AI products, Meta.
- Tier 2: AI-native startups (Cohere, Mistral, Perplexity-type companies; general knowledge), Adobe, Databricks, NVIDIA, Salesforce, Scale AI.
- Tier 3: AI product teams at banks, pharma, consultancies and enterprise software firms.

Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact.

**How to enter:** Rarely a first job at a lab (Anthropic's PM, Claude posting prefers 8+ years and products with millions of users), but associate-PM doors exist: Databricks' 2027 new-graduate APM and Meta's rotational PM level. Typical routes: associate PM programmes at big tech, engineer-to-PM, data scientist-to-PM, consultant-to-PM, startup founder. Degrees: any; computer science, engineering and business dominate; an MBA helps at some companies. Skills: designing evaluations, writing a spec with acceptance criteria for probabilistic behaviour, using LLM APIs, understanding cost and latency. Portfolio: shipped side projects, a teardown of an AI product, a prototype with an evaluation set. Interviews: product sense, execution, analytics, behavioural and technical-fluency. Certifications: little weight. Common mistakes: confusing demos with products; neglecting evals and safety; chasing hype over user needs. Alternative routes: technical program managers and customer success/solutions staff moving into product; non-target-school candidates through startup experience. Entry difficulty: 4/5 at frontier labs and top big tech; 3/5 elsewhere.

**Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for:** Roadmaps get overturned when models change; you are accountable for outcomes you do not control; and AI PM is a crowded aspiration, so many candidates compete for few roles. Not a fit for people who need stable, long-horizon plans, or who dislike ambiguity about technical feasibility. Not a fit for those who want to do the technical work themselves.

**Commentary.** Labs and big tech pay several times more than government, academia and nonprofits for comparable technical skill, but nonprofits like METR post US salaries ($328,000-$578,000 for evaluation roles) competitive with big tech. Within the labs, posted bands overlap heavily (OpenAI's $380,000-$500,000 research bands sit inside Anthropic's wider $350,000-$850,000 interpretability band), so no single lab sets the market. Governments offer stability and hybrid working at lower pay. Consulting and enterprise buyers are where the volume of governance and solutions jobs is, because every regulated company must comply with the EU AI Act from August 2026. In forward-deployed roles, labs pay more than consultancies for the same hours (Palantir, Databricks and Scale AI sit between) but ask for more experience.

**Regional differences.**
- US versus Europe: London lab postings (Anthropic GBP 260,000-630,000; Cohere CA$250,000-535,000) are high by UK standards, but the UK research scientist median on Levels.fyi is GBP 116,501, Switzerland CHF 119,153, and UK AISI tops out at GBP 145,000, so the lab premium over the local market is large. Italy's software engineer median is EUR 39,258 total and I found no Italian frontier-lab research roles; ambitious Italians work in London, Zurich, Paris, the US or remotely.
- London, Zurich, Paris: London hosts DeepMind, Anthropic, OpenAI and Cohere offices, AISI, Apollo and the London Initiative for Safe AI (host of Pivotal and LASR); Zurich hosts DeepMind, OpenAI and Anthropic postings; Paris hosts Mistral, DeepMind, Cohere and customer roles at Anthropic, OpenAI and Databricks. Brussels is the centre of EU policy; Washington DC of US policy (CAISI, NIST, Horizon placements, lab policy roles).
- Italy: strong universities and the FAIR network give PhD and postdoc routes but low pay; governance and compliance demand is rising with Law 132/2025 and the EU AI Act (Il Sole 24 Ore reports Hays seeing more searches for AI governance specialists); I found no priced Italian posting beyond Anthropic's Milan role (which requires Italian).
- Gulf and Asia: published pay is thin. Levels.fyi (self-reported) shows G42 research scientists at a median AED 429,653 (tax-free), UAE software engineers AED 337,547, Singapore research scientists SGD 90,299 and Japan about JPY 10.5 million; MBZUAI, TII and Sakana AI advertise roles without pay. Palantir, Databricks, OpenAI, Cohere and Scale AI post FDE or applied-AI roles in Tokyo, Seoul, Singapore, Sydney, Abu Dhabi, Dubai, Riyadh and Doha without ranges. China has its own frontier labs (not covered). Hong Kong: no reliable data found.
- Immigration and talent flows: the AI Index 2026 reports that the number of AI researchers and developers moving to the US fell 89% since 2017, including 80% in the last year. Horizon does not sponsor visas and many fellowships need local work authorisation; Anthropic Fellows do not sponsor visas; Astra offers visa support; the UK AISI can sponsor visas for technical roles.
