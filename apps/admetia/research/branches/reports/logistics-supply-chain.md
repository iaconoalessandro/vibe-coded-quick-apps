# Logistics & Supply Chain

*Italian pay: where this report says "no reliable data found" for Italy, see [italy-pay-addendum.md](italy-pay-addendum.md) for recruiter-guide, contract (CCNL) and posting figures. Shared rating scales and the cross-branch comparison are in [../index.md](../index.md).*

## 1. What this branch is

Logistics and supply chain work is about getting the right goods, in the right quantity, to the right place at the right cost: buying materials and products (procurement), deciding how much to make and stock (planning), moving things by road, rail, air and sea (transport and shipping), storing and handling them (warehousing), and making them (manufacturing operations). It is a large, mostly practical branch with many entry points and a lot of graduate openings compared with finance or consulting, but pay at entry is usually moderate and a good share of jobs are operational (shifts, sites, firefighting). Since 2020 the field has been in the news constantly: pandemic shortages, the Red Sea and Suez diversions from late 2023, US tariff turbulence, and in 2026 a new Gulf and Strait of Hormuz crisis. That makes the work more visible and more stressful, and it has raised the value of people who can plan around disruption. Technology (planning software, warehouse robots, AI "agents") is changing the entry-level jobs fastest, so the safest entrants are those who combine operational understanding with data skills.

**Scope notes:** Global view with explicit US, UK, continental Europe (Italy always called out) and Asia/Middle East (Singapore, Dubai) comparisons; "entry" means a graduate with a bachelor's or master's and no MBA; figures are dated and sourced, and where a figure is from a job posting, recruiter blog or aggregator it is labelled as a weak signal; no reliable Italian recruiter salary guide for 2025-26 was found, so Italian pay rests on a few postings and a training-site table and is marked indicative; generic operations/supply-chain analytics is covered in the data analytics report, general operations in the management report, consulting in the management consulting report, and physical commodity trading (which shares chartering skills) in the finance report; facts that come from my background knowledge rather than a source I opened are marked "unverified".

## 2. Map of areas and sectors

**2026 context that shapes every area (dated).**
- *Red Sea/Suez and Hormuz.* Most container lines left the Red Sea route from late 2023 and sailed round the Cape of Good Hope, adding up to about two weeks to Asia-Europe voyages and absorbing roughly 2.5 million TEU (twenty-foot equivalent units, the standard container measure) of capacity out of a 34 million TEU fleet, about 7% (Xeneta, 2026, via Maritime Executive). Early 2026 saw a cautious return: large containership Suez transits rose 215% in the first half of 2026 versus a year earlier and Maersk restored a second Suez service (AGBI, July 2026). That was disrupted by the February 2026 US-Israel strikes on Iran and the Strait of Hormuz crisis that followed: Shanghai-Jebel Ali spot rates went from about US$1,800 per container in January 2026 to over US$7,000 by late March (Freightos data cited by AGBI/trade press), carriers added war-risk surcharges (Hapag-Lloyd: US$3,000 per 40-foot box, reported), and Maersk introduced an Emergency Freight Rate for several Gulf states in September 2026. Xeneta said in early October 2026 that rates had probably peaked, but Far East-US West Coast spot rates were still about 344% above 28 February levels and would stay elevated for the rest of the year (Xeneta via AJOT, 2 Oct 2026). These are trade-press summaries; check rates before using them for anything quantitative.
- *Tariffs.* The US Supreme Court ruled against the IEEPA tariffs in February 2026; a 10% Section 122 surcharge ran until 24 July 2026 and was replaced the same day by Section 301 tariffs of roughly 10-12.5% by country (USMCA-qualifying goods exempt), with Section 232 tariffs still in force and a Section 301 "excess capacity" action against 16 economies still pending in early October 2026; refund claims and appeals are ongoing (law-firm and trade-press summaries, July-October 2026; verify with USTR/CBP). Practical effect: more work for customs, trade compliance, sourcing and planning teams.
- *Nearshoring.* Surveys point to firms moving some sourcing from China to Mexico and Southeast Asia (Fictiv 2024: 54% of North American manufacturers had adopted nearshoring strategies, via a secondary write-up), but mid-2026 reporting says Mexico's advantage is being eroded by US policy on USMCA and component tariffs. Treat the trend as real but unstable.
- *Automation and AI.* Amazon passed one million warehouse robots in June 2025 and in 2026 cut about 30,000 corporate jobs (reported), mostly not warehouse staff. Gartner reports that supply chain job postings requiring AI skills rose 387% between Q1 2023 and Q1 2026 (35 million postings analysed, via Supply Chain 24/7). Kuehne+Nagel is cutting more than 2,000 roles, mainly attributed to technology-enabled efficiency, and DSV says it has removed over 8,000 white-collar positions since integrating Schenker (trade press, 2026). A job-risk website cites Gartner (February 2026) as saying 55% of supply chain leaders expect agentic AI to reduce entry-level hiring (could not confirm at source: unverified). Net: demand for planners and analysts is not collapsing, but routine coordination and data-entry work is the first to be automated, which makes entry-level back-office roles less safe than before.

### 2.1 Procurement and purchasing
- **Definition.** Buying what an organisation needs. *Direct procurement* buys what goes into the product (steel, chemicals, components, packaging); *indirect procurement* buys everything else (IT, marketing, facilities, travel, professional services). *Sourcing* finds and qualifies suppliers and runs tenders; *category management* owns a whole spend category (for example plastics resins) with a strategy; *supplier management* handles performance, risk and relationships.
- **In practice.** You compare quotes, negotiate price and terms, run a request for proposal, check supplier financial and sustainability risk, and chase late deliveries. In 2025-26 it increasingly means re-sourcing around tariffs and switching suppliers at short notice.
- **Employers.** Manufacturers (automotive, FMCG, pharma, electronics, aerospace and defence), retailers, energy and utilities, the public sector (NHS, defence, ministries), and procurement outsourcers. In the US, BLS counts 516,100 buyers and purchasing agents (2025), 22% in manufacturing, 14% wholesale, 13% government (BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook, May 2025 data).

### 2.2 Supply chain planning
- **Definition.** *Demand planning* forecasts what customers will buy; *supply planning* works out what to produce or buy to meet that; *S&OP* (sales and operations planning) or *IBP* (integrated business planning) is the monthly cycle where sales, finance, operations and planning agree one plan; *inventory management* sets *safety stock* (buffer inventory held against uncertainty) and reorder rules.
- **In practice.** You own a forecast in a system, explain forecast errors, balance factory capacity against sales promotions, and decide trade-offs such as air-freighting a shortfall. It is spreadsheet and system heavy, meeting heavy, and the first function blamed when stock is wrong.
- **Employers.** FMCG and consumer goods (P&G, Unilever), pharma, retail and e-commerce, electronics, automotive, and planning-focused consultancies. P&G's European planning centre in Warsaw is described by P&G as the region's largest planning hub (P&G careers, 2025-26).

### 2.3 Logistics and transportation (freight forwarding, 3PL, customs)
- **Definition.** *Freight forwarders* arrange cargo moves for shippers without necessarily owning ships, planes or trucks (they buy space from carriers and add documents, customs and consolidation). *Third-party logistics (3PL)* firms run warehousing and transport on contract for a client. *Customs and trade compliance* covers classification (HS codes), duty, origin rules, sanctions and licences, and *Incoterms* (standard trade terms that say who pays for and bears risk on each leg, for example FOB or DAP). *Last-mile* is final delivery to the customer.
- **In practice.** A forwarder's day is quotes, bookings, documents and exception handling (a delayed vessel, a missing certificate, a customs hold). Peak periods and disruptions raise workload sharply.
- **Employers.** By Armstrong & Associates' 2025 ranking of the top 25 forwarders, Kuehne+Nagel and DSV share first, then DHL, Sinotrans, Nippon Express and CEVA (revenue: DSV about US$37.4bn, DHL Supply Chain & Global Forwarding about US$35.5bn, K+N about US$33.8bn; Sinotrans led ocean volume at 4.93m TEU; via Supply Chain 24/7, one secondary source). Also integrators (UPS, FedEx, DHL Express), 3PLs such as GXO, DB Schenker (now part of DSV), Geodis and CEVA, plus many regional firms. In Italy: Savino Del Bene, Fercam, Arcese, BRT and DHL Italy appeared at the top of the older Top 1000 ranking (2018 data, published 2020; dated, treat as a list of names not a current ranking). The Italian contract-logistics market was forecast at about EUR 112.4bn in 2025 (+1.9%) with roughly 79,000 firms (Politecnico di Milano Contract Logistics Observatory via Shipping Italy, Nov 2025).

### 2.4 Warehousing and distribution operations
- **Definition.** Receiving, storing, picking, packing and dispatching goods in warehouses, fulfilment centres and cross-docks, usually managed through a *WMS* (warehouse management system).
- **In practice.** A graduate "area manager" or shift supervisor runs 20-100 operatives, tracks productivity and safety, and fixes flow problems, usually on a shift pattern. It is physical, metric-driven and hands-on.
- **Employers.** Retail and e-commerce (Amazon, Zalando, Tesco, Walmart), 3PLs (DHL Supply Chain, GXO, CEVA, Kuehne+Nagel contract logistics), grocery wholesalers, and manufacturers' distribution arms. Amazon's own listings describe the entry-level Area Manager as on-site, with 10-12 hour shifts and weekends/nights common (Amazon postings, 2025-26).

### 2.5 Manufacturing and production operations / lean
- **Definition.** Running a plant: production scheduling, shift management, quality, maintenance coordination, continuous improvement. *Lean* is a method for removing waste from processes; *Six Sigma* uses statistics to cut defects; the two are often combined as Lean Six Sigma (belt levels: yellow, green, black).
- **In practice.** Early career means supervising a line, owning daily output and safety targets, and running small improvement projects.
- **Employers.** FMCG, automotive, pharma, food, chemicals, aerospace and defence. For Italy this is a core part of the economy (northern manufacturing districts), though the 2025-26 industrial picture is mixed (see the separate industrial-automotive-defence research note for the automotive side).

### 2.6 Shipping and maritime
- **Definition.** *Container lines* (liner shipping) run scheduled services; *tramp/bulk and tanker shipping* is chartered on demand. *Ship brokers* intermediate between shipowners and charterers (companies hiring a ship); *chartering* is hiring a ship, on a voyage or time basis; *port operators* run terminals. *Laytime* is the time allowed to load or discharge, and *demurrage* is the penalty charged when it is exceeded.
- **In practice.** Liner companies employ customer service, pricing, network planning, equipment (container repositioning) and port-operations staff; brokerages employ small desks of brokers who live on phone, chat and market information.
- **Employers.** Alphaliner's mid-2026 data (secondary reports) put MSC first with about 7.33m TEU (21.6% of global capacity), Maersk about 4.65m and CMA CGM about 4.29m, followed by COSCO, Hapag-Lloyd, ONE, Evergreen, HMM, Yang Ming and ZIM. Ports: Rotterdam 14.2m TEU in 2025, Antwerp-Bruges 13.6m, Hamburg 8.3m (US-bound trade down 25.6% on US customs measures); Singapore's PSA terminals 44.5m TEU (record, +8%); Jebel Ali about 15.55m TEU (flat, +0.1%), with DP World noting reduced inbound vessel traffic due to the Iran conflict (port authorities and trade press, 2025-26). Italy: Italian ports handled about 12.8m TEU in 2025 (+7.1%), with Gioia Tauro above 4.5m TEU (+14%, MSC transhipment hub); Trieste was Italy's largest port by tonnage at nearly 60m tonnes; Genoa is the main gateway for northern Italian cargo (Ports Europe / Shipping Italy, 2026). Ship brokers: Clarksons, Braemar, SSY (Simpson Spence Young), Howe Robinson, and many Nordic and Greek brokers.

### 2.7 Supply chain technology and analytics
- **Definition.** Implementing and running the systems: ERP (SAP, Oracle), advanced planning systems/APS (SAP IBP, Kinaxis, o9, Blue Yonder), transport and warehouse systems (TMS, WMS), control towers and analytics.
- **In practice.** You configure planning parameters, clean master data, build dashboards (SQL, Power BI/Tableau, Python), and train users. Vendors are all pushing "agentic AI": Kinaxis announced Maestro Agent Studio, SAP has added AI agents for planning (vendor claims, 2026).
- **Employers.** Software vendors, system integrators (Accenture, Capgemini, Deloitte and boutique SAP/Kinaxis/o9 partners), large manufacturers and retailers' IT/digital supply chain teams.

### 2.8 Supply chain consulting (brief)
- Operations and supply chain practices exist at Kearney, McKinsey Operations, BCG, Bain, A&M, AlixPartners, the Big 4 and Accenture, plus boutiques. See the management consulting report for entry routes and pay. Typical projects: network design, inventory reduction, procurement savings, S&OP transformation, tariff-response sourcing studies, ERP/planning system implementations.

## 3. Role families

### 3.1 Buyer / procurement specialist / category manager

**What you actually do:** You source suppliers, run RFQs and tenders, negotiate price and terms, manage contracts, track supplier performance, and fix problems (late delivery, quality failure, price rise request). At category-manager level you own a spend category and its multi-year strategy and savings target. Tools: ERP purchasing modules (SAP Ariba or similar), spend-cube spreadsheets, e-sourcing platforms, supplier risk databases. Deliverables: savings reports, sourcing strategies, signed contracts. Example: you replace a Chinese packaging supplier hit by a new tariff with a Polish one, re-validate quality samples, and negotiate a 6% saving against a 4% price rise elsewhere.

**A typical day and week:** Morning: review open purchase orders and supplier messages in ERP; midday: price negotiation call with a supplier and an internal meeting with engineering on specifications; afternoon: update a savings tracker and draft a tender document. Weekly: category review with the business owner, supplier performance meeting, month-end savings reporting. Senior roles add board-level supplier risk reviews.

**Hours, stress and lifestyle:** Typical 37-45 hours a week; peaks 50-55 when a supplier fails or at month/quarter end. UK Prospects gives 37-40 hours mostly office-based, with occasional overseas travel (Prospects, CIPS salary guide 2025 data). Stress: 3/5 (rises to 4 in direct procurement when a plant is about to stop). Hybrid working is common in indirect procurement and office-based sourcing; direct buyers are often at or near plants. Supplier visits and trade shows mean occasional travel, more for category managers who audit suppliers in Asia.

**Human vs. quantitative profile:** People/communication: 4/5. Quantitative/technical: 3/5. Negotiation, influencing internal stakeholders and reading contracts matter as much as cost modelling; the maths is mostly total-cost-of-ownership, price indices and spreadsheet analysis.

**Compensation (approximate):**
- US: BLS median for buyers and purchasing agents US$77,710 (May 2025), lowest 10% under US$48,380, highest 10% above US$128,870 (BLS). Entry in practice roughly US$50-65k (inferred from the BLS low end and ASCM; no reliable direct entry figure found). ASCM's 2025 report puts the median buyer at US$69,300 and procurement managers at US$95,000-142,700 (ASCM 2025 salary report via Supply Chain 24/7). BLS purchasing managers' median was US$139,510 (May 2024).
- UK: entry about GBP 27,000; purchasing officers about GBP 28,000 up to 40,000; procurement/purchasing managers about GBP 54,576, rising to about GBP 76,000; very senior over GBP 122,000 (Prospects quoting the 2025 CIPS Procurement Salary Guide).
- Italy: no reliable data found for general buyers. Indicative: junior procurement specialist EUR 30-38k and technical buyer EUR 32-40k in renewable energy (Talenty.green 2026 guide; sector-specific and likely above average); a training-site table gives EUR 30-38k for "Responsabile acquisti e logistica" at 0-2 years (Masterin.it, weak source). Mercer data show average first-job pay of EUR 30,500 in Italy (ItaliaOggi, 2024).
- Singapore: buyer/planner about S$50k, S$75k, S$90k at 5-10, 10-15 and 15+ years (Morgan McKinley 2025 permanent data); procurement managers about S$110-115k at 5-10 years (Morgan McKinley, 2025-26). Entry not given.

**Career path:** Junior buyer/purchasing assistant (0-2 years) → buyer/procurement specialist (2-4) → senior buyer or category manager (4-8) → procurement manager / head of category (8-12) → director / chief procurement officer (12+). Large companies run rotational programmes; smaller ones may require changing employer to progress (Prospects).

**Exit opportunities:** After 2-3 years: planning, supplier quality, project procurement, or procurement at a larger or better-known firm. After 7-10 years: head of procurement, supply chain director, consulting (procurement/cost-reduction practices), private-equity operating partner roles at the senior end, or moving into commercial/contract management.

**Tier list of employers:** Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact.
- *Tier 1 (large, structured, best brand and training):* Gartner Supply Chain Top 25 style companies, the 2025 list led by Schneider Electric (No. 1) and NVIDIA (No. 2), with "Masters" Amazon, Apple, P&G and Unilever (Gartner 2025, via Supply Chain 24/7); also large pharma, automotive OEMs (VW, BMW, Stellantis) and defence primes. Sizeable category scope, formal programmes, but narrow slices at entry.
- *Tier 2 (large industrial and consumer groups, strong regional brands):* Italian groups such as Barilla, Ferrero, Luxottica/EssilorLuxottica, Pirelli, Leonardo, Fincantieri (employer names in Italy are general knowledge, programmes and procurement hiring not verified), mid-cap manufacturers, big retailers' buying teams.
- *Tier 3 (smaller firms, distributors, public bodies):* wider variety, faster responsibility but weaker structured training; public sector (NHS Procurement Graduate Scheme is a two-year paid programme, Prospects).

**How to enter:** Business, supply chain, economics, engineering (for technical buying), or any degree plus procurement exposure. Programmes: Unilever Future Leaders Programme supply chain track (36 months in the UK, STEM, other science-based or supply chain degrees; UK window ended 12 October 2026 per a careers mirror; Dutch track closed 21 March 2026 and asks for a Dutch WO master's); P&G supply chain graduate roles; NHS Procurement Graduate Scheme; Maersk, DHL and Kuehne+Nagel programmes mostly lean logistics-side rather than procurement (Maersk has run procurement graduate programmes in other markets, e.g. South Africa 2021, not currently verified). Recruiting calendar: UK large-company schemes open from autumn and close roughly November-March; Prospects advises looking from late October; Dutch tracks close around March. Certifications: CIPS (Chartered Institute of Procurement & Supply; MCIPS needs accredited qualification, three years' experience and ethics certificate, CPD of 30 hours a year per Prospects) matters in the UK and for Commonwealth/Gulf employers; CPSM (ISM) is the US equivalent (unverified, BLS does not name it); in Italy and Germany certifications matter much less than a relevant master's. Skills: Excel, a spend-analysis tool, SAP or Ariba familiarity, contract basics, negotiation practice, Power BI. Common mistakes: applying only to big-name schemes; underestimating how important commodity or technical knowledge is in direct buying. Alternative routes: start as purchasing assistant and do a CIPS apprenticeship (levels 3, 4 and 6) or take a first job in customer service or planning at a manufacturer. Entry difficulty: 2/5.

**Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for:** Cost-saving targets can push you into unpleasant negotiations and conflicts with engineers and sales; you are often the person blamed for late materials; strategic sourcing roles at well-known employers are fewer than operational buying roles; AI and automation are removing routine "tail-spend" purchasing work. Not a good fit for people who dislike confrontation or who want a mostly analytical, solo job.

### 3.2 Demand / supply planner and S&OP analyst

**What you actually do:** A demand planner produces and improves the statistical forecast, adjusts it for promotions and launches, and works with sales on consensus. A supply planner converts that into a production and purchasing plan against capacity, materials and lead times. An S&OP/IBP analyst prepares the monthly cycle: gap analysis, scenarios, financial view. Tools: SAP IBP, Kinaxis RapidResponse/Maestro, o9, Blue Yonder, Excel and SQL/Power BI. Example: you re-run the 12-week forecast in SAP IBP, find a 20% shortfall on a top SKU after a competitor's promotion, and negotiate an expedited air shipment while telling finance the margin impact.

**A typical day and week:** Morning: check overnight system exceptions (stock-outs, overstocks, late POs); midday: sales/customer forecast review; afternoon: supply review with plants and procurement; weekly: demand review, supply review, then the S&OP executive meeting once a month. Month-end is heavy; during disruptions (tariffs, port closures) you replan constantly.

**Hours, stress and lifestyle:** 40-45 hours typical; 50-55 in S&OP weeks or crises. Stress: 3/5, spiking to 4 during shortages. Largely office-based and hybrid-friendly; limited travel (some plant visits). Time-zone calls for global teams are common (e.g. P&G's Warsaw hub works across Europe).

**Human vs. quantitative profile:** People/communication: 3/5. Quantitative/technical: 4/5. The maths is forecasting error metrics, safety stock formulas and heavy spreadsheet or SQL work; the people part is negotiating a forecast with sales who over-promise and operations who under-deliver.

**Compensation (approximate):**
- US: ASCM 2025 report shows analysts at a median of US$85,000 across all levels (ASCM via Supply Chain 24/7); supply chain managers US$100,000-145,000; supply chain directors US$160,000-184,200 (ASCM 2025). Entry planner pay: no reliable data found (inferred US$55-70k from BLS logistician/buyer ranges; weak).
- UK: coordinators, planners and analysts GBP 27,000-29,000 rising to about GBP 37,000 (Prospects, current page); P&G graduate GBP 37,000 plus bonus (2024 cycle); DHL Supply Chain graduate programme GBP 30,500 plus GBP 500 joining payment (2027 start).
- Italy: Milan junior demand planner at a staffing agency RAL EUR 25-28k under the CCNL Commercio (a single job listing); a training-site table gives EUR 26-32k for logistics analysts at 0-2 years (Masterin.it, weak). Aggregated averages (Jooble) are not reliable for junior pay.
- Singapore: planning manager about S$115k / S$160k / S$200k at 5-10, 10-15 and 15+ years (Morgan McKinley 2026 contract bands, not permanent).

**Career path:** Planning analyst/junior planner (0-2) → demand or supply planner (2-4) → senior planner / S&OP lead (4-7) → planning manager (7-10) → supply chain director / VP planning (12+). Graduate schemes at FMCG often rotate through planning, logistics and procurement for 2-3 years.

**Exit opportunities:** After 2-3 years: procurement, logistics, project roles, planning-systems roles, or specialist planning at another industry. After 7-10 years: head of supply chain, S&OP/IBP transformation lead, supply chain consulting, planning-software vendor roles (solution consultant), or general operations management.

**Tier list of employers:** Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact.
- *Tier 1:* P&G, Unilever, Nestle, Coca-Cola, big pharma, Apple/Amazon, major electronics firms; structured rotations, sophisticated tools and strong CV value (Gartner Top 25 "Masters" include P&G, Unilever, Amazon, Apple).
- *Tier 2:* Italian and European consumer and industrial companies, retailers' central planning teams, specialised planning consultancies and system integrators (Accenture, Capgemini, Deloitte, boutiques around Kinaxis/o9/SAP).
- *Tier 3:* SMEs and distributors (often planning in Excel with less tool exposure; broader responsibility, narrower training).

**How to enter:** Supply chain management, industrial engineering/ingegneria gestionale, economics, mathematics, statistics or business; engineering or science helps for Unilever's UK track. Programmes: Unilever Future Leaders Programme (supply chain, 36 months; includes roles such as response planner, logistics projects and procurement executive), P&G supply network operations programme (Warsaw hub roles in production planning, demand planning, materials supply, artwork, distribution), DHL Supply Chain Supply Chain & Logistics Specialist Graduate Programme (2-2.5 years, any degree subject, no minimum grade, September 2027 start, no visa sponsorship), DE&S Operations Supply Chain Graduate Scheme (UK, two years, GBP 27,840-28,880, SC security clearance required). Calendar: typically autumn to early spring for large schemes (see 3.1). Certifications: ASCM CPIM (planning and inventory) and CSCP: they matter in the US and Asia and for mid-career moves, much less for graduate entry. ASCM's 2025 report links certification to higher pay (roughly 18-20% in different sources; confounded by seniority). Skills: Excel at an advanced level (pivots, lookups), SQL, Power BI, basic forecasting statistics, an introduction to SAP IBP or Kinaxis (vendor training is free), and a Python basics module. Common mistake: treating the forecast as a pure number and not talking to sales. Alternative routes: customer service at a manufacturer, inventory control roles, supply chain analyst jobs in 3PLs, a master's in supply chain management (the MIT and Rotterdam-type programmes are well regarded; unverified). Entry difficulty: 3/5.

**Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for:** You will be blamed for forecast error even when sales caused it; routine forecasting tasks are being automated and some sources claim agentic AI will reduce entry-level hiring (unverified); the work can feel like spreadsheets and meetings. Not a good fit if you want a clearly visible product or hands-on work, or if you dislike ambiguity and repeated re-planning.

### 3.3 Logistics and transport coordinator/manager (incl. freight forwarding and customs)

**What you actually do:** Arrange and track shipments by road, rail, air and sea; book carriers; prepare or check documents (bills of lading, invoices, certificates of origin); manage customs declarations and duty; handle exceptions; negotiate rates. In a forwarder you work in teams per mode (ocean, air, road) or per customer; in a shipper you buy transport from forwarders and carriers. Tools: TMS (transport management system), carrier portals (K+N myKN), customs systems, Excel, email. Example: a vessel is rolled (bumped) at Rotterdam; you rebook on another carrier, update the customer's ETA, and check the new Incoterm cost impact.

**A typical day and week:** Morning: review overnight alerts, vessel and flight schedules, customs holds; midday: quotes and bookings; afternoon: documentation, invoices, calls with agents in other time zones; weekly: customer reviews. Peak weeks (before Chinese New Year, Q4 peak, tariff deadlines) bring double-hours. Month-end closes can pull people into overtime (employee reviews at DSV in Dubai and Singapore report this; self-reported, weak signal).

**Hours, stress and lifestyle:** Typical 40-45; peaks 50-60. Prospects says logistics managers can work up to 60 hours and may work shifts, evenings and weekends in 24-hour operations. Stress: 4/5 in forwarding and transport operations; 3/5 in a planned shipper-side role. Mostly office-based with desk phones and screens; some roles require port, airport or site visits; remote work possible but limited; on-call duty is common in air freight and customs.

**Human vs. quantitative profile:** People/communication: 4/5. Quantitative/technical: 3/5. You spend the day talking to carriers, customers and agents; the technical load is rules (customs, Incoterms, dangerous-goods regulations) and cost arithmetic.

**Compensation (approximate):**
- US: BLS logisticians median US$82,320 (May 2025), 10th percentile under US$50,890, 90th over US$133,160; transportation and warehousing industry median US$63,530; federal government US$103,790; manufacturing US$83,990 (BLS OOH). 255,100 jobs, growth projected at 18% in 2025-35.
- UK: entry about GBP 25,000 average (Prospects); real postings: freight forwarding operations coordinator GBP 34,000 (Redditch), import/export coordinator GBP 30,000-32,000 (Coventry), customs specialist GBP 30,000-35,000 (Essex, April 2025), European operations coordinator GBP 30,000-35,000 (Selby) (job postings, weak signal); managers GBP 41,000-65,000 and heads of logistics over GBP 90,000 (Prospects). Kuehne+Nagel UK graduate GBP 28,500 plus GBP 1,000 joining bonus (2026 listing); DHL graduate GBP 30,500.
- Italy: logistics clerk roles EUR 22,000-28,000 (Randstad listing, Tortona, not graduate); logistics analyst EUR 26,000-32,000 at 0-2 years (Masterin.it, weak). No reliable recruiter salary guide found.
- Netherlands: average freight forwarder base about EUR 34,206 (Payscale, 2026). Germany: one export specialist posting EUR 50,000-60,000 (posting, upper-range example).
- Singapore: logistics specialist about S$48,000 and S$62,000 at 5-10 and 10-15 years (Singsaver 2026 blog, source unclear).

**Career path:** Operations/coordinator trainee (0-2) → senior coordinator or customs specialist (2-4) → team lead / key-account manager (4-7) → branch or operations manager (7-12) → regional/country director. Customs routes lead to compliance manager or director of trade compliance.

**Exit opportunities:** After 2-3 years: procurement, planning, carrier sales, or customs brokerage. After 7-10 years: head of logistics or supply chain at a shipper, branch/country manager at a forwarder, trade-compliance director, consulting, or founding your own forwarding agency (common among long-experienced forwarders).

**Tier list of employers:** Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact. Reference: Armstrong & Associates top forwarders by revenue/volume (2025) and the Alphaliner list for carriers.
- *Tier 1 (global forwarders/integrators):* Kuehne+Nagel, DSV (with DB Schenker), DHL Global Forwarding, UPS Supply Chain Solutions, Expeditors, CEVA, Sinotrans, Nippon Express. Systematic graduate programmes and training; K+N and DSV are cutting white-collar roles with technology, and employee review sites show more overtime complaints at DSV than at K+N (anecdotal).
- *Tier 2 (large regional/national forwarders and 3PLs):* In Italy Savino Del Bene, Fercam, Arcese, Gruber, Geodis, BRT; in the Netherlands and Belgium large regional forwarders; asset-based trucking groups. More personal culture, hands-on responsibility.
- *Tier 3 (small forwarders, customs brokers):* low pay, high autonomy, little formal training.

**How to enter:** Any business, geography, economics, languages or international trade degree; logistics-specific degrees help; a driving licence is often required for UK programmes (Prospects, K+N, DHL listings). Programmes: Kuehne+Nagel graduate programme (UK: two-year rotation with a professional qualification, mentor and buddy; the official page is dated and asks for a 2:2, HR scheme a 2:1 and right to work; no sponsorship), Maersk UK Graduate Programme (pathways including sales, customs, customer experience and finance; video interview, assessment centre at East Midlands Gateway, leadership interview; no visa sponsorship; an Maersk Contract Logistics Management scheme closed May 2026), DHL Supply Chain & Logistics Specialist Graduate Programme (see 3.2). DSV and CEVA run graduate schemes (unverified). Calendar: UK schemes mostly open September-November with rolling closings; forwarders also hire all year round for coordinator roles. Certifications: CILT (Chartered Institute of Logistics & Transport, UK; accredits some degrees, offers diplomas and chartered membership per Prospects); FIATA diploma (unverified); customs qualifications such as the US licensed customs broker exam (unverified, twice yearly) or Italian "spedizioniere doganale" licensing (unverified). They matter for customs roles but not for generalist operations roles. Skills: Incoterms, shipping documents, customs classification, Excel, a TMS, a second language (German, Italian, Spanish, Mandarin are valued in different hubs). Common mistakes: ignoring documentation detail (a single wrong HS code can freeze a container); expecting 9-to-5. Alternative routes: entry-level forwarding clerk/coordinator roles with no degree, then CILT part-time study; customs clerk to customs broker. Entry difficulty: 2/5 for coordinator posts; 3/5 for Tier 1 graduate programmes.

**Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for:** Reactive, deadline-driven, often poorly paid at entry with variable bonus; forwarders are in cost-cutting mode and automating booking and quoting; you are measured on exceptions. Not a good fit for people who need predictable hours or who dislike phone-heavy, interrupt-driven work.

### 3.4 Warehouse / distribution operations manager

**What you actually do:** Run a shift or a department of a warehouse or fulfilment centre: staffing, safety, picking and packing productivity, inbound and outbound flow, inventory accuracy, and customer service levels. You use a WMS, labour management dashboards and daily KPIs, and you lead operatives, often through supervisors. Example: a pick-path jam delays outbound trailers; you re-allocate 12 people, change the batching and clear the backlog before the trucks leave.

**A typical day and week:** Shift start briefing and safety talk; walk the floor, review KPIs; solve problems (equipment faults, absenteeism, damage); meet with the next shift. On a 4-on, 3-off or 12-hour pattern; peak season (November-December, the pre-Christmas "Q4 peak") means extra shifts and compulsory overtime.

**Hours, stress and lifestyle:** 45-55 hours on shift patterns; 60+ in peak; nights and weekends common. Amazon postings describe on-site work, 10-12 hours of standing and walking per day, some roles 12-hour shifts, 3x2 pattern in Brazil, 4-on/3-off in Australia (Amazon postings, 2025-26). Stress: 4/5. No remote work. Travel is limited to the site (relocation is common); UK programmes expect mobility and a driving licence (DHL, K+N).

**Human vs. quantitative profile:** People/communication: 5/5. Quantitative/technical: 2/5. It is leadership of a large front-line workforce; the numeracy is KPIs, rate calculations and basic process analysis.

**Compensation (approximate):**
- US: Amazon Area Manager listing in Portland US$70,000-90,900 (2025 listing, one data point); Amazon Canada Area Manager graduate program C$69,000 base plus sign-on and equity (2025). Senior operations manager US$142,688-182,700 (Patterson, California listing). Third-party aggregators vary widely (US$60,000-85,000), and are unreliable. BLS warehouse/logistics-sector median for logisticians US$63,530 (transportation & warehousing, May 2025) is a different job family.
- UK: DHL Supply Chain graduate GBP 30,500 (2027 start); K+N contract logistics graduates around GBP 28,500 (project support listing, 2026); experienced logistics/distribution managers GBP 41,000-65,000 (Prospects). Amazon UK area manager pay: no reliable data found.
- Italy: no reliable data found for graduate-level warehouse managers. Operational logistics clerks EUR 22,000-28,000 (Randstad listing).
- Singapore: no reliable data found.

**Career path:** Shift supervisor / area manager (0-2) → operations manager / site department head (2-5) → general manager of a site (5-9) → regional operations director (9-14) → VP operations. Transfers into continuous improvement, planning, or network design are common.

**Exit opportunities:** After 2-3 years: continuous improvement, planning, transport management, or retail operations. After 7-10 years: site general manager, regional operations director, supply chain director in a retailer, consulting (operations), or operations lead in start-ups or quick-commerce.

**Tier list of employers:** Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact.
- *Tier 1 (best structured training, brand value, scale):* Amazon operations (high scale and volume, exposure to robotics, but tough hours), DHL Supply Chain, Kuehne+Nagel contract logistics, GXO, big grocery chains' DCs, P&G/Unilever manufacturing distribution.
- *Tier 2:* Regional 3PLs, fashion and retail distribution (Zalando, Inditex), pharma logistics.
- *Tier 3:* Small 3PLs, local distributors; pay and training weaker, autonomy higher.

**How to enter:** Any degree (logistics, business, engineering) or none; the key hiring signal is leadership, resilience and shift flexibility. Programmes: Amazon Area Manager programme (entry-level, on-site role), DHL Supply Chain graduate programme (any degree, no minimum grade, mobile across UK sites), Maersk contract logistics management graduate programme (logistics, business or related degree, driving licence; 2026 intake closed in May), Kuehne+Nagel graduate programmes. Calendar: Amazon, DHL and 3PLs hire year-round with some autumn graduate cycles. Certifications: not required; lean or Six Sigma yellow/green belt helps; IOSH/NEBOSH-type safety certificates are valued in the UK (unverified); CILT for later career. Skills: Excel, KPI reading, basic WMS, leadership. Common mistakes: underestimating physical and shift demands; thinking this is an office job. Alternative routes: team leader from the floor with part-time study; military logistics veterans are well regarded (unverified). Entry difficulty: 2/5.

**Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for:** Long shifts, nights and weekends; heavy exposure to injuries, strikes and burnout; automation reduces the number of operative roles, and Amazon-style productivity metrics are controversial (unions are critical, reported). Not a good fit for people who want desk-based, predictable hours or who dislike being on their feet.

### 3.5 Shipping and maritime roles (chartering, ship broking, liner operations)

**What you actually do:** *Ship brokers* help shipowners find cargoes and charterers find ships, in dry bulk, tankers, containers or sale-and-purchase (S&P); they quote, negotiate rates, draft fixtures on BIMCO-based contracts, and follow up post-fixture. *Chartering/operations* in a shipowner or trader: they manage voyages, laytime and demurrage claims, port agents and bunkers (fuel). *Liner operations* at a container line: customer service, pricing, network planning, equipment repositioning, port and terminal coordination. A trainee broker at Hagland, as posted in 2026, shadows client meetings, drafts charter contracts, tracks vessel positions and prepares market reports (TargetJobs listing).

**A typical day and week:** Brokers: early start on market reports and vessel positions, phones and chats most of the day, deals in the afternoon, client entertainment in the evening. Liner operations: monitoring vessel schedules, rerouting around Hormuz or the Cape, handling customer exceptions. Both are highly sensitive to geopolitical shocks.

**Hours, stress and lifestyle:** Brokers: 50-60 hours typical (longer during active markets) with an early start and client entertainment; stress 4/5. Liner operations: 40-50 hours with weekend and on-call coverage; stress 3-4/5. Mostly office-based, some travel (Posidonia in Athens, Marintec, ports); remote work rare for junior brokers. Hours data for brokers rests on practitioner descriptions rather than a published survey (unverified).

**Human vs. quantitative profile:** People/communication: 5/5 for brokers and 3-4/5 for liner customer-facing; Quantitative/technical: 3/5. Brokers need fast arithmetic (freight rate and voyage estimates), market knowledge and relationships; liner network planning is more analytical (Excel, SQL).

**Compensation (approximate):**
- US: no reliable data found (chartering is concentrated in London, Singapore, Athens, Oslo and the Gulf; liner and port roles in the US are typically paid like other logistics jobs).
- UK: trainee shipbroker GBP 25,000-28,000 plus performance bonus (Edinburgh posting); around GBP 27,000 plus potential bonus, with funded ICS exams (Hagland Aberdeen, 2026); London trainee pay at Clarksons, Braemar and SSY: no reliable data found (anecdotally higher; unverified). Experienced broker pay depends on bonus and is highly variable; Clarksons' CEO total package of GBP 11.9m is not representative.
- Italy: no reliable data found (Genoa/Trieste ship-agency and liner jobs are paid within logistics bands, EUR 25-35k; unverified).
- Singapore/Gulf: no reliable data found.

**Career path:** Trainee broker or operations trainee (0-1, often with a 12-18 month training) → junior broker (1-3) → broker (3-7) → senior broker / desk head / director (7-15) with equity or bonus participation. Liner: customer service or pricing analyst → sales/pricing manager → regional head.

**Exit opportunities:** After 2-3 years: another broker desk, shipowner operations, trading house chartering desks (Cargill, Trafigura, Vitol and similar; see the finance report on commodity trading), or port agency. After 7-10 years: head of chartering at a shipowner or trader, establishing a brokerage, shipping finance, or liner commercial management.

**Tier list of employers:** Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact.
- *Tier 1 brokers/shipping groups:* Clarksons, Braemar, SSY, Howe Robinson (these appear in trainee-scheme searches; Clarksons runs a one-year Global Trainee Broker Programme, SSY a 12-month London trainee scheme), Maersk, MSC, CMA CGM, Hapag-Lloyd, COSCO, Evergreen, and major trading houses' shipping desks.
- *Tier 2:* Regional and specialist brokers (Nordic, Greek, Singapore), mid-size carriers (ONE, HMM, Yang Ming, ZIM), large terminal operators (PSA, DP World, APM Terminals, Hutchison; PSA handled 105m TEU globally in 2025).
- *Tier 3:* Small agencies and local port-agent firms, family-owned Italian shipping companies and agencies.

**How to enter:** Any degree (economics, business, languages, geography, law) with motivation for shipping; maritime degrees (Southampton Solent, Plymouth, Genoa, Trieste, Rotterdam/Antwerp programmes, unverified) help. Programmes: Clarksons Global Trainee Broker Programme (one year, rotations, possible overseas secondment), SSY trainee scheme (London, 12 months; applications opened 1 February for a September intake in 2024), Braemar trainee scheme, Howe Robinson trainee shipbroker, Maersk UK Graduate Programme (customs, sales, customer experience, finance pathways; no sponsorship). The ICS Graduate role with Maritime UK is also listed. Calendar: broker schemes often recruit in winter/spring for a September start (SSY); smaller brokers recruit ad hoc. Certifications: ICS (Institute of Chartered Shipbrokers) Professional Qualifying Examinations lead to MICS membership; Foundation and Advanced Diplomas exist (ICS website); many brokers fund them. They matter for broker careers (employers fund and expect them), not for general logistics. Skills: market curiosity, clear writing, Excel, numeracy, network building, familiarity with shipping news (TradeWinds, Lloyd's List). Common mistakes: applying with no evidence of interest in shipping; ignoring right-to-work (many UK brokers cannot sponsor visas, e.g. Hagland). Alternative routes: junior operator at a ship agent, port or terminal roles, then lateral moves. Entry difficulty: 4/5 for Clarksons-type schemes (few places, high competition); 2-3/5 for liner and port roles.

**Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for:** Earnings swing with the freight market, early hours, a heavy culture of entertainment and long working weeks, a small industry where reputation follows you, and a trend toward consolidation. Liner roles are exposed to cyclical profit swings. Not a good fit for people who dislike sales-oriented, relationship-based work, who want fixed pay, or who need structured work-life balance.

### 3.6 Supply chain analyst / supply chain systems specialist

**What you actually do:** Analysts clean and combine data from ERP, WMS and TMS; build dashboards (inventory, service level, transport cost); run scenarios (for example a tariff impact on landed cost); and recommend actions. Systems specialists configure, test and support planning and execution systems (SAP, Kinaxis, o9, Blue Yonder), manage master data, and work with IT and vendors. Tools: SQL, Excel, Power BI/Tableau, Python or R, and the planning systems. Example: you model a new tariff on a Vietnamese supplier and show a Mexican source is cheaper for 40% of volume.

**A typical day and week:** Pull data, validate it, build or refresh reports, answer ad hoc requests from planners and managers; workshops with users; test releases for systems. Project work follows a sprint or milestone pattern.

**Hours, stress and lifestyle:** 38-45 hours typical; peaks 50 during go-lives and quarter-end. Stress: 2-3/5 (project go-lives are 4). Hybrid or remote is most common in this family; travel to plants or integrators for rollouts. Consultancy-side systems roles have more travel.

**Human vs. quantitative profile:** People/communication: 3/5. Quantitative/technical: 4/5. You need data skills (SQL, modelling) and the ability to explain results to non-technical operations managers.

**Compensation (approximate):**
- US: analysts median US$85,000 across experience levels (ASCM 2025 via Supply Chain 24/7); entry level: no reliable data found (BLS logisticians 10th percentile under US$50,890).
- UK: coordinators, planners and analysts GBP 27,000-29,000 rising to about GBP 37,000 (Prospects); P&G supply chain graduate GBP 37,000 plus bonus (2024 listing); systems roles at integrators are higher (no reliable data found).
- Italy: logistics analyst EUR 26,000-32,000 at 0-2 years (Masterin.it, weak); no reliable data for SAP/planning systems specialists.
- Singapore: supply chain analyst average about S$65,000 (Morgan McKinley 2026).

**Career path:** Analyst (0-2) → senior analyst / systems specialist (2-5) → team lead / solution architect / supply chain data manager (5-9) → head of supply chain analytics or digital supply chain (9-14). Consulting or vendor roles accelerate pay.

**Exit opportunities:** After 2-3 years: data analyst/data scientist in other functions, planning or procurement, ERP consultant, product management. After 7-10 years: head of supply chain analytics or digital, supply chain consulting partner track, planning-software vendor solution architect, or operations research (see the data science report).

**Tier list of employers:** Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact.
- *Tier 1:* Large FMCG/manufacturers' analytics centres, Amazon, Kinaxis/o9/SAP/Blue Yonder (vendors), Accenture/Deloitte supply chain technology practices.
- *Tier 2:* Mid-size manufacturers and retailers' internal teams, regional integrators, 3PL analytics teams.
- *Tier 3:* Small firms using Excel with little formal tooling; broad exposure, little training.

**How to enter:** Industrial engineering, computer science, data science, mathematics, statistics, economics, supply chain; strong SQL and Excel are the core. Programmes: P&G Talent Development Programme (analysis projects in Warsaw), DHL technology specialist graduate programme (prefers IT degrees but welcomes supply chain interest), Unilever, Kuehne+Nagel digital/analytics tracks (unverified), DE&S Operations Supply Chain scheme. Calendar: autumn to early spring for graduate schemes; analyst jobs hire all year. Certifications: ASCM CPIM/CSCP are optional signals; SAP certifications and vendor-specific training (Kinaxis, o9) matter for systems roles; Lean Six Sigma green belt helps for process analytics. Skills: SQL, Excel, Power BI, Python, supply chain fundamentals, one planning system, clear presentation. Common mistake: dashboards with no recommended action. Alternative routes: planner or logistics coordinator who becomes the team's "Excel person" and moves into analytics; bootcamps plus a supply chain portfolio project. Entry difficulty: 3/5.

**Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for:** Data is messy and ERP data is often wrong; systems projects overrun; AI tools are automating routine reporting, so pure dashboard builders are less safe than analysts who also own decisions. Not a good fit for people who want frontline operations or negotiation, or who dislike repetitive data cleaning.

### 3.7 Supply chain consultant (shorter)

**What you actually do:** Work in a team for a client on a project of weeks to months: analyse inventory, design a distribution network, reduce procurement spend, redesign an S&OP process, assess tariff exposure, or implement a planning system. Deliverables are models, slide decks and implementation plans. For detail on firms, hours and recruiting see the management consulting report.

**A typical day and week:** Client site or video workshops, analysis in Excel/Alteryx/Python, slides for steering committees, weekly status review. Travel to client sites 3-4 days a week is common in the UK, Germany and the US; Italy and Gulf projects are often local or regional (management consulting report).

**Hours, stress and lifestyle:** 50-60 hours typical, 60-70 at deadlines (practitioner norms; see management consulting report); stress 4/5; travel heavy; remote only partly.

**Human vs. quantitative profile:** People/communication: 4/5. Quantitative/technical: 4/5.

**Compensation (approximate):**
- US: Big 4 consulting analyst base US$78-112k and Accenture Strategy about US$105k (Road to Offer / PrepLounge 2026, via the management consulting report); operations-specific figures not separated.
- UK: Big 4 and Accenture graduate base roughly GBP 32-37k, Capgemini Invent GBP 51k (PrepLounge UK 2026, via the management consulting report).
- Italy: no reliable data found; indicative EUR 26-30k at Deloitte/Accenture Italy (Indeed Italy, self-reported, management consulting report).
- Senior: Big 4 manager US$105-150k base; UK senior consultant/manager GBP 92-125k (management consulting report). Supply-chain-specific bands: no reliable data found.

**Career path:** Analyst/consultant (0-2) → senior consultant (2-4) → manager (4-7) → senior manager/director (7-10) → partner. Many leave before manager level.

**Exit opportunities:** After 2-3 years: supply chain roles in industry (planning, procurement, strategy), MBA. After 7-10 years: head of supply chain or transformation at a corporate, private-equity operations partner, or partner track.

**Tier list of employers:** Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact.
- *Tier 1:* McKinsey, BCG, Bain operations practices, Kearney (supply chain strength; unverified ranking).
- *Tier 2:* Big 4 operations practices, Accenture Operations, Capgemini Invent, Alvarez & Marsal, AlixPartners.
- *Tier 3:* Boutique planning/ERP consultancies and local firms (BIP, Reply in Italy; see the management consulting report).

**How to enter:** Engineering, business, economics, supply chain; a master's helps; relevant internship in planning or procurement. Programmes and calendar: see the management consulting report (UK Big 4 recruitment is rolling, opening around September with deadlines in spring). Certifications: ASCM CSCP is a modest plus; no certification is needed at strategy firms. Skills: Excel/PowerPoint, structured problem solving, supply chain fundamentals, SQL/Alteryx. Common mistakes: applying only to MBB; treating supply chain as a purely technical subject. Alternative routes: 2-3 years in industry planning or procurement, then lateral hire into an operations practice; technology-implementation roles at integrators. Entry difficulty: 4/5 for MBB, 3/5 for Big 4/Accenture operations practices.

**Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for:** Long hours and travel, an up-or-out culture, work on slides rather than operations, and a slowdown since 2023 with AI changing junior work (management consulting report). Not a good fit for people who want to own a real operation, need stable hours, or dislike presenting.
