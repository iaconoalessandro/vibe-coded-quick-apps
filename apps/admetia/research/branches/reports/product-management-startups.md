## 1. What this branch is

Product management (PM) is the job of deciding what a software product or digital service should do next and getting a team of engineers and designers to build it, with the product manager owning the outcome (usage, revenue, customer satisfaction) rather than writing the code. Around it sit the "operator" jobs that help a product team or a young company run (technical programme managers, product operations, chiefs of staff, business operations), and the founder path, where you build the company yourself and raise money from investors to do it. Startups are small companies trying to grow very fast; most fail, a few become very valuable, and almost everyone who works in them accepts lower or riskier pay in exchange for scope, speed and equity (a share of ownership). This branch suits people who like ambiguity, working with engineers and customers, and being judged on results. Entry is hard in 2026: big-tech junior openings have shrunk, and the quickest route into product or startups is often a first job next to the product (operations, analytics, customer-facing work) rather than a "PM" title.

**Scope notes:** Global view with the US (Bay Area, New York), UK (London), continental Europe (Italy always called out, plus Berlin, Paris, Amsterdam, Stockholm where data exists) and Asia/Gulf (Singapore, Tokyo, Hong Kong, Dubai/Abu Dhabi, Riyadh) where evidence was found; AI product management, software engineering, product marketing, venture capital and consulting are covered in other branches and only cross-referenced here. "Entry" means a graduate with 0-2 years' experience. Search allowance was limited (about 25 searches plus page fetches): Amsterdam, Stockholm, Hong Kong and Tokyo pay data were not found and are marked "no reliable data found". Many programme and funding details come from secondary summaries and are flagged "unverified" where they could not be checked at the source.

## 2. Map of areas and sectors

### The 2024-2026 context in numbers

- **Product jobs overall are not collapsing, but junior ones are scarce.** Lenny's Newsletter's early-2026 market update reports PM openings at their highest level in over three years, driven by AI roles (summary seen via search; the post is paywalled). A LinkedIn-based tracker reported global PM postings down 9% in April 2026 but up 6.4% year on year (secondary summary, April 2026). A December 2025 Mind the Product salary report (Levels.fyi and 3Search data) says entry-level pay has barely moved while the number of trainee PM, product analyst and product owner roles has fallen, and some roles draw 400+ applications within days, including from experienced people willing to step down (Mind the Product, 19 December 2025).
- **Big tech hires fewer new graduates and is flattening management.** SignalFire found new graduates were 7% of big-tech hires in 2024, down 25% on 2023 and more than 50% on 2019, and that non-technical functions such as recruiting, product and sales "keep shrinking" (SignalFire, May 2025; US data, engineering-heavy sample). "Flattening" of management layers at big tech is widely reported but was not verified at source here; Mind the Product's data is consistent with it, since senior individual-contributor PM pay rose while Head of Product, VP and CPO offers fell (December 2025).
- **"Builder PM" expectations.** Job posts increasingly ask PMs to prototype with AI tools (for example Claude, Cursor, Lovable, v0) rather than only write specifications; examples include an MVST "Builder PM" post and a Certa "Associate Product Manager - AI Builder" post that still asks for 1-3 years' experience (job listings, seen October 2026). "Builder PM" is not a standard title yet and the idea is mostly practitioner commentary; some employers label ordinary PM jobs "AI PM" (University of Rochester careers blog, December 2025).
- **Startup money is huge but concentrated in AI.** Crunchbase counted US$159bn of global venture funding in Q3 2026, with AI companies taking about US$102bn (64%) and 27 rounds of US$1bn or more; US startups took about 57% (figures from secondary write-ups of the Crunchbase report, October 2026; original not opened). On Carta's platform, US startups raised US$30.4bn in Q1 2026 with over 60% going to AI, the highest share Carta has recorded (Carta State of Private Markets Q1 2026, published 29 May 2026, via search summary). Europe is smaller: about US$44bn expected for 2025, up 7% (Atomico State of European Tech 2025, data to 30 September 2025). European early-stage rounds have fallen about 20% a year since 2022, mainly sub-US$1m rounds, while median seed and Series A round sizes rose 23% and 25% in 2025 (Atomico). The median European seed round is about half the US one in size (Atomico). Fewer, bigger rounds mean fewer startups funded and higher bars for those that are.
- **Founders are starting more companies anyway.** Atomico counted over 27,000 new European founders in 2025, nearly 60% more than in 2023 (via secondary summary of Atomico 2025).

### 2.1 Product management at tech companies

- **What it is.** A product manager owns the "what and why" of a product: researches user problems, sets priorities, writes requirements, agrees scope with engineering and design, launches, and measures results. Key terms: a *PRD* (product requirements document) is the document describing a feature and why it matters; a *roadmap* is the ordered plan of what the team will build and roughly when; an *OKR* (objectives and key results) is a quarterly goal-setting format; *product-market fit* means enough customers genuinely want and keep using the product.
- **In practice, the main flavours:**
  - *Consumer PM* (apps such as Spotify, Uber, Booking.com): decisions are driven by experiments (A/B tests) and engagement metrics.
  - *B2B/SaaS PM* (software sold to companies, such as Salesforce, Atlassian, Stripe): fewer users, longer sales cycles, more customer interviews, and tight links to sales and customer success.
  - *Platform / technical PM*: owns internal or developer-facing systems (APIs, payments infrastructure, data platforms, ML platforms). Needs enough engineering fluency to discuss architecture trade-offs; usually draws from engineers.
  - *Growth PM*: owns acquisition, activation, retention or monetisation funnels; very quantitative; runs many experiments.
- **Who employs it.** Big tech (Google, Meta, Amazon, Microsoft, Apple), scale-ups (Revolut, Stripe, Spotify, Uber, Booking.com, Adyen, Bending Spoons), and venture-backed startups where the founder often acts as de facto head of product until the first hires.

### 2.2 Product management at non-tech companies

- **What it is.** Banks, insurers, retailers, telecom operators, utilities and media groups now run apps, websites and internal platforms, so they employ product managers, "digital product owners" (the Scrum term for the person who prioritises the team's backlog) and "digital transformation" teams.
- **In practice.** Slower release cycles, heavier compliance and legacy systems; the business sponsor often has more power than the product team; less access to experimentation data. Titles are inconsistent: "product owner" may mean a delivery-focused backlog administrator, not a strategic PM. Banks and telcos often ask for Scrum certifications (see 3.1).
- **Pay signal.** In London, JPMorgan's PM median on Levels.fyi was £101k against £302k at Meta (all levels, 9 October 2026), a sign that corporate PM jobs pay materially below tech-hub PM jobs; it is self-reported and small-sample.

### 2.3 Associate / rotational product manager (APM/RPM) programmes

- **What it is.** Structured early-career programmes that rotate new graduates through several product teams over 18-24 months, usually with mentoring and a bootcamp, ending in a full PM role.
- **Verified as running (October 2026):** Meta RPM: 18 months, four-week bootcamp, three team rotations, Menlo Park and New York, starts March and September 2027, applications open once a year in late summer or early fall (Meta Careers page, October 2026; the page also states four or more years of related experience while third-party guides say under one year of PM experience, so the eligibility text is inconsistent: check before applying). Google APM: programme page exists, says application windows are announced per cohort and gives no dates for London or Bengaluru graduate cohorts; Google describes the target as technically oriented graduates (Google Careers, October 2026). Uber APM: two-year programme with three rotations; the 2026 window reportedly closed on 26 September (Leland guide, secondary; official Uber page confirms the programme exists). Atlassian APM (two one-year rotations; the Australia/New Zealand 2026 round ran in January 2026) and a Salesforce "Associate Product Manager" summer-2026 posting also exist (job listings). Other names appear on third-party lists (Stripe, Intuit RPM, Bloomberg) but were not verified here.
- **Reality.** Cohorts are tiny (a third-party blog estimates about 10 Uber APMs a year from 3,000+ applicants; unverified) and skew to computer science and engineering graduates. Several programmes are US-centred. Italy has no APM programme comparable to Google's; the nearest equivalents are Bending Spoons' product and growth roles or a junior product owner job.

### 2.4 Technical programme management (TPM) and product operations

- **TPM.** A technical programme manager coordinates large engineering efforts that span several teams: dependencies, schedules, risks, launches (for example a data-centre migration or a payments platform rebuild). Concentrated at Google, Amazon, Meta, Microsoft and large infrastructure-heavy firms.
- **Product operations ("product ops").** The team that makes product managers more effective: runs planning cycles, sets up analytics and feedback pipelines, manages tooling and launch processes, and ties product to sales and support. The role has no agreed definition and the most-cited surveys date from 2022, so treat descriptions as practitioner consensus.
- **Who employs it.** Scale-ups and big tech (TPM); scale-ups from about 100-200 staff (product ops).

### 2.5 Startups as employers

- **Early employee** (first 5-30 hires): a generalist or specialist with broad responsibility, lower cash pay, an equity grant, and a high chance of the company failing or being acquired cheaply.
- **Founder's associate / chief of staff (CoS).** A chief of staff is a right-hand person to a founder or executive who runs projects, prepares board materials, runs the operating cadence and fills any gap. "Founder's associate" is the junior version, typically reporting to a CEO of a company under 50 people. These roles are mostly filled through personal introductions and are rarely advertised (practitioner consensus; no primary data found).
- **BizOps, strategy & operations ("strategy and ops"), startup generalist.** An analyst-style role that builds models, tracks company metrics, plans pricing or expansion, and runs cross-team projects. Revolut's "Strategy & Operations Manager" graduate programme asks for a STEM 2:1 and SQL, Python or R, so it screens out business graduates without coding (Revolut posting, 2026, from prior Admetia research).
- **Employers.** Venture-backed startups, scale-ups, startup studios, and fast-growing fintechs. The Italian base is small: Italian venture investment was about €1.735bn across 436 rounds in 2025, second-best year after 2022, but with only nine new funds closing a total €545m and a recognised shortage of late-stage money and exits (Growth Capital and Italian Tech Alliance Observatory, January 2026, preliminary). Registered "innovative startups" number roughly 11,000-12,000, depending on the source (Cribes January 2026: 11,090; Cerved end-2025: 11,820; L'Espresso September 2026: 12,073); the status carries tax and legal benefits and, since 2025, must be earned through growth (Law 193/2024 and a MIMIT circular of 29 July 2025, via secondary summaries). The most prominent Italian tech employer is Bending Spoons (Milan), which now has offices in London, Madrid and Warsaw and recruits students directly (company newsroom, 2026).

### 2.6 Entrepreneurship: founding, funding, equity and visas

- **Founding.** Starting a company, usually with a co-founder, to build a product or service. Most founders begin with a problem they know well, a prototype, and a few paying users, not a business plan.
- **How startups are funded.**
  - *Bootstrapping*: funded by the founders' savings and early revenue; slower, keeps full ownership.
  - *Angels and pre-seed*: individuals or small funds put in typically a few hundred thousand dollars/euros for an early stake.
  - *Accelerators*: Y Combinator invests US$500,000 per company (US$125,000 for 7% via a post-money SAFE plus US$375,000 on an uncapped "most favoured nation" SAFE; YC deal page, October 2026). A *SAFE* ("simple agreement for future equity") is a contract that converts into shares at the next priced round. Techstars' package is reported at US$220,000 (US$200,000 uncapped MFN SAFE plus US$20,000 for 5% common stock; secondary, 2025-26); an aggregator claims Techstars is closing several European programmes in 2026 (unverified; Techstars London lists a 2026 cohort). Entrepreneur First (EF) pays a £6,000 equity-free "Talent Investment", then invests US$125,000 for 8% via a post-money SAFE in teams passing its investment committee, plus an optional further US$125,000 if they relocate to San Francisco (EF London FAQ, 2025-26).
  - *Pre-seed / seed / Series A*: successive funding stages. Carta's median post-money valuation in Q4 2025 was US$24m at seed and US$78.7m at Series A (via secondary summary of Carta data).
  - *Venture debt and public funding*: Europe's venture debt hit a record US$5.6bn in 2025 (Atomico). In Italy, CDP Venture Capital runs a national accelerator network (17 hubs in 2024, three batches of 8-10 startups each; EconomyUp).
- **Equity and dilution basics.** *Dilution* is the shrinking of your ownership percentage each time new shares are issued to investors or employees. *Vesting* means you earn your shares over time (the standard is four years with a one-year *cliff*, meaning nothing vests until month 12). *Runway* is how many months of cash the company has left. *ARR* (annual recurring revenue) is the main revenue metric for subscription software.
- **Founder visas and programmes (status checked October 2026).**
  - *UK Innovator Founder visa*: your idea must be new, viable and scalable and endorsed by an approved body; endorsement costs £1,000, the visa fee is £1,357 from outside the UK, it lasts 3 years, can be extended in 3-year blocks, and settlement may be possible after 3 years; the endorser checks progress at 12 and 24 months (GOV.UK, October 2026). Third-party summaries say that only three business endorsing bodies can endorse new applications (UK Endorsing Services, Innovator International, Envestors) plus the government's Global Entrepreneurs Programme for invited founders, and that "legacy" bodies can only re-endorse founders they endorsed before 13 April 2023 (mirrors of the GOV.UK list, 2026; confirm on GOV.UK).
  - *Italia Startup Visa*: for non-EU founders of an "innovative startup"; a MIMIT committee reviews applications (reported at about 30 days) and applicants must show at least €50,000 available (secondary immigration-consultancy sources, 2026; check the official Italia Startup Visa portal).
  - *Singapore EntrePass*: for foreign founders of venture-backed or technology-based companies; one-year first issue, renewals tied to performance (consultancy sources, 2026; confirm with MOM).
  - *Japan Startup Visa*: up to two years to set up a business, nationwide since January 2025; the follow-on Business Manager visa capital threshold rose from ¥5m to ¥30m in late 2025 (secondary). Hong Kong and Stockholm: no reliable data found.
  - *UAE*: a Golden Visa route for entrepreneurs exists but criteria vary across guides (incubator approval versus a minimum project value of AED 500,000) and one source claims AED 2m; verify with UAE authorities. Abu Dhabi's Hub71 offers in-kind incentives of up to AED 250,000 plus AED 250,000 cash-for-equity (dated 2023 reports; confirm current terms). MENA startup funding fell to US$1.7bn in H1 2026, down 18% year on year (Wamda, July 2026); full-year 2025 was a record but trackers disagree (MAGNiTT: more than US$3bn; Wamda: US$7.5bn including debt, with Saudi Arabia at US$5bn). Saudi Arabia (Riyadh) is the largest Gulf market but debt-heavy.
- **Base rates.** Most seed-funded startups never raise a Series A (Carta, 2026): only about 10% graduate within 12 months and a further 15.6% within 12-24 months, with roughly 80% of eventual graduates having done so by year three (Carta analysis, February 2026, via secondary summary). Shutdowns on Carta's platform rose 25.6% in 2024 to 966 (Carta, via secondary summary; counts only Carta customers). Looking at all new US businesses (not only venture-backed ones), about half of establishments are still operating after five years (BLS series, via secondary summary; includes sales and mergers, so overstates failure). For venture-backed startups the usual outcomes are a few big winners and many zero-return outcomes; no reliable figure for "the share of venture-backed startups returning investors' money" was found in the sources checked.

### 2.7 Venture builders/studios and corporate innovation (brief)

- **Venture studios / startup generators** create companies in-house and hire "entrepreneurs in residence", operators and associates. Examples: Antler (hired two LBS MiM 2024 graduates), EF (talent-first, no idea required) and Founders Factory (general knowledge; not verified here).
- **Corporate innovation / digital units**: bank "digital factories", telco innovation labs, and corporate venture arms run pilots and partnerships. Autonomy is limited by the parent company, but the job is stable and pay is closer to corporate scales.

## 3. Role families

### 3.1 Product manager (including growth and platform/technical PM)

- **What you actually do:** You decide what the team should build next and why. Concretely: interview customers and read support tickets; define a problem and success metric; write a one- to two-page PRD (requirements document) for, say, a new onboarding flow; agree scope with two or three engineers and a designer; prioritise the backlog; run an experiment (A/B test); launch, then review the results dashboard (SQL, Amplitude/Mixpanel/Looker-type tools) and decide to iterate or stop. You maintain a roadmap, run quarterly planning with OKRs, and present to leadership. *Growth PMs* own conversion and retention funnels and run many experiments; *platform/technical PMs* own APIs, data platforms or developer tools and spend more time with engineers on architecture; *B2B PMs* spend more time with sales and customers; *consumer PMs* spend more time on data and design.
- **A typical day and week:** A day is mostly meetings and writing: a morning stand-up with the engineering team, a design review, a customer call or data pull, afternoon stakeholder alignment (sales, legal, marketing), and an hour of writing specs or reviewing metrics. A week includes sprint planning (the team's one- or two-week work cycle), a roadmap or prioritisation discussion, a customer interview or two, an experiment readout, and a leadership update. About half your time is communication; deep work is squeezed into early mornings or evenings.
- **Hours, stress and lifestyle:** Typical 45-55 hours a week; peak 60-70 around launches, planning deadlines or incidents at startups. Stress **3/5** (steady deadlines, metrics pressure, constant trade-offs; 4/5 at an early-stage startup where the product is the company). Lifestyle is flexible in some firms but big tech has tightened return-to-office (Amazon five days a week; Google, Meta and Apple three or four, per prior Admetia research on tech RTO, 2025-26: unverified at source here). Travel is low. Remote PM postings rose 31% in 2025 (Mind the Product, December 2025).
- **Human vs. quantitative profile:** People/communication: 4/5. Quantitative/technical: 3/5 (4/5 for growth and platform PMs). In practice you must persuade engineers and executives without authority over them, and still be comfortable reading funnel data, writing basic SQL and discussing API or system trade-offs.
- **Compensation (approximate):**
  - *US:* Levels.fyi new-offer total compensation (base plus bonus plus stock), 2025: PM median US$234k (10th-90th percentile US$136k-350k); Senior PM US$381k; Group PM US$480k; Director US$606k (Mind the Product with Levels.fyi, December 2025; skewed to large tech and the Bay Area). All-level medians on Levels.fyi on 9 October 2026: Bay Area US$312.5k (n=1,297), New York US$211k (n=983). Entry PM (0-3 years) at smaller companies is lower; no reliable data found for non-tech corporate PM salaries in the US.
  - *UK:* London median £109,949 (n=350, 25th-75th percentile £84k-147k) on Levels.fyi, 9 October 2026; recruiter 3Search data for 2025 puts "Associate PM / PM" at £50k-80k, Senior PM at £70k-110k, Head of Product £90k-125k, and says Director offers have fallen (Mind the Product, December 2025). Revolut UK PM median £135k (n=59, Levels.fyi, 30 September 2026, in prior Admetia research).
  - *Italy:* Levels.fyi lists an Italian PM median of €51,181 (n=23, so thin), with Milan at €62,727 and a 25th-75th percentile range of €40.5k-70.2k (9 October 2026). Aggregator NettoCalc (Glassdoor-based estimates, 2026) gives Milan median €66,000 gross, junior (0-3 years) €48,000, and Italy-wide median €50,000, junior €36,000; an older Product Heroes/Sec Newgate survey (2023) gave €43,000 average, €35,000 junior. Italian pay is mostly base; equity is rare outside Bending Spoons-style companies.
  - *Elsewhere:* Germany median €95,144 (n=239), Berlin €99,214; France €70,510 (n=54), Paris €72,220; Singapore SGD 150,330 (n=148) (all Levels.fyi, 9 October 2026). Amsterdam, Stockholm, Dubai, Tokyo, Hong Kong, Riyadh: no reliable data found.
  - *Senior:* see the US and UK lines above (Group PM US$480k, Director US$606k; London Head of Product £90-125k at typical employers).
- **Career path:** Typical ladder: Associate PM or PM (0-3 years), PM II / Senior PM (4-7 years), Group PM or Principal PM (7-10 years), Director / VP Product (10+), Chief Product Officer. Many people stall at Senior PM; promotion typically takes 2-3 years per level (practitioner estimate).
- **Exit opportunities:** After 2-3 years: a bigger PM role, founder's-associate/chief of staff roles, growth or strategy roles, or a startup. After 7-10 years: Head of Product or CPO at a smaller company, general manager (GM) roles, founder, venture capital partner or operating partner (see Finance branch), or product consulting/fractional PM (fractional roles are growing, per Mind the Product).
- **Tier list of employers** *(Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact.)*
  - *Tier 1 (highest pay and brand):* Google, Meta, Apple, Amazon, Microsoft, plus frontier AI labs and Stripe-level scale-ups. Meta London PM median £301k and Amazon £147k on Levels.fyi (all levels, 9 October 2026). Hardest to enter; best brand for later moves.
  - *Tier 2 (strong product cultures):* Uber, Airbnb, Spotify, Booking.com, Adyen, Revolut, Atlassian, Salesforce, Bending Spoons, well-funded venture-backed scale-ups. Pay lower than Tier 1 outside the US but more scope.
  - *Tier 3 (corporate / non-tech):* banks, insurers, retailers, telcos, consultancies' product teams. Lower pay, slower cycles, more titles called "product owner".
- **How to enter:** *Degrees:* any degree works in principle; computer science, engineering, data, economics and design have the highest hit rate at tech firms, but business and management graduates do get PM roles, mostly after a first role in operations, analytics or a startup. *Pathways:* APM/RPM programmes (3.2); a lateral move after 1-3 years in an adjacent role; MBA entry (Amazon, Microsoft and others run MBA PM tracks; the experience-requiring MBA route is common in the US); startup first. *Calendar:* APM/RPM applications run from late summer to winter (Meta in late summer/early fall, Google historically in winter, Uber late summer/September); ordinary PM roles are filled year round. *Interview formats* (practitioner consensus): product sense (design or improve a product), execution/metrics (diagnose a metric drop, define success metrics), estimation, behavioural, a technical round for platform/technical PMs (system design lite), and a take-home product case or presentation at startups and scale-ups. *Certifications:* Scrum Product Owner (Scrum.org PSPO or Scrum Alliance CSPO), Pragmatic Institute and Product School certificates are visible in Italian and European corporate job ads, but there is no evidence they matter for big-tech or scale-up hiring; treat them as a low-cost signal for non-tech and consulting roles, not a substitute for shipped work (practitioner consensus; no hard data). *Skills and portfolio:* basic SQL, ability to read experiment results, customer interviewing, clear writing, and demonstrable shipped work: a side project with users, an internal tool, a case study with real metrics. AI-assisted prototyping is increasingly expected (Builder PM postings). *Common mistakes:* applying only to the biggest brands; writing generic "I love products" statements; no evidence of shipping; ignoring SQL/analytics; underestimating how competitive junior roles are (400+ applicants within days; Mind the Product). *Alternative routes:* engineer → PM (strongest at tech firms); consultant → PM (common for B2B and strategy-heavy roles); analyst → PM (data-led companies); internal transfer after joining in operations, support or sales; startup first, where product responsibility arrives early. Entry difficulty: **4/5**.
- **Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for:** You are accountable for outcomes without authority over the people who build them. Junior openings have shrunk; the job is increasingly squeezed between engineering-led AI tooling and flatter organisations. Pay in Europe is far below US levels; Italy is lower again. Not a good fit if you dislike ambiguity and constant negotiation, cannot tolerate being blamed for decisions made by committees, or want deep technical craft or a predictable 9-to-5.

### 3.2 Associate / rotational product manager (entry programmes)

- **What you actually do:** You work as a junior PM inside a structured programme: owning a feature or small product area, running analyses and experiments, writing specs and shadowing a senior PM. At Meta's RPM you complete a four-week product bootcamp and then rotate through three product teams over 18 months (Meta Careers page, October 2026). Uber's APM runs three rotations over two years (Berkeley-hosted posting, secondary); Atlassian's runs two one-year rotations.
- **A typical day and week:** Similar to 3.1 but more supervised: your manager reviews your PRDs, you pull data, write user stories, run weekly metrics checks, and attend programme sessions (talks, mentor meetings) in addition to team work.
- **Hours, stress and lifestyle:** 45-55 hours typical, 60 at peak (launches, rotation reviews). Stress **3/5** (evaluation pressure: you are assessed for conversion to a full PM role). Hybrid; Meta's RPM is based in Menlo Park and New York, Uber's APM in the US (travel to rotations or relocation required); Google's London and Bengaluru APM cohorts have no announced dates.
- **Human vs. quantitative profile:** People/communication: 4/5. Quantitative/technical: 3-4/5. Programmes skew toward technical graduates (Google targets "technically oriented" graduates), so expect questions on data, systems and sometimes coding.
- **Compensation (approximate):** *US:* Levels.fyi new-offer APM total compensation median US$171k (10th-90th percentile US$100k-245k) in 2025 (Mind the Product, December 2025); a third-party guide cites US$160-190k for Uber (unverified). *UK:* the 3Search range for "Associate PM / PM" is £50k-80k in 2025; there is no verified London APM total-comp figure for Google or Meta. *Italy:* no reliable data found; no comparable Italian APM programme exists, and the nearest junior product jobs pay about €35,000-48,000 gross (NettoCalc, 2026, Milan junior €48k, Italy junior €36k; aggregator). At about 5 years APM alumni are typically PM II or Senior PM, so use the 3.1 figures for that stage and above.
- **Career path:** APM/RPM (1-2 years) → PM (after the programme, 1-3 years) → Senior PM (about 4-7 years) → Group PM. Conversion to a full-time role is "considered", not guaranteed (Meta page).
- **Exit opportunities:** After 2-3 years: PM roles at scale-ups, startup head-of-product or founder's-associate roles, MBA. After 7-10 years: same as 3.1, with APM alumni often over-represented among startup founders and CPOs (practitioner view; unverified).
- **Tier list of employers** *(Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact.)*
  - *Tier 1:* Google APM, Meta RPM, Uber APM (very small cohorts, estimated acceptance below 1% by a third-party blog, unverified).
  - *Tier 2:* Atlassian APM, Salesforce APM, Intuit RPM (the latter's 2026 window opened in September 2025), Microsoft, LinkedIn and Adobe early-career PM roles (details not verified), Stripe and Bloomberg rotational programmes named by one guide (unverified).
  - *Tier 3:* corporate/consultancy "digital product" graduate schemes and startup associate PM roles (for example the Certa AI-builder associate PM post).
- **How to enter:** *Backgrounds:* computer science, engineering, math, data and design degrees dominate; business-only graduates are rarely selected unless they show software or analytics projects (prior Admetia research on EMEA APMs, 2026; consistent with Google's "technically oriented" wording). *Calendar:* Meta applications open once a year in late summer/early fall (page says open now, with March and September 2027 starts); Google's windows vary by cohort and include London internships from 5 October to 12 November; Uber's closed on 26 September 2026 (secondary); Atlassian's ANZ round ran in January 2026. *Interviews:* resume plus short questionnaire (Meta), then product sense, execution, behavioural and sometimes technical rounds. *Preparation:* mock product questions, a shipped side project, SQL; an internship in a software team helps. *Alternative routes:* a product internship (many programmes recruit from interns), a startup associate PM role, or analyst → PM. *Certifications:* not relevant for these programmes. *Common mistakes:* applying late, generic product-sense answers, no technical evidence. Entry difficulty: **5/5**.
- **Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for:** Extremely low odds and heavy US concentration; programmes are paused or reshaped when companies cut costs; conversion is uncertain. Not for you if you cannot relocate, have no technical or analytical evidence, or need a guaranteed outcome. For most business graduates this is a lottery ticket; plan on the lateral route.

### 3.3 Technical programme manager and product operations

- **What you actually do:** *TPM:* run large, multi-team engineering programmes: map dependencies, build and track project plans, chase risks, write status reports for executives, run launch readiness reviews and post-incident reviews, and sometimes make architecture trade-off decisions with engineers. *Product ops:* build the machinery around product teams: planning cycles and OKR tracking, feedback and research repositories, launch checklists, analytics dashboards and tooling, vendor management and cross-functional communication. Tools: Jira, Confluence, Looker or Tableau, SQL, spreadsheets, Notion.
- **A typical day and week:** TPM: stand-ups across teams, a risk review, status updates to leadership, chasing owners, and a weekly programme review; product ops: pulling data for a quarterly planning pack, running a feedback review with support and sales, fixing a broken launch process, and training new PMs on tools.
- **Hours, stress and lifestyle:** 45-55 hours typical; peak 60+ during major launches or incidents (TPM). Stress **3/5**, 4/5 during outages or deadline crunches. Hybrid is the norm; TPMs in global firms handle frequent cross-time-zone calls and some travel.
- **Human vs. quantitative profile:** People/communication: 4/5. Quantitative/technical: 3/5 for product ops, 3-4/5 for TPM. In practice TPMs need enough technical literacy to understand system dependencies; product ops needs spreadsheet and SQL fluency and process design skills.
- **Compensation (approximate):** *US (TPM):* Google TPM median total compensation about US$302k in the US (Levels.fyi, updated 30 June 2026; L3 US$182k, up to US$852k at L8) and Amazon about US$290k (Levels.fyi, 7 August 2025); Google L5 ("TPM III") average about US$338k and Amazon L5 about US$214k (averages, not medians; self-reported). *UK (TPM):* London all-level median £121,504 (n=62, 25th-75th percentile £92k-166k; Meta £232k, Google £168k, Amazon £122k median; Levels.fyi, 9 October 2026). *Italy:* no reliable data found (the Levels.fyi TPM sample for Italy is too small). *Product ops:* no robust survey. Levels.fyi's "Operations Product Manager" median is US$191k (updated 9 October 2025), a PM-track proxy; the vendor LaunchNotes quotes base bands of US$85-105k entry, US$105-140k mid, US$140-180k+ senior (unsourced vendor estimates, May 2025). Treat product ops pay as 10-30% below equivalent PM roles (practitioner estimate; unverified).
- **Career path:** TPM → Senior TPM → Principal TPM / Programme Director (about 8-12 years); product ops associate → manager → head of product operations. Many TPMs move to engineering management, PM or operations leadership.
- **Exit opportunities:** After 2-3 years: PM (more common from product ops), engineering programme management, operations roles. After 7-10 years: Director of Programme/Operations, Chief of Staff to a CTO or CPO, VP Operations, general management.
- **Tier list of employers** *(Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact.)*
  - *Tier 1 TPM:* Google, Amazon, Meta, Microsoft, Apple (the highest pay on Levels.fyi). *Tier 2:* Uber, Booking.com, Atlassian, Salesforce, Revolut, large scale-ups. *Tier 3:* consultancies, banks' technology programme offices and IT services firms (programme management titles with delivery focus).
- **How to enter:** *TPM:* usually engineers, QA/SRE or IT project managers; some come from consulting delivery or from agile coaching. Most TPM hires have 3+ years' experience (practitioner consensus). *Product ops:* from analysts, bizops, project coordination or support-ops roles; typically hired at companies with 100+ staff. *Interviews:* programme-management case (plan a complex launch), technical discussion, behavioural. *Certifications:* PMP/PRINCE2 appear in corporate job ads; at tech firms they carry little weight; agile (Scrum Master, SAFe) badges are common in banking and consulting programme offices (practitioner consensus). *Skills:* project planning, SQL/analytics, comfort with technical systems, strong written communication. *Common mistakes:* presenting as a "project coordinator" without technical depth. *Alternative routes:* engineer → TPM; consultant → TPM/product ops; analyst → product ops. Entry difficulty: **3/5** for product ops and non-tech programme roles; **4/5** for big-tech TPM.
- **Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for:** Credit often goes to the teams you coordinate; roles are among the first to be cut in layoffs because they are "overhead"; career ceilings are lower than PM at some firms; product ops has no standard definition and can turn into administration. Not for you if you want ownership of what is built, need clear measurable output, or dislike chasing others for updates.

### 3.4 Startup operator: founder's associate, chief of staff, bizops / strategy & operations

- **What you actually do:** You take work off the founder's or a leader's desk: build the financial model and the monthly dashboard, run the company's quarterly goals, prepare investor updates and board packs, analyse pricing or a new market, run special projects (a first enterprise sales process, an international launch, an acquisition integration), and fill gaps (hiring, contracts, partnerships). Tools: Excel/Google Sheets, SQL, a BI tool, Notion/Slack, presentation software. Deliverables are models, memos, operating rhythms (weekly metrics reviews), and tracked projects.
- **A typical day and week:** A day might start with a metrics check-in with the CEO, then a data pull, then a customer or partner call, then a draft of the board pack; there is no fixed job description, and priorities change weekly. A week includes the leadership meeting, an investor-update draft, one cross-functional project push, and ad hoc "fire drills".
- **Hours, stress and lifestyle:** 50-60 hours typical; peak 70+ before board meetings, fundraises and launches. Stress **4/5** (close to the founder's cash and survival worries; the startup may run out of money). Mostly in-office at early-stage startups.
- **Human vs. quantitative profile:** People/communication: 4/5. Quantitative/technical: 3/5. In practice you must earn trust quickly with founders and employees and also build models and read metrics well enough to be useful.
- **Compensation (approximate):** *US:* no reliable survey found for founder's associate. Product-flavoured chief of staff roles at companies under ~200 staff: US$110-135k (LaunchNotes vendor guide, 2025; unsourced). FounderPath's SaaS salary benchmark (109 salaries, February 2026) shows a median chief-of-staff base around US$116k; its early-stage bottom-quartile (~US$30k) looks like a data error. A seed-stage entry-level figure around US$81k appears on a recruiter site citing an unverified "COS Network 2025 Salary Report". *UK/Europe:* no reliable data found for role titles; for context, a junior business hire at seed in Europe earned €50-70k cash plus options of about 5-15% of salary per year (Index Ventures, 2018 data, stale). LBS MiM graduates entering tech had a mean starting base of about £35-36k in 2024-25, below consulting (LBS MiM employment reports 2024 and 2025, via prior Admetia research). *Italy:* no reliable data found; expect Milan startup junior business roles broadly in line with junior PM pay (€30-45k gross; an inference from the PM figures above, not a sourced figure). *Equity:* Carta reports Series A companies average 16 employees (down from about 25) and equity packages about 50% smaller than in 2022 outside AI (Carta compensation trends 2025, via search summary). A claimed 0.05-0.25% equity for chiefs of staff (attributed to Carta 2025) could not be verified. Value the equity near zero when comparing offers.
- **Career path:** Founder's associate / analyst (0-2 years) → Chief of Staff or Head of Strategy & Ops (2-5) → VP/Head of Operations, General Manager or Head of a function (5-10) → COO or founder. Titles are fluid and speed of progression depends on company survival.
- **Exit opportunities:** After 2-3 years: PM, product ops, strategy roles at bigger tech firms, VC associate roles, consulting (MBB lateral moves at some firms), or a second startup. After 7-10 years: COO/GM, operating partner at a VC fund (see Finance branch), or founder.
- **Tier list of employers** *(Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact.)*
  - *Tier 1:* top-quartile venture-backed companies post-Series B (Stripe, Revolut, Ramp-type companies in the US; Revolut, Mistral, ElevenLabs, Bending Spoons in Europe), and high-profile AI labs (no entry-level business roles found at OpenAI or Anthropic on 30 September 2026; roles ask 4-6+ years).
  - *Tier 2:* seed-to-Series B startups backed by top-tier funds (Index, Atomico, Balderton, Accel, Creandum, Seedcamp), and venture studios such as Antler (investor-brand signals).
  - *Tier 3:* unbacked or small bootstrapped companies, agencies, corporate innovation units.
- **How to enter:** *Backgrounds:* management, economics, finance, data analytics, engineering — any, if you can model and write. *Routes:* warm introductions via investors' talent teams (Index Ventures runs a public portfolio job board); part-time or intern roles at startups while studying; summer internships; Antler- and EF-type programmes. Founders' associate roles are mostly unadvertised (practitioner consensus; unverified); Revolut's Strategy & Operations graduate scheme requires STEM and coding; Bending Spoons hires students year round (company FAQ, 2026). *Calendar:* year round, tied to funding events, not graduation cycles. *Interviews:* case or take-home analysis (build a model, size a market), a founder conversation, and a work trial. *Certifications:* none matter. *Skills and portfolio:* a financial model of a real business, a dashboard built on public data, a market analysis, shipped side projects, SQL; strong writing. *Common mistakes:* joining a weak team for the title, overvaluing equity, taking a chief-of-staff title with no real scope, ignoring the runway. *Alternative routes:* analyst or consultant first; internal transfer; BizOps at a scale-up. Entry difficulty: **3/5**.
- **Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for:** Pay is below consulting or banking at entry in Europe; equity is usually worth little; the company may fail and your job with it; the role is vague and depends on one person; you may do a lot of low-prestige work. Not for you if you need job security, structured training or clear boundaries, or if you want a recognised credential rather than a learning-by-doing path.

### 3.5 Founder / entrepreneur

- **What you actually do:** You find a problem, build and sell a first version, hire and fund the company. Early on, that means talking to 50+ potential customers, building a prototype (often with AI tools), selling to the first users, setting up a legal entity and cap table (list of who owns what), and fundraising. Later: hiring, managing cash and runway, product decisions, board and investor communication. Tools: spreadsheets for cash and metrics, a CRM, analytics, whichever product-building tools your team uses.
- **A typical day and week:** There is no typical day: customer calls, building or reviewing product, recruiting, investor meetings, and fire-fighting. A week at seed stage might include two customer calls a day, a hiring interview, a fundraising pitch, and time to build; investor updates and cash checks come monthly.
- **Hours, stress and lifestyle:** 60-80 hours a week is common and peak weeks higher; stress **5/5** when runway is short or a fundraise fails (the scale's own example of a founder close to running out of cash), 3-4/5 in stable periods. Work and life blend; investors and talent cluster in hubs.
- **Human vs. quantitative profile:** People/communication: 5/5. Quantitative/technical: 3/5 (4/5 for technical founders building the product). In practice you sell, recruit and persuade constantly, and also have to understand your numbers and, in software, your technology.
- **Compensation (approximate):** Founder pay is usually low until a financing. Kruze Consulting's payroll data (reported secondarily, 2026) puts median startup CEO salary at US$147k at seed in 2025 and US$153k in its 2026 benchmark; US$203k at Series A; US$216k at Series B. A much lower Pilot survey of 1,844 founders reported an average of US$75k, down 43% (2025; includes bootstrapped founders). For Europe, Creandum's 2025 data cites a median CEO salary near €115k, though the stage is unclear. *US/UK/Italy at the earliest stage:* often zero or a living stipend; EF pays £6,000 (London, equity-free) to join; YC invests US$500k per company but does not pay a salary. *Honest outcome distribution:* most seed-funded companies do not raise a Series A (Carta: about 10% within 12 months, another 15.6% within 12-24 months; Carta's analysis found graduation rates between stages barely improving, February-March 2026, secondary); startup shutdowns are rising (Carta 966 in 2024, +25.6%); returns to founders are extremely concentrated in a handful of companies. *Italy:* founders raise less and exits are scarcer (Growth Capital observatory, 2026). For a typical student founder, expected cash income in the first three years is below an employed graduate's.
- **Career path:** Idea/prototype (months) → pre-seed/accelerator (6-12 months) → seed (1-2 years) → Series A (about 2-3 years; Carta's median seed-to-Series A interval was 616 days in Q2 2025) → scale or shut down. Most founders' first company does not reach Series A; many go back into employment with strong operator credentials.
- **Exit opportunities:** After 2-3 years (company folded or sold): senior PM or product/strategy roles, chief of staff, founder's associate, VC platform or associate roles, "acqui-hire" employment at a larger firm (the buyer hires the team, not the product), or a second venture. After 7-10 years: continued CEO, operating partner or investor roles, serial founder, or angel investing.
- **Tier list of employers** *(Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact; for founders: accelerators and investors.)*
  - *Tier 1 accelerators/talent investors:* Y Combinator (US$500k for 7% plus MFN SAFE), Entrepreneur First (£6k talent investment then US$125k for 8%; majority-technical cohorts; takes few non-technical people, per EF FAQ, 2025-26).
  - *Tier 2:* Techstars (US$220k, reported), Antler, Station F-type campus programmes and top regional programmes such as Hub71 (Abu Dhabi) and CDP Venture Capital's Italian accelerator network (17 hubs counted in 2024), plus Seedcamp (Europe; from general knowledge, not verified here).
  - *Tier 3:* university incubators, corporate accelerators, local/regional programmes and bootstrapped paths.
  - *Investors (seed/Series A, perception):* in Europe Atomico, Index, Balderton, Accel, Creandum and Speedinvest are widely cited; in Italy CDP Venture Capital, United Ventures and P101 are widely named (not verified for current status).
- **How to enter:** *How first-time founders realistically start:* (1) work on a problem you know from a job or university; (2) find a co-founder (EF says 80% of participants find one within 8 weeks, employer-stated); (3) build the smallest working version, increasingly with AI tools, and get ten paying or committed users before raising; (4) apply to an accelerator or angels only with traction; (5) keep personal runway for 12-18 months. *Backgrounds:* computer science, data science and AI graduates have the easiest route (EF explicitly prefers technical founders); business graduates should pair with a technical co-founder. *Calendar:* YC runs batches throughout the year (Fall 2026 applications closed 27 July); Techstars and EF cohorts have set dates; no gatekeepers for bootstrapping. *Interviews:* founder pitches and investor interviews, not exam-style. *Visas:* see 2.6 (UK Innovator Founder endorsement £1,000 and fee £1,357; Italia Startup Visa with €50,000 funds; Singapore EntrePass; Japan Startup Visa; UAE and Saudi programmes). *Certifications:* none matter. *Skills and portfolio:* shipped product, customer discovery notes, early revenue, fluent writing and selling. *Common mistakes:* building without customer contact; raising too early; giving away equity in unequal splits or without vesting; running out of runway; accepting terms without legal advice. *Alternative routes:* join an early-stage startup first (3.4), a venture studio, or a corporate innovation unit. Entry difficulty: **2/5** to start a company (no gatekeeper) but **5/5** to reach YC-level funding or a funded outcome.
- **Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for:** Most companies fail; you may earn less than a graduate job for years, carry personal financial risk, and lose co-founder friendships; stress can be extreme. Dilution means even successful founders can own a small percentage. Not for you if you need income stability or have large debts, dislike selling, or want work-life balance in the first years.

## 4. Banks vs. other employer types

Banks do not employ this branch's roles in large numbers, so this section compares where product and operator roles sit: big tech, scale-ups, early-stage startups, bank/corporate digital units, consultancies/agencies, venture studios, and the founder path. Ratings are practitioner-consensus judgements except where figures are cited.

| Employer type | Hours / week | Stress (1-5) | Pay (cash vs equity) | Culture | Autonomy | Job security |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Big tech (Google, Meta, Amazon, Microsoft) | 45-55; peaks 60 | 3 | Highest: US PM median US$234k total, much of it stock (Levels.fyi 2025); London Meta PM median £301k vs Amazon £147k (Levels.fyi, 2026) | Process-heavy, metrics-driven, return-to-office tightened | Medium (strong frameworks, many stakeholders) | Medium (repeated layoffs: US tech layoffs at least 95,667 in 2024 and 127,000 in 2025 per Crunchbase; layers being cut) |
| Scale-up (Revolut, Stripe, Spotify, Uber, Booking.com, Bending Spoons) | 50-60; peaks 70 | 4 | Good cash; equity meaningful but illiquid; Revolut UK PM median £135k; Bending Spoons pays "at the high end" for engineers and puts equity in your own hands | Fast, demanding, ownership-heavy; Bending Spoons calls itself "demanding" with a steep learning curve | High | Medium |
| Early-stage startup (seed/Series A) | 55-65; peaks 80 | 4-5 | Lower cash (Europe seed junior business €50-70k in 2018); options 5-15% of salary per year of grant (Index 2018, stale); value near zero | Informal, mission-driven, chaotic | Very high | Low (company may fail; Carta: most seed startups never reach Series A) |
| Bank / corporate digital unit | 40-50; peaks 55 | 2-3 | Moderate base; bonus; little or no equity; London JPMorgan PM median £101k (Levels.fyi, 2026) | Hierarchical, risk and compliance, slower | Low-medium | High |
| Consultancy / agency product teams | 50-60; peaks 70 | 4 | Mid pay, bonus, no equity; varies | Client-driven, project-based | Medium; client dictates scope | Medium |
| Venture studio / startup generator | 50-60 | 4 | Moderate salary plus carried equity in portfolio companies; Antler/EF pay stipends | Entrepreneurial, cohort-based | High | Low-medium (programme-dependent) |
| Founder path | 60-80 | 5 | Low cash, very large variance; seed CEO ~US$147-153k median among funded companies (Kruze) but many unpaid; equity can be worth zero or very large | Entirely your own | Total | None |

**Commentary.**

- **Cash vs equity and how to value startup equity honestly.** An option is the right to buy shares at a fixed price; it only has value if the company's share price later exceeds that price and there is a way to sell. To value it: take the percentage you are offered, multiply by a realistic exit value, apply dilution from future rounds (expect to lose roughly 20% of your stake per major financing round, a rule of thumb), and then multiply by a low probability of success. Investors' preferences mean common shares (what employees hold) are paid after investors. In practice the sums are small: junior business hires at seed were granted options worth 5-15% of salary a year at the time of Index's 2018 benchmark (stale), and Carta reports equity grants about half of 2022 levels outside AI. Check also the exercise window after leaving (many European startups block leavers from exercising until an exit) and taxes, which vary by country. In Italy, innovative startups benefit from favourable treatment of equity compensation under the Startup Act; confirm the current rules with an adviser (not verified in this research). Compare offers on cash, learning and company quality.
- **Hours, stress and security track company stage more than function:** the same PM job is 3/5 at a mature firm and 4-5/5 where the product is the company; security is lowest at early-stage startups and highest at banks/corporates.
- **Regional differences.**
  - *US vs Europe:* US PM median total comp of US$234k (Levels.fyi, 2025) versus Germany €95k, London £110k, Paris €72k and Italy €51k (Levels.fyi, 9 October 2026; all levels; self-reported). Europe's funding is also much smaller: US private tech investment reached US$177bn in the first nine months of 2025 versus an expected US$44bn for Europe over the whole year; the median European seed round is about half the US one (Atomico).
  - *Italy:* a small ecosystem (€1.735bn VC in 2025, nine new funds closing a combined €545m), thin late-stage funding and few exits, with salaries well below London or Berlin; Bending Spoons is the standout employer; Milan is the main hub, with CDP Venture Capital's accelerator network spread over several cities.
  - *London, Paris, Berlin:* London has the deepest talent pool (UK startups raised US$14.4bn in 2025, Germany US$7.4bn, France US$6.1bn per Atomico); Berlin has the highest European PM pay in the Levels.fyi sample (€99k median); Paris is lower on pay (€72k) but has a strong state-backed startup ecosystem (Station F; general knowledge).
  - *Gulf:* Abu Dhabi (Hub71) and Dubai offer incentive packages and Golden Visa routes; Saudi Arabia (Riyadh) led MENA funding in 2025 but with large debt components, and MENA funding fell 18% in H1 2026 to US$1.7bn (Wamda, July 2026).
  - *Singapore:* PM median SGD 150k (Levels.fyi); startup funding reportedly fell 34% in 2025 (low-quality aggregator sources; unverified); the EntrePass visa supports funded or technology-based founders.
  - *Tokyo, Hong Kong:* no reliable data found beyond Japan's Startup Visa (2.6).
