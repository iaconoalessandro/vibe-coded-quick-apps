## 1. What this branch is

Management consulting means selling expert advice and project teams to organisations that want to solve a business problem: where to grow, how to cut costs, how to merge two companies, how to roll out a new system or use AI. A client (a company, a government, an investor) hires a firm for a project of a few weeks to a year, and a small team of consultants works on it, usually at or near the client's offices. Firms make money by charging for time or for fixed project fees, and they hire many junior people to do the analysis under a few senior people who sell the work (the "leverage pyramid"). The branch runs from elite strategy firms (McKinsey, BCG, Bain) through the consulting arms of Deloitte, PwC, EY, KPMG and Accenture, to specialist boutiques and in-house "internal consulting" teams at large companies. Since 2023 the industry has been in a slowdown after the 2021-22 boom, and AI is changing what junior consultants do, so entry is still prestigious but less certain than it looked five years ago.

**Scope notes:** Global view with explicit US, UK, continental Europe (Italy always called out), Gulf and Asia comparisons; "entry" means a pre-experience graduate (bachelor's or master's, no MBA); economic consulting, audit, deal advisory, IT/system-integration engineering and data-science consulting sit in other branches and are only cross-referenced; Italian entry pay for MBB and tier-2 firms could not be verified from any reliable source, so it is marked "no reliable data found" rather than estimated; Asia (Hong Kong, Tokyo) data is thin and flagged as such.

## 2. Map of areas and sectors

### How the industry makes money (applies to every sub-area)

- **Project fees and day rates.** A client pays either a fixed fee for a defined scope (common at strategy firms; a typical top-tier team of 4-6 people is billed at a weekly fee in the hundreds of thousands of dollars or euros, but firms do not publish rates, so no reliable data on exact fees) or a rate per person-day (common at Big 4, Accenture and implementation work). Public-sector contracts are often tendered at a fixed day-rate card.
- **The leverage pyramid.** One partner sells the work, a few managers run it, and many analysts and consultants do the analysis. Profit comes from "leverage": the more junior hours sold per senior hour, the higher the profit per partner. "Utilization" is the share of a consultant's working time billed to clients; time between projects is "the bench".
- **Up or out.** Strategy firms (and some others) expect people to be promoted on a schedule; those who are not are "counselled out" (told to find another job, often with help). This keeps the pyramid shaped and supplies a steady flow of alumni into industry.
- **Engagement.** The consulting word for a client project, run by a "case team".

### The 2023-2026 slowdown, with dated data

- **Peak and fall.** UK consulting grew 23% in 2022 at its peak; member revenues of the Management Consultancies Association (MCA, the UK trade body) then rose only 3% in 2025 to about £21.8 billion, against an earlier forecast of 11% growth. The MCA's firms forecast 6% for 2026 and 8% for 2027 (Consultancy.uk, citing the MCA/Oxford Economics report, 2026; City AM, 2026).
- **MBB (McKinsey, BCG, Bain).** McKinsey's headcount fell from about 45,100 (end-2023) to about 40,000 (May 2025) (Fortune, 29 May 2025, as summarised in earlier project research). The firm said in September 2025 it would grow its North American workforce by 12% in 2026 and in December 2025 was reported to be cutting about 10% of non-client-facing roles over 18-24 months (Storyboard18 citing Reuters/WSJ, Sept 2025; People Matters citing Bloomberg, Dec 2025). BCG reported 2025 revenue of $14.4 billion (+7%) and about 33,500 staff (BCG press release, 23 April 2026). Management Consulted, a coaching firm that collects offer data from clients, reports US MBB starting salaries flat for a third year running for 2025 offers (Poets&Quants, 26 January 2026). Starting-salary freezes are a signal that firms have more junior supply than they need.
- **Big 4.** Global revenue growth in the latest fiscal years was modest: Deloitte $70.5bn in FY2025 (+4.8% local currency), PwC $56.9bn (+2.7%), EY $53.2bn (+4%) (CPA Practice Advisor, Oct 2025). Graduate intake was cut: a leaked PwC US slide showed junior hiring falling from 3,242 to a projected 2,197 by fiscal 2028 (about -32%; PwC did not confirm the numbers; Entrepreneur, 2025); PwC UK cut its graduate scheme from 1,500 to 1,300 (Consultancy.uk, 12 Sept 2025); the press reported UK Big 4 graduate cuts at KPMG (1,399 to 942), Deloitte (1,700 to 1,400) and EY (1,800 to 1,600) between 2023 and 2024 (Scottish Financial News/Telegraph, June 2025; press-reported, not firm-published).
- **Accenture.** FY2026 (ended 31 Aug 2026) revenue was $74.2 billion (+6% in USD, +5% local currency), bookings $84.5 billion; Q4 consulting revenue was $9.28 billion (Accenture results, 1 Oct 2026). Earlier, it cut 11,419 staff in one quarter in 2025 under an $865 million restructuring, taking headcount to about 779,000, and its CEO said it would exit people "where reskilling is not a viable path" (People Matters, 2025).
- **AI's effect on junior work.** The routine tasks AI absorbs fastest (desk research, first-draft slides, data clean-up, meeting notes) are the tasks that traditionally trained first-year analysts. Evidence so far: starting pay frozen at the top firms (Management Consulted, 2026); Big 4 graduate intake cut; McKinsey piloting case interviews where candidates must use its internal AI tool and be judged on how well they check its output (CFO.com citing the Financial Times, 14 Jan 2026). Counter-evidence: McKinsey and BCG both say client-facing hiring continues and BCG says AI and tech work is over 40% of its revenue (BCG, April 2026); 78% of UK consultancies expect AI and digital demand to drive 2026 growth (MCA survey, Management Today, 2026). Honest summary: nobody knows whether the pyramid becomes a diamond; the likeliest outcome is smaller generalist intakes, more technical hires, and an AI-fluency filter on everyone. Two Big 4 executives were reported to expect UK graduate recruitment across major firms to fall by roughly half in a year (secondary reporting, late 2025; unverified).
- **Overall labour-market view.** The US Bureau of Labor Statistics counts 1,077,100 management analysts (its category for consultants) in 2025, median pay $101,860 (May 2025), and projects 10% growth 2025-2035, "much faster than average" (BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook, accessed October 2026). BLS projections tend to lag firm-level slowdowns and cover all consultants, not only elite-firm graduates.

### 2.1 Strategy consulting: MBB and tier-2

- **What it is.** Advice to boards and CEOs on high-stakes decisions: which markets to enter, whether to acquire a company, how to restructure a portfolio, how to set pricing, how to respond to AI. Work is project-based, short (6-16 weeks typical) and heavily analytical.
- **Clients.** Large corporates (about 60-70% of work at most firms; unverified, firm mix varies), private-equity funds (commercial due diligence: testing a target company's market in 3-6 weeks), governments and sovereign funds, and non-profits.
- **MBB.** McKinsey, BCG and Bain: the three largest and most selective. They are generalist at entry and specialise later by industry or function. A fourth tier-1-adjacent group is sometimes added in practice: Oliver Wyman (financial services and risk), and in Europe Roland Berger.
- **Tier-2 strategy.** Kearney (operations and procurement), Oliver Wyman, Roland Berger (DACH, automotive, restructuring), Strategy& (PwC's strategy arm), EY-Parthenon and Monitor Deloitte (Big 4 strategy arms, strong in PE due diligence), L.E.K. Consulting (healthcare, PE), OC&C (consumer, retail, PE), Simon-Kucher (pricing), Arthur D. Little, Alvarez & Marsal and AlixPartners (turnaround, performance improvement; closer to "restructuring").
- **How they differ in practice.** MBB bill more per person, recruit mostly from target schools, and have the strongest exit brand. Tier-2 firms often pay close to MBB in some countries (Germany: Kearney reported at a base similar to MBB by PrepLounge, Dec 2025, single aggregator) and often have better work-life balance claims, though hours are not clearly lower (see section 3.1).

### 2.2 Big 4 consulting and Accenture

- **What it is.** The consulting, "advisory" or "transformation" arms of Deloitte, PwC, EY and KPMG, plus Accenture (a pure consulting-and-technology company, not an audit firm). Work spans operations, finance and HR transformation, technology and ERP implementation, risk and regulatory compliance, cybersecurity, people and change management, and public-sector programmes. A smaller strategy layer (Monitor Deloitte, Strategy&, EY-Parthenon, KPMG's strategy group) overlaps with 2.1.
- **Clients.** Large corporates, banks and insurers, governments and public bodies, health systems. Often long engagements (months to years) and multi-team programmes.
- **Economics.** Day-rate or time-and-materials billing, higher utilisation targets than MBB, large offshore delivery centres, and a wide pyramid. Deloitte and KPMG earn the largest share of revenue from advisory; PwC and EY from assurance (CPA Practice Advisor, 2025).
- **Cross-reference.** Big 4 deal advisory/transaction services is in the Finance branch; Big 4 audit in Accounting; the engineering side of system integrators in Computer Science.

### 2.3 Specialist and boutique consulting

- **What it is.** Firms that dominate a niche: healthcare and life sciences (L.E.K., ZS Associates, ClearView Healthcare Partners, Trinity-type pharma specialists), pricing and revenue management (Simon-Kucher), network and telecom/media (Altman Solon), consumer/retail strategy (OC&C), sustainability and energy-transition advisory, operations and supply chain, restructuring and turnaround (A&M, AlixPartners, FTI Consulting).
- **Italian examples that could be verified.** *BIP* (Business Integration Partners), founded in Milan in 2003, says it has 4,000+ staff in Italy, 11 Italian offices and 5,000+ people in 13 countries (BIP Italy page, accessed 2026; Management Consulted profile); it sits between management consulting and technology implementation. *Value Partners*, founded in Milan in 1993 (PitchBook), is a management consultancy; I could not verify its current size or revenue (a registry figure of about €5-6 million refers to one legal entity and does not show the group). *The European House - Ambrosetti (TEHA)* is a Milan-area think-tank and advisory firm in the 201-500 staff band (RevenueBase, 2026; data-vendor band, headquarters not confirmed by that source). No 2025 revenues for any of the three were found. Other Italian firms exist (for example Reply, which is closer to IT consulting) but were not verified here.
- **How they hire.** Smaller graduate classes, often year-round, less standardised processes than MBB.

### 2.4 Operations and implementation consulting

- **What it is.** Making a change actually happen: redesigning a factory floor or warehouse network, procurement savings, cost-out programmes, post-merger integration, setting up a transformation office that tracks every initiative. Includes lean/Six Sigma-style improvement.
- **Clients.** Manufacturers, consumer goods, logistics, utilities, PE-owned companies that need margin improvement fast, and governments.
- **Who does it.** Kearney, McKinsey Operations, Bain performance improvement, Alvarez & Marsal and AlixPartners (PE performance improvement), Big 4 operations practices, Accenture Operations, and many boutiques. It is the most "hands-on" part of consulting and often involves staying on site for months.

### 2.5 In-house/internal strategy and transformation teams

- **What it is.** A company's own team of strategists and project leaders: corporate strategy, "strategy and transformation office", internal consulting arms (some large banks, industrials, pharma and tech companies run teams that staff projects like a consulting firm), M&A and corporate development.
- **Who hires.** Large corporates; many hire mainly ex-consultants. Direct graduate entry exists mainly through rotational/leadership development programmes; pure strategy roles usually need 2-5 years of experience.
- **How the economics differ.** No client billing; the "client" is another business unit. Lower pay at entry than elite consulting, but steadier hours and a path to general management.

### 2.6 Public-sector consulting

- **What it is.** Advising governments, regulators, EU institutions and state-owned entities on policy design, digital government, health-system reform, and delivery of large funded programmes.
- **Clients and drivers.**
  - *Italy and the EU:* the National Recovery and Resilience Plan (PNRR) has fuelled consulting for public administrations. Assoconsult (Italy's consulting association) estimated the market at about €6.6 billion in 2023 and said 2024 could exceed €7 billion, with public-sector demand driven by the PNRR (Il Sole 24 Ore, 2024-25; definitions vary: Mordor Intelligence puts Italy at about US$7.8 billion in 2025). Public procurement is politically sensitive: Italy's anti-corruption authority ANAC reported direct awards for services below tender thresholds rising from 1,549 in 2021 to 13,879 in 2025 (ANAC annual data via Teleborsa, April 2026), and McKinsey's Italian public-sector work has been a recurring press controversy.
  - *Gulf national visions:* Saudi Arabia's Vision 2030 and the UAE's programmes have made the Gulf the fastest-growing consulting region. The Gulf consulting market was projected at about $8.3 billion in 2025, +12% (Consultancy-me, May 2025, based on Source Global Research); Saudi consulting revenue grew 14.1% to $4.3 billion in 2024 (same source). But there are signs of cooling: reports of the Public Investment Fund (PIF) reviewing spend and, according to Bloomberg/FT coverage cited by trade sources, banning PwC from new PIF advisory work for a year (secondary reporting; unverified for exact terms). Saudi government entities reportedly only award contracts to firms with a Riyadh regional headquarters since January 2024 (secondary; unverified).
  - *Local-hire rules:* Saudi Arabia required 35% Saudi staffing in consulting roles from March 2023, rising to 40% in the second phase from 2024 (Consultancy-me, 2023-24); the UAE's Emiratisation targets require companies with 50+ employees to reach 10% Emiratis by end-2026 (Middle East Briefing, 2026). For expatriate graduates this means fewer entry slots and more competition than headline growth suggests.
- **Who does it.** Big 4, Accenture, McKinsey/BCG/Bain public-sector practices, Italian and European firms, and many specialist policy consultancies.

## 3. Role families

### 3.1 Strategy consultant (MBB and tier-2), analyst/associate to partner

**What you actually do**
You answer a business question in a few weeks. A typical project: a consumer-goods client asks whether to enter a new country. You build a market-sizing model in Excel, run 10-15 interviews with industry experts and customers, benchmark competitors, pull data from client systems and paid databases, and draft the steering-committee deck (often 20-40 slides) that your manager rewrites with you. Tools: Excel (heavily), PowerPoint (very heavily), Alteryx or Python at some firms, internal knowledge databases, and now firm AI tools (McKinsey's Lilli, BCG's GENE/Deckster) to summarise documents and draft slides. Deliverables: a model, a storyline, a final recommendation, sometimes an implementation roadmap. At MBB you are often "staffed" on your own module within the case, presenting results to the client within weeks.

**A typical day and week**
Hours 8-9 am: team check-in; morning: refine one analysis; midday: expert call or client working session; afternoon: build slides; late afternoon: problem-solving session with your manager ("what does this say?"); evening: finish the draft for the partner. In the traditional US/UK model the week is Monday to Thursday at the client's site and Friday in the home office; the travel day (usually Thursday evening) is a de facto lost evening. Post-2020 many teams are hybrid and total hours have not fallen much (StrategyCase, updated July 2026; a coaching site). Roland Berger states that consultants usually spend four days on-site and Friday in the office, with over 75% of its work cross-border (Roland Berger FAQ, 2026).

**Hours, stress and lifestyle**
- *Hours:* typical 55-70 per week at MBB; peaks of 75-85 before key client meetings or in due-diligence sprints (StrategyCase, July 2026; PrepLounge coaching forum answers, 2025; no firm discloses hours; an old BCG study of North American staff found 92% worked 50+ hours and the average week fell from 64.5 to 57.7 hours after "predictable time off" rules, HBR/Wharton, 2009-12, stale). UK ONS paid-hours data shows only 38 hours because salaried overtime is unpaid, so ignore it as evidence on hours (earlier project research).
- *Stress:* **4/5.** Frequent high-stakes client pressure, constant evaluation, and an up-or-out ladder.
- *Lifestyle/flexibility:* low control over schedule between projects; partner and client demands dominate; first project assignment is mostly out of your hands. Parental leave and part-time policies exist at MBB but are used by a minority.
- *Travel:* high in the US and UK (weekly flights), often cross-border within Europe; Gulf and Asia teams often fly in for the week or are based locally. Hybrid has softened but not removed the four-day on-site norm.
- *Remote/hybrid:* partial; most client work involves some on-site days.

**Human vs. quantitative profile**
People/communication: 4/5. Quantitative/technical: 3/5. You need to be good with spreadsheets and structured problem-solving, but the differentiator is communicating a clear answer to senior people under time pressure; advanced maths is rare outside specialised teams.

**Compensation (approximate)**
Entry (undergraduate/non-MBA master's):
- *US:* MBB base $110-112k (BCG $110k; McKinsey and Bain $112k) for 2025 offers; total cash with maximum bonus and signing about $137-140k (Management Consulted via Poets&Quants for Undergrads, Jan 2026). PrepLounge's US guide gives about $130-134.5k total (Jan 2026).
- *UK:* McKinsey £50,000 base (up to ~£60k total), BCG £49,500 (up to £60k), Bain £52,000 (up to £63k) (PrepLounge UK, updated 2026). Levels.fyi self-reports London McKinsey Business Analyst base about £55.3k (30 Sept 2026; self-reported). The two upstream data sets may overlap.
- *Germany/DACH (comparison):* McKinsey about €80k base, BCG and Bain about €75k (PrepLounge DACH, Dec 2025); a master's earns a higher entry grade than a bachelor's at McKinsey Germany (e-fellows.net McKinsey FAQ, 2026).
- *Italy:* **no reliable data found** for MBB or tier-2 graduate pay in Milan or Rome. Only scattered points: Oliver Wyman Italy about €43k base for undergraduate/master's level (Management Consulted 2026, single source, as noted in earlier research); a levels.fyi Milan listing showing Bain at an average of about €100k total across all levels (not entry). One PrepLounge forum user says Zurich pays "almost double" Milan (Aug 2025, anecdotal).
- *Middle East:* Dubai: McKinsey Business Analyst AED 255k base / AED 276k total, tax-free (levels.fyi, updated Sept 2026; self-reported); a coach estimates AED 20-30k per month for entry (PrepLounge forum, anecdotal). Riyadh: only post-MBA data found (BCG $304k, Bain $266k, McKinsey $242k total; PrepLounge Saudi 2025).
- *Asia:* Singapore MBB fresh graduates S$90-120k total (Skillup, 2026, aggregator); Hong Kong: no entry-level data found (Road to Offer gives MBA-level base HKD 1.2-1.8m); Tokyo: a Japanese career site says McKinsey Japan new graduates start at roughly ¥5-7m (unverified, third-party).

~5 years (consultant to engagement manager/project leader):
- *US:* total roughly $250-400k for years 2-4 and $350-550k for the manager level (4-6 years) at MBB; Tier 2 about $200-350k and $280-450k (Road to Offer, 2026, a US aggregator; wide ranges).
- *UK:* experienced consultant £65-100k and senior consultant/manager £92-125k across firms (PrepLounge UK, 2026, not firm-specific); MBA/PhD hires at MBB £100-110k base, up to £117-125k total.
- *Italy:* no reliable data found.

Senior (partner): MBB partners are widely said to earn well over $1 million a year (US coaching sources cite $1-5m+); PrepLounge UK gives £200k to £1m+ across all UK firms. Partner pay depends on firm profit; firms do not publish it, so treat as unverified.

**Career path**
Entry titles differ: McKinsey Business Analyst, BCG Associate, Bain Associate Consultant; then Associate/Consultant (often after an MBA or 2-3 years) → Engagement Manager/Project Leader/Case Team Leader (4-7 years) → Associate Partner/Principal → Partner/Director. Expect roughly 2 years per step at the junior levels; promotion is competitive at every step. "Up or out" means people not promoted are typically asked to leave within 1-3 years of the expected promotion date. Most undergraduates do not stay to partner (a small minority, often well under 10%, is a common practitioner estimate; no reliable data found). Many firms sponsor an MBA: you leave for 2 years paid in part and return as a post-MBA Associate/Consultant, usually with an obligation to return; McKinsey Germany has historically offered paid leave for the MBA (e-fellows.net, undated, older). Some firms run a "pre-MBA" analyst programme that ends with the MBA.

**Exit opportunities**
- *After 2-3 years:* MBA (top-10 schools; often sponsored), corporate strategy and business-development roles, private equity associate roles (especially after tier-2 PE-diligence work), tech product/strategy and operations, startups and venture-backed scale-ups.
- *After 7-10 years:* corporate strategy director/VP or business-unit general manager, chief of staff/CSO/COO roles, private-equity operating partner, start-up COO/CEO, founder roles. A coaching site estimates 15-20% of MBB consultants take corporate strategy jobs between years 3 and 8, and calls PE the largest exit by volume (StrategyCase, May 2026; single coach source, unsourced).

**Tier list of employers**
*Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact.*
- **Tier 1 (MBB):** McKinsey, BCG, Bain. Highest selectivity, highest pay at entry, broadest alumni network.
- **Tier 1.5/elite specialists:** Oliver Wyman (financial services), Roland Berger (Europe/DACH), Kearney (operations), L.E.K. and OC&C (healthcare/consumer, PE diligence), Simon-Kucher (pricing). Sometimes described as "MBB-adjacent"; pay near MBB in some countries.
- **Tier 2:** Strategy&, EY-Parthenon, Monitor Deloitte, Arthur D. Little, Alvarez & Marsal, AlixPartners, Altman Solon, Capgemini Invent, Accenture Strategy; Big 4 strategy arms sell on the parent's relationships and are heavily used by PE.
- **Tier 3:** Smaller regional strategy boutiques and corporate-strategy arms of IT firms.
- **Differences:** rank follows client prestige, selectivity, alumni destinations and pay, not size.

**How to enter**
- *Degrees/backgrounds:* business, economics, STEM, law, humanities; MBB say they hire from many fields. Strongest evidence is academic distinction plus leadership and quantitative aptitude. In Germany/Austria, McKinsey says fluent German is mandatory (e-fellows.net interview with McKinsey recruiter, 1 Aug 2025), and BCG's round 2 includes a case in German (BCG Germany/Austria, 2026). Roland Berger asks for the local language in every office.
- *Internships/programmes:* summer/spring internships are the main funnel at MBB. BCG's Visiting Associate (8-12 weeks) leads to a binding "FAST FORWARD" offer without further interviews (BCG Germany/Austria careers page, 2026). McKinsey Germany's Fellow Intern is 8-12 weeks (e-fellows.net, 2026). Bain London has no internship programme (Road to Offer UK guide, 2026).
- *Recruiting calendar (2026 cycle; verify on firm sites):*
  - **US:** McKinsey Business Analyst internship deadline 29 March 2026, full-time (undergrad/master's) 11 August 2026; Bain internship 29 March (spring) and 31 August (fall), full-time Associate Consultant 19 July 2026; BCG full-time around 7-14 July 2026 and internships June-September (sources disagree; Tufts/Management Consulted/Road to Offer 2026). Over 60% of interview slots reportedly fill in the first application wave (one analysis; unverified).
  - **UK:** McKinsey portal opened 1 Jan 2026 with a first-round deadline of 29 March, expected to reopen autumn 2026; Bain London reopens in September with first-round cases mid-November (Road to Offer UK, 2026). Rolling review: apply in the first weeks.
  - **DACH:** BCG wants applications at least six months before the start date; McKinsey reviews Fellow Intern applications on a rolling basis (e-fellows.net / BCG, 2026).
  - **Italy:** I could not verify a published calendar for MBB Milan or Rome; check each firm's Italian careers page and university career services.
  - **Gulf/Asia:** campus calendars vary; Singapore and Hong Kong recruit mostly from local and global target universities; no reliable calendar found.
- *Interview formats:* a 20-30 minute online assessment first (McKinsey Solve, BCG's online case/"Casey", Bain's online test), then two or three rounds of case interviews plus a "fit" interview. McKinsey's Solve is a game-style assessment; in 2026 candidate reports describe Redrock Study plus Sea Wolf (about 65 minutes) as the common version, with a longer variant (Management Consulted, Road to Offer and Leland, 2026; third-party descriptions, check your invitation). McKinsey interviews combine a case with a Personal Experience Interview (PEI: a deep behavioural question on leadership, connection, growth and drive; renamed dimensions, summer 2025, third-party). BCG's global process is application → skills (behavioural) interview → case interview → team interview (BCG careers, 2026). Bain describes a recruiter call and several case-interview rounds with objective scoring (Bain, 2026). McKinsey has piloted AI-assisted case interviews (CFO.com citing the FT, Jan 2026).
- *Certifications:* none matter at strategy firms. Language certificates matter in DACH and sometimes Italy.
- *Skills to build:* structured problem-solving (case practice, 30-50 cases is a common recommendation; coaching-industry advice), mental maths, clear writing, Excel; the ability to use AI tools and sanity-check what they say.
- *Common mistakes:* memorising frameworks instead of reasoning; no concrete leadership examples; applying late; underestimating the German requirement; treating internships as optional in DACH.
- *Alternative routes:* non-target schools should use referrals and alumni networking and a master's at a target school; a non-target brand mostly hurts at CV screening, less after that (coaching sources, unverified). Big 4 strategy arms (Monitor Deloitte, Strategy&, EY-Parthenon) are the most common lateral feeders: one coaching firm found over 39% of MBB experienced hires came from other consulting firms (unverified; coaching firm). PhD and "advanced degree" hiring is a separate channel: PhDs typically enter at post-MBA level, up to two years after graduating (coaching sources; check firms). MBA (full-time at a top school) remains the largest route into MBB's higher entry level.
- **Entry difficulty: 5/5**

**Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for**
- Long hours and travel in the first years; limited control over staffing; constant evaluation; a pyramid that is no longer growing. Graduate pay has been flat three years running and, per a UK trade piece, real Big 4 pay for new hires has fallen 5-13% depending on the firm (eFinancialCareers via Road to Offer, 2026).
- Much of the junior work is slide-building and data prep; the "strategy" part comes later. AI is shrinking the routine tasks, which can speed up responsibility (good) or reduce hiring (bad).
- Not a fit if you need predictable evenings, dislike constant travel, hate working with ambiguity, or want deep technical craft (engineering, research). Also poor fit if you are in it only for the brand with no interest in business problems; the work is client-facing and political.
- In Italy, local MBB offices are small and pay is unverified; choose carefully if you need Italian-market earning.

### 3.2 Management / operations / transformation consultant at Big 4, Accenture and similar

**What you actually do**
You help a client change how it operates and make the change stick. Typical projects: design a new finance operating model for a bank, run a PMO (project management office) for a government digital programme, map and redesign a purchase-to-pay process, lead change management for a new HR system, assess a regulatory-risk framework, or support a cost-reduction programme. Day-to-day: process mapping in Visio/Miro, workshops with client staff, Excel and Power BI analysis, status reports and project plans, and deliverables such as a target operating model, a business case or an implementation roadmap. Compared to strategy, there is more stakeholder management, more documentation and longer project timelines.

**A typical day and week**
Morning: team stand-up, then a workshop with client process owners; midday: update the issue log and timeline; afternoon: client meeting with the programme director; evening: finalise the weekly status deck and next-week plan. Travel to the client site 3-4 days a week is common in the UK, Germany and the US; Italy and Gulf projects are often local or regional. Utilisation targets are tracked (hours billed to clients); weeks between projects ("on the bench") are used for training and internal work.

**Hours, stress and lifestyle**
- *Hours:* typical 45-60 per week; peak 60-70 at go-lives and bid deadlines (StrategyCase, July 2026 suggests Big 4 advisory/strategy 50-65; PrepLounge coach answers; no firm data). The Italian Big 4 are often quoted at long hours for juniors (consumer sites; unverified).
- *Stress:* **3/5.** Steady deadline pressure, occasional high-stakes go-lives and bid work.
- *Lifestyle/flexibility:* more predictable than MBB, with formal flexible-working policies. Staffing can still be demanding, and utilisation targets create pressure to stay on projects.
- *Travel:* moderate to high depending on client location.
- *Remote/hybrid:* hybrid is common; some delivery work is remote or offshore.

**Human vs. quantitative profile**
People/communication: 4/5. Quantitative/technical: 3/5. You spend a lot of time persuading client staff to change how they work. Spreadsheet and process analysis skills are needed, but you do not usually need advanced maths.

**Compensation (approximate)**
Entry:
- *US:* Big 4 consulting analyst base: Deloitte $90-100k, KPMG $93-112k, PwC $80-90k, EY $78-91k (Road to Offer, 2026); Accenture Strategy about $105k base (PrepLounge USA, Jan 2026, via earlier research). City premium of $10-20k for New York.
- *UK:* base: Deloitte £37k, PwC £34k, EY £32k, KPMG £32k, Accenture £33.5k; Capgemini Invent £51k (PrepLounge UK, 2026). Other guides give £30-40k for Big 4 in London (LearnSignal and Grant Thornton commentary, 2026). Grant Thornton said Big 4 graduate pay "barely moved in three years" and set its own London pay at £40k from September 2027 (secondary report, 2026).
- *Italy:* no firm-level data found. Indeed shows about €26.6k average for Deloitte Italia "Analyst" and about €28k for Accenture Italia data analyst; a Milan graduate consulting vacancy at Michael Page lists up to €26k; Indeed gives Milan "Consulente" average about €34k across seniority (Indeed Italy, 2026; self-reported, mixed levels). So roughly €26-30k gross entry is the best reading, flagged as indicative.
- *Germany (comparison):* Deloitte about €55k, other Big 4 €50-55k, Accenture about €58k (PrepLounge DACH, Dec 2025).
- *Middle East/Asia:* Dubai Big 4 said to be about 40% below MBB (forum, anecdotal); Singapore Big 4 consulting S$55-70k (Skillup, 2026); Hong Kong Big 4 analyst HKD 500-700k (Road to Offer, 2026).

~5 years (senior consultant/manager): US Big 4 manager base $105-150k (Road to Offer, 2026) and total $200-350k at manager to senior manager level; UK senior consultant/manager £92-125k (all firms, PrepLounge, 2026). Italy: no reliable data found.

Senior (partner/managing director): UK partner pay across firms £200k to £1m+ (PrepLounge UK, 2026); partner equity pay at Big 4 is not published; no reliable data on Italy.

**Career path**
Typical Big 4 ladder: Analyst/Consultant → Senior Consultant → Manager → Senior Manager → Director → Partner. Accenture uses Analyst → Consultant → Manager → Senior Manager → Managing Director. Time per step is about 2 years at the lower levels, 3-4 at manager and above. There is a formal "up or out" at some levels but it is softer than at MBB, with more lateral moves. MBA sponsorship exists but is less common and less generous than at MBB.

**Exit opportunities**
- *After 2-3 years:* move to corporate roles (finance transformation, operations, programme management), switch to another Big 4 line, join a tier-2 strategy firm, start an MBA, or join a vendor/software company.
- *After 7-10 years:* senior manager/director roles in corporates, transformation director or COO in a mid-sized company, partner (rare), or independent consulting/freelance. Lateral hires from Big 4 strategy practices into MBB occur but the route is narrow and mostly from the strategy arms.

**Tier list of employers**
*Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact.*
- **Big 4 strategy arms (closest to tier 2):** Strategy& (PwC), EY-Parthenon, Monitor Deloitte, KPMG strategy group: stronger selectivity and higher pay.
- **Big 4 management/operations/risk practices:** Deloitte Consulting (largest advisory business), PwC Advisory, EY Consulting, KPMG Advisory.
- **Accenture:** the largest pure consulting-and-technology firm (FY2026 revenue $74.2bn; about 779,000 staff at last confirmed count after 2025 cuts).
- **Other large consultancies:** Capgemini, Cognizant, IBM Consulting, Infosys Consulting, Sopra Steria, plus regional firms such as BIP and Reply in Italy.
- **Differences:** these firms recruit more widely (many universities, more roles per year), pay less at entry than strategy firms, and offer broader exposure and longer implementation work.

**How to enter**
- *Degrees/backgrounds:* business, economics, engineering, IT, supply chain, social sciences. Most graduate programmes are open to all disciplines.
- *Programmes:* spring weeks/insight programmes (UK), summer internships, graduate schemes. UK Big 4 recruitment is rolling and opens in September (Deloitte deadline 1 May 2026, PwC 1 June 2026, EY and KPMG 24 April 2026, but seats close early; Road to Offer UK, 2026). Accenture opens its graduate programmes in August-September (Road to Offer / aggregators).
- *Interview formats:* online application, situational judgement test, numerical/verbal reasoning (SHL-type), video interview, assessment centre with case study, group exercise and interview; Deloitte immersive assessment (about 40 SJT-style questions) (Road to Offer UK, 2026). Case interviews are lighter than at MBB.
- *Certifications:* PMP, Prince2, Lean Six Sigma, Agile (Scrum) and vendor certifications (SAP, Salesforce, ServiceNow) can help at the margin for operations and implementation roles but are rarely required at graduate entry; employers often pay for them later.
- *Skills to build:* Excel/Power BI, process mapping, project management basics, clear business writing, stakeholder skills.
- *Common mistakes:* assuming it is easy (acceptance rates are lower than the number of openings suggests; no reliable published figures); not tailoring to the service line; ignoring intake cuts and expecting a safe fallback.
- *Alternative routes:* internal transfers from audit or tax; apprenticeships in the UK; experienced hires from industry; Big 4 to MBB lateral for strategy-arm people.
- **Entry difficulty: 3/5**

**Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for**
- Lower entry pay than strategy; intake cuts and AI pressure mean this is no longer a guaranteed safety net (PwC US plan, PwC UK, Accenture cuts above). Work can be repetitive (documentation, PMO reporting), and utilisation targets can feel like a timesheet culture.
- Not a fit if you want to do original strategic analysis from day one, if you dislike process and change-management work, or if you struggle with bureaucracy.
- In Italy entry pay is low relative to cost of living in Milan (indicative €26-30k gross), though this is typical of the Italian graduate market (Mercer puts Italian first-job pay at about €30.5k for 2024, as cited by Sky TG24, 2025).

### 3.3 Specialist / boutique consultant (sector or function specialists)

**What you actually do**
You advise on one sector or function, using deep content. At a healthcare and life-sciences boutique (for example L.E.K., ZS, ClearView) you might size a drug's market, build a revenue forecast, interview doctors and payers, and write a launch strategy; at a pricing specialist (Simon-Kucher) you design a price architecture and test willingness to pay with customer surveys; at a sustainability or energy boutique you model decarbonisation pathways and help with disclosure; at a restructuring/performance-improvement firm (A&M, AlixPartners) you may build a 13-week cash-flow forecast for a distressed company and help run the turnaround. Tools: Excel and PowerPoint, specialist databases, survey tools, sometimes SQL or Python. Deliverables: models, market reports, due-diligence memos and implementation plans.

**A typical day and week**
Similar to strategy but more content-driven: morning on data and calls with sector experts; afternoon building a model; evening on a deck for the partner. Due-diligence projects (PE-sponsored commercial diligence) run 3-6 weeks with heavy hours; longer retainer projects are steadier. Many boutiques have a smaller team (3-5) and more direct partner contact, so juniors take on client exposure earlier.

**Hours, stress and lifestyle**
- *Hours:* typical 50-65 per week; peaks 70-80 on PE due diligence or restructuring (StrategyCase, July 2026: boutiques 45-70; wide variation by firm and practice).
- *Stress:* **4/5** in due-diligence and restructuring boutiques; **3/5** in many sector and pricing practices.
- *Lifestyle/flexibility:* depends heavily on the firm; some boutiques offer better balance than MBB, others work PE timelines.
- *Travel:* usually lower than MBB in Europe; high in US consulting boutiques.
- *Remote/hybrid:* hybrid common.

**Human vs. quantitative profile**
People/communication: 3/5. Quantitative/technical: 4/5. Specialist work is usually more modelling- and data-heavy (healthcare forecasting, pricing analytics), and clients value your content knowledge more than your presentation polish.

**Compensation (approximate)**
Entry:
- *UK:* L.E.K. £50,000 base (up to ~£54k total), OC&C £51,000 (up to £58k), Simon-Kucher £52,000 (up to £55k), Altman Solon £50,000 (up to £56k), ClearView £50,000 (up to £54k), ZS £47,000 (up to £51.5k), Arthur D. Little £40,000, Alvarez & Marsal £35,000 (up to £41k), FTI £40,000, Kearney £41,000, Oliver Wyman £34,000 (PrepLounge UK, 2026; these figures conflict with other sources, which place Oliver Wyman and Kearney closer to MBB, so treat London tier-2 numbers as a range and check the offer).
- *US:* Oliver Wyman undergraduate analysts $95-132k; L.E.K. MBA base $110-180k (Road to Offer, 2026; US bonus 20-30% at L.E.K.).
- *Germany:* Kearney about €80k base, Simon-Kucher €68k, Roland Berger €72k, Oliver Wyman about €60.5k (PrepLounge DACH, Dec 2025, single source).
- *Italy:* no reliable data found for sector boutiques. Indirect: Oliver Wyman Italy about €43k base (Management Consulted 2026, single source, via earlier research); Alvarez & Marsal Spain €50k base (same). Treat Italian boutique pay as likely above Big 4 entry level but unverified.
- *Gulf/Asia:* Dubai tier-2 and boutique packages are reported around AED 22-25k per month for strategy houses (forum, unverified); Singapore: PrepLounge says non-MBB strategy firms offer analyst packages in a similar range to MBB, with the gap opening later (summarised from PrepLounge Singapore, 2026).

~5 years: approximately US $200-350k total at the 2-4 year consultant level and $280-450k at manager for tier-2 firms (Road to Offer, 2026); UK experienced consultant £65-100k; Italy no reliable data.

Senior: specialist practice leaders and partners can earn as much as strategy partners at top boutiques; no reliable published data.

**Career path**
Analyst/Associate → Consultant → Senior Consultant/Engagement Manager → Principal/Director → Partner. Boutiques often promote faster and give earlier responsibility, but promotion pools are smaller and the firm may be sold or restructured (many boutiques have been acquired by larger groups). MBA sponsorship exists at some, less systematically than at MBB.

**Exit opportunities**
- *After 2-3 years:* move into the industry you advised (pharma strategy, retail pricing, energy), private equity or venture (especially from PE-diligence boutiques), larger strategy firms or a business school.
- *After 7-10 years:* director or VP roles in the sector, partner at the boutique, or an operating role in a PE-owned company. Specialist knowledge makes you hireable but can limit the breadth of moves compared with MBB generalists.

**Tier list of employers**
*Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact.*
- **Top specialists (near tier-2 prestige):** L.E.K. (healthcare, PE diligence), OC&C (consumer), Simon-Kucher (pricing), Oliver Wyman (financial services), Kearney (operations), Altman Solon (telecom, media, tech), Roland Berger.
- **Strong functional specialists:** Alvarez & Marsal, AlixPartners, FTI Consulting (restructuring and performance improvement), ZS and other pharma-commercial consultancies.
- **Smaller regional and national boutiques:** in Italy, BIP, Value Partners and The European House - Ambrosetti are examples; their size and prestige vary and were not verified beyond headcounts quoted above.
- **Differences:** sector depth, case-led interviews, and clientele (PE and corporates).

**How to enter**
- *Degrees/backgrounds:* the sector helps: life sciences or medicine for healthcare consulting, engineering or economics for energy and operations, finance or economics for restructuring. Many boutiques hire generalists and train them.
- *Programmes:* graduate and analyst programmes, internships; many boutiques recruit year-round, with timing mirroring PE cycles. Applications are often direct and less formal than MBB.
- *Interview formats:* case interviews (often more data/modelling heavy and sector-specific), fit interviews; some firms add Excel tests and written exercises.
- *Certifications:* mostly not required; CFA is rarely needed outside restructuring and finance-heavy boutiques; project management certifications are marginal.
- *Skills to build:* modelling, sector knowledge, written communication.
- *Common mistakes:* applying with no evidence of interest in the sector; assuming boutiques are easier (healthcare and PE boutiques are very selective).
- *Alternative routes:* PhD/MD/PharmD hires for life-sciences consulting; lateral moves from industry; Big 4 or internal teams into specialist practices.
- **Entry difficulty: 4/5** for the top specialists (L.E.K., OC&C, Simon-Kucher, Oliver Wyman); 3/5 for smaller national boutiques.

**Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for**
- Smaller firms have thinner training and brand; pay is less transparent and varies more; exits can be tied to the sector. Smaller bench means you can be staffed on the wrong sector for years.
- PE-driven work can be as intense as MBB, with weekend crunches.
- Not a fit if you want a broad generalist's CV and flexibility in what you do next, or if you dislike committing to a sector early.

### 3.4 Technology and digital consultant (business-side)

**What you actually do**
You help business leaders decide on and deliver technology change, without necessarily writing the code. Typical work: define the business requirements for a CRM or ERP implementation, run a digital-strategy project (e-commerce, customer journey, data and AI use cases), design a data governance or cybersecurity-risk programme in business terms, manage an agile delivery team as a product owner or project manager, or advise on AI adoption and business cases. Deliverables include requirements documents, process flows and user stories, vendor selection analyses, roadmaps, change plans and training. Tools: Jira, Confluence, Visio/Miro, Excel/Power BI, sometimes SQL for data checks. The engineering side (building and coding systems) is covered in the Computer Science branch; data-science consulting is covered in the Data Science branch.

**A typical day and week**
Morning stand-up with developers and business owners; midday: requirements workshop or demo; afternoon: update backlog, risks and plans; evening: steering-committee pack. Projects are long (6-24 months) and have a clear delivery schedule. Go-live and release weekends bring peaks.

**Hours, stress and lifestyle**
- *Hours:* typical 45-55 per week; peaks 60-70 at go-live or testing (no reliable survey data; consistent with practitioner descriptions).
- *Stress:* **3/5.** Steady deadlines; incident-driven peaks at go-live.
- *Lifestyle/flexibility:* more predictable than strategy; often hybrid; offshore teams create early or late calls.
- *Travel:* moderate, depends on client; many programmes are on a long-term client site.
- *Remote/hybrid:* higher remote share than strategy work.

**Human vs. quantitative profile**
People/communication: 4/5. Quantitative/technical: 3/5. You need to speak both business and IT; you do not need to code, but you need to understand data, systems and delivery methods.

**Compensation (approximate)**
Entry:
- *US:* Accenture Strategy about $100-125k total; Big 4 technology consulting analysts $78-112k base (Road to Offer / PrepLounge USA, 2026); non-strategy technology consulting often sits at the lower end.
- *UK:* Accenture £33.5k base; Deloitte £37k, PwC £34k; Capgemini Invent £51k (PrepLounge UK, 2026). Graduate guides give £30-40k in London for Big 4.
- *Italy:* no firm-specific data found. Indeed Italy shows around €28k for Accenture data analysts and €26-28k for junior Deloitte analysts in Milan (2026; self-reported, indicative). Junior SAP consultant averages are often quoted around €25-30k in Italy (Payscale snippet, not verified in detail).
- *Gulf/Asia:* Dubai junior business-analyst figures AED 6-8k per month for fresh graduates and AED 8-12k for 1-3 years (Resumevera, sourced from job boards; weak source); Singapore Big 4 consulting analysts S$55-70k (Skillup, 2026).

~5 years: senior consultant/manager US $110-150k base for Big 4 managers (Road to Offer, 2026); UK £65-125k across firms (PrepLounge, 2026). Italy: no reliable data. Technical specialisations (SAP, cloud, data/AI, cybersecurity) command premiums that vary widely.

Senior: partner/managing director pay is not published; no reliable data.

**Career path**
Analyst/Consultant → Senior Consultant → Manager → Senior Manager → Director/Managing Director → Partner. Specialisation by technology (ERP, cloud, cybersecurity, data/AI) is common from year 2-3. Because delivery work is measured on utilisation, people with niche platform skills (SAP, Salesforce) can have more bargaining power than generalists.

**Exit opportunities**
- *After 2-3 years:* in-house IT/digital programme or product management, software vendors (pre-sales, solution consulting), another consulting practice, or a data/AI role.
- *After 7-10 years:* head of digital transformation, CIO/CTO office roles, product leadership, or partner at a technology consultancy. Technology skills tend to be portable.

**Tier list of employers**
*Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact.*
- **Business-led digital strategy and transformation:** McKinsey Digital, BCG X, Bain digital, Accenture (Song and Strategy), Capgemini Invent, Deloitte Consulting.
- **Big 4 technology consulting:** Deloitte, PwC, EY, KPMG technology practices.
- **Large implementation/system integrators:** Accenture, Capgemini, IBM Consulting, Cognizant, Infosys, TCS, Sopra Steria (their engineering side is in Computer Science).
- **Specialists:** SAP, Salesforce, ServiceNow and cloud partners; Italian firms such as BIP (business-IT hybrid) and Reply (more IT-heavy).
- **Differences:** strategy-led digital teams pay more and recruit from the same funnel as strategy; implementation consultancies recruit broadly.

**How to enter**
- *Degrees/backgrounds:* IT, information systems, engineering, business, data analytics, AI and any degree plus evidence of technical interest. Business-side consulting accepts non-technical graduates.
- *Programmes:* graduate schemes and internships at Accenture, Capgemini, Big 4, Reply, BIP and IT consultancies; Accenture and Big 4 recruit in autumn (August-October) in many countries.
- *Interview formats:* online tests, video interview, assessment centre; light case studies; technical questions for specialist tracks.
- *Certifications:* more useful here than elsewhere: cloud (AWS/Azure), SAP, Salesforce, ITIL, Scrum/PSM, PMP/Prince2, Lean Six Sigma for operations-leaning roles. They help get interviews and promotions but rarely substitute for experience.
- *Skills to build:* requirements analysis, SQL/data basics, agile delivery, Excel/Power BI, AI literacy.
- *Common mistakes:* choosing a legacy platform with no growth, ignoring communication skills, overstating technical depth.
- *Alternative routes:* career switch from IT or analytics roles; internal moves from client side; bootcamps plus certifications.
- **Entry difficulty: 3/5**

**Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for**
- Work can be tied to long, politically complicated programmes; some roles become "body-shopping" to a client; growth in generative AI could automate documentation and test work, and Accenture's 2025 cuts show the exposure. Entry pay is among the lowest in consulting.
- Not a fit if you want strategy-level glamour, dislike technology delivery, or want to code (move to engineering instead).

### 3.5 Internal strategy / corporate transformation / in-house consulting

**What you actually do**
You do strategy and change work for your own employer. Examples: build the three-year strategic plan and the annual budget narrative, evaluate an acquisition target for corporate development, run the transformation office that tracks cost savings across divisions, staff an internal consulting team on a business-unit problem, prepare materials for the CEO and board, and track market and competitor trends. Tools: Excel, PowerPoint, business-intelligence tools, financial models. Because you live with the results, more of your time goes on follow-through than on the initial analysis.

**A typical day and week**
Morning: sync with finance and business-unit leaders; midday: modelling; afternoon: prepare a board paper; evening: usually off. Peaks occur around planning cycles, board meetings and M&A deals. You attend more meetings and negotiate with internal stakeholders who have their own agendas.

**Hours, stress and lifestyle**
- *Hours:* typical 42-50 per week; peaks 55-60 (StrategyCase, May 2026 and July 2026 cite 40-55 hours for corporate strategy; coach sources, no survey).
- *Stress:* **2/5** to **3/5.** Occasional crunch around budget, board and M&A cycles.
- *Lifestyle/flexibility:* generally the best in the branch; hybrid and remote policies follow the company.
- *Travel:* low to moderate.
- *Remote/hybrid:* high; follows company policy.

**Human vs. quantitative profile**
People/communication: 4/5. Quantitative/technical: 3/5. Success depends on internal credibility and influence more than on analytical brilliance.

**Compensation (approximate)**
Entry: graduates usually join through rotational/leadership development programmes or finance/strategy analyst roles; pure strategy jobs usually need 2-5 years of experience. No reliable entry-level data found for the US, UK or Italy by programme; graduate-programme pay at large corporates is generally in line with the national graduate market rather than consulting (inference; Mercer cites about €30.5k for Italian first-job pay in 2024, as quoted by Sky TG24, Jan 2025).

~5 years: a coaching site gives corporate strategy total compensation for a senior manager of $250-350k in tech, $200-280k in pharma, $200-260k in consumer and $180-240k in industrials (StrategyCase, May 2026; a coach's table with internal inconsistencies, treat as indicative). UK and Italy: no reliable data found.

Senior: Directors $260-600k, VPs $450k-1.2m and SVP/CSO $700k-2.5m+ depending on sector in the same table (US, 2026; indicative); equity makes a big difference at tech companies.

**Career path**
Strategy analyst/associate → manager → senior manager → director → VP strategy/CSO or business-unit general manager. Median tenure at a first corporate strategy employer was reported at 2-3 years (StrategyCase, May 2026, coach source). Typical entry is after 2-5 years of consulting or an MBA; exits to P&L roles are the aim.

**Exit opportunities**
- *After 2-3 years:* business-unit roles, product or marketing management, corporate development, or move to a consulting firm.
- *After 7-10 years:* CSO, COO, business-unit general manager, chief of staff to the CEO, startup executive, private-equity operating roles.

**Tier list of employers**
*Tier groupings reflect industry consensus and practitioner perception, not objective fact.*
- **Internal consulting arms with consulting-style staffing:** large technology firms (strategy and operations teams), some large banks, industrials and pharma groups. These are the most sought after and often recruit ex-MBB.
- **Corporate strategy/corp dev at blue-chips:** big tech, pharma, consumer goods, energy, financial services.
- **Rotational leadership programmes:** the main route for graduates (for example at consumer-goods, industrial and financial groups).
- **Differences:** whether "strategy" means CEO-adjacent decisions or running a PMO varies a lot; some teams are title-only (StrategyCase, 2026).

**How to enter**
- *Degrees/backgrounds:* business, economics, engineering, finance and any degree with a strong analytical record. Direct graduate entry is mostly through rotational programmes, finance or planning analyst roles, or business-analytics roles.
- *Programmes:* graduate/leadership development schemes (many open in autumn; vary by employer); internships; MBA internships and post-MBA recruiting for strategy roles.
- *Interview formats:* structured behavioural interviews, case/strategy exercises, presentations to a panel; 3-5 rounds over 4-8 weeks is described for corporate strategy (StrategyCase, 2026).
- *Certifications:* usually irrelevant; Lean Six Sigma or PMP help in operations transformation roles.
- *Skills to build:* financial modelling, understanding of the industry, executive communication, ability to build coalitions.
- *Common mistakes:* assuming a degree alone gets a strategy job; skipping industry knowledge; underrating politics.
- *Alternative routes:* start in finance, FP&A, operations or product and move into strategy; join as a consultant first (the common route); internal transfer within the company.
- **Entry difficulty: 3/5** for graduate rotational programmes at well-known companies; **4/5** for strategy-team roles at top companies (few seats, experience required).

**Honest downsides and who it is NOT a good fit for**
- Smaller teams and fewer promotions; strategy teams are cut in downturns; politics and slow decision-making; lower pay than consulting at the junior and mid levels; limited exposure to many industries.
- Not a fit if you want fast learning across many industries, want to be compensated for long hours, or dislike working inside one organisation for years.

## 4. Banks vs. other employer types

Because this is the consulting branch, the comparison is across consulting employer types and against investment banking (IB), the usual alternative for the same graduates. Ratings are my synthesis of the sources cited; stress uses the shared 1-5 scale.

| Dimension | MBB | Tier-2 strategy | Big 4/Accenture advisory | Boutique/specialist | In-house strategy | Investment banking (analyst, comparison) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hours/week (typical, peak) | 55-70 (80+) | 55-75 (80) | 45-60 (70) | 45-70 (80) | 40-50 (60) | 70-100 (100+) |
| Stress (1-5) | 4 | 4 | 3 | 3-4 | 2-3 | 5 |
| Entry pay US (base) | $110-112k | $95-132k (OW analysts) | $78-112k | varies, $95k+ top boutiques | not comparable | ~$110k base + $70-110k bonus (NYC bulge bracket, 2026) |
| Entry pay UK | £49.5-52k base | £34-52k (wide) | £32-37k | £35-52k | no reliable data | £90-120k all-in (one guide, 2026) |
| Travel | high | high | moderate-high | moderate | low | low (but long hours at desk) |
| Culture | meritocratic, up-or-out, competitive | similar, slightly less brand pressure | large, process-driven, wide range of quality | smaller, closer to partners | corporate, political | hierarchical, deal-driven |

**Commentary**
- **MBB vs tier-2.** In the US the entry gap is small; in the UK reported base pay at top tier-2 firms (OC&C, Simon-Kucher, Strategy&, L.E.K.) is close to MBB (£50-52k), but some tier-2 figures are far lower (Kearney £41k, Oliver Wyman £34k in PrepLounge UK), a data conflict I could not resolve. In Germany reported Kearney pay equals MBB (single source). The real MBB premium is exit brand, selectivity and later pay.
- **Consulting vs IB.** IB juniors earn more at entry (US NYC bulge bracket about $180-220k all-in versus about $137-140k at MBB; London IB £90-120k per one guide versus £60-63k at MBB) and work longer hours, with more repetitive modelling and live-deal stress. Consulting is broader, more client-facing, and involves travel; IB is deeper in finance. The compensation gap narrows at manager level. The StrategyCase author describes the intensity as comparable but differently shaped (consulting has travel; IB has more hours). Exits differ: consulting to corporate strategy and PE; IB to PE, hedge funds and corporate finance.
- **Regional differences.**
  - *US:* the four-night travel model is strongest in US offices; Monday-Thursday on site with return Thursday evening. Salaries are higher and standardised across MBB offices.
  - *UK/Europe:* more local staffing within regions; cross-border within Europe common; pay is lower than the US. In DACH a master's means a higher entry grade and the local language is mandatory. Roland Berger states a four-day on-site week as default.
  - *Italy:* the consulting market was about €6.6bn in 2023 with growth tied to the PNRR (Assoconsult via Il Sole 24 Ore); entry pay is low compared with the UK and Germany, and the gap between MBB and the Big 4 cannot be quantified for Italy from reliable sources. Hours at Italian Big 4 firms are described by non-expert sites as long (unverified). Milan is the hub; Rome serves public-sector and energy clients.
  - *Gulf:* project teams are often staffed in-region, with fly-in staff from Europe and local-hire quotas (Saudisation 35% then 40% in consulting roles; Emiratisation targets 10% by end-2026). Tax-free pay with housing/transport components in Saudi packages (PrepLounge Saudi 2025 says about 50% base, 25% housing, 25% transport/benefits; post-MBA data). Spending is more selective since 2025 (reported PIF review and budget tightening).
  - *Asia:* Singapore and Hong Kong are the main hubs; Singapore MBB graduate packages about S$90-120k total, about double Big 4 consulting arms (Skillup, 2026). Hours in Southeast Asia are described by a regional coach as 65-70 per week (PrepLounge forum, Nov 2025; anecdotal). Tokyo: very selective local recruiting through the "shinsotsu" (new-graduate) cycle; competition at the Big 4 is reported at 20-40 applicants per place (a Japanese career site; unverified); entry data limited.

## 5. Which backgrounds fit this branch

### (a) Background ratings

Ratings: **strong** = a common, direct route with a clear advantage; **possible** = accepted with some extra proof or in some firm types; **stretch** = unusual and needs a strong story or additional credentials.

| Background | Rating | One-line reason |
|---|---|---|
| Management | Strong | Direct fit for business-side consulting; MBB recruit from business schools, but a pure business degree has no advantage over other majors. |
| Logistics & Supply Chain | Strong | Core for operations consulting (Kearney, A&M, Big 4 operations) and a good story for strategy in industrials and retail. |
| Finance | Strong | Valuable for strategy, PE-diligence, restructuring and finance transformation; the cross-over with deal advisory is in the Finance branch. |
| Accounting | Possible | Fits Big 4 risk, finance transformation and restructuring; MBB and strategy view it as narrower, so extra strategy exposure is needed. |
| Marketing | Possible | Good for commercial, pricing, consumer boutiques and customer strategy; needs quantitative evidence for MBB. |
| Data Analytics | Strong | Analytics and AI-enabled consulting are growing; strong for operations, pricing and healthcare boutiques and for digital practices. |
| Economics | Strong | The classic MBB feeder; also the foundation for economic consulting (another branch). |
| Computer Science | Possible | Strong for tech/digital consulting; MBB hire technical specialists (e.g. BCG X) but the generalist route needs business evidence. |
| Cybersecurity | Possible | Strong for risk and cyber advisory at Big 4; limited for strategy generalist tracks. |
| Data Science | Possible | Good for specialist tracks (BCG X, McKinsey QuantumBlack, analytics boutiques); generalist strategy needs business communication proof. |
| Artificial Intelligence | Possible | Rising demand for AI strategy and implementation; BCG's hiring skew to AI engineers and data scientists supports technical routes (BCG, April 2026), but generalist entry is uncertain. |
