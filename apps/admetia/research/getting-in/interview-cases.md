# Interview cases and exercises by career area

**How often, and how sure.** Each exercise below carries a frequency word and an evidence tag.

- **Always**: the employer states that the stage is part of the process for that role (for example "for most client-facing roles, you will participate in a personal experience interview, followed by a problem-solving interview", McKinsey).
- **Usually**: several employers state it, or one says it applies to "most" candidates, or practitioner sources agree without exception.
- **Often**: practitioner consensus or repeated candidate reports, but no employer states it as a rule.
- **Sometimes**: reported for some firms, offices or years only.

Evidence tags follow the library: [employer-stated], [practitioner consensus], [anecdotal], [data]. "Admetia practice case" marks an exercise written for this site in the style of the official ones; it is not an employer's case.

## Bottom line

1. **Strategy consulting always pairs a case with a behavioural interview.** McKinsey runs a Personal Experience Interview (PEI) and a problem-solving interview "for most client-facing roles", Bain runs case interviews "only for consulting roles" alongside a fit interview, and BCG describes a "realistic business challenge" you work through "step-by-step" (McKinsey, Bain, BCG interview pages, read 10 Oct 2026) [employer-stated]. Case-only preparation misses half of the scored material.
2. **The firms publish their own practice cases, free.** McKinsey: Beautify, Diconsa, Electro-Light and Talbot Trucks. Bain: Coffee Shop Co. and FashionCo. BCG: the OneDay@BCG job simulation (same pages) [employer-stated]. Between them they cover the recurring case types: product launch, investment or market entry, profitability decline, operating-model change, a new service for a non-profit, and a small-business go/no-go.
3. **McKinsey says Solve needs no preparation; it asks for two stories per PEI theme.** "There's no need to prepare" for Solve, and the PEI page suggests "coming prepared with two personal examples" for each of four areas (McKinsey, read 10 Oct 2026) [employer-stated]. That is eight stories, which is more than most candidates bring.
4. **Banking interviews are behavioural plus technical; the case study is the exception at analyst level.** Employers describe "technical ability" and competency questions, not a syllabus (applications-and-interviews.md §2) [employer-stated]. The technical core that practitioners agree on is three-statement linking, enterprise-to-equity value, DCF and WACC, and multiples [practitioner consensus]. Written or modelling case studies are standard in private equity, not in graduate banking.
5. **Private equity tests with models, in three formats.** A paper LBO (minutes, no spreadsheet), a timed LBO modelling test (one to three hours) and a take-home case with an investment memo (days). Timed tests are typical of large-fund, fast recruiting; open-ended take-homes of smaller funds, off-cycle recruiting and Europe (Mergers & Inquisitions; Financial Edge) [practitioner consensus]. Most graduates meet these only after two years in banking or consulting.
6. **Investment roles ask for a stock pitch.** Fidelity International's equity research programme asks for a one-page stock case in the application and runs a "stock pitching exercise" at the assessment centre (Fidelity, read 10 Oct 2026) [employer-stated; an MBA programme]. Practitioners treat a prepared pitch as expected in asset management, equity research and hedge fund interviews [practitioner consensus].
7. **Quantitative trading tests numbers and probability, not finance.** Jane Street's final round covers "problem solving, probability and statistics, coding … data analysis, and general interests" and "won't require previous knowledge of finance" (Jane Street, read 10 Oct 2026) [employer-stated]. Optiver allows its online assessments "once every 8 months" (Optiver Europe FAQ, read 10 Oct 2026) [employer-stated]. Its widely discussed 80-question, 8-minute arithmetic test is unpublished and known only from candidate reports [anecdotal].
8. **FMCG puts a business case inside the recorded interview.** Unilever UK & Ireland: "The digital interview will consist of 3 short questions followed by a business case", reviewed "by a real person"; the final Discovery Centre has "project meetings, business challenges, a team meeting … and an interview" (Unilever UFLP 2026 page, read 10 Oct 2026) [employer-stated].
9. **Big 4 firms publish their stages but not their final-stage exercises.** EY's last stage is an "Experience Day", KPMG's an in-person half day that "combines assessed and non-assessed activities", Deloitte's an in-person final stage after a job simulation (employer pages, read 10 Oct 2026 and 30 Sep 2026) [employer-stated]. A read-and-present case at PwC's final stage is reported by graduate guides and candidates only [anecdotal].
10. **Tech employers lean hardest on structured behavioural answers.** AWS recruiters: "Amazon engages in behavioral-based interviewing through the STAR (situation, task, action, and result) method", with "one narrative for each Leadership Principle … with data and specifics" (AWS careers blog, read 10 Oct 2026) [employer-stated]. Coding interviews on data structures and algorithms are the usual technical screen for software roles [practitioner consensus; Google's own pages did not render].

## 1. Strategy consulting (MBB, tier 2, strategy arms)

| Exercise | How often | What the employer says it scores | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Case (problem-solving) interview | **Always** at MBB for consulting roles | McKinsey: "analytical thinking and approach to solving complex problems". Bain: "how you think, structure problems, and build on ideas"; "We're not looking for a single right answer". BCG: "structure your approach, ask thoughtful questions, analyze data, perform quick calculations" | McKinsey, Bain, BCG pages [employer-stated] |
| Behavioural / fit (McKinsey PEI) | **Always** | McKinsey: "two personal examples" for each of four areas, "focusing on your specific role and describing the key actions". Bain: past experience and operating principles. BCG: a behavioural portion | McKinsey; Bain; BCG via applications-and-interviews.md §2 [employer-stated] |
| Market sizing / estimation | **Usually**, as a step inside a case | Not published as a separate stage; it appears as a question within cases | applications-and-interviews.md §3 [practitioner consensus] |
| Game-based screen (McKinsey Solve) | **Usually** at McKinsey: "one of the exciting steps in our process for most consulting roles" | Problem solving; "There's no need to prepare" | McKinsey [employer-stated] |
| Online case (BCG "Casey" chatbot) | **Sometimes**: office- and year-dependent | Third-party guides describe one case in 8–10 timed questions over 25–35 minutes, ending in a one-minute recorded recommendation; BCG's own pages read here do not describe it | Management Consulted; IGotAnOffer [practitioner consensus; unverified] |
| Written case (documents, then a short deck or memo) | **Sometimes** | Reported for some early rounds and some tier-2 and European offices | Prep-site summaries [anecdotal] |

**Who leads the case.** In an *interviewer-led* case (associated with McKinsey) the interviewer moves you through set questions: framework, then an exhibit, then a calculation, then a recommendation. In a *candidate-led* case (common at BCG and Bain) you choose what to analyse next and ask for the data (applications-and-interviews.md §2.3) [practitioner consensus]. McKinsey's own sample cases are written as a sequence of numbered questions, which is the interviewer-led shape [employer-stated, by format]. Offices vary, so ask the recruiter.

**The official practice cases, and the case type each one trains.**

| Firm | Case | What it asks | Case type |
|---|---|---|---|
| McKinsey | Beautify | Whether a global beauty company should train in-store consultants to use virtual channels | Operating-model change |
| McKinsey | Diconsa | Using the Diconsa store network in Mexico to offer basic financial services to the rural poor, for the Gates Foundation | New service, social sector |
| McKinsey | Electro-Light | Designing the launch of a reduced-sugar, electrolyte sports drink for a major beverage company (client: SuperSoda) | Product launch |
| McKinsey | Talbot Trucks | Whether a leading European truck maker should invest in producing and selling electric trucks | Investment decision / new market |
| Bain | Coffee Shop Co. | "Help a friend determine whether opening a coffee shop is a good idea" | Small-business go/no-go, break-even |
| Bain | FashionCo. | Why a fashion player's revenues have declined for five years | Revenue / profitability decline |
| BCG | OneDay@BCG | "A free job simulation that provides insights into the challenges we solve" | Simulation, not a single case |

Sources: McKinsey, Interviewing (https://www.mckinsey.com/careers/interviewing, read in the browser pane, 10 Oct 2026); Bain, Preparing for the case interview (https://www.bain.com/careers/hiring-process/case-interview/, 10 Oct 2026); BCG, Case interview preparation (https://careers.bcg.com/global/en/case-interview-preparation, 10 Oct 2026) [employer-stated].

**The recurring case types, with what a good answer contains** [practitioner consensus, consistent with the official cases above]:

1. **Profitability decline** (FashionCo.): split profit into revenue (price × volume, by segment or channel) and costs (fixed and variable); find where the change sits before proposing fixes.
2. **Market entry or investment** (Talbot Trucks): market size and growth, competition, the client's capabilities, the economics (investment, margin, payback), risks; then a go/no-go with conditions.
3. **Product launch** (Electro-Light): customer and segment, product and price, channel, competitor response, the share needed to break even, launch risks.
4. **Small-business go/no-go** (Coffee Shop Co.): revenue per day from footfall and conversion, fixed and variable costs, break-even volume, payback.
5. **Operating-model change** (Beautify): customer reaction, staff capability and incentives, cost of training and technology, effect on revenue per consultant.
6. **New service in a public or social setting** (Diconsa): need and reach, cost to serve, partner incentives, how impact is measured.

## 3. Sales and trading (markets)

| Exercise | How often | Notes | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fit and "why sales and trading" | **Always** | Same competency framework as the bank's other divisions | [employer-stated, via §2] |
| Market views and a trade idea ("pitch me a trade") | **Often** | Candidate reports from Morgan Stanley, Goldman Sachs (London) and Barclays mention a "quick trade idea", a "current view and take on economy", "commercial awareness" | Glassdoor candidate reports, 2013–2026 [anecdotal] |
| Mental arithmetic, probability and brainteasers | **Often** | Reported examples: estimating the square root of 6,000; choosing between two basketball shots by their win probabilities | Glassdoor candidate reports [anecdotal] |
| Options basics | **Sometimes** | Calls, puts, payoffs; reported at Barclays, Goldman and Wells Fargo | Glassdoor candidate reports [anecdotal] |

A trade idea that survives questioning has a view, the reason the market disagrees, the instrument, a catalyst, the risk and where you would be wrong (an exit level). This is the stock-pitch logic of §5 applied to a rate, currency, commodity or index [practitioner consensus].

## 4. Private equity (associate; graduate entry is rare)

| Exercise | How often | What it looks like | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Paper LBO | **Often** at large funds, especially in fast recruiting | Round numbers, pen and paper or mental arithmetic: entry multiple, leverage, growth, exit multiple, then IRR or money multiple in about 10–20 minutes | Mergers & Inquisitions; Leland [practitioner consensus] |
| Timed LBO modelling test | **Usually** in on-cycle (US-style) recruiting | 1–3 hours in Excel, on site or remote; graded on a working model, realistic assumptions and clear outputs | Mergers & Inquisitions; Financial Edge "90-minute LBO modeling test" [practitioner consensus] |
| Take-home case and investment memo | **Often** at smaller funds, off-cycle and in Europe | Days to a week; pick or receive a company, build a model, recommend for or against; judged mostly on the thesis and the defence | Mergers & Inquisitions [practitioner consensus] |

**A paper LBO you can check by hand (Admetia practice case).** EBITDA 100, bought at 10× (EV 1,000) with 60% debt (600) and 40% equity (400). EBITDA grows to 150 in five years; exit at 10× (EV 1,500). The business repays 300 of debt from cash flow over the five years, so 300 of debt remains. Equity at exit = 1,500 − 300 = 1,200. Money multiple = 1,200 / 400 = 3.0×. IRR: 3.0× over five years is about 25% (1.25⁵ ≈ 3.05). Sources of the gain: EBITDA growth 500 of EV, debt paydown 300, no multiple expansion.

## 5. Asset management, equity research and hedge funds

| Exercise | How often | Notes | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stock pitch | **Always** at Fidelity International's equity research programme; **usually** elsewhere | Fidelity: a one-page stock case in the application ("convince us in 1 page or less why we should invest in the stock") and a "stock pitching exercise" at the assessment centre | Fidelity International, Equity research MBA page (read 10 Oct 2026) [employer-stated; MBA-level programme] |
| Market and portfolio questions | **Often** | Your view on a sector, a macro question, how you would size a position | [practitioner consensus] |
| Valuation and accounting technicals | **Usually** | As in banking (§2), with more weight on earnings quality and cash flow | [practitioner consensus] |

**A pitch that holds up** [practitioner consensus]: a clear buy or sell call; the variant view (what the market has wrong and why); valuation (multiples against peers, a simple DCF if asked); two or three catalysts with dates; the main risks and what would make you change your mind. Two to five minutes, then questions. Pick a company you know, not the most covered mega-cap.

## 10. Behavioural answers that score: the STAR structure and what employers name

The employers above publish the criteria, not the rubric. Read together they ask for the same evidence:

| Employer | What it asks a story to show | Source |
|---|---|---|
| McKinsey | "your specific role", "the key actions that were critical to success", two examples for each of four themes | McKinsey [employer-stated] |
| Amazon | One story per Leadership Principle in STAR, "with data and specifics", "your specific actions, and the measurable results" | AWS [employer-stated] |
| BCG | Integrity, intellectual curiosity, creative thinking, collaborative mindset, drive | applications-and-interviews.md §2 [employer-stated] |
| Bain | Past experience and Bain's operating principles | applications-and-interviews.md §2 [employer-stated] |
| J.P. Morgan | Problem-solving, analysis, communication | applications-and-interviews.md §2 [employer-stated] |
| EY UK | Proactive, doing the right thing, curiosity, relationship-building, agility, critical thinking, embracing technology, motivation | EY UK (read 10 Oct 2026) [employer-stated] |

Five stories cover most prompts across these lists: **leadership** (you moved people without formal authority), **failure** (what went wrong, your share of it, what you changed), **conflict** (a disagreement you resolved and how), **innovation** (an idea you originated and carried through), **high pressure** (a deadline or crisis and the order in which you did things) [practitioner consensus]. Each needs your own actions ("I", not "we"), a number in the result where one exists, and a sentence on what you learned. McKinsey's "two examples" per theme means a second version of the strongest stories.

## Hypotheses tested

**H1. "Every consulting interview is a case."** Verdict: **not supported.** McKinsey's PEI, Bain's fit interview and BCG's behavioural portion are separate, scored stages (employer pages) [employer-stated].

**H2. "Graduate banking interviews include a modelling test."** Verdict: **not supported as a rule.** Employers describe behavioural, technical and video stages; modelling tests and written cases are standard in private equity and reported only at some boutiques and offices at analyst level [practitioner consensus; anecdotal].

**H3. "Quant trading firms test finance knowledge."** Verdict: **not supported.** Jane Street says the interview needs no finance knowledge; probability, problem solving, coding and data analysis are the stated content [employer-stated].

**H4. "Big 4 final stages always contain a case study."** Verdict: **insufficient evidence.** Firms name the final stage but not its exercises; a case is reported at PwC by candidates and guides only [anecdotal].

**H5. "Official practice cases are enough to learn the case types."** Verdict: **partly supported.** The seven official cases cover the six recurring types listed in §1; they do not train the written case or the chatbot format, which no firm read here publishes.

## Common mistakes and myths

1. **"Prepare cases; fit is small talk."** The PEI asks for eight prepared examples; fit stages are scored (McKinsey) [employer-stated].
2. **"Memorise a framework per case type."** Bain and BCG say there is no single right answer and score the reasoning; a recited framework that ignores the client's question is the error both describe [employer-stated].
3. **"You need finance to get into a trading firm."** Jane Street says otherwise [employer-stated].
4. **"Practise Solve for weeks."** McKinsey: "There's no need to prepare" [employer-stated]. Use the time on cases and stories.
5. **"Retake the trading test next month."** Optiver allows assessments once every eight months [employer-stated]; EY one programme application every six months (recruiting-calendar.md §6) [employer-stated].
6. **"A stock pitch is about being right."** Practitioners and Fidelity's own format test the reasoning and the defence of a one-page case, not the later share price [employer-stated format; practitioner consensus].

## Decision rules

1. **If** you target consulting, **prepare** the four McKinsey cases and the two Bain cases before paid material, **and** eight stories for the PEI themes, because both are published and both are scored.
2. **If** a case is interviewer-led, **answer** the question asked and stop; **if** candidate-led, **state** your structure, then say which branch you want to test first and why.
3. **If** you target banking, **learn** the technical core (three statements, enterprise to equity value, DCF, WACC, multiples) and one recent deal per bank, rather than modelling tests, which appear mainly in PE.
4. **If** you target asset management or equity research, **keep** two pitches ready (one long, one short) and a one-page written version.
5. **If** you target quant trading, **practise** mental arithmetic and expected-value problems under time pressure **before** opening the first online test, because a failed attempt locks you out for eight months at Optiver.
6. **If** you target an FMCG scheme, **practise** a two-minute recorded answer to a brand case (launch or turnaround) with a clear recommendation, because Unilever puts the case inside the recorded interview.
7. **If** you meet a "case study" stage with no published format (Big 4, corporates), **ask** the recruiter whether it is written or spoken, individual or group, and how long you have.
