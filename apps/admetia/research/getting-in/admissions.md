# Admissions and applications

## Bottom line

1. **Round 1 is now.** For entry in 2027, HEC's MiM round 1 closes on 7 October 2026, ESSEC's MiF on 14 October, Bocconi's international round 1 on 29 October, and Bocconi's round for Italian non-Bocconi graduates on 2 November (school pages, read 30 Sep 2026) [data]. Bocconi's online test for round 1 must be taken by 28 October, booked by 26 October. An Italian student who has not booked a test this week has effectively missed round 1 at Bocconi.
2. **At Bocconi about 65% of MSc seats go to Bocconi's own bachelor graduates** (Bocconi admissions page, 2026, https://www.unibocconi.it/en/applying-bocconi/master-science-and-ma-programs/application-and-admissions/admissions) [data]. External candidates (Italian and international) compete for the remaining ~35%. A 75-seat class has roughly 26 external places. The two "Global Experience" programmes are the exception: no internal quota applies.
3. **Early rounds matter for funding almost everywhere, and for admission at some schools.** Bocconi says the most sought-after slots and most scholarships go in earlier rounds; LBS says criteria are constant but "competition is often intense in the final months" and ties scholarship consideration to 8 March; Imperial says early application strengthens your scholarship position; LSE programmes are rolling and close when full (school pages, 2026) [data]. No school in this set publishes admit rates by round.
4. **The competitive geography flipped in 2024–25.** In GMAC's surveys the median UK business master's admitted 25% of applicants in 2024 (15% in 2023), while the median continental European one admitted 38% (49% in 2023), and applications to Europe outside the UK "soared" in 2025 as international applicants moved away from the US, UK and Canada (GMAC Application Trends Survey 2024 regional profiles; ATS 2025 report) [data]. Small, changing samples; treat as direction, not level.
5. **GMAT Focus scores run about 50–60 points below old-edition scores for the same percentile.** An old 700 equals Focus 645–655 (86th–90th percentile); a Focus 555, which LBS recommends as a minimum, is the 47th percentile (GMAC concordance table, Aug 2026; percentiles are revised every year, the Aug 2024 edition gave 88th–91st and 49th) [data]. Schools quote targets in both editions, so always convert before comparing.
6. **Selective UK programmes are competitions, not transcript thresholds.** LSE's Master's in Management received 1,125 applications for 75 places (15:1) and MSc Finance 1,581 for 136 (12:1) in 2024; LSE says most MSc Finance admits held a First, which for an Italian laurea LSE equates to 110/110, while the published 2:1 floor is 104/110 (LSE programme and Italy pages, 2026) [data].
7. **Prerequisite credits are gates a test cannot buy back on the continent, but UK schools let a test evidence quant ability.** Bocconi requires missing CFU in named disciplinary sectors to be earned by the round deadline (graduates) or by October 2027 (current students); LSE and Imperial explicitly let a GMAT/GRE stand in as evidence of quantitative ability for their MiM (school pages, 2026) [data].
8. **Deposits are mostly sunk, and the window to decide shrinks as the cycle runs.** LSE takes a non-refundable 10% (MiM) or 15% (MSc Finance) deposit; in July–August the MSc Finance deposit is due within 7 days of offer. Imperial asks £7,000. Bocconi, unusually, lets you request a refund of the first instalment within round-specific deadlines (school pages, 2026) [data].
9. **The GMAT is shrinking but has become a European master's instrument.** 78,429 people took it in TY2024 versus 117,510 in TY2020, but Western European programmes now receive 59% of all GMAT score reports sent to business master's programmes, up from 22% in TY2015 (GMAC Geographic Trend Report TY2024) [data]. For a European MiM applicant, "the GMAT is dying" is the wrong read.
10. **Recorded video is now a standard stage, and schools say they watch for AI.** Imperial runs a recorded online interview for shortlisted MSc candidates and warns that answers found "not genuine" count against you; LBS moves waitlisted MiM candidates to a Kira video assessment; Bocconi offers an optional video and makes it mandatory for CEMS, Asia and ESSEC double-degree tracks (school pages, 2026) [data].

## 1. Tests: GMAT Focus, GRE, TAGE MAGE, the Bocconi test and waivers

### The GMAT Focus scale and what a score means

The GMAT Focus Edition replaced the 10th edition from 31 January 2024. It is scored 205–805 in 10-point steps, with three sections (Quantitative, Verbal, Data Insights) each scored 60–90; geometry, sentence correction and the essay were removed (Wikipedia summary of GMAC changes, accessed 2026, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graduate_Management_Admission_Test) [practitioner consensus — secondary summary of GMAC facts].

The percentile column below was updated on 4 Oct 2026 to GMAC's concordance table published in August 2026 (percentiles from tests taken 1 July 2021 to 30 June 2026; https://go.gmac.com/hubfs/07.Assessments/GMAT%20Exam/GMAT_Total_Concordance_Aug2026.pdf) [data]. A claim circulating in an external audit that Focus 705 is the 99th percentile does not match it: 705 is 97.5% and 715 is 98.6%. Earlier edition: GMAC's concordance table links the two editions using 2017–2022 data, and its percentiles use tests taken between 1 July 2019 and 30 June 2024; GMAC says percentiles are updated each year in the third quarter (GMAC Score Concordance Table, Aug 2024, https://www.gmac.com/-/media/files/gmac/gmat/2024-percentiles/gmat-total-score-concordance-table_august2024.pdf) [data]. The anchors that matter for this reader:

| GMAT Focus | Percentile | Old (10th ed.) equivalent | Who quotes this level |
|---|---|---|---|
| 705 | 97.5% | 750 | — |
| 685 | 95.1% | 730–740 | — |
| 665 | 91.2% | 710–720 | HEC's published median of 710 (old) sits here (see calculator data) |
| 655 | 89.6% | 700–710 | — |

Sources for the right-hand column: LBS MiM apply page (2026, https://www.london.edu/masters-degrees/masters-in-management/apply) [data]; Imperial MSc Management admissions page (2026, https://www.imperial.ac.uk/business-school/masters/management/admissions/) [data].

**Non-obvious:** the top of the Focus scale is compressed. Moving from 655 to 705 takes you from about the 90th to the 97th-98th percentile (89.6% to 97.5% in the Aug 2026 table), and there are "over 20K GMAT test takers with percentiles higher than 89%" per year (GMAC Geographic Trend Report TY2024, https://www.gmac.com/-/media/files/gmac/research/geographic-trends/gmat-geographic-trend-report-testing-year-2024-pdf.pdf) [data]. For a MiM applicant, the marginal value of the last 30–50 points is probably small at schools that publish no average, and the time is better spent on the essays or the interview (inference, not a school statement).

### Is the GMAT losing ground?

| Metric (GMAC testing years run 1 July–30 June) | TY2020 | TY2023 | TY2024 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unique test takers | 117,510 | 74,810 | 78,429 |
| Exams taken | 173,176 | 108,851 | 115,286 |
| Share of score reports sent to business master's | 36% | — | 30% |
| Share of exams by candidates with no work experience | 35% | — | 23% |
| Share of exams by candidates under 25 | 52% | — | 47% |

Source: GMAC Geographic Trend Report TY2024 (2024) [data]. The report attributes much of the TY2020–22 fall to COVID and to schools changing test requirements.

Read the other way, the test's European role grew. Between TY2015 and TY2024, Western European programmes' share of GMAT score reports sent to business master's rose from 22% to 59%, and US programmes' share fell from 68% to 23%. Non-US citizens sent 35.9% of their reports to Western Europe in TY2024, up from 29.4% in TY2020, which GMAC links partly to US work-visa difficulty (same source) [data]. Italy produced 2,333 GMAT exams in TY2024, France 2,343 and Germany 2,830 (same source) [data].

Verdict: the GMAT is losing volume, and the young, no-experience test taker (the MiM applicant) is a shrinking share of takers. But the European pre-experience schools in the calculator have mostly **kept** a test requirement, which is the opposite of the US MBA trend. No GMAC figure for TY2025 volumes could be read this session (the "Current GMAT Volume" page requires a login); see Claims to verify.

### The Bocconi online test: the cheap option with a catch

- 50 multiple-choice questions in 75 minutes across verbal reasoning, data insights and quantitative reasoning. A correct answer scores 1, a blank 0 and a wrong answer loses 0.25 to 0.33 points, so guessing blindly costs you; a total below 15/50 (penalties included) excludes you (Bocconi test page, 2026, https://www.unibocconi.it/en/applying-bocconi/master-science-and-ma-programs/application-and-admissions/online-bocconi-test-international-graduate-applicants) [data].
- €60 per attempt, at most three attempts per academic year, never on the same or consecutive days, remote-proctored by an external platform (same source) [data].
- If you have several scores, Bocconi "will automatically take the most competitive score into account" (Bocconi admissions page, 2026) [data].
- The catch: the Bocconi test is accepted only by Bocconi. A GMAT at about $300 (price not verified this session) is reusable at HEC, LBS, ESSEC, LSE and Imperial. **If Bocconi is one of several targets, the GMAT dominates; if Bocconi is the only target, the Bocconi test is cheaper per attempt and you get three tries.** Its question types mirror GMAT Focus sections, so GMAT preparation transfers.

### TAGE MAGE

HEC and ESSEC accept TAGE MAGE, the French national management test, as one of several options next to the GMAT, GMAT Focus and GRE; it is not compulsory (school pages, 2026) [data]. It is mainly useful for French-system applicants; for an Italian applicant applying beyond France it has no reuse value.

Format, from French test-prep and education sources (not the test owner's page) [practitioner consensus]: 90 questions in six subtests of 15, scored 0 to 600, with +4 for a correct answer, -1 for a wrong one and 0 for a blank. Third-party sources quote minimum and recommended scores of about 400 and 440 for HEC and 380 and 400 for ESSEC; schools set these themselves and change them, so treat the figures as unverified and read the school page. Some circulating summaries give a negative lower bound for the scale or call the test mandatory; neither matches the sources above.

### English-language tests (IELTS, TOEFL) and the waiver trap

A GMAT does not prove English. Schools that teach in English ask for a separate test unless you qualify for a waiver, and waiver rules differ by school, so a degree taught in English is not a universal exemption.

| School | Standard requirement | Waiver rule | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| LSE (standard level, used by most graduate programmes) | IELTS Academic 7.0 overall, 6.5 in each of reading, listening, writing and speaking; TOEFL iBT, PTE Academic and Cambridge C1/C2 also accepted; tests must be under two years old on the 1 October of entry | Waived for nationals and residents of majority English-speaking countries with English as first language, and for degree holders who studied at least three years (undergraduate) in an English-speaking country on UKVI's list. **A degree taught in English in a non-English-speaking country does not count**, and 2+2 degrees with under three years in an English-speaking country or overseas campuses of English institutions do not qualify | LSE English language requirements page, read 4 Oct 2026 [data] |
| Oxford | Standard level IELTS 7.0 overall and 6.5 per component; higher level 7.5 overall and 7.0 per component; all in one sitting. Which level a course needs is set per course, so check the course page | A degree of at least nine months taught in English, completed no more than two academic years before the course, can support a waiver request; no evidence needed for nationals of majority English-speaking countries. Within 0.5 overall and 1 point per component of the minimum, an online pre-sessional can meet the condition | Oxford graduate admissions English-language pages (search result summary; page not read in full) [data, unverified detail] |
| Bocconi | Not read this session | Not read | Claims to verify |

Practical rules: (1) never assume your English-taught triennale waives the test at LSE; read the school's list of countries. (2) LSE says you need not send the score with the application, but the offer can be conditional on it, and the score must still be valid on the entry date, so check the validity window before booking. (3) One sitting must clear every sub-score at schools that say so, so a 7.5 overall with a 6.0 in writing fails.

### What applying actually costs (author calculation)

Applications are a budget line before any tuition. Using the fees in money/costs-and-funding.md section 6: five continental MiM applications (HEC €180, ESSEC €180, ESCP €180, Esade €150, EDHEC €100) come to €790 in application fees. Add two GMAT Focus sittings at about $275 (test centre) to $300 (online) each, from third-party summaries of mba.com that were not read at source, and one IELTS sitting at roughly €235 to €290 depending on centre and date (third-party summaries; British Council prices vary). That is roughly €1,500 to €1,700 for five applications, one English test and two GMAT attempts. It rises with extra score reports beyond the free recipients, sworn translations and apostilles of transcripts, and a second English test; budget closer to €2,000 for a UK-and-France list. Treat this as an order of magnitude: the GMAT and IELTS prices above are unverified and the translation cost is not sourced.

## 2. How competitive are these programmes really?

### What schools publish

| Programme | Applications | Places | Ratio | Year | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| LSE Master's in Management | 1,125 | 75 | 15:1 | 2024 | LSE MiM page [data] |
| LSE MSc Finance | 1,581 | 136 | 12:1 | 2024 | LSE MSc Finance page [data] |

An applications-to-places ratio is not an acceptance rate. Schools make more offers than places, because some admits go elsewhere. With a yield of, say, 50–60% (an assumption, not an LSE figure), LSE's MiM would be admitting roughly 11–13% of applicants. LSE's own framing for MSc Finance is stricter than its entry floor: "In previous years the majority of students accepted into the programme had obtained 1st class honours degrees or the equivalent" (LSE MSc Finance page, 2026) [data].

Other schools in this set (HEC, ESSEC, LBS, Imperial, Bocconi) publish no acceptance rate on the pages read. UK Freedom of Information disclosures of master's offer rates exist for some universities, but none could be read at source in this session; see Claims to verify.

### What the GMAC survey medians say

| Segment (GMAC Application Trends Survey) | Median acceptance 2023 | Median acceptance 2024 | Apps per seat 2024 | Programmes responding 2024 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| UK business master's | 15% | 25% | 10 | 31 |
| Europe excl. UK, business master's | 49% | 38% | 3 | 46 |
| Europe excl. UK, Master in Management | 53% | 45% | 3 | 14 |
| France, all programmes | — | 35% | 3 | 20 |
| Germany, all programmes | — | 51% | 3 | 20 |

Sources: GMAC ATS 2024 UK profile (https://www.gmac.com/-/media/files/gmac/research/admissions-and-application-trends/ats-regional-profiles-united-kingdom-2024.pdf) and Europe profile (https://www.gmac.com/-/media/files/gmac/research/admissions-and-application-trends/ats-regional-profiles-europe-2024.pdf), 2024 [data]. GMAC warns about samples under 30.

**Non-obvious:** for an EU applicant, the UK became relatively easier to enter in 2024–25 (falling international demand after the 2024 dependant-visa changes and fee levels; see places/visas-and-work-rights.md), while France, Italy and Spain became more crowded with non-European applicants. Top-brand UK programmes such as LSE and LBS stay highly selective whatever the median does.

### Why round choice matters differently by school

- **Ranked-list schools with seat quotas per round (Bocconi).** Bocconi says the result depends on a ranking "considering the spots available during each selection round, as well as the programs chosen, the number of applications received" (Bocconi application page, 2026) [data]. So a given score can pass in round 1 and fail in round 4 if the popular programmes have filled. Bocconi also forbids overlapping applications: you cannot apply to two rounds at once, you get at most two applications per year, and the second must show at least one improved element such as a higher test score or new programme choices (Bocconi admissions page, 2026) [data]. **Practical consequence:** round 1 is the only round with a guaranteed retry.
- **Multi-round holistic schools (HEC, ESSEC, LBS, Imperial).** None publishes admit rates by round. LBS says criteria do not change; Imperial and LBS both link scholarships to earlier deadlines. The calculator's model tags HEC as a school where later rounds are harder, citing an HEC statement; that statement was not found on the pages read this session (see Claims to verify).
- **Rolling UK programmes (LSE).** No round advantage in principle, but a full programme closes to everyone. LSE says "We recommend applying as early as possible" (LSE MiM page, 2026) [data].

### Scholarship deadlines tied to rounds

LBS: scholarship applicants "strongly encouraged to apply by no later than Monday 8 March" (LBS apply page, 2026) [data]. Imperial: candidates who apply before the scholarship deadlines and receive an offer are "automatically" considered, unless an award needs a video (Imperial admissions page, 2026) [data]. LSE: funding consideration requires a complete application by the funding deadline (LSE programme pages, 2026) [data]. Detail on awards is in money/costs-and-funding.md.

## 4. What admissions committees say they look for

What schools write on their own pages, read in full this session:

- **LBS MiM:** essays "are a vital part of your application"; they must be specific to this application, and "personal statements will not be accepted in lieu of the essays". LBS wants evidence of motivation, leadership, teamwork, why a MiM, and contribution to the community. It targets graduates within two years of graduating with at most two years of work (LBS apply and programme pages, 2026) [data].
- **HEC MiM:** the interview is where candidates "discuss their unique background, motivation, interests, and goals"; admissibility to interview is judged only on what was submitted by the deadline (HEC admissions page, 2026) [data].
- **ESSEC MiM:** the 45-minute interview aims "to understand you better: your personality and your values" and to test whether your professional project fits the programme (ESSEC admission page, 2026) [data].
- **Imperial MiM:** "a holistic approach", considering "who you are, what motivates you and how you collaborate". A Quantitative Experience Statement listing your most quantitative courses and their level is "a key part of the application form". Imperial also warns that some employers have strict A-level grade screens (Imperial admissions page, 2026) [data].
- **Bocconi:** three compulsory elements (test, GPA, dossier and motivation). The programme director reviews the dossier, which must be in English. Preference goes to applicants still studying or who graduated less than two years before applying; others are "considered at a later stage" (Bocconi admissions page, 2026) [data].
- **LSE:** academic achievement, statement of academic purpose, two references and a CV; for MSc Finance, "good quantitative skills" and at least A-level maths or equivalent (LSE pages, 2026) [data].

The pattern: **UK and Bocconi processes are read by academics or ranked by numbers; French processes are interview-led.** This matches the calculator's profiles. Published admissions-director interviews and blog posts on "what applicants get wrong" (HEC, LBS, ESSEC, St. Gallen, IE) could not be gathered at source this session; see Claims to verify and Common mistakes.

## 5. AI in applications and the shift to recorded video

- **Imperial Business School** puts an "AI/Chat GPT guidance" note next to its recorded video interview: the interview is to see "how you approach problems, your motivations and wider interests", and "If we identify malpractice or answers provided are not genuine then this may factor negatively". It also runs a "zero-tolerance policy to fraudulent applications" and reports findings to the Home Office (Imperial admissions page, 2026) [data].
- **LBS** uses live virtual interviews with alumni or staff and says who interviews you does not change the outcome; waitlisted MiM candidates are "invited to engage with us via Kira, a video interview platform" (LBS apply page, 2026) [data].
- **Bocconi** offers an optional recorded English video answering two set prompts (international attitude; going beyond your comfort zone), and makes a video mandatory for IM CEMS, IM Asia and ESSEC double-degree tracks (Bocconi admissions page, 2026) [data].
- **HEC and ESSEC** interview live on Zoom or videoconference, 30 and 45 minutes (school pages, 2026) [data].

**Non-obvious:** the more a school relies on essays alone, the more AI has devalued the essay as a signal. Schools respond by adding a stage that is hard to outsource: timed recorded answers (Imperial, Kira at LBS) or live interviews (HEC, ESSEC). Expect your essay claims to be tested live. An essay that is fluent but generic, followed by a hesitant interview, is the worst combination. No school read this session publishes an outright ban on AI for drafting essays, nor says which detection tools it uses; see Claims to verify.

## 6. Undergraduate-to-master's fit: 3-year degrees, prerequisites and grade conversion

### Three-year Bologna bachelor's

- HEC accepts "at least a three-year bachelor's degree in any field", and Join a School in France accepts "a minimum three-year degree" from outside France (HEC admissions page, 2026) [data].
- LSE publishes Italian laurea equivalences for taught master's, so the laurea triennale is treated as a full first degree (LSE Italy page, 2026) [data].
- Bocconi requires a "valid and recognized 1st cycle qualification", obtained by October 2027 for 2027 entry (July 2027 for IM Asia China MIM) (Bocconi admissions page, 2026) [data].
- US programmes: some US master's programmes in the calculator are reported to prefer or require the equivalent of a four-year US bachelor's. That could not be verified at source this session; see Claims to verify. The EU/UK pages read raise no such issue.

### Grade conversion: what LSE actually publishes for Italy

- 2:1 equivalent for taught master's: "the laurea with marks of at least **104/110**".
- First-class equivalent: "at least **110/110**".
- Research MPhil/PhD from a laurea magistrale: 106/110.

Source: LSE Italy country page (2026, https://www.lse.ac.uk/study-at-lse/International-students/Italy) [data]. So the commonly quoted "105/110 for a 2:1" is wrong for LSE: the figure is 104. LSE also reports 237 Italian graduate students joining in 2025 (same source) [data].

**Non-obvious, and important for Italians:** most applicants apply before graduating, so they have no final 110-based mark. Bocconi scores Italian applicants on the weighted average (media ponderata) of exams **registered by the round deadline**. Exams taken but not yet registered do not count, and exchange exams count only if already on your record (Bocconi Italian candidates page, 2026) [data]. International applicants convert their own GPA to Bocconi's scale, and "the Admissions Office … reserves the right to recalculate the GPA" (Bocconi admissions page, 2026) [data]. At LSE and other UK schools an offer before graduation is conditional, typically on the final grade. For MSc Finance, since most admits hold a First, the effective target is 110/110 even though the floor is 104. The calculator's 30-point converter handles the numbers; see places/italy-playbook.md for the lode and triennale specifics.

### Prerequisite gaps and bridging

- **Bocconi** states direct-access bachelor's classes for each MSc; other degree classes must show a minimum number of CFU in named disciplinary sectors (SSD), for example "at least 24 CFU" in listed sectors for some programmes. Graduates must meet the curricular requirements by the round deadline, for example through single courses (corsi singoli). Current students can fill gaps in their own study plan by the graduation deadline (Bocconi Italian candidates page, 2026) [data]. This is a hard gate that a test score does not waive.
- **LSE MSc Finance** requires at least A-level maths or equivalent (basic calculus and statistics) (LSE MSc Finance page, 2026) [data].
- **Imperial** lets you evidence quant through the Quantitative Experience Statement and transcript, and names its quant-heavy programmes (Finance, Finance & Accounting, FinTech, Risk Management & Financial Engineering, Business Analytics & AI, among others) where quant experience is "a key selection criterion" (Imperial admissions page, 2026) [data].
- **ESSEC's Flexible MiM track** starts with refresher courses (accounting, economics, finance, maths, statistics and more), assigned by background. Intensive-track students are exempt (ESSEC admission page, 2026) [data]. This is bridging built into the programme rather than a gate.

## 7. References, CVs and interviews

| School | References | CV | Interview |
|---|---|---|---|
| LBS MiM | Exactly one; professional preferred, academic acceptable | One page, LBS template suggested | Virtual, alumni or staff, same criteria; Kira video if waitlisted |
| HEC MiM | One confidential academic reference; ask 4+ weeks ahead | One page | 30 min on Zoom with programme director(s) and alumni, in English |
| ESSEC MiM | Online recommendation via link | — | 45 min videoconference after shortlisting |
| Imperial MiM | Referees notified on submission; delays can push your decision date | CV template | Recorded online, about 20 min |
| LSE | Two references | CV (for MSc Finance, show maths level and grades) | None on the standard route |
| Bocconi | None on the standard route | Dossier | None; optional video (mandatory on special tracks) |

Sources: school pages as above, 2026 [data].

**Non-obvious operational risks:**
- At HEC and LBS your file is incomplete, and cannot progress, until the **referee** has submitted by the deadline. HEC advises asking four weeks ahead. Imperial says a decision "may" miss the round date if references are delayed or documents are missing for more than five working days (school pages, 2026) [data]. A slow professor is a real risk to a round-1 application.
- LBS allows **one** reference and prefers a professional one. An internship manager who can speak to performance beats a famous professor who barely knows you (LBS apply page, 2026) [data].
- HEC and LBS judge the interview shortlist on the file "as is" at the deadline. There is no chance to add a better test score afterwards (school pages, 2026) [data].

## 8. Internal candidates: who really gets the seats

- **Bocconi:** "Approximately 65% of the total available spots are made available to Bocconi applicants." Bocconi graduates have their own two rounds: a main round assigning "a maximum of 60% of spots" and a residual round of about 5% (Bocconi admissions and current-students pages, 2026, https://www.unibocconi.it/en/current-students/application-and-admissions) [data]. Classes have 50–75 places; International Management has 480 across six class groups, including 65 for CEMS MIM. The IM Global Experience and Finance Global Experience programmes select for nationality, gender and background diversity, and "Bocconi applicants will not be assigned the usual 65%" there (Bocconi admissions page, 2026) [data].
- **What this means:** an external applicant to a 75-seat Bocconi MSc is competing for about 26 seats, split across four international rounds and two Italian rounds. The internal share is not bad news in itself: it means Bocconi graduates compete in a separate ranking, not against you. But it makes any headline "Bocconi MSc acceptance rate" meaningless for outsiders.
- **St. Gallen, Mannheim, RSM, Warwick:** internal-share figures could not be read at source this session (see Claims to verify). The calculator notes that RSM closes at 300 applications for some programmes, which is a capacity cap rather than an internal quota.

## 9. Deposits, conditional offers and the decision timeline

| School | Deposit | Deadline to pay | Refundable? |
|---|---|---|---|
| LSE MiM | 10% of tuition (£42,900 → £4,290) | Within six weeks of offer | No |
| LSE MSc Finance | 15% of tuition in two instalments | Offer by 30 Apr: 10% within 28 days, 5% by 1 June. Later offers: all 15% within 21 days (May), 14 days (June), 7 days (July–Aug) | No |
| Imperial MiM | £7,000 | After meeting offer conditions and a Qualification Check | Not stated on page read; carried over if you defer (once) |
| Bocconi MSc | First instalment ("advanced payment") at enrolment | About three weeks after results (enrolment period 2–3 weeks) | Refund can be requested within round-specific deadlines |

Sources: LSE MiM and MSc Finance pages; Imperial admissions page; Bocconi admissions and application pages — all 2026 [data].

**How to hold several offers:**
- Bocconi explicitly allows you to choose among multiple Bocconi offers, but you cannot reapply in the same year once admitted, and you cannot change programme after the enrolment deadline (Bocconi admissions page, 2026) [data].
- Imperial asks you not to decline other offers until the official offer is confirmed on its portal, since central admissions runs final checks (Imperial admissions page, 2026) [data].
- The clock problem: HEC round 1 results arrive by 19 November, Bocconi international round 1 by 17 December, LSE decisions roll. An early Bocconi admit must enrol within about three weeks, often before HEC round 2 (results by 1 February) or LBS decisions. Bocconi's refund window makes it the cheapest offer to hold; LSE's non-refundable deposit makes it the most expensive to hold "just in case", especially late in the cycle when the payment window shrinks to 7 days.

## Hypotheses tested

**H1. At UK one-year MSc programmes, admission is mostly a transcript threshold, not a competition.**
Verdict: **partly supported — false at the top, plausible below it.** At LSE the published floor (2:1 = 104/110) is far below the typical admit: MSc Finance says most admits held Firsts, and the MiM and MSc Finance had 15:1 and 12:1 application ratios in 2024 (LSE pages, 2026) [data]. The median UK business master's in GMAC's sample admitted 25% in 2024 (GMAC ATS 2024 UK) [data]. Holds for: less selective UK programmes with rolling admission and no interview (not verified this session). Fails for: LSE, LBS and Imperial, which rank, interview or both.

**H2. Applying in round 1 materially raises the odds at French grandes écoles.**
Verdict: **insufficient evidence for admission odds; supported for funding and for retry options.** HEC and ESSEC publish four rounds and no per-round admit rates (school pages, 2026) [data]. The clearest published round effect is at Bocconi, not France: sought-after slots and most scholarships go early, and only an early rejection leaves room for the one permitted retry (Bocconi, 2026) [data]. Holds where seats are allocated per round from a ranked list. Fails as a blanket claim: LBS says criteria do not change by round.

**H3. A high GMAT cannot compensate for missing prerequisites.**
Verdict: **supported for formal credit gates; not supported for UK "evidence of quant" requirements.** Bocconi's CFU-by-sector requirements must be met by the round deadline regardless of test score (Bocconi Italian page, 2026) [data]. At LSE MiM and Imperial MiM, a GMAT/GRE is explicitly a way to evidence quant ability when your degree does not (LSE MiM page; Imperial page, 2026) [data]. So the test replaces missing quant evidence in the UK but not missing credits on the continent.

**H4. Internal candidates take most seats at some continental programmes, so external admit rates are lower than headline rates.**
Verdict: **supported for Bocconi; untested elsewhere.** About 65% of Bocconi MSc seats go to Bocconi graduates (Bocconi, 2026) [data]. St. Gallen, Mannheim and RSM are unverified.

**H5. The GMAT Focus has made scores harder to compare, and schools now weight the test less.**
Verdict: **partly supported.** Comparison is harder in practice, because schools quote a mix of editions: Imperial gives its average in old-edition points (653) alongside a percentile floor, LBS gives a Focus number (555), and Bocconi gives both (school pages, 2026) [data]. GMAC's concordance makes conversion possible (GMAC, 2024) [data]. On weighting, the evidence is mixed. Imperial made the test non-obligatory for its MiM and LSE exempts UK-degree holders, but HEC, LBS, ESSEC and Bocconi still require a test [data]. No school publishes a change in weight.

**H6 (own). An Italian applicant is judged on the final 110-point mark.**
Verdict: **not supported at the application stage.** Bocconi ranks Italians on the weighted average of exams registered by the round deadline; LSE's 104/110 and 110/110 equivalences mainly govern offer conditions for applicants who have not yet graduated (Bocconi Italian page; LSE Italy page, 2026) [data]. Implication: registering exams before the round deadline can move a Bocconi ranking; the final 110 matters for meeting a UK condition.

**H7 (own). Offer deposits are a sunk cost that makes holding multiple offers expensive.**
Verdict: **supported for UK schools, not for Bocconi.** LSE is non-refundable (10–15%) and Imperial asks £7,000; Bocconi lets you request a refund of the first instalment within set deadlines (school pages, 2026) [data].

**H8 (own). Continental Europe got more competitive and the UK less so in 2024–25.**
Verdict: **partly supported.** GMAC medians moved in opposite directions: UK business master's acceptance went from 15% to 25%, continental from 49% to 38%. In 2025 international applications fell at UK programmes and "soared" in continental Europe (GMAC ATS 2024 profiles; ATS 2025) [data]. Caveat: different programmes answer each year, and samples are small.

**H9 (own). Recorded video interviews exist mainly to check that the written application is the candidate's own work.**
Verdict: **partly supported.** Imperial ties its video interview to explicit AI/ChatGPT guidance and says answers that are "not genuine" count against you (Imperial, 2026) [data]. Bocconi frames its video as a chance for international applicants to add information, not a check (Bocconi, 2026) [data]. So verification is one purpose, not the only one.

## Common mistakes and myths

1. **"LSE needs 105/110."** LSE's published 2:1 equivalent is 104/110; a First is 110/110 (LSE Italy page, 2026) [data]. For MSc Finance, aim for First-equivalent anyway.
2. **"Bocconi's acceptance rate is X%."** Headline rates mix internal and external candidates, while about 65% of seats are reserved for Bocconi graduates (Bocconi, 2026) [data].
3. **Comparing a Focus score with an old-edition average.** A Focus 655 is not "below" an old 700; they are the same percentile band (GMAC concordance, 2024) [data].
4. **Waiting for the "perfect" GMAT and missing round 1.** HEC and LBS judge the file as it stands at the deadline; Bocconi requires the score to exist before you apply (school pages, 2026) [data]. A round-1 file with a good score often beats a round-3 file with a great one at schools that allocate seats or scholarships by round (inference from the round rules above).
5. **Treating the Bocconi test as a free practice run.** You get three attempts a year, never on consecutive days, and dates near deadlines "sell out quickly" (Bocconi test page, 2026) [data].
6. **Assuming references take care of themselves.** Your file is incomplete until the referee submits; HEC advises four weeks' notice (HEC, 2026) [data].
7. **Applying to two Imperial or LBS programmes as a hedge.** Both allow one programme per cycle (Imperial may consider you for alternatives on its own initiative) (school pages, 2026) [data].
8. **Paying an LSE deposit "to be safe" in July.** It is non-refundable, and late offers leave 7 days to pay (LSE MSc Finance, 2026) [data].
9. **Letting an AI polish your essays into something you cannot defend live.** Imperial warns that non-genuine answers count against you, and HEC and ESSEC test motivation in live interviews (school pages, 2026) [data]. Forum advice (GMAT Club, Reddit) says the same [anecdotal — not read at source this session].

## Decision rules

1. **If Bocconi is a target and you are in a non-Bocconi Italian bachelor's, apply in the first Italian round (closes 2 November 2026)**, because it is one of only two rounds for your category and only an unsuccessful first application leaves room for the single permitted retry.
2. **If Bocconi is one of three or more targets, take the GMAT rather than the Bocconi test**, because only the GMAT (or GRE) is reusable at HEC, LBS, ESSEC, LSE and Imperial.
3. **If Bocconi is your only target and your quant is solid, use the Bocconi test** (€60, three attempts a year) and book early, because slots near deadlines sell out.
4. **If you are comparing your score with a school figure, convert both to percentiles first**, using GMAC's concordance table (old 700 ≈ Focus 645–655).
5. **If your Focus score is at or above about 655 (91st percentile), stop retesting for MiM applications** unless a school publishes a higher median; put the time into essays and interview preparation, because the percentile gain per point is small at the top.
6. **If your degree lacks the required CFU/ECTS in a sector at a continental school, fix the credits (single courses, study-plan changes) before the round deadline**, because no test score waives a curricular requirement.
7. **If your degree lacks visible quant at a UK MiM (LSE, Imperial), submit a GMAT/GRE with a strong quant section**, because these schools treat the test as quant evidence.
8. **If you are Italian and targeting LSE MSc Finance, plan on a First-equivalent (110/110)**, because most admits hold Firsts; 104/110 only makes you eligible.
9. **If you want scholarship consideration at LBS, submit by 8 March at the latest; at Imperial and LSE, by the published funding deadlines**, because consideration is tied to those dates.
10. **If you hold an early Bocconi offer and are waiting on HEC, LBS or LSE, enrol at Bocconi and note the refund deadline**, because Bocconi's first instalment can be refunded within set windows, unlike an LSE deposit.
11. **If an LSE offer arrives after 1 May, decide within the deposit window (21, 14 or 7 days) before other results arrive, or accept losing the place**, because the deposit is non-refundable.
12. **If a programme is rolling (LSE), apply in the first months the portal is open**, because it closes once full, whatever your profile.
13. **If the school reads the file "as is" at the deadline (HEC, LBS), line up your referee four or more weeks ahead and have test scores in hand before submitting**, because nothing added later counts for that round.
14. **If a school uses recorded or live interviews (Imperial, LBS, HEC, ESSEC), write essays you can expand on unscripted**, because the interview is where AI-assisted claims get tested.
