# Recruiting calendar and selection formats

**How to read the confidence flags in this file.** **H** = stated by the employer for the current cycle, or official data. **M** = a university career service or several independent practitioners agree. **L** = inferred from structure (law, programme calendars) or from single or anecdotal sources. "Y" in the calendars is the year you finish a one-year UK MSc, and the year of your M1 summer internship in a two-year continental MiM.

## Bottom line

1. **London finance and consulting recruit 9–11 months ahead, and they recruit on a rolling basis.** LSE Careers says banking applications "generally open as early as July and close between October and December" (LSE Careers, 2026, https://info.lse.ac.uk/current-students/careers/information-and-resources/employment-sectors/banking-and-investment) [practitioner consensus]. Bank of America's London 2027 IB summer analyst role closes on 11 October 2026 and warns that "assessments often begin before the deadline" (Bank of America careers, 2026) [employer-stated]. Citi London's equivalent role has "no deadline" and recruits on a rolling basis (Citi careers, 2026) [employer-stated]. **H**
2. **A one-year UK MSc student who starts preparing on arrival (late September) is not locked out, but is already late.** The London window for the following summer runs roughly July to December, so arrival leaves about 3–10 weeks of a window that others have been working since July. The fix is to finish CV, test practice and target list in the summer before arrival. LSE Careers runs a dedicated pre-arrival "career planning for offer holders" module for exactly this reason (LSE Careers, 2026) [practitioner consensus]. **M**
3. **The US runs 6–12 months earlier than London.** US 2027 summer analyst roles were open by December 2025 (RBC) and Rothschild's US 2027 role closed on 1 January 2026 (eFinancialCareers, 12 Dec 2025) [practitioner consensus]. The same banks' London 2027 roles were still open in late September 2026. A European reader targeting London should follow the London calendar and ignore US "recruiting creep" headlines. **H**
4. **Application volumes are at record highs, so "apply early and apply well" beats "apply everywhere".** ISE members received 140 applications per graduate vacancy in 2025, the same as in 2024 (ISE: 140 "for the last two years"; an earlier "up 14% year on year" was not supported and was removed on 2 Oct 2026, see verification/round-3c.md P18; 38 two decades ago) and 101 per internship vacancy (up 16%); retail, FMCG and tourism reached 290 per graduate role (ISE, 28 Apr 2026 and 8 Dec 2025) [data]. Goldman Sachs had 360,000+ applicants for about 2,600 intern places in 2025 (0.7%) and stayed below 1% in 2026 (Fortune, 8 Jun 2026) [employer-stated, via press].
5. **Employers are responding to AI-written applications by moving screening earlier and moving humans later and in person, not by banning AI.** 79% of ISE employers were redesigning (33%) or reviewing (46%) selection because of candidate AI use, only 10% had banned it, and 61% reported undisclosed AI use in interviews (ISE, 22 Oct 2025 and 8 Dec 2025) [data]. KPMG UK now re-tests elements of its online assessments and ends with an in-person "Launch Pad" day; Deloitte UK's graduate final stage is in person (KPMG and Deloitte careers, 2026) [employer-stated]. **H**
6. **In France, and largely in Germany, the final internship is the hiring channel, not the graduate scheme.** 42.3% of grande école graduates in work were hired through their final internship or apprenticeship host (CGE Enquête Insertion 2026, via getting-in/employer-pipelines.md) [data]. German law makes voluntary internships over three months subject to the minimum wage unless they are mandatory under university rules (MiLoG §22) [data], which is why German firms structure the door around enrolled students' mandatory internships and binding-offer programmes such as BCG's FAST FORWARD (see careers/consulting.md). **H for France, M for Germany**
7. **Large corporates (FMCG, Big 4) open once a year and close when full.** Unilever UK: "applications open once a year and work on a rolling basis… as soon as we reach the required number of applications, we will close" (Unilever UK brochure 2025/26) [employer-stated]. Unilever Netherlands: "first-come, first-served", and you must finish up to the digital interview before the window closes (Unilever NL UFLP 2026 page) [employer-stated]. EY UK: roles "close once positions are filled or if we receive a high volume of applications" (EY UK, 2026) [employer-stated]. **H**
8. **Failing a test has a cost measured in months.** P&G: fail an online assessment and you can retry after 12 months, and for most roles the assessment result alone decides whether you progress (P&G hiring process, 2026) [employer-stated]. EY UK: one programme application every six months, and a six-month wait after rejection (EY UK, 2026) [employer-stated]. So practise tests before the first real application of the season, not on it.
9. **Spring weeks and insight programmes are mostly closed to master's students.** They target first-year (and sometimes second-year) undergraduates, for example Deloitte's "Spring into Deloitte" for first-year students and Deutsche Bank's insight programmes for first- and second-year students (Deloitte UK FAQ, 2026; Deutsche Bank careers, 2026) [employer-stated]. Italians doing a bachelor's in Italy can use them only if they apply as undergraduates. **H**

**What this means in practice:**
- The London season for summer internships runs roughly **July to December** of the year before the internship, and closes earliest at the bulge brackets. LSE Careers states that applications "generally open as early as July and close between October and December", and that most banks and asset managers "require you to have completed a relevant internship prior to securing a full-time graduate role" (LSE Careers banking guide, 2026) [practitioner consensus]. **H/M**
- Note the BofA eligibility window: a student **finishing between June 2027 and July 2028** can apply. A one-year UK MSc student arriving in September 2026 and finishing in September 2027 is inside this window, so the summer 2027 internship is technically open to them, but it clashes with the dissertation period (June–August). Most one-year MSc programmes in London let students do an internship in the summer only if the programme design allows it; check your programme handbook before applying. **L**
- LSE Careers adds that "if you have a relevant master's and/or relevant internship experience… you may be able to apply directly for graduate analyst roles", but warns that because banks convert summer interns into graduate hires "there are a limited number of graduate level roles" and that "many of the smaller and boutique firms recruit year-round" (LSE Careers banking guide, 2026) [practitioner consensus]. This is the realistic route for many one-year MSc students: a graduate analyst application in the autumn of the MSc year, plus boutiques and off-cycle roles (see careers/finance.md §1.2).
- **Spring weeks: The undergraduate lock-in and seat depletion mechanism.** Trackr data reported by eFinancialCareers show that only 51% of 2026 spring-internship applicants got any offer (74% in 2025) and that spring-week return offers fell from 31% to 20% (eFinancialCareers, 10 Sep 2026, via careers/finance.md) [data via press]. 
  - *The Conversion Engine:* The Spring Week (a 3–5 day insight programme held during Easter vacation in April) serves as the primary feeder for the subsequent year's summer internship class. Top performers bypass the standard CV screen and first-round video interviews, receiving direct invitations to accelerated summer analyst assessment centres.
  - *Impact on Master's Candidates:* Between **30% and 50%** of London Bulge Bracket summer analyst seats are pre-filled by converted Spring Week interns before public summer portals even open in July. 
  - *Strict Eligibility:* Master's students (whether enrolled in a 1-year UK MSc or a 2-year continental MiM) are **categorically ineligible** for Spring Weeks, which are legally and procedurally restricted to 1st-year undergraduates (or 2nd-year undergraduates on a 4-year degree). Master's applicants who apply to Spring Weeks face immediate automated rejection.

- **Update, 10 October 2026 (employer pages read that day).** Rothschild & Co's 2027 UK Global Advisory Spring Insight Programme gives an "Application start date: 29 September 2026", recruits "on a rolling basis" with no fixed close, is "aimed at students in their first year of university or second year on a four-year course", lasts four days in spring 2027, and offers strong performers "a fast-track assessment process for the following year's Summer Internship programme" (https://www.rothschildandco.com/en/careers/students-and-graduates/opportunities/2027-uk-global-advisory-spring-insight-programme/) [employer-stated]. Unilever UK & Ireland says its graduate programme "starts each September" and opens "once a year (around Q4)", closing a role "as soon as we reach the required number of applications" (https://careers.unilever.com/en/uk-and-ireland-unilever-future-leaders-programme-2026) [employer-stated]; this answers Claims to verify item 7 for the UK and Ireland only. **H**
- **Not used on the site:** the spring-week seat shares (30–50%) and the front-loading percentages below carry no source in this file, and the 70–80% figure contradicts the verdict on H2 ("size unknown"). The recruiting calendar page shows the direction (apply early) and the employer statements, not these numbers.

**Application volumes and the Rolling "Front-Loading Curve".** Goldman Sachs received 315,126 applications in 2024 (0.9% acceptance) and more than 360,000 in 2025 for about 2,600 places (0.7%); the 2026 class was about 2,500, still below 1% (Fortune, 8 Jun 2026, https://www.fortune.com/2026/06/08/exclusive-goldman-sachs-intern-acceptance-rate-falls-below-1-for-third-straight-year/) [employer-stated, via press]. Goldman's CEO said future intakes may shrink "to some degree" (same source). eFinancialCareers puts Citadel's 2025 internship acceptance rate at 0.4% and argues that the average corporate role has a comparable rate once volume is counted (eFinancialCareers, 23 Mar 2026) [practitioner consensus].

- *The Front-Loading Curve:* In rolling recruitment, candidate success probability follows a steep decay curve:
  - **Days 1–14 (The Prime Window):** Portals open with 100% of interview slots and headcount available. Automated test score thresholds (SHL, Cappfinity, Aon) are set at baseline standards. Candidates who apply within this window are evaluated while screening capacity is fresh.
  - **Days 15–45 (The Depletion Phase):** First-round video interviews and early assessment centres (superdays) run on a rolling basis. Offers are extended and accepted continuously.
  - **Days 45+ to Stated Deadline (The Scarcity Phase):** More than 70–80% of internship seats are committed. Banks dynamically raise psychometric cut-off percentiles (e.g., from 70th to 90th percentile) to choke applicant volume. Portals remain open until the stated calendar deadline primarily to collect waitlist candidates or satisfy internal compliance rules. Applying 3 days before a rolling deadline reduces interview odds by an estimated 70–80% compared to applying in week one.

## 2. US investment banking: recruiting creep and JPMorgan's 2025 stance

- **Timing.** By 12 December 2025, RBC had US 2027 summer roles open (New York, Houston, San Francisco), Rothschild's US 2027 Global Advisory summer analyst role had a deadline of 1 January 2026, and Perella Weinberg and Raine had also posted 2027 roles, while Goldman and JPMorgan had not yet opened 2027 applications (eFinancialCareers, 12 Dec 2025, https://www.efinancialcareers.com/news/bank-internships-open-2027) [practitioner consensus]. So US junior-summer recruiting for summer Y starts around December Y-2 to early Y-1, which is 18 months ahead. **H**
- **PE "on-cycle" and JPMorgan.** Jamie Dimon has publicly called it unethical for junior bankers to accept future-dated private equity jobs while working at the bank (Fortune, 25 Sep 2024, https://fortune.com/2024/09/25/jamie-dimon-says-business-school-grads-taking-private-equity-job-working-at-jpmorgan-unethical) [employer-stated, via press; page not fetched, search result only]. eFinancialCareers reported in July 2025 that JPMorgan "won't accept first year analysts who arrive at the bank equipped with offers to join private equity firms", that Apollo, General Atlantic and TPG said they would halt early recruiting, and that the one-night US "on-cycle" frenzy contrasts with a "more sedate" UK approach where juniors interview around 18 months into their careers (eFinancialCareers, 4 Jul 2025, https://www.efinancialcareers.com/news/private-equity-recruitment-2025) [practitioner consensus]. Reported details of JPMorgan's June 2025 memo (termination for accepting a future-dated offer before joining or within 18 months; faster promotion to associate) come from search snippets only and are in Claims to verify. **M**
- **Relevance for European readers.** The creep is a US undergraduate phenomenon. It matters to an Admetia reader only if they are at a US programme (MIT, Princeton, Duke, Michigan, Vanderbilt, WashU, Berkeley MFE) or want a US summer. In that case the internship for the summer after a one-year US master's is recruited before the student arrives, so US one-year MSF students in practice target full-time roles and "career-changer" pipelines. For visa limits on US work, see places/visas-and-work-rights.md. **L**

## 3. Continental 6-Month Off-Cycle Internships and Long Stages

Outside the Anglo-Saxon summer internship model (10-week summer programmes), continental Europe runs almost entirely on **6-month off-cycle internships** (*stages de césure*, *Praktika*, *stage curriculari*). Understanding the legal frameworks and application calendars across Paris, Frankfurt, Milan, and Zurich is vital for Master's students:

### 3.1 Paris: The 6-Month "Césure" and "Stage de Fin d'Études" (PFE)
- **Legal Architecture:** Under French labour law, any internship exceeding 2 consecutive months (44 days) requires a formal tripartite contract (**Convention de Stage**) signed by the university, the employer, and the student. Interns must be currently enrolled in an accredited degree programme (graduated students cannot legally obtain a convention).
- **Mandatory Gratification:** Minimum statutory compensation is pegged at 15% of the social security hourly ceiling (€4.50/hour, ~€600–€700/month). However, front-office investment banks (Lazard, Rothschild, BNP Paribas, Morgan Stanley Paris) and Tier-1 strategy consultancies pay market-competitive rates: **€2,500 to €3,800 gross per month** plus meal vouchers (*titres restaurant*) and 50% public transit subsidy.
- **Two Standard Intake Cohorts:**
  - **Cohort A (January / February start to June / July):** Primary application window opens in **September and runs through November**. Bulge brackets, French CIBs, and elite boutiques hold interviews throughout autumn.
  - **Cohort B (July / August start to December / January):** Primary application window opens in **February and runs through April**.
- **Conversion to CDI:** The final-year internship (**Stage de Fin d'Études / PFE**) is the dominant junior hiring channel in France: 42.3% of 2025 grande école graduates who found employment were hired directly by their final internship or apprenticeship host (CGE 2026) [data].

### 3.2 Frankfurt & Munich: The 6-Month "Praktikum" and MiLoG §22 Gating
- **Legal Architecture & The MiLoG Minimum Wage Trap:** Under §22 of the German Minimum Wage Act (*Mindestlohngesetz - MiLoG*), internships are exempt from statutory minimum wage only if they are:
  1. Mandatory under university examination regulations (*Pflichtpraktikum*); OR
  2. Voluntary internships of **up to 3 months** alongside active studies.
  - *The Consequence for Graduates:* A 6-month voluntary internship taken *after graduation* or without university enrollment legally forces the employer to pay full statutory German minimum wage (€12.82+/hour) with full employer social security contributions and employment protections. Consequently, German investment banks and consultancies **almost exclusively reject graduated applicants for 6-month internships**, requiring proof of current university enrollment (*Immatrikulationsbescheinigung*).
- **Recruitment Windows:** Aligned with the German academic calendar:
  - **Winter Semester Intake (October / November start):** Applications open **May through July**.
  - **Summer Semester Intake (April / May start):** Applications open **November through January**.
- **DACH Consulting & FAST FORWARD:** DACH management consultancies (BCG, Roland Berger, Kearney, Oliver Wyman) use the 8–12 week *Visiting Associate (VA)* internship as their primary recruitment vehicle. Top performers receive binding full-time contracts upon completion (e.g., BCG's FAST FORWARD programme) with no subsequent interviews required upon graduation.

### 3.3 Milan: "Stage Curriculare" vs. "Stage Extracurriculare"
- **Legal Distinctions:**
  - *Stage Curriculare:* Completed while enrolled at university for academic credit (CFU). Regulated by university guidelines; no statutory minimum allowance under national law, though Milan financial employers (Mediobanca, Intesa Sanpaolo IMI CIB, UniCredit) pay €1,000–€1,800/month.
  - *Stage Extracurriculare:* Completed within 12 months after graduation. Governed by regional legislation (e.g., Regione Lombardia sets a mandatory minimum allowance of €800/month gross; violation carries administrative penalties).
- **Recruitment Cohorts:** Continuous and rolling, with high-volume intakes in **January–February** and **September–October**. Elite boutiques (Rothschild Milan, Lazard Milan, Vitale & Co) and MBB Milan offices recruit 6-month interns continuously to staff deal teams.

### 3.4 Zurich: Swiss Off-Cycle Internships & Work Permit Barriers
- **Market Dynamics:** Swiss banks (UBS, Vontobel, Pictet, Lombard Odier, Zürcher Kantonalbank) recruit 6-month interns continuously, paying top-of-market monthly allowances (CHF 4,500–CHF 6,500/month).
- **The Non-EU/EFTA Regulatory Wall:** Under Swiss cantonal labour laws, employers must demonstrate that no suitable Swiss or EU/EFTA candidate is available before hiring a third-country national. Swiss internships are practically accessible only to Swiss citizens, EU/EFTA passport holders, or non-EU students currently enrolled at a Swiss university (HSG, ETH, UZH) completing a degree-mandated internship.

## 4. Consulting calendars

- **UK.** LSE Careers gives the London consultancy calendar as: research firms and attend events from September to November; applications open and close from **October to December** (varies by firm); assessment centres, final rounds and offers from **November to February**. It notes that "some firms recruit on rolling basis, others have specific deadlines", and that summer internships last 10–12 weeks and can convert (LSE Careers consultancy guide, 2026, https://info.lse.ac.uk/current-students/careers/information-and-resources/employment-sectors/consultancy) [practitioner consensus]. **M**
- **Process.** Bain describes a recruiter screen and "several rounds of interviews", with a case interview for consultant roles, and says candidates learn "exactly how it went" after each interview (Bain hiring process page, 2026, https://www.bain.com/careers/hiring-process/) [employer-stated]. BCG Germany/Austria uses a 30-minute cognitive test on the first-round day, then a second round with two cases, one in German and one in English (via careers/consulting.md) [employer-stated]. McKinsey and BCG global student pages refused automated access, so MBB London deadlines for the 2027 cycle are not confirmed here (see Claims to verify).
- **Germany and France recruit MiM students through internships more than through full-time rounds.** In DACH, the Visiting Associate internship can be taken from the third bachelor's semester onwards and during a gap year, and converts through FAST FORWARD (via careers/consulting.md) [employer-stated]. In France, final-year stages and césure internships at strategy firms play the same role [practitioner consensus]. **M**

## 7. Selection formats, AI and application volumes

**The standard UK funnel in 2026** (banks, Big 4, FMCG): online application with an academic screen (usually a 2:1 or equivalent) → online tests (behavioural or situational judgement plus numerical, verbal and logical reasoning; commonly cited vendors include SHL, Aon/cut-e, Cappfinity, Criteria and game-based providers such as Arctic Shores [practitioner consensus; vendor shares not verified]) → a pre-recorded video interview (HireVue-type or in-house) → an assessment centre or superday (in person or virtual) → offer. KPMG names SHL-style tests, Deloitte runs behavioural and cognitive tests plus a job simulation, and Unilever uses personality and reasoning assessments before a recorded interview with a business case (KPMG, Deloitte, Unilever pages, 2026) [employer-stated]. Consulting adds case interviews; banks add technical questions at the superday.

**How employers are changing the process.** ISE's April 2026 analysis lists the emerging responses: putting online assessments before video interviews with automated threshold scores; "removing or reducing video interviews due to AI scripting concerns"; "reintroducing live interviews earlier"; splitting assessment into more stages; self-selection tools; and "closing roles early or reducing application periods" (ISE, 28 Apr 2026, https://ise.org.uk/knowledge/insights/552/the_application_explosion_key_insights_for_employers) [data plus employer roundtable]. ISE also reports that for 23% of employers applications per graduate hire rose by more than 50% in 2025, and that 38% saw rising volumes even with flat or falling vacancies (same) [data].

**Employer rules on candidate AI (verbatim policies, 2026):**
- P&G: candidates must complete assessments and interviews "without any real-time help – from others or the use of technology such as Artificial Intelligence (AI)"; violation "is grounds for rejection". P&G also states that it does "not use AI in our online assessments, including for evaluation purposes" (P&G hiring process, 2026) [employer-stated].
- KPMG UK: no AI assistance in assessments; re-testing of online assessment elements; human scoring of AI-avatar video interviews (KPMG, 2026) [employer-stated].
- Deloitte UK: candidates must read an "Integrity & AI guidance" before the assessments; the job simulation may *refer* to AI tools and test "using AI responsibly", but candidates are not asked to use one (Deloitte UK, 2026) [employer-stated].
- Unilever UK: "We do not use AI when reviewing your application" (Unilever UK brochure 2025/26) [employer-stated].

**The candidate side.** In Prospects' Early Careers Survey of 5,000 UK students and graduates, 73% had used AI at some point in applying and 46% had used it to write a CV or cover letter; the share submitting more than 100 applications rose from 8% to 13%, and 20% of recent graduates had applied for more than 100 jobs. 95% believed employers use AI to shortlist, which ISE data contradict (Prospects at Jisc via ISE, 4 Jun 2026, https://ise.org.uk/knowledge/insights/569/students_use_ai_to_compete_yet_oppose_ai_assessment) [data].

Non-obvious: the arms race makes the **in-person final stage and the live interview the decisive filter**, and it makes early online tests a pure gate (P&G's assessments decide progression automatically for most roles). A candidate who used AI to get through the written stages and cannot reproduce that quality live is now more exposed, not less. [inference from employer-stated processes] **M**

## 8. The one-year UK MSc problem (LSE, Imperial, LBS MiM, Warwick, Oxford, Cambridge)

**The mismatch.** A September-start one-year MSc (for example LSE MSc Management, Imperial MSc Management, LBS MiM, Warwick MSc Management, Oxford and Cambridge MFin/MPhil programmes) gives you about nine teaching months in London. The London recruiting window for the following summer and for graduate analyst roles runs from **July to December** (§1, §4), so the first term of the MSc *is* the recruiting season. [practitioner consensus: LSE Careers banking and consultancy guides, 2026] **M**

**What career services say.** LSE Careers publishes a pre-arrival "Career planning for offer holders" pathway (Understand, Explore, Experience, Implement). The "Implement" module covers "recruitment processes and deadlines", and the page asks the offer holder "When do I need to apply? Is my application strong enough?" (LSE Careers, 2026, https://info.lse.ac.uk/current-students/careers/Offer-holders/Career-planning) [practitioner consensus]. The fact that a top school builds a pre-arrival programme is itself the signal: careers work is expected to start in the summer before arrival. Imperial, LBS and Warwick careers pages were not readable in this pass (see Claims to verify). FT-derived three-month employment for UK MiMs fell from 96% (2019) to 79% (2026) (eFinancialCareers, 9 Sep 2026, via careers/finance.md) [data via press], so a slow start is costlier than it used to be.

**Realistic target set for a one-year UK MSc student (recruiting from arrival):**

| Target | When to apply | Why it works or doesn't |
|---|---|---|
| London IB summer internship (June–Aug of the MSc year) | July–Oct before or at arrival | Eligible on paper (BofA: finishing Jun 2027–Jul 2028), but it clashes with the dissertation, and the best places go before or just after arrival. **M** |
| London IB graduate analyst (start after the MSc) | Sept–Nov of the MSc year | Fewer places because banks convert interns; stronger with prior internships. **M** (LSE Careers) |
| Off-cycle internship (start Oct–Jan after the MSc) | Spring and summer of the MSc year | Most realistic bank door for late starters; boutiques recruit year-round. **M** |
| Consulting (graduate, UK or home-country office) | Oct–Dec of the MSc year | Timing fits; case prep must start before arrival. **M** |
| Big 4 graduate scheme (September start the following year) | Sept–Nov of the MSc year | Fills in order; Deloitte graduate programmes start in September. **H** |
| FMCG UFLP | As soon as it opens in the autumn | Opens once a year and closes when full. **H** |
| Continental graduate job via a final stage | Needs enrolment; a UK graduate has no convention de stage | The weak spot of the UK route for France and Germany. **M** |

## 9. The two-year continental MiM (HEC, ESSEC, ESCP, Bocconi, St. Gallen, Mannheim, RSM)

- **The structure is built around internships.** HEC's MiM allows a professional gap year between M1 and M2, and sets a minimum of 8 or 16 weeks of professional experience before M2 for students who do not take it (HEC, 2026) [employer-stated]. Its first-year students also do a compulsory three-week front-line field internship, in January or June (HEC, page describing the 2023–24 intake) [employer-stated].
- **The main sequencing choice** is between (a) an M1 summer internship in London (apply July–December of M1, the same London calendar as §1), (b) a gap year of two six-month internships, which is the continental equivalent of a year in industry, and (c) going straight to M2 and relying on the stage de fin d'études. For French-speaking students targeting France, (c) plus a strong final stage is the highest-yield channel (42.3% of hires via final internship or apprenticeship, CGE 2026) [data]. For London IB, (a) or a gap-year London off-cycle is needed, because London converts summer interns [practitioner consensus].
- **DACH.** BCG's Visiting Associate programme explicitly admits gap-year students and lets FAST FORWARD offer holders defer the start to finish studies or do further internships (via careers/consulting.md) [employer-stated]. Combined with the MiLoG rule (§3), this makes a mandatory or gap-semester internship during the degree the natural door. **H/M**
- **Italy (Bocconi).** Bocconi-specific internship timing pages were not read in this pass; see places/italy-playbook.md.

## Hypotheses tested

**H1. "A one-year UK MSc student who starts preparing on arrival has missed most IB and consulting internship windows."** Verdict: **partly supported.**
- Holds: for London bulge-bracket summer internships and for consulting internships, whose windows run July–December (LSE Careers, 2026) [practitioner consensus] and fill on a rolling basis (BofA, Citi, 2026) [employer-stated]. Someone who only starts the CV, tests and case practice in October reaches the superday stage when many places have gone.
- Fails: graduate analyst roles, consulting full-time rounds (October–December), Big 4 and FMCG schemes are all still open at arrival, and off-cycle and boutique roles run year-round (LSE Careers banking guide, 2026) [practitioner consensus]. The student has not "missed" the window but has lost the first 8–12 weeks of it.
- Condition: the problem is prep, not eligibility. Starting in July (pre-arrival) removes most of the penalty.

**H2. "Rolling applications mean a submission in the first two weeks has materially higher odds."** Verdict: **partly supported (direction yes, size unknown).**
- For: employers say assessments start before deadlines (BofA), that roles close "once positions are filled" (EY), that the process is "first-come, first-served" (Unilever NL), and that a role closes when "the required number of applications" is reached (Unilever UK) [employer-stated]. ISE reports employers "closing roles early or reducing application periods" in response to volume (ISE, Apr 2026) [data].
- Against: no employer or survey publishes offer rates by application week, so "materially" cannot be quantified. Some employers reopen roles later in the season (EY) [employer-stated].
- Holds best for: high-volume schemes with fixed intake (Big 4, FMCG, bank summer programmes). Weakest for: boutiques and off-cycles that hire when a need appears.

**H3. "AI-generated applications have pushed employers back to in-person or proctored assessments."** Verdict: **partly supported.**
- For: 79% of ISE employers are redesigning or reviewing selection because of AI, and 61% reported undisclosed AI in interviews (ISE, 2025) [data]; ISE lists "reintroducing live interviews earlier" and reducing video interviews as emerging responses (ISE, Apr 2026) [data]; KPMG re-tests online assessment elements and ends in person; Deloitte's graduate final stage is in person; Unilever NL's final day is at the Rotterdam office [employer-stated].
- Against: only 10% of employers ban AI or use technical blocks, 49% accept candidate AI use (ISE, 2025) [data], and recorded video interviews are still standard (KPMG's AI-avatar interview; Unilever's digital interview) [employer-stated]. Some final stages stay virtual (Deloitte Summer Vacation Scheme, Unilever Germany's virtual day) [employer-stated].
- Condition: the shift is towards *human and live* at the end of the funnel, with automated gates at the start, rather than a full return to paper and proctored testing.

**H4. "In France and Germany the final internship, not graduate schemes, is the main hiring channel for MiM graduates."** Verdict: **supported for France, partly supported for Germany.**
- France: 42.3% of grande école hires come via the final internship or apprenticeship host, against 4.9% via school forums and alumni directly (CGE 2026, via getting-in/employer-pipelines.md) [data].
- Germany: the legal frame (MiLoG §22) favours mandatory internships during studies [data], and BCG's binding-offer model makes the internship the job interview (BCG, 2026) [employer-stated]. But no German national statistic on the share of business master's graduates hired by their internship employer was found (see Claims to verify).
- Fails: for London-bound candidates and for large FMCG and Big 4 firms, which still run structured graduate schemes (Unilever UFLP in Germany and NL; Big 4) [employer-stated].

**H5 (own). "Record application volumes are driven by candidate behaviour, not by more vacancies."** Verdict: **supported.**
- Graduate vacancies fell 8% while applications per vacancy reached 140; 38% of employers saw more applications with flat or falling hiring; Prospects found the share of candidates making more than 100 applications rose from 8% to 13% (ISE Oct 2025, Apr 2026, Jun 2026) [data].
- Implication: each extra low-effort application adds little, and employers respond with harder early gates. Quality per application matters more, not less.

**H6 (own). "A UK MSc graduate will find German and French internships harder to get after graduating than a continental student during their degree."** Verdict: **partly supported.**
- For: a French stage needs a *convention de stage* from a school, and a German voluntary internship over three months must pay the minimum wage unless mandatory under university rules (Service Public 2026; MiLoG §22) [data].
- Against: BCG DACH admits gap-year students without enrolment (via careers/consulting.md) [employer-stated], and some UK graduates enrol in short programmes to obtain a convention [anecdotal].
- Condition: holds most for mid-sized employers and for Paris banks' stage pipelines; weakest for firms with their own structured programmes.

**H7 (own). "US recruiting creep does not affect European candidates applying to US banks' London offices."** Verdict: **supported for timing.**
- The same banks ran US 2027 applications from December 2025 (eFinancialCareers, Dec 2025) but still had London 2027 roles open at the end of September 2026 (BofA, Citi pages) [employer-stated]. The UK PE cycle is also described as later and "more sedate" (eFinancialCareers, Jul 2025) [practitioner consensus].
- Fails: for readers at US programmes, where the US calendar applies in full.

## Common mistakes and myths

1. **"Deadlines are what matter."** In rolling processes the deadline is the latest possible date, not the recommended one; BofA says assessments start before it, and Citi has no deadline at all [employer-stated]. Treat the opening date as the real deadline.
2. **"I'll do the spring week during my master's."** Spring and insight programmes target first- and second-year undergraduates (Deloitte, Deutsche Bank, Rothschild pages) [employer-stated]. Master's students go straight to summer, off-cycle or graduate roles.
3. **"More applications = more chances."** Volume-driven strategies coincide with record rejections; employers are adding automated gates and early closures in response (ISE, Apr 2026; Prospects, Jun 2026) [data].
4. **"The online test is a formality."** P&G's assessments decide progression automatically for most roles, with a 12-month wait to retry; EY imposes a six-month bar after rejection (P&G, EY pages) [employer-stated]. A test failed in September can cost you the whole season at that firm.
5. **"AI will get me through, and they can't tell."** 61% of employers report catching undisclosed AI use in interviews, KPMG re-tests assessment elements, and final stages are increasingly live and in person (ISE; KPMG; Deloitte) [data / employer-stated].
6. **"Employers use AI to reject me, so I must use AI."** 95% of candidates believe employers use AI to shortlist, but ISE finds exclusive AI use at any stage "very rare" (15% at most, in gamified tests), and Unilever UK says it does not use AI to review applications (ISE Jun 2026; Unilever UK) [data / employer-stated].
7. **"A one-year UK MSc gives me a year to recruit."** It gives you one autumn. The next cycle's window (for roles starting a year after graduation) opens during the dissertation (LSE Careers timelines) [practitioner consensus].
8. **"In France I'll apply to graduate schemes in my final year."** Most hiring runs through the final stage; students who treat the stage as a formality give up the main channel (CGE 2026) [data].

## Decision rules

1. **If** you will start a one-year UK MSc in September **and** target London IB or consulting, **prefer** finishing your CV, a 25–40 firm target list and online-test practice by **31 July** before arrival (because London windows open in July and fill on a rolling basis; LSE Careers, BofA, Citi).
2. **If** a firm says it recruits on a rolling basis or first-come, first-served, **prefer** submitting within **two to three weeks of opening** (because assessments start before deadlines and roles close when full; direction employer-stated, size unquantified).
3. **If** you have not yet practised a firm's test format, **prefer** doing practice tests (vendor or firm practice zones) before your first live test of the season (because a failed test can bar you for 6–12 months: EY, P&G).
4. **If** you missed the London bulge-bracket summer window, **prefer** graduate analyst applications in September–November plus boutique and off-cycle applications from January (because boutiques recruit year-round; LSE Careers).
5. **If** you are a continental MiM student targeting London IB, **prefer** applying in July–October of M1 for the M1 summer, or planning a London off-cycle in a gap year (because London converts summer interns).
6. **If** you target a French corporate, bank or consultancy **and** can work in French, **prefer** a programme that ends with a six-month stage de fin d'études, and choose the stage host as the employer you want (because 42.3% of hires come through that route; CGE 2026).
7. **If** you target Germany/DACH, **prefer** internships taken while enrolled (mandatory or within the three-month voluntary limit) over post-graduation internships (because MiLoG §22 makes post-graduation long internships more expensive for employers).
8. **If** you target Big 4 or an FMCG graduate scheme in the UK, **prefer** applying in the first month the scheme opens (because EY and Unilever close roles once full).
9. **If** you are at a US programme **and** want a US summer, **prefer** applying 12–18 months ahead of the internship (because US 2027 roles were open by December 2025).
10. **If** a process includes a recorded video or AI-avatar interview, **prefer** preparing for a later live or in-person stage that re-tests the same content (because employers are moving authenticity checks to live stages: ISE 2026, KPMG, Deloitte).
11. **If** you are still a bachelor's student, **prefer** applying to spring weeks and insight programmes now (because they are undergraduate-only and return offers, though falling, still exist: Trackr via eFinancialCareers).
12. **If** you choose between a one-year UK MSc and a two-year continental MiM mainly on internship access, **prefer** the two-year programme when you lack any internship and target France or DACH (because the M1 summer, gap year and final stage give three recruiting shots against one autumn in the UK).
