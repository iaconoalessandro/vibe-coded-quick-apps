# Sweden: country brief

Today is 3 October 2026. Tags: `[data]` official statistics or government pages, `[employer-stated]`, `[practitioner consensus]` (published indices and specialist synthesis), `[anecdotal]`.

## 1. Bottom line

- Stockholm is the national centre by a wide margin. Eurostat counts 117,000 of Sweden's 221,000 information-and-communication jobs (53%) and 68,000 of its 107,000 finance and insurance jobs (64%) in the Stockholm metropolitan region in 2021 [data]. It is rated 5 nationally, 4 in Europe and 2 in the world for software and IT, and 5 / 3 / 2 for finance.
- Stockholm is the Nordic start-up leader and a top-tier European ecosystem: Startup Genome's 2026 Top 40 has it 23rd in the world, level with Amsterdam-Delta, up eight places and "the standout" European performer; its Europe chapter names Stockholm in the top tier after London, Paris and Berlin and fourth in Europe for AI-native ecosystem value ($5.8 billion) [practitioner consensus]. The world step stays at 2 because 23rd is outside a top-fifteen reading of the rubric.
- Sweden has the highest ICT-specialist share of employment among the 33 European countries Eurostat lists, 8.9% in 2025 against 5.0% in the EU [data].
- The finance step is a regional 3: the Global Financial Centres Index 40 (September 2026) ranks Stockholm 35th of 117 and twelfth among European centres, and 17th in the world for fintech [practitioner consensus]. Spotify, Klarna, SEB and Handelsbanken are headquartered in the city.
- Gothenburg is the industrial second city (Volvo Group, 99,000 employees worldwide, and Volvo Car Group, both headquartered there) and second in Sweden for ICT jobs (33,000); Malmö is third; Uppsala is a university town [data, employer-stated].
- Pay is clearly higher in Stockholm: in 2025 the average monthly salary was SEK 48,300 in the Stockholm region against SEK 42,900 nationally and SEK 40,500-42,300 in the other three hubs' regions (Statistics Sweden) [data]. These are all-employee averages, not graduate pay.
- The graduate market is two-speed: 91.1% of recent tertiary graduates aged 20-34 were in work in 2025 (EU 85.3%), but unemployment among 15-to-24-year-olds was 24.3% (EU 15.2%), the highest in the Nordic countries [data]. Corporate graduate programmes recruit in English but many roles need Swedish.

## 2. The economy and the graduate labour market

**Size.** Eurostat's metropolitan table gives Sweden a GDP of €563 billion in 2022, 10.52 million people (2023) and 5.2 million in work; the Stockholm region produced €171 billion in 2021 and has 2.44 million residents [data].

**Structure.** Information and communication employed 221,000 and finance and insurance 107,000 across the country in 2021 (Eurostat met_10r_3emp) [data]. Industry is led by vehicles and engineering (Volvo Group, Volvo Cars, Axis in Lund), banking (SEB, Handelsbanken, Swedbank) and technology platforms (Spotify, Klarna). Of SSE's 2025 master's class, 40% went into consulting, 29% into finance and 9% into technology, with first employers including McKinsey, BCG, SEB, Handelsbanken, Swedbank, Klarna and H&M; over 60% started their careers in Sweden and 38% of international graduates stayed [data, school report via places/iberia-and-nordics.md].

**Graduates and youth.** 91.1% of recent tertiary graduates aged 20-34 were in work in 2025 (EU 85.3%); youth unemployment was 24.3% (EU 15.2%) (Eurostat edat_lfse_24, une_rt_a) [data]. Graduates do well; entering is slow for the young generally.

**Entry pay.** No primary source for graduate starting pay by field was found. Statistics Sweden's regional average (2025, all employees, all sectors): SEK 48,300 in the Stockholm region, 42,300 in West Sweden, 41,700 in South Sweden, 40,500 in East-Central Sweden [data]. Tax, net pay and the expert tax relief (not available to someone who studied in Sweden) are in the record and in places/iberia-and-nordics.md.

**How hiring works.** SEB's International Trainee Programme runs every other year from September; the 2026-27 intake closed on 11 January 2026 and some roles require Swedish; Nordea's 1.5-year programme starts in September (2026 applications 9 to 28 February) [employer-stated]. No calendar was found for Spotify, Klarna, Handelsbanken or Volvo.

**Language and permits.** Many roles need Swedish. Since 11 June 2026 non-EU students may work 15 hours a week in term; a graduate permit allows up to a year to look for work; a work permit needs at least SEK 34,470 a month since 16 June 2026 [data]. See the unified immigration guide in visas_immigration/sweden/sweden_visas_immigration_guide.md.

## 3. Hubs

### Stockholm

**What it is.** The capital: 2,440,027 residents in the metropolitan region (2023), €171.3 billion of GDP (2021) (Eurostat), average monthly salary SEK 48,300 in the region (2025, Statistics Sweden) [data]. Rent: see the comparison table.

**Sectors and named employers.** Spotify gives Regeringsgatan 19 as its headquarters; Klarna Sveavägen 46 and says it supports 26 countries; Handelsbanken has its head office at Kungsträdgårdsgatan 2 and more than 12,000 employees; SEB runs an international trainee programme; Swedbank is among SSE graduates' first employers [employer-stated, data]. McKinsey and BCG are among SSE graduates' first employers [data]. Group headcounts are global.

**Universities.** QS World University Rankings 2027: KTH 82nd and Stockholm University 167th (Lund 71st and Uppsala 87th complete the Swedish top four) [employer-stated]. The Stockholm School of Economics (SSE) is the calculator's Swedish school; no SSE ranking was read for this brief.

**Start-up ecosystem.** Startup Genome: 23rd in the 2026 Top 40, ecosystem value $56 billion against a global average of $25 billion, $1.6 billion of early-stage funding in 2023-2025 and $34 billion of exits in 2021-2025 [practitioner consensus].

### Gothenburg

**What it is.** West Sweden's centre: 1,758,656 people in the metropolitan region, €88.6 billion of GDP (2021), regional pay SEK 42,300 [data]. Eurostat has 33,000 ICT jobs (15% of the national total, second to Stockholm) and 10,000 finance jobs in 2021 [data].

**Employers.** AB Volvo (headquarters, 99,000 employees worldwide) and Volvo Car Group (Gunnar Engellaus väg 8) [employer-stated]. Startup Genome shows an ecosystem value of $2 billion, $120 million of early-stage funding and $1 billion of exits [practitioner consensus]. GFCI lists Gothenburg only as an associate centre.

**Standing.** Management 4 / 2 / 1, IT 4 / 2 / 1, finance 4 / 1 / 1 (second in the country for finance jobs; GFCI lists it only as an associate centre).

### Malmö

**What it is.** 1,414,324 people in the metropolitan region, €62.4 billion of GDP, regional pay SEK 41,700 (South Sweden) [data]. Third in Sweden for ICT (25,000 jobs) and finance (8,000) in 2021 [data]. Axis Communications gives Lund as its headquarters (around 5,000 employees); Lund University is 71st in QS 2027 [employer-stated]. Standing: IT and finance 3 / 1 / 1.

### Uppsala

**What it is.** 400,682 people in the metropolitan region, €19.1 billion of GDP, regional pay SEK 40,500 (East-Central Sweden) [data]. Uppsala University has 50,000 students and 7,539 staff and is 87th in QS 2027 [employer-stated]. 5,000 ICT jobs and 1,000 finance jobs in 2021; no family is rated. Standing: IT 2 / 1 / 1.

## 5. Hypotheses tested

1. **"Stockholm is Sweden's only graduate market."** Mostly supported: 53% of ICT and 64% of finance jobs and the headquarters of Spotify, Klarna, SEB and Handelsbanken. It fails for vehicles and engineering (Gothenburg and Lund). Evidence: Eurostat, employers' pages [data, employer-stated].
2. **"Stockholm is a top-five European start-up ecosystem."** Partly supported: it is in the top tier after London, Paris and Berlin with Amsterdam and Munich, and fourth in AI-native value, but Startup Genome's wording is "top tier", not a numbered top five [practitioner consensus].
3. **"Swedish graduates walk into jobs."** Not supported: 24.3% youth unemployment in 2025, the highest in the Nordics; recent graduates themselves do well (91.1% in work) [data].
4. **"Pay is much higher in Stockholm."** Partly supported: 13% above the national average (SEK 48,300 against 42,900), but regional averages mix all occupations and no graduate pay was read [data].

## 6. Common mistakes and myths

- Treating Spotify's or Klarna's headquarters address as a sign of Swedish graduate hiring: the pages read give no Swedish headcount or programme.
- Assuming English is enough: SEB says some trainee roles require Swedish.
- Quoting the "expert tax" relief: it excludes anyone resident in Sweden in the previous five years, so it does not apply to a graduate who studied there.
- Assuming "Malmö" means only Malmö: Eurostat's metropolitan region and the Lund employers are wider than the city.

## 7. Decision rules

1. If you want software, fintech or banking, prefer Stockholm; it holds about half of the country's ICT jobs and two-thirds of its finance jobs.
2. If the target is vehicles, trucks or engineering management, look at Gothenburg first (Volvo Group, Volvo Cars).
3. If you need a visa, check the 15-hour weekly limit and the one-year search window before choosing Sweden over Denmark or Finland.
4. If a role needs Swedish and yours is weak, expect a longer search: youth unemployment is 24.3%.
5. If you compare offers, compare after tax and rent: use the net-pay calculations in places/iberia-and-nordics.md, not gross figures.
6. If you want a start-up career, Stockholm's ecosystem ranks above Copenhagen's and Helsinki's in Startup Genome's report, but outside Stockholm the market is thin.

## 8. Gaps and claims to verify

1. No primary source for graduate starting pay; the pay figures are all-employee regional averages.
2. Eurostat's employment counts are for metropolitan regions and 2021.
3. Swedbank, Ericsson, H&M and AstraZeneca pages could not be read; not listed as employers.
4. SSE's own ranking was not read; calendars for Spotify, Klarna, Handelsbanken and Volvo were not found.
5. Rent figures are crowd-sourced (Numbeo) and one-off.
