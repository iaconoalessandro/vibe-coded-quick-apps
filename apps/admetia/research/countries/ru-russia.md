# Russia: country brief

Today is 3 October 2026. Tags: `[data]` statistics, published indices and government pages, `[employer-stated]`, `[practitioner consensus]`, `[anecdotal]`. Routes in the Atlas are for EU/EEA/Swiss and UK passports only, and for Russia they consist of advice, sanctions and money, not of job routes.

## Read this first: the official advice

- **The UK Foreign Office advises against all travel to Russia** (the whole country, "avoid all travel"), because of the continuing invasion of Ukraine. It reports an increased risk of British nationals being detained, Russia's record of holding foreign nationals as leverage, a high likelihood of terrorist attacks, and drone attacks that disrupt airports at short notice (FCDO, updated 18 September 2026, https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/russia; the current update concerns drone attacks leading to airspace and airport restrictions) [data].
- **The US State Department rates Russia Level 4, do not travel**, citing the war, wrongful detention, arbitrary enforcement of laws and terrorism, and tells US citizens in Russia to leave immediately (advisory of 29 December 2025, read through the Department's advisories RSS feed) [data].
- **Sanctions bind you.** The EU has sanctioned Russia since 2014 and expanded the measures massively after February 2022; its 21st package was adopted on 23 July 2026, and the financial, economic and trade measures bind all individuals and organisations under EU jurisdiction (European Commission, updated 25 September 2026) [data]. The UK and other governments have also sanctioned Russia, including its airlines (FCDO) [data]. Check with a qualified adviser whether a job or contract would breach sanctions before accepting.
- **Money.** Visa and Mastercard cards issued abroad do not work in Russia, and Russian cards work only inside Russia (FCDO) [data].
- **Entry.** British citizens need a visa before travelling, with fingerprints taken at a visa centre in London, Manchester or Edinburgh; overstaying can bring fines, court hearings, deportation or a re-entry ban (FCDO) [data].
- Italy's Viaggiare Sicuri page and the Council of the EU's sanctions page could not be read by script.

## 1. Bottom line

- **The Atlas does not recommend Russia and does not rate demand there.** No family is rated in either hub; "most-requested roles" is empty.
- **Moscow is by far the largest city and economy; St Petersburg is second.** Moscow had 13.3 million residents on 1 January 2025 (mayor's statement, as reported by Expert) and produced RUB 31.8 trillion of regional output in 2023 on the city's own estimate, about 20% of all Russian regions' output (Expert RA, 13 November 2024) [data]. St Petersburg had 5.563 million residents on 1 January 2025 and RUB 10,908 billion of output in 2023 (Petrostat, quoted by Centrum Balticum) [data].
- **Neither is a significant international financial centre.** The Global Financial Centres Index 40 ranks Moscow 100th and St Petersburg 104th of 117 (Z/Yen, September 2026) [data].
- **The big head offices are in the two cities.** On the Fortune Global 500 of 2026, Sberbank (64), Rosneft (109) and Lukoil (239) are headquartered in Moscow and Gazprom (89) in St Petersburg [data]. This is a size statistic; sanctions may apply to dealings with such firms and you must check.
- **Statistics are thin and from secondary reporters.** Foreign-trade statistics for St Petersburg stopped being published after April 2022 (Centrum Balticum) [data].

## 2. The economy and the graduate labour market

Not assessed. The Atlas states the advisories and sanctions and stops there. No graduate pay, unemployment, hiring calendar or work-permit rule was researched. The sectors the record lists (oil and gas, metals and mining, banking, defence, agriculture) are the country's general structure, not hiring evidence. Defence and some banking and energy employers are subject to sanctions: do not treat the list as a job market.

## 3. Hubs (statistical picture only)

Populations are for each city (area: city); output is gross regional product for each city in 2023 (area: city). The two cities' figures come from different compilers: Moscow's population is the mayor's statement and its output the city's own estimate; St Petersburg's are Petrostat's. No pay or rent figure is used: Moscow's 2025 average (about RUB 180,900 a month, a Rosstat series as compiled by StatBase) and St Petersburg's (RUB 117,200 for the first 11 months of 2025, Petrostat via Delovoy Peterburg) cover different periods. Standing steps are [national, Europe, world], and they describe the size of a financial centre, not a recommendation.

### Moscow

13.3 million residents; output RUB 31.8 trillion (2023, city estimate). The record's older claim: the metropolitan area accounted for more than a quarter of Russia's GDP and almost half of its stock of foreign direct investment (Moscow Analytical Center, April 2021, before the full-scale invasion) [data]. Sberbank, Rosneft and Lukoil have their head offices here (Fortune 2026).

**Standing.** Finance [5, 2, 1]: national 5 (the largest Russian financial centre in the GFCI and the seat of the large banks); Europe 2 and world 1, since it is 100th of 117 and far below the European centres. Not rated for demand.

### St Petersburg

5.563 million residents; output RUB 10,908 billion (2023), about EUR 117.5 billion at the official average rate (Petrostat via Centrum Balticum) [data]. Gazprom has moved its head office to the city from Moscow; the port has been Russia's biggest "window" for trade with Europe, but its trade statistics are no longer published [data].

**Standing.** Finance [4, 2, 1]: national 4 (the second Russian centre in the GFCI, 104th); Europe 2 and world 1. Not rated for demand.

## 5. Hypotheses tested

1. **"Moscow is a major international financial centre."** Not supported: 100th of 117 in the GFCI [data].
2. **"A European graduate can freely take a job in Russia."** Not supported by anything read: the official advice is to avoid all travel, sanctions bind EU persons, and the money system is cut off from Visa and Mastercard.
3. **"Russia's big companies are a graduate-employer list."** Rejected: they are head-office and size statistics; sanctions may make dealings unlawful.

## 6. Common mistakes and myths

- Reading a ranking or a population figure as a signal that you can or should work there.
- Assuming a card issued in the EU will work, or that a bank account can be funded from abroad.
- Treating any single source as the official statistic: here the figures are reported, not read on Rosstat.
- Assuming that travel insurance or consular help is available as usual: the FCDO warns of detention risk and the US tells citizens to leave.

## 7. Decision rules

1. Read the FCDO (and your own government's) advice before anything else; it says avoid all travel.
2. Do not accept a job, contract or internship without legal advice on sanctions.
3. Do not plan to rely on cards issued outside Russia.
4. Use this brief as a map of size, not a list of opportunities; for comparison, use the Gulf, Turkey or the EU hubs in this guide.
