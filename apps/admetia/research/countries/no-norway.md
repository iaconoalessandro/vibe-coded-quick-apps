# Norway: country brief

Today is 3 October 2026. Tags: `[data]` official statistics or government pages, `[employer-stated]`, `[practitioner consensus]` (published indices and specialist synthesis), `[anecdotal]`.

## 1. Bottom line

- Oslo leads, but less overwhelmingly than Stockholm or Copenhagen lead their countries. Eurostat counts 47,000 of Norway's 105,000 information-and-communication jobs (45%) and 22,000 of its 48,000 finance and insurance jobs (46%) in the Oslo metropolitan region in 2021 [data]; Statistics Norway found about half of the country's financial-services output generated in Oslo (2015 data) [data].
- Oslo is rated 5 nationally but only 2 in Europe and 1 in the world for finance, software and IT. The finance step rests on the Global Financial Centres Index 40 (September 2026): Oslo 48th of 117, up 16 places, sixteenth among European centres, and 33rd for fintech [practitioner consensus]. Startup Genome's Oslo page shows an ecosystem value of $12 billion, below the regional average of $14.3 billion [practitioner consensus].
- Pay is the highest in the Nordics and varies little between cities: in 2025 average monthly earnings by place of work were NOK 70,690 in Oslo, NOK 69,670 in Stavanger, NOK 64,170 in Bergen and NOK 62,760 in Trondheim against NOK 62,070 nationally (Statistics Norway, table 12852) [data]. The oil-and-gas premium makes Stavanger almost as well paid as the capital.
- Named anchors: DNB (largest bank, autumn trainee programme in Oslo), Nordea's four-country graduate programme, Norges Bank Investment Management (head office Bankplassen 2, 676 people from 37 countries), Equinor (head office at Forus, Stavanger, about 4,500 people there; about 3,000 in Bergen and 1,600 in Oslo) [employer-stated].
- NHH in Bergen is the business-school route: its 2024 master's class reported a median gross salary of NOK 580,000 six months after graduating, for those working in Norway (52% responded) [data].
- Youth unemployment is lower than the EU's (14.0% against 15.2% in 2025) but is rising (11.0% in 2023), and nearly all roles are in Norwegian [data].
- Practical advice: go to Oslo for banking, investment management or ICT; to Stavanger or Bergen for energy and maritime employers; use NHH or a named programme as the door; learn Norwegian.

## 2. The economy and the graduate labour market

**Size and structure.** Eurostat's metropolitan table gives Norway a GDP of €425 billion in 2021, 5.49 million residents (2023) and 2.82 million in work [data]. Oil, gas and energy, banking and finance, shipping and seafood, the public sector and technology are the main sectors in the record. ICT specialists were 5.9% of employment in 2025 against 5.0% in the EU, ninth of 33 European countries [data].

**Graduates and youth.** 91.8% of recent tertiary graduates were in work in 2025 and graduate unemployment was 3.4%, the lowest in the Nordic and Iberian comparison (Eurostat, via places/iberia-and-nordics.md) [data]. Youth unemployment was 14.0% (EU 15.2%) in 2025 (Eurostat une_rt_a) [data]. 62% of students from outside the EU and EEA who completed a Norwegian degree were still resident the year after, and 86% of those still in Norway were employed [data].

**Entry pay.** NHH's 2024 master's class: median gross NOK 580,000 six months after graduating (52% response; those working in Norway) [data]. City averages for all employees in 2025 are in the bottom line; they are not graduate pay.

**How hiring works.** DNB's trainee programme in Oslo recruits in the autumn; Nordea's 1.5-year Graduate Programme starts in September and took 2026 applications from 9 to 28 February [employer-stated]. A search result gave Equinor's 2026 graduate window as 7 to 27 August but this was not confirmed on a primary page and is not relied on.

**Language and permits.** Most roles are in Norwegian; no source says which employers recruit in English. Norway is in the EEA: EEA nationals register with the police within three months (*registreringsbevis*, 0 NOK). International students may work 20 hours a week in term. A skilled-worker permit requires at least NOK 624,700 a year for a master's-level job or NOK 545,400 for a bachelor's-level job from 1 May 2026 (UDI). Graduates can apply for a 1-year job-seeker permit. For the full verified legal guide and rules, see `visas_immigration/norway/norway_visas_immigration_guide.md`.

## 3. Hubs

### Oslo

**What it is.** The capital: 709,037 residents in the metropolitan region (2023, Eurostat), €71.0 billion of GDP (2021), average earnings NOK 70,690 a month (Statistics Norway, 2025) [data]. Rent: see the comparison table.

**Sectors and named employers.** DNB (trainee programme, autumn); Nordea (four-country graduate programme); Norges Bank Investment Management (Bankplassen 2; 676 people; offices in London, New York and Singapore); Equinor (about 1,600 people in Oslo) [employer-stated]. About half of the country's financial output was generated in Oslo, and finance, real estate, ICT and technical services made 38% of the city's output (2015 data) [data].

**Start-up ecosystem.** Startup Genome: ecosystem value $12 billion against a regional average of $14.3 billion; $418 million of early-stage funding in 2023-2025; $2 billion of exits in 2021-2025 [practitioner consensus].

Demand: finance, IT and software dominant; banking strong.

### Stavanger

**What it is.** The oil and gas capital: 492,350 people in the metropolitan region, €31.6 billion of GDP (2021), average earnings NOK 69,670, the second highest of the four hubs [data]. Equinor's head office is at Forus, about 4,500 people [employer-stated]. 8,000 ICT jobs, joint second in Norway; only 2,000 finance jobs [data]. Standing: business 3 / 1 / 1, IT 3 / 1 / 1.

### Bergen

**What it is.** The second city: 646,205 people in the metropolitan region, €36.5 billion of GDP, average earnings NOK 64,170 [data]. Second for finance (6,000 jobs) and joint second for ICT (8,000) [data]. NHH has about 3,700 students and the Triple Crown of accreditations; Equinor has about 3,000 people in Bergen [employer-stated]. Standing: IT and finance 4 / 1 / 1.

### Trondheim

**What it is.** The technical university town: 478,470 people in the metropolitan region, €26.1 billion of GDP, average earnings NOK 62,760 [data]. NTNU has 43,500 students and 8,560 employees [employer-stated]. 7,000 ICT jobs and 4,000 finance jobs; no family is rated. Standing: IT 3 / 1 / 1.

## 5. Hypotheses tested

1. **"Oslo is the Nordic finance centre."** Not supported: Oslo is 48th in GFCI 40 against Copenhagen 25th and Stockholm 35th, and sixteenth in Europe [practitioner consensus]. It is the national leader only.
2. **"Pay is much higher in Oslo than elsewhere in Norway."** Not supported at the all-employee level: Stavanger's average is 98.6% of Oslo's and Bergen's 91% [data]. The premium is Norway's, not Oslo's.
3. **"Norway is the easiest Nordic graduate market."** Partly supported: lowest graduate unemployment in the comparison and the highest pay, but nearly all roles are in Norwegian and youth unemployment rose from 11.0% to 14.0% in two years [data].
4. **"Business school pay in Bergen is the benchmark."** Partly supported: NHH's median is a school survey with a 52% response and covers only those working in Norway [data].

## 6. Common mistakes and myths

- Assuming English-only roles are common; the record has no source naming employers that recruit in English.
- Comparing Oslo's metropolitan population with Bergen's: the Eurostat regions differ in extent.
- Reading Equinor's head-office headcount (4,500 at Forus) as total Norwegian hiring.
- Using the 2015 financial-output statistic as current: Eurostat's 2021 employment shares are the better measure.

## 7. Decision rules

1. If you want banking or investment management, prefer Oslo (DNB, Nordea, NBIM).
2. If you want energy or maritime employers, compare Stavanger (Equinor head office) and Bergen on pay: Stavanger's average is almost Oslo's.
3. If you are choosing a business master's in Norway, NHH in Bergen is the route with published pay (median NOK 580,000), but remember the 52% response rate.
4. If you do not speak Norwegian, treat Norwegian as a requirement for most roles and check each posting.
5. If you need a visa, read the verified guide at visas_immigration/norway/norway_visas_immigration_guide.md.
6. If you are comparing Nordic capitals for finance, Copenhagen and Stockholm rank well above Oslo in GFCI 40.
