# Ireland (Atlas brief, round 5, 3 October 2026)

Tags: [data], [employer-stated], [practitioner consensus], [anecdotal]. The record is `data/atlas/ie.js`; the quotes behind each claim are in `research/verification/round-5b.md`.

## 1. Bottom line

- **Dublin is Ireland's market for graduates in finance and IT.** It holds 65% of the country's jobs in information and communication and 68% of its jobs in finance and insurance (Eurostat, 2021) [data]. Cork, the second centre, has 11% and 7%.
- **It is also a large one by European standards.** Among 152 European metropolitan regions with 2021 data Dublin is tenth for information and communication jobs and fourth for finance and insurance, behind only Paris, Warsaw and Milan (London, Berlin, Frankfurt and Madrid report no figure) [data]. The Global Financial Centres Index 40 puts it 30th in the world and 10th in Western Europe [practitioner consensus].
- **The economy is the multinationals.** IDA-supported companies employed 312,468 people in 2025, about 11% of national employment; 112,300 of them in information and communication [data]. This also means GDP figures overstate the local economy.
- **Fund administration and aircraft leasing are niche, high-paying employers.** 19,519 people work directly in funds and asset management (2023) and 3,005 in aircraft leasing at an average of €206,324 (2024) [data, industry-body figure for the first].
- **Pay and rent.** Median annual earnings in County Dublin are €49,224 (2024), €4,102 a month; the RTB's average one-bedroom rent is €1,741 a month in County Dublin and €1,153 in County Cork [data].
- **Entry pay is good for computing.** One year after graduating, the 2022 cohort's median weekly earnings were €855 for ICT master's graduates and €650 for business, administration and law master's graduates [data].
- **For non-EU students the route is generous**: Stamp 1G gives 12 months after a bachelor's and a further 12 after a master's, and the Critical Skills permit needs €40,904 (€36,848 for a fresh graduate) [data]. EU and Irish-resident readers need no permit.

## 2. The economy and the graduate labour market

**Structure.** Ireland's growth runs through foreign direct investment. IDA Ireland's 2025 report gives 312,468 jobs in IDA-supported companies (+1.5% on 2024; 295,116 of them full-time), in 1,800+ operations; by sector, 114,962 in modern manufacturing, 112,300 in information and communication, 61,155 in business, financial and other services and 22,913 in traditional manufacturing. Regionally, Dublin has 142,501, the South-West 53,535, the West 32,562 and the Mid-West 28,125; 169,967 are outside Dublin (54%) [data]. The average salary in the investments IDA approved in 2025 was €78,579 and 98% of approved jobs paid over €35,000 [data].

**Labour market.** The CSO's seasonally adjusted unemployment rate was 5.0% in September 2026 and 12.5% for 15-to-24-year-olds [data]. 84% of the 2022 higher-education cohort were in substantial employment a year after graduating; the median weekly earnings of that cohort were €625 [data].

**Entry pay by field (CSO, 2022 graduates, one year out, median weekly earnings).** Master's: business, administration and law €650; ICT €855; all fields €730. Bachelor's: business €570; ICT €770; all fields €610 [data]. Weekly figures: multiply by about 4.33 for a monthly sense, but the CSO figure covers earnings in substantial employment, not a starting salary.

**How hiring works.** The large professional-services and banking employers run annual graduate programmes: KPMG Ireland (graduate programmes; named Graduate Employer of the Year 2026 on its page), Deloitte Ireland (Future Leaders Academy), EY Ireland (2026 programme, with Tax and Law and Technology routes) and Bank of Ireland (its 2027 programme is closed; registration of interest is open for 2028) [employer-stated]. Nothing here is a statistic on how many places each offers.

**Language.** English; only 2.4–2.8% of Irish job postings say English is not required [data, library].

**Visa headline.** EU, EEA and Swiss citizens need no permit; British citizens move freely under the Common Travel Area. Non-EU students on Stamp 2 may work 20 hours a week in term and 40 in holidays; Stamp 1G is 12 months, plus 12 after a master's; Critical Skills Employment Permit €40,904 or €36,848 for a degree under 12 months old [data]; General Employment Permit €36,605 (€34,009 for Irish graduates). Full guide: `visas_immigration/ireland/ireland_visas_immigration_guide.md`.

## 3. Hubs

**Metric basis.** Population and GDP: Eurostat metropolitan regions (population 1 January 2023; GDP 2021, the latest year for Ireland); pay and rent are county figures from the CSO (DEA08 median annual earnings of employees in work for 50+ weeks, 2024, ÷ 12; RTB average monthly rent, one bed, all property types, Q4 2025). Cork City's own rent is €1,235 but the county figure is used so that both hubs use the same kind of area.

### Dublin
- **What it is.** The capital and the metropolitan region of about 2.28 million people; GDP €230.4 billion (2021; inflated by multinationals' profits); median pay €4,102 a month; rent €1,741 [data].
- **Employment.** 1,073,000 people in work (2021): 97,700 in information and communication (65% of Ireland) and 80,700 in finance and insurance (68%) [data]. IDA companies employ 142,501 in the Dublin region [data].
- **Sectors and named employers.** Technology (US companies' European headquarters, per the library), funds administration, aircraft leasing, professional services, banking. KPMG Ireland (new Dublin headquarters at Harcourt Square, opening 2026), Deloitte Ireland, EY Ireland, Bank of Ireland (graduate programmes); the funds industry (19,519 direct jobs) and aircraft leasing (3,005 jobs, nationally) [employer-stated / data].
- **Universities and start-ups.** Not researched here beyond the calculator; no Dublin-specific start-up ranking was read.
- **Standing.** IT 5/3/2: first in Ireland by a wide margin; tenth of 152 metros in Europe for jobs (a 3, not higher, because the table omits London, Berlin and Madrid); world 2. Finance 5/3/2: first nationally; GFCI 30th world, 10th in Western Europe; fourth of 152 metros for jobs, but the cities above it in the GFCI (London, Zurich, Paris, Frankfurt) are mostly absent from the Eurostat data. Business 5/2/1: IDA's multinationals are concentrated here (46% of their jobs); the step beyond Ireland is small.
- **Demand.** Information technology dominant; finance dominant; asset management strong; accounting present (KPMG, Deloitte); business strong (existing).

### Cork
- 756,254 people in the metropolitan region; GDP €115.7 billion (2021); median pay (County Cork) €3,868 a month; rent (County Cork) €1,153 [data].
- Eurostat 2021: 339,530 in work; 16,660 in information and communication and 8,150 in finance and insurance, second in Ireland for both [data]. IDA's South-West region (Cork and Kerry) employs 53,535 [data].
- Employer read: Apple (its page shows an Irish facility building iMac, and a Cork team giving telephone support across Europe) [employer-stated].
- **Standing.** IT and finance 4/2/1: second in Ireland by jobs; small beside the leading European metros (Cork ranks 55th of 152 for ICT jobs and 68th for finance).
- **Demand.** IT and finance strong.

### Galway and Limerick (not mapped)
IDA's West (32,562 jobs) and Mid-West (28,125) regions are large, but no city-level employer or sector source could be read. They would need their own evidence before being added.

## 5. Hypotheses tested

1. **"Ireland's graduate jobs are all in Dublin."** Supported for finance and IT: Dublin holds two thirds of both; but 54% of multinational jobs are outside Dublin, mostly in manufacturing [data].
2. **"Irish GDP tells you how rich the labour market is."** Not supported: Dublin's GDP per Eurostat is €230.4 billion, but multinational profit booking inflates it; median pay of €49,224 is a better guide [data].
3. **"Computing graduates out-earn business graduates immediately."** Supported on CSO data: ICT master's graduates' median weekly earnings were €855 against €650 for business, administration and law (2022 cohort) [data].
4. **"Dublin is a finance centre like Frankfurt."** Partly supported: the GFCI 40 scores are almost identical (Frankfurt 29th with 730, Dublin 30th with 729) [practitioner consensus]; but the Eurostat counts omit Frankfurt, so the two cannot be compared on jobs here, and Dublin's strength is in funds administration and leasing rather than banking.

## 6. Common mistakes and myths

- Treating Dublin GDP as local output; use pay and employment.
- Assuming a Stamp 1G gives a permanent route; it ends after 12 or 24 months, and a permit needs the salary thresholds.
- Assuming London-style pay: median pay in County Dublin is €49,224 against London's £49,692 a year (ONS, 2025; different currencies and bases).
- Forgetting that rent in Dublin is the highest in the country by 51% over County Cork.

## 7. Decision rules

1. If you want IT, fintech or fund administration in an English-speaking EU country, prefer Dublin.
2. If you want a cheaper second centre with multinational employers, consider Cork (rent €1,153 against €1,741), accepting a much smaller market.
3. If you are a non-EU master's student, plan for the 24-month Stamp 1G window and the €36,848 permit threshold.
4. If you target the Big Four or a bank, apply to the annual graduate programme the year before you start (Bank of Ireland's 2028 intake already invites registration).
5. If you compare countries on GDP, do not use Ireland's.

## 8. Gaps and claims to verify

1. Galway and Limerick: no city-level source read.
2. Eurostat metropolitan data are for 2021, and omit London, Berlin, Frankfurt and Madrid.
3. Employer headcounts in Dublin were not read; the CRO company register could not be queried.
4. Software, data and AI are not rated.
5. The Irish Funds employment figure comes from an industry-body page quoting an Indecon report.
6. The CSO graduate-earnings units (weekly) are confirmed by the release text for the all-graduate figure (€625 a week); the field-level figures are the table's P50 values.
