# Finland: country brief

Today is 3 October 2026. Tags: `[data]` official statistics or government pages, `[employer-stated]`, `[practitioner consensus]` (published indices and specialist synthesis), `[anecdotal]`.

## 1. Bottom line

- Helsinki is the graduate market. Eurostat counts 72,970 of Finland's 119,200 information-and-communication jobs (61%) and 25,780 of its 43,700 finance and insurance jobs (59%) in the Helsinki metropolitan region in 2021, against 12,810 and 2,970 in Tampere and 6,260 and 3,150 in Turku [data].
- Finland has the third-highest ICT-specialist share of employment among 33 European countries: 7.8% in 2025 against 5.0% in the EU, after Sweden (8.9%) and Luxembourg (8.7%) [data]. Helsinki is rated 5 nationally, 3 in Europe and 2 in the world for software and IT. Startup Genome's Helsinki page shows an ecosystem value of $23 billion, above the regional average ($14.3 billion) and just below the global average ($25 billion), with $10 billion of exits in 2021-2025 [practitioner consensus].
- Finance is national only: the Global Financial Centres Index 40 (September 2026) ranks Helsinki 85th of 117, so the rating is 5 / 2 / 1. Nordea's group headquarters are in Helsinki and its graduate programme recruits in all four Nordic countries [employer-stated].
- Pay is highest in Uusimaa (the Helsinki region): €4,513 average monthly earnings for full-time earners in 2024 against €4,075 nationally, €3,951 in Pirkanmaa (Tampere) and €3,806 in Southwest Finland (Turku) (Statistics Finland) [data]. These are all-employee averages, not graduate pay.
- The market is hard for foreigners: 53% of foreign citizens with a Finnish degree were employed in Finland three years later (2023) against 87-88% of Finns, and 63% of those employed were in expert jobs against 77% of Finns [data]. Youth unemployment was 21.8% in 2025 (EU 15.2%), up from 16.2% in 2023 [data].
- Helsinki has the lowest rents of the Nordic capitals: at €45,000 gross it leaves about €1,770 a month after tax and a central one-bed rent of about €1,078, the most of the Nordic and Iberian cities compared [data, library calculation].
- Tampere is the clear second city for ICT; Turku is third. Aalto, Helsinki, Turku and Tampere universities are ranked 126th, 123rd, 398th and 436th in QS 2027.

## 2. The economy and the graduate labour market

**Size and structure.** Eurostat's metropolitan table gives Finland a GDP of €268 billion in 2022 and 5.56 million residents (2023); the Helsinki region produced €98.7 billion in 2021 and has 1.73 million residents [data]. The record lists technology and gaming, banking and finance, forestry and paper, engineering and telecoms equipment, and the public sector as main sectors. KONE, headquartered in Espoo, had over 60,000 employees in close to 70 countries at the end of 2025 [employer-stated].

**Graduates and youth.** In 2025, 86.6% of recent tertiary graduates aged 20-34 were in work (EU 85.3%), the lowest of the Nordic countries bar Denmark; youth unemployment was 21.8% (EU 15.2%) (Eurostat edat_lfse_24, une_rt_a) [data]. Aalto's 2024 business graduates went into consultancy (21%), finance (13%) and the public sector (9%); 90% were employed or entrepreneurs a year out and 92% of those employed worked in Finland [data].

**Entry pay.** No graduate starting-pay source was read; Aalto's figures could not be extracted. Statistics Finland's regional averages are in the bottom line. Tax: Finland's expert tax regime (25% flat from 2026) needs €5,800 a month and no Finnish residence in the previous five years, so it does not apply at graduate pay [data].

**How hiring works.** Nordea's 1.5-year Graduate Programme starts in September in four countries; 2026 applications ran from 9 to 28 February [employer-stated]. No other Finnish calendar was found.

**Language and permits.** Non-EU graduates of Finnish degrees get up to two years to look for work or start a business, applied for within five years of the study permit ending; students may work an average of 30 hours a week; a work residence permit needs at least €1,600 gross a month in 2026 [data].

## 3. Hubs

### Helsinki

**What it is.** The capital region: 1,733,033 residents in the metropolitan region (2023), €98.7 billion of GDP (2021) (Eurostat), €4,513 average monthly earnings in Uusimaa (2024, Statistics Finland) [data]. Rent: see the comparison table.

**Sectors and named employers.** Nordea (group headquarters; graduate programme); Supercell (lists Helsinki first among its offices and is registered there); KONE (headquarters in Espoo, over 60,000 staff worldwide) [employer-stated].

**Universities.** QS 2027: University of Helsinki 123rd, Aalto University 126th (Study in Finland, a state agency) [practitioner consensus].

**Start-up ecosystem.** Startup Genome: ecosystem value $23 billion, $997 million of early-stage funding in 2023-2025, $10 billion of exits in 2021-2025 [practitioner consensus].

### Tampere

**What it is.** The second city for ICT: 530,552 people in the metropolitan region, €22.1 billion of GDP (2021), €3,951 average monthly earnings in Pirkanmaa [data]. 12,810 ICT jobs but only 2,970 finance jobs [data]. Tampere University is 436th in QS 2027. Standing: IT 4 / 1 / 1.

### Turku

**What it is.** The south-west: 485,567 people in the metropolitan region, €20.0 billion of GDP, €3,806 average monthly earnings in Southwest Finland [data]. 6,260 ICT jobs and 3,150 finance jobs; the University of Turku is 398th in QS 2027. Standing: IT 3 / 1 / 1.

## 5. Hypotheses tested

1. **"Finland is Helsinki."** Supported for finance and ICT: 59-61% of jobs, against 5-11% in Tampere and Turku; Tampere is the exception in ICT depth [data].
2. **"Finland is a top European tech market."** Supported for ICT intensity (third in Europe by share of employment) but not for scale: Helsinki's ecosystem value is below the global average and the city is outside Startup Genome's Top 40 [data, practitioner consensus].
3. **"A Finnish degree opens the Finnish labour market."** Partly supported: 53% of foreign degree-holders were employed three years on, against 87-88% of Finns [data]; the gap is language and fit, not permits (non-EU graduates get two years to search).
4. **"Cheap rent offsets lower pay."** Partly supported: Helsinki leaves the most after tax and rent among the Nordic and Iberian cities compared at €45,000 gross [data, library calculation]; the pay premium of Uusimaa over the national average is 11% [data].

## 6. Common mistakes and myths

- Treating Espoo employers (KONE, Nokia) as outside Helsinki: Eurostat's metropolitan region and the record treat the capital region together.
- Assuming the expert tax regime applies: it needs €5,800 a month.
- Reading a global headcount (KONE 60,000) as Finnish hiring.
- Expecting a banking market like Stockholm's: GFCI 40 has Helsinki 85th of 117.

## 7. Decision rules

1. If you want ICT, games or fintech, prefer Helsinki; if you want a smaller ICT market with lower rents, look at Tampere.
2. If you need a visa, use the two-year search window and apply within five years of the study permit ending.
3. If you are a non-Finnish speaker, plan for the stay-rate gap: 53% against 87-88% three years on.
4. If you compare Nordic capitals on net pay after rent, include Helsinki: it leaves the most in the library's calculation.
5. If you are targeting finance, treat Helsinki as a Nordea and OP market, not a global one.
