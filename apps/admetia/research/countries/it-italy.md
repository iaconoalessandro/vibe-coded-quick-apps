# Italy (Atlas brief, round 5, 3 October 2026)

Tags: [data], [employer-stated], [practitioner consensus], [anecdotal]. The record is `data/atlas/it.js`; the quotes behind every claim are in `research/verification/round-5b.md` (P38).

## 1. Bottom line

- **Milan is the only hub that leads Italy in both finance and technology.** Eurostat counts 100,700 jobs in finance and insurance and 147,200 in information and communication in its metropolitan region (2021), first in Italy for both; Rome has 68,800 and 121,800, Turin 53,600 and 40,300 [data]. Among 152 European metropolitan regions that report, Milan is third for finance jobs and second for information and communication jobs [data].
- **Rome is a real second market, not only the state.** It is second in Italy for both families (11% of Italy's finance jobs, 19% of information and communication) and is where Eni, Enel, Leonardo, Poste Italiane and Ferrovie dello Stato have their headquarters [data, Global LEI Index]. In the Global Financial Centres Index 40 it ranks 47th, ahead of Milan at 62nd [practitioner consensus].
- **Turin is the quiet third.** 53,600 finance and insurance jobs, tenth among the 152 European metros, with Intesa Sanpaolo's headquarters at Piazza San Carlo; its start-up ecosystem sits in the 81st–90th emerging band [data / practitioner consensus].
- **Milan's pay premium is large, and mostly Milan's.** Average declared employment income of residents is €3,231 a month in Milan, €2,458 in Rome, €2,300 in Turin and €1,847 in Naples (Ministry of Economy and Finance, tax year 2024); Milan is 31% above Rome and 75% above Naples [data; arithmetic mine].
- **The graduate market is healthy at home, weak against abroad.** Italian unemployment is 6.2% (20.3% under 25) in August 2026 [data]; 80.8% of 2024 second-level graduates were employed after a year at €1,495 net a month, and those working abroad earn €673 a month more than those in the South [data]. Italians have good reasons to look abroad (see the playbook) and good reasons to start in Milan.
- **Milan's own foreign-company base is the draw for English-language entry roles.** The Milan province had 6,043 foreign-participated enterprises in 2025, 32.7% of Italy's, and Milan ranks seventh among eleven benchmark cities for greenfield foreign investment [data / practitioner consensus]. But Assolombarda's benchmark also puts Milan last of the eleven on average position across 33 international rankings [practitioner consensus].
- **Elsewhere, choose by employer, not by city.** Bologna (Unipol, Ducati, Marchesini), Florence (Ferragamo, Menarini), Padua-Venice (Banca Ifis, Safilo), Genoa (Ansaldo Energia, Costa Crociere, Esaote, ERG) and Naples (Kimbo) are one- or two-employer markets in the sources read [data].

## 2. The economy and the graduate labour market

**Structure.** Italy's economy is concentrated in the North: the Milan, Rome and Turin metropolitan regions alone hold 48% of the country's information and communication jobs and 36% of its finance and insurance jobs (Eurostat 2021; shares are arithmetic on 642,600 and 620,400 national jobs) [data]. Milan, Monza Brianza and Lodi together have over two million people in work, 8.5% of Italy's workers; Milan's unemployment rate is 3% and its employment rate nearly 73% (Camera di commercio, July 2026) [data]. The Chamber also reports 26,262 new enterprises registered in the Milan metropolitan city in 2025 [data].

**Labour market.** Eurostat's monthly series gives Italian unemployment of 6.2% and 20.3% for under-25s in August 2026 [data]. Milan's NEET rate (15 to 29) fell to 7.7% (from 10.6% in 2024) [data].

**Entry pay.** AlmaLaurea's 2026 report: 81.2% of first-level and 80.8% of second-level graduates (class of 2024) are employed a year on; average net pay is €1,491 and €1,495 a month, rising to €1,684 and €1,695 at three years. Working in the North adds €68 a month, working abroad €673, against the South [data, a model estimate controlling for other factors]. AlmaLaurea also covers only its 78–81 member universities and not Bocconi, Politecnico di Milano, LUISS or Cattolica, so these are public-university averages (see the playbook). Five-year pay for economics magistrale graduates is about €2,006 net a month and for finance about €2,126 (survey 2025, in the library) [data, library]; AlmaLaurea's own report says graduates abroad earn about €2,900 against €1,800 at home at five years [data, library].

**How hiring works.** Large banks, insurers and consultancies run annual graduate programmes and rolling applications; the calendars are in getting-in/recruiting-calendar.md. Italian is expected: about 4% of Italian job postings say Italian is not required (Indeed Hiring Lab, October 2024) [data, library]; Milan's international teams and the foreign-owned firms are the exception.

**Visa headline.** EU citizens register at the comune within three months; non-EU students request a permesso di soggiorno within eight working days (€116.46 fee; €50 visa), may work 20 hours a week (1,040 a year), and after an Italian degree can stay 9 to 12 months to look for work (art. 39-bis.1); tax: the first codice fiscale at the consulate or Revenue Agency; returning workers may use the impatriati regime (50% exempt for five years, 60% with a minor child) [data]. Full verified guide and single source of truth: `visas_immigration/italy/italy_visas_immigration_guide.md` and the record's route.

## 3. Hubs

**Metric basis.** Population and GDP: Eurostat metropolitan regions (population 1 January 2023; GDP 2021, current prices) [data]. Pay: the Ministry of Economy and Finance's open data on declared income by comune, tax year 2024: employment income (reddito da lavoro dipendente e assimilati) divided by the number of declarants with such income, annual ÷ 12; it is based on where people live, includes part-time and part-year work, and is not a starting salary [data]. Rent: not sourced (Numbeo refused automated access; no official one-bedroom figure exists). Padua and Venice are one hub: their populations and GDP are added and pay is weighted by declarants.

**Standing basis.** Standing steps are [national, Europe, world]. National steps follow the Italian rank in Eurostat's 2021 job counts (first = 5, second to fifth = 4, sixth to fifteenth = 3). European and world steps use the 152-metro ranks, the GFCI 40 and the GSER 2026, and stay low unless a ranking supports more.

### Milan
- **What it is.** Italy's finance, consulting, fashion and consumer-brand capital: 4.33 million people in the metropolitan region, GDP €228.4 billion (2021), average declared employment income €3,231 a month [data].
- **Jobs.** 100,700 in finance and insurance (16% of Italy), 147,200 in information and communication (23%), out of 2,447,500 in work (2021) [data]. In 2025, 47 greenfield foreign investment projects (21% financial services, 21% business services, 19% software and IT) [practitioner consensus]; 6,043 foreign-participated enterprises in the province [data].
- **Named employers (headquarters, Global LEI Index).** UniCredit (Piazza Gae Aulenti), Mediobanca (Piazzetta Cuccia), Banco BPM (Piazza Meda), FinecoBank (Piazzale Durante), Banca Mediolanum (Basiglio, Milano 3, in the metropolitan area), Azimut Holding (Via Cusani); Pirelli, Prada, Moncler, Luxottica, Snam, Prysmian, Italgas; Bending Spoons (apps) [data].
- **Schools.** Bocconi (the calculator lists five programmes: MSc Management, International Management, Finance, Marketing Management, and the SDA Bocconi MBA) and Politecnico di Milano, among others.
- **Ecosystem.** GSER 2026 values Milan's start-up ecosystem at $25 billion (the global average; above the European average of $14.3 billion), with $788 million of early-stage funding in H2 2023–2025 [practitioner consensus].
- **Standing.** Finance 5/3/2: first in Italy by jobs and 1.5 times Rome; third of 152 metros for jobs; but GFCI 40 puts Milan 62nd in the world and outside the Western European top 15, so no higher than 3 in Europe and 2 in the world. IT 5/3/2: first in Italy; second of 152 for jobs; GSER at the global average. Business 5/3/2: a third of Italy's foreign-participated enterprises; seventh of eleven benchmark cities for greenfield investment.
- **Demand.** IT dominant; finance dominant; business strong; marketing, management, software present; asset management present (Azimut).
- **Cost.** Not sourced here; the library's money/salaries-and-roi.md §5 has Milan net pay and rent.

### Rome
- 4.23 million people in the metropolitan region; GDP €163.5 billion; average declared employment income €2,458 a month [data].
- 68,800 finance and insurance jobs (11% of Italy) and 121,800 information and communication jobs (19%) [data].
- Eni (Piazzale Mattei), Enel (Viale Regina Margherita), Leonardo (Piazza Monte Grappa), Poste Italiane (Viale Europa), Ferrovie dello Stato Italiane (Piazza della Croce Rossa) [data]. The library describes Rome's graduate market as shaped by the public sector, the energy and utility headquarters and the state-owned groups [practitioner consensus].
- GFCI 40: 47th in the world, 15th in Western Europe. GSER 2026: Ecosystem Value $2 billion, $157 million of early-stage funding [practitioner consensus].
- **Standing.** Finance 4/3/2; IT 4/3/1; business 4/2/1.
- **Demand.** IT strong; finance strong; business present.

### Turin
- 2.20 million people; GDP €75.9 billion; pay €2,300 [data].

- Intesa Sanpaolo (Piazza San Carlo), Stellantis Europe S.p.A. (Corso Giovanni Agnelli) [data]. Start-up ecosystem ranked 81st–90th among emerging ones [practitioner consensus].

- **Demand.** IT strong; finance strong; business present.

## 5. Hypotheses tested

1. **"Milan is Italy's finance capital in every sense."** Partly supported. By jobs Milan leads (100,700, 1.5 times Rome); but the GFCI 40 ranks Rome 47th and Milan 62nd (Milan fell from 45th), and Rome holds 11% of Italian finance jobs against Milan's 16% [data / practitioner consensus]. For an entry-level graduate, Milan is where banks and asset managers are headquartered; Rome adds the public sector.
2. **"Moving to Milan from the South pays for itself."** Supported on gross pay, with a rent caveat: average declared employment income is 75% higher in Milan than in Naples [data; the cost of living was not sourced here].
3. **"Southern graduates are better off abroad than in Milan."** Partly supported: AlmaLaurea's model puts the premium of working abroad at €673 a month against the South and the North's at €68 [data]; the model does not compare Milan with a foreign city, nor adjust for living costs.
4. **"Turin is just a car town."** Not supported: its finance job count (53,600) is third in Italy and tenth among 152 European metros, because Intesa Sanpaolo's headquarters is there [data].
5. **"Every city has a tech scene."** Not supported: Milan and Rome have 147,200 and 121,800 information and communication jobs; Turin 40,300; no other Italian metro reaches 25,000 [data].

## 6. Common mistakes and myths

- Quoting AlmaLaurea averages as Bocconi outcomes: Bocconi is not an AlmaLaurea member [data, library].
- Treating Italian income statistics as starting salaries: the MEF figure covers all declarants with employment income, including part-time.
- Reading GDP as wealth of residents: commuters and registered office effects inflate it for Milan.
- Assuming Rome is only the public sector: it is second in Italy for finance and IT jobs.
- Assuming English suffices: about 4% of Italian postings say Italian is not required.
- Mixing metropolitan regions with comuni: Eurostat's Milan region has 4.3 million people; the comune has about a third of that.

## 7. Decision rules

1. If you want investment banking, asset management, insurance or consulting in Italy, prefer Milan (first by jobs; headquarters of UniCredit, Mediobanca, Banco BPM, FinecoBank, Azimut).
2. If you want energy, defence, state-owned groups or the public sector, prefer Rome (Eni, Enel, Leonardo, Poste, Ferrovie dello Stato).
3. If you want bank headquarters work outside Milan, consider Turin (Intesa Sanpaolo) before Bologna or Florence.
4. If you want a technical or engineering employer in a smaller city, pick by employer: Bologna (Ducati, Marchesini), Genoa (Ansaldo Energia, Esaote, ERG), Padua (Safilo), Naples (Kimbo).
5. If you compare Italian offers across cities, adjust for the Milan pay premium (31% above Rome, 75% above Naples) and for rent (not sourced here).
6. If you are an Italian graduate weighing abroad, read the playbook: pay abroad is higher at five years (about €2,900 net against €1,800) but few come back.
7. If you need a Bocconi-level outcome, use Bocconi's placement report, not AlmaLaurea.
8. If your Italian is weak, target Milan's foreign-owned firms (6,043 in the province) and international teams first.

## 8. Gaps and claims to verify

1. AlmaLaurea field-level and city-level pay (economics, ICT) were not read this round; the library has 2025 field data.
2. Eurostat job counts are for 2021 and omit London, Berlin, Madrid and Frankfurt; Italian ranks among 152 metros are my counts.
3. Rent was not sourced for any hub.
4. Headquarters come from the Global LEI Index, which shows registration, not hiring volumes; Lavazza, Reale Mutua and Generali Italia (Mogliano Veneto, outside the Padua-Venice metros) were not used.
5. No count by role family (analytics, data, accounting, marketing) was read for any city; those families are not rated.
6. The Milan Chamber of Commerce press release was read from a mirror on the Unione Confcommercio Milano website because the Chamber's own site blocked automated access.
7. The impatriati and visa figures are the library's (read 2 October 2026).
8. Hubs beyond these eight (Bari, Verona, Brescia, Bergamo, Trieste, Palermo) rank in the Eurostat counts but are not mapped.
