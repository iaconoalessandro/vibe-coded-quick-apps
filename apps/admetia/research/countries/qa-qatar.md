## 1. Bottom line

- **Qatar has one hub, Doha, and its European-graduate door is narrow.** The Qatar Investment Authority's Journey programme requires a Qatari national or the child of a Qatari mother; its international hiring is for highly experienced specialists (library) [employer-stated]. QatarEnergy's graduate pages are for "recent Qatari graduates" and its vocational and scholarship programmes are for Qataris and children of Qatari women (QatarEnergy careers) [employer-stated].
- **The free zone is where firms are arriving.** The Qatar Financial Centre onboarded 1,135 firms in the first half of 2026, 37% more than a year earlier, for a total of over 4,700; the largest groups were technology and innovation (544), media (173) and consulting and professional services (166) (QFC, 24 August 2026) [data]. These are registrations, not jobs.
- **Doha is a mid-table financial centre.** The Global Financial Centres Index 40 (September 2026) ranks it 52nd of 117 (48th in March), fifth in the Middle East and Africa after Dubai, Abu Dhabi, Casablanca and Riyadh (Z/Yen and CDI) [data].
- **Pay in finance and ICT is high on average, but that is not entry pay.** In 2023, 28,856 paid workers in financial and insurance activities averaged QAR 29,403 a month and 25,965 in information and communication QAR 28,517, against QAR 11,025 across all 2.17 million paid workers (National Planning Council open data; weighting of male and female averages by the author) [data].
- **The start-up scene is small and early-stage.** Over 300 technology start-ups raised $58.7 million in 2025, nearly double the year before, with 93% of deals at early stage (Startup Genome, 2026) [data].
- **Every job is employer-sponsored.** EU citizens need a sponsored work permit in every Gulf state and residence is tied to the contract (library) [data]. No Qatarisation rule was read on an official page.

## 2. The economy and the graduate labour market

**Size and structure.** Real GDP grew 2.9% in the third quarter of 2025 against QAR 180.9 billion a year earlier; non-hydrocarbon activities grew 4.4% and 15 of 17 activities grew; the council notes a comprehensive revision of the national accounts due in the first quarter of 2026 (National Planning Council, 28 December 2025) [data]. Natural gas dominates exports; no sector split was read for 2026.

**Population.** Doha municipality had 1,186,023 residents on the council's 2020 census base, about 40% of the country (NPC open data; Gulf Times headline) [data]. Al Rayyan, with 826,786, is part of the same urban area, so the figure understates Greater Doha.

**Youth and graduate unemployment.** Not read.

**Entry pay by field.** None. Whole-country averages by activity for 2023: finance and insurance QAR 29,403; ICT QAR 28,517; professional, scientific and technical QAR 16,000; construction QAR 5,978 (NPC open data) [data]. These mix nationals, who are paid more in government and energy, with a large expatriate workforce.

**How hiring works.** The sovereign fund, QatarEnergy and Qatar Airways run national graduate programmes (Qatar Airways's page refused automated reads, so only its search listing was seen). Multinational and free-zone firms hire expatriates through sponsored permits.

**Language of work.** English in the free zone; no source read measured it.

**Visa headline.** Tutti i visti, le procedure QID, le riforme Kafala, la Qatarizzazione e i percorsi Education City sono verificati nell'unica fonte di verità: [`visas_immigration/qatar/qatar_visas_immigration_guide.md`](../../visas_immigration/qatar/qatar_visas_immigration_guide.md). ## 3. Hubs

### Doha

The capital and seat of the Qatar Financial Centre; the only city mapped. Population 1,186,023 (municipality, 2020 base). No GDP, wage or rent for the city is published.

**Standing, [national, Middle East, world].** Finance [5, 4, 2]: Doha is Qatar's only GFCI-listed centre (national 5); fifth in the Middle East and Africa and fourth among Middle Eastern centres once Casablanca is set aside (regional 4), which rests on the GFCI ranking; 52nd of 117 is a mid-table centre (world 2) [data]. Software [5, 3, 1]: the national ecosystem is small (over 300 start-ups, $58.7 million raised in 2025), with no ranking above third tier read (regional 3, world 1) [data].

**Employers.** The Qatar Financial Centre (over 4,700 firms), the Qatar Investment Authority (Qataris only), QatarEnergy (Qatari graduates only), and the start-up ecosystem coordinated by the Qatar Development Bank, the commerce ministry and the research council (Startup Genome) [employer-stated].

## 5. Hypotheses tested

1. **"Free-zone registrations equal graduate jobs."** Not supported: 1,135 new registrations are firms, many tiny, and the largest group is technology and innovation vendors; no headcount was read.
2. **"Qatar is easier than Saudi Arabia for a European graduate."** Insufficient evidence: no Qatarisation rule was read, and the three national graduate schemes read are all for Qataris.
3. **"High average pay means high entry pay."** Not supported: the finance average of QAR 29,403 mixes nationals and senior staff and includes no entry-level split.

## 6. Common mistakes and myths

- Reading GFCI 52nd as a "top Gulf hub": it is fifth in the Middle East and Africa, behind Dubai and Abu Dhabi by 40 places or more [data].
- Quoting the whole-economy average wage (QAR 11,025) as what a graduate earns: it is dominated by 668,300 construction workers on QAR 5,978 [data].

## 7. Decision rules

1. If you want the Gulf as a first job and have no sponsor, start with Dubai or Abu Dhabi before Doha (their finance free zones list far more firms and workers).
2. If a Doha offer comes from a free-zone firm, ask who sponsors the permit and what the contract says about notice and exit; residence is tied to it.
3. If the programme says "Qatari national or child of a Qatari mother", do not apply.
4. If you are weighing Doha for security, read the FCDO and Farnesina pages dated within the last month.
