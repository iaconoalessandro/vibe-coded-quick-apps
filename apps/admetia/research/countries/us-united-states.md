# United States: country brief

Today is 3 October 2026. Tags: `[data]` official statistics, government pages and audited school reports, `[employer-stated]`, `[practitioner consensus]` (published indices and specialist synthesis), `[anecdotal]`. Scope: routes in the Atlas are given for EU/EEA/Swiss and UK passports only; a US passport looking at the US is at home.

## 1. Bottom line

- **The visa decides more than the city.** A European master's alone does not give US work rights: Optional Practical Training (OPT) needs a US degree, and the H-1B advanced-degree cap is for US master's holders; routes that need no US degree exist (J-1 Intern/Trainee, L-1, E-2 for Italian and UK nationals) (USCIS rules; visas_immigration/united_states/united_states_visas_immigration_guide.md, Caso 2 and 3) [data]. From FY2027 the H-1B lottery is weighted by wage level: DHS projects about a 15% chance at Level I and 31% at Level II against over 61% at Level IV (Federal Register final rule, 29 Dec 2025) [data]. An entry salary buys one or two entries, so pay level and city both matter.
- **New York is the one world-scale finance centre.** The Global Financial Centres Index 40 (September 2026) ranks it first of 117, four rating points ahead of London, and the only North American centre in the top ten (Z/Yen and CDI) [data]. SIFMA counts about 197,300 people in the city's securities industry; the NYC Comptroller counts a record 201,500 in 2024 [data]. For investment banking, asset management and trading, start in New York.
- **The Bay Area is the software and AI centre of the world.** Silicon Valley and San Francisco drew 49% of all US venture capital in 2025, and the 20 largest tech employers there held about 215,000 jobs (Joint Venture Silicon Valley, 2026 Index) [data]. Startup Genome's 2026 ranking keeps Silicon Valley first, New York second and London third in the world [data]. For software and AI, the Bay Area is the only 5-of-5 at world scale, followed at a distance by New York and Seattle.
- **Headquarters are spread out.** Houston ties Chicago for second place in the Fortune 500 headquarters count (27 each, the Greater Houston Partnership's count; New York 62), with Dallas–Fort Worth at 24 and metro Atlanta at 14 to 15, depending on who draws the metro area (Greater Houston Partnership, RealPage, Dallas Regional Chamber) [data]. Corporate-finance, management and accounting graduates have real choices outside New York: Houston (energy), Dallas (banking, telecoms, airlines), Charlotte (Bank of America, Truist), Chicago (food, airlines, industrials).
- **Pay is the highest in this Atlas, but so is rent.** The BLS mean wage across all occupations ranges from $67,190 a year in Miami to $119,230 in San Jose, and one-bedroom median gross rent from $1,273 a month in Houston to $2,465 in San Jose (BLS OEWS May 2025; Census ACS 2024) [data]. These are all-worker averages, not graduate pay.
- **The graduate market is soft.** US recent graduates had an unemployment rate of about 5.6% in Q2 2026 against 4.3% for all workers, and 42% were underemployed (New York Fed, via evidence/trends.md) [data]. Federal employment in the Washington region fell from about 375,800 to 312,500 between January 2025 and May 2026 (AP reporting BLS) [data], so DC is not the safe public-sector haven it was.
- **Internship timing is early.** US summer analyst roles for 2027 were open by December 2025 (RBC) with Rothschild's deadline on 1 January 2026 (employer pages, via getting-in/recruiting-calendar.md) [employer-stated]. A European student who wants a New York bank summer job needs to be applying a year and a half ahead.

## 2. The economy and the graduate labour market

**Size and structure.** The 14 hubs together hold about 103.2 million residents (the sum of the 14 metro areas below, Census Bureau Vintage 2025) [data]. The BLS counted, in May 2025, 1,687,890 software developers (mean pay $148,100), 361,980 financial and investment analysts ($116,800), 262,440 data scientists ($126,800), 898,280 management analysts ($113,790) and 1,449,500 accountants and auditors ($94,750) across the whole country (OEWS, national estimates) [data]. These five occupations are the nearest official match to the Atlas role families, and the record rates each hub on its share of them (see section 5 for the rule used).

**Output.** The BEA stopped publishing GDP by metropolitan area after the 2023 data; its 2024 county release says it "has discontinued publication of statistics for metropolitan statistical areas" (BEA, GDP by county, 2024) [data]. The Atlas therefore uses the **sum of BEA 2024 county GDP over the counties of each Census metropolitan area**: New York $2,442.5 billion, Los Angeles $1,354.7 billion, Chicago $923.1 billion, Dallas $800.6 billion, San Francisco $801.3 billion. This is Admetia's derived figure on 2023 area definitions, not a BEA metro statistic.

**Youth and graduate unemployment.** About 5.6% for recent graduates in Q2 2026, with 42% underemployed (New York Fed) [data]. No city-level graduate unemployment series was read.

**Entry pay by field.** Official data do not give entry pay by city. Two audited school reports give a benchmark: MIT Sloan's master of finance class of 2025 (89% international) had 97.1% of job seekers with an offer within six months, at a median base of $125,000, with 38.1% of accepted offers in New York and 62.9% in the US overall (MIT CDO, 2025, via places/beyond-europe.md) [data]. Duke's MMS class of 2025 had a median base of $75,000 for visa-needing graduates against $82,500 for those with work rights, and 71% against 85% with an offer at six months (Duke Fuqua, 2025) [data]. The gap between those two reports, and between the visa and no-visa groups, is itself the finding.

**Language of work.** English. No other language is needed in the 14 hubs; Spanish helps in Miami and Houston, but no source was read that measures it.

**How hiring works.** Investment banks recruit summer analysts a year to eighteen months ahead and convert most interns to full-time analysts the next year (Goldman Sachs 2027 Summer Analyst Program page: nine-to-ten-week internship, usually in the third or penultimate year) [employer-stated]. Technology and consulting run on similar autumn calendars. A European master's student on a visa has one OPT year (12 months, plus 24 months of STEM extension only if the degree's classification code is on the DHS STEM list; in business only management science, business statistics, actuarial science, quantitative management, financial mathematics and analytics qualify, not general finance or business administration) [data].

**Visa headline.** F-1 student visa on a Form I-20, on-campus work up to 20 hours a week in term, OPT after graduation, then H-1B through a wage-weighted lottery. The $100,000 payment for new H-1B petitions was extended to 21 September 2027 by Proclamation 11069, but its implementing policies were vacated by two federal courts (June and 30 September 2026) and it is not being collected for now; appeals are pending. A change of status inside the US, such as from F-1 or OPT, is outside the payment only if it is granted, not if it is denied or the person leaves the US first (visas_immigration/united_states/united_states_visas_immigration_guide.md, section 3.2) [data]. Detail is in the Atlas route.

## 3. Hubs

Each hub's population is the Census Bureau's Vintage 2025 estimate for its metropolitan statistical area (CBSA), GDP the sum of BEA 2024 county GDP over that area's counties, pay the BLS OEWS May 2025 mean annual wage of all occupations ÷ 12, and rent the Census Bureau's ACS 2024 median gross rent of one-bedroom rented homes (rent plus utilities). All are for the metro area. Standing steps are [national, North America, world].

### New York

**What it is.** The largest metro area: 20,112,448 residents, GDP $2,442.5 billion, mean pay $86,310 ($7,193 a month) and one-bedroom rent $1,708 (±$15). **Finance.** About 197,300 people work in the city's securities industry (219,100 in the state), and 1 in 13 city jobs is tied to it; the industry paid $6.7 billion, 8.4% of city tax revenue (SIFMA, 10 Feb 2026) [data]. It employed a record 201,500 in 2024, with an average bonus of about $247,000 forecast for 2025 (NYC Comptroller, Dec 2025) [data]. Goldman Sachs, RBC, Rothschild, Perella Weinberg and Raine all recruit for 2027 US summer roles through employer pages [employer-stated]. **Business and software.** 62 Fortune 500 headquarters on the Greater Houston Partnership's count (49 on RealPage's narrower one) and 20 Fortune Global 500 headquarters, fourth in the world after Beijing, Tokyo and Paris (Dallas Regional Chamber, 2025) [data]. In May 2025 the metro had 121,000 software developers (7.2% of the US total, mean pay $165,870), 53,870 financial and investment analysts (14.9% of the US total) and 111,930 accountants and auditors [data]. **Universities.** Columbia and NYU Stern appear in the calculator's MBA list. **Ecosystem.** Second in the world and in North America in Startup Genome 2026 [data].

**Standing.** Finance [5, 5, 5]: first in the GFCI, which makes it the leader at every scale (us-gfci-ny, us-sifma). Business [5, 5, 4]: most Fortune 500 headquarters; fourth in the world for Global 500 headquarters, behind Beijing, Tokyo and Paris, so world 4. Software [4, 4, 4]: second to Silicon Valley in both the North American and world start-up ranks. Data science, management, accounting, marketing [4, 3, 2]: large BLS employment (the New York metro is 14.9% of US financial analysts), but no cross-city ranking was read, so the regional 3 and world 2 are conservative judgements.

### San Jose and Silicon Valley

**What it is.** 1,984,473 residents, GDP $441.8 billion (Santa Clara and San Benito counties), mean pay $119,230 ($9,936 a month, the highest of the 14), rent $2,465 (±$67, the highest). Tech is an estimated 27% of employment in the San Jose area (CompTIA, 27 Mar 2026) [data]. The 20 largest tech employers of Silicon Valley and San Francisco held about 215,000 jobs, 9% of the region's workforce (Google, Apple, Meta, Amazon, Cisco, Tesla); NVIDIA added 1,000 Bay Area jobs in 2025 while Google cut 4,000 (Joint Venture Silicon Valley) [data]. The region hosts about half of US unicorns and nearly 140,000 software developers [data]. The BLS counts 87,350 software developers in the metro (5.2% of the US) with mean pay $221,710 [data]. **Ecosystem.** Startup Genome puts Silicon Valley first in the world [data]. Stanford GSB (MBA) is the calculator's school here.

**Standing.** Software, IT and AI [5, 5, 5]: first in the world in Startup Genome, 49% of US venture capital. Business [4, 4, 3]: 9 Global 500 headquarters (San Jose), 21 Fortune 500 on RealPage's count. Finance is not rated: only 3,880 financial and investment analysts (1.1% of the US).

### Chicago

**What it is.** 9,434,123 residents, GDP $923.1 billion, mean pay $73,380 ($6,115 a month), rent $1,310 (±$29). Fortune 500 headquarters: 27 on the Greater Houston Partnership's count, 30 on RealPage's; Illinois has 29. Named headquarters: Archer Daniels Midland (rank 58), United Airlines (81), McDonald's (170), Kraft Heinz (184), Exelon (189), GE HealthCare (217), Motorola Solutions (378) (Illinois DCEO) [employer-stated]. GFCI 40 ranks it 15th of 117 and fourth in North America [data]. BLS: 17,150 financial and investment analysts (4.7% of the US), 40,370 software developers [data].

**Standing.** Finance [4, 4, 3]: GFCI 15th world, fourth in North America (top five, so 4; within the top fifteen of 117, so 3). Business [4, 4, 3]: Fortune 500 second or third; 8 Global 500 headquarters. Management and logistics [4, 3, 2]: BLS counts.

### Los Angeles

**What it is.** 12,844,441 residents, GDP $1,354.7 billion, mean pay $77,270 ($6,439 a month), rent $1,822 (±$18). Los Angeles County's economy exceeds $1 trillion and grew 2.4% in 2025, though the county lost about 400,000 residents since before the pandemic and the 2028 Olympics are projected to add up to $18 billion (LAEDC) [employer-stated]. GFCI 14th, third in North America [data]. Only 4 Global 500 headquarters [data]. Startup Genome puts it fourth in North America [data]. BLS: 19,600 financial analysts, 70,640 accountants, 55,540 software developers [data]. Named employers are thin: Los Angeles agencies' pages refused automated reads (see gaps).

**Standing.** Finance [4, 4, 3] on the GFCI. Management, accounting, logistics, marketing [4, 3, 2] on BLS employment (accountants 70,640, logisticians 10,950).

### Houston

**What it is.** 7,904,627 residents, GDP $758.3 billion, mean pay $67,610 ($5,634 a month), rent $1,273 (±$20, the lowest of the 14). Twenty-seven Fortune 500 headquarters: Exxon Mobil (rank 9), Chevron (21), Phillips 66 (29), Sysco (55), ConocoPhillips (75), Hewlett Packard Enterprise (133), Baker Hughes (164) (Greater Houston Partnership, 4 Jun 2026) [data]. Houston, Dallas and Charlotte are not among the GFCI's 117 centres [data]. BLS: 22,940 software developers, 28,400 accountants.

### Dallas

**What it is.** 8,477,157 residents (Dallas–Fort Worth), GDP $800.6 billion, mean pay $70,630 ($5,886 a month), rent $1,473 (±$21). 24 Fortune 500 headquarters, among them McKesson (a Fortune 10 company), AT&T (35), American Airlines (86), CBRE (118) and Texas Instruments (252); seven are on the Global 500 (Dallas Regional Chamber, 2026) [data]. ## 5. Hypotheses tested

1. **"The best American job is in New York."** Partly supported. For finance, yes (GFCI first of 117; securities industry about 197,000 jobs). For software and AI, no: the Bay Area is first in the world and New York second. For headquarters, New York leads on count but Houston, Chicago and Dallas each hold less than half its number, so corporate graduates have credible alternatives. Fails for energy (Houston) and federal-adjacent consulting (Washington).
2. **"A high-pay city is a high-value city."** Supported only before rent. San Jose pays the most ($9,936 a month mean, all workers) but has the highest one-bed rent ($2,465); the monthly pay-to-rent ratio is about 4.0 there against about 4.4 in Houston ($5,634 ÷ $1,273), 4.7 in Chicago ($6,115 ÷ $1,310) and 4.2 in New York ($7,193 ÷ $1,708). Rent here is a median over all one-bedroom rented homes, not a new-lease asking rent, so the ratio flatters long-term tenants. Fails when graduates take entry jobs well below the all-worker mean.
3. **"A European master's is enough to work in the US."** Not supported. OPT and the H-1B advanced-degree cap need a US degree; the weighted lottery favours higher wage levels (DHS projects 15% at Level I). Holds only for roles that sponsor from abroad or intra-company transfers, which the record does not cover.
4. **"Washington means a safe government career."** Not supported for 2026: federal employment fell by about 63,000 in 16 months and the metro lost 100,500 jobs in all between May 2025 and May 2026 (AP reporting BLS). Management consultancies and Amazon's HQ2 are partial offsets.
5. **"Texas is cheap, so entry pay goes further."** Partly supported. Houston has the lowest rent of the 14 and Dallas (fifth-lowest) saw tech jobs grow 26% in 2021–24; but their mean pay is also about 22% (Houston) and 18% (Dallas) lower than New York's, and neither is a finance centre by the GFCI (Houston and Dallas are not among the 117).

## 6. Common mistakes and myths

- **Treating the metro mean as a graduate salary.** The BLS mean pay in the table is across all occupations and all ages; the real starting benchmarks are the school reports (MIT $125,000 median base; Duke $75,000 for visa-needing graduates).
- **Reading a county GDP as a metro GDP.** New York County is Manhattan alone at $1,006.7 billion; the metro is $2,442.5 billion. The Atlas now sums counties so hubs compare like with like.
- **Assuming OPT is automatic with any degree.** The STEM extension depends on the programme's classification code, and general finance and business administration are not on the list (DHS STEM list, via places/beyond-europe.md) [data].
- **Expecting a Miami finance job to be a Wall Street job.** Miami-Dade has 150,000 finance jobs but is 36th in the GFCI; the Atlas rates it strong in finance nationally only.
- **Applying in the spring for a summer analyst role.** Banks open a year and a half ahead (RBC, Rothschild, Perella Weinberg, Raine for 2027) [employer-stated].

## 7. Decision rules

1. If you want investment banking, sales and trading or asset management and can work in the US, prefer New York (GFCI first of 117; 197,300 securities jobs), and apply a year and a half ahead.
2. If you want software, AI or venture capital, prefer the Bay Area (San Jose or San Francisco), not New York (49% of US venture capital; first in Startup Genome).
3. If your degree has a STEM-listed classification code, prefer the cities with deeper developer markets (Seattle, Dallas, Washington) when the visa clock matters: 36 months of OPT allows up to three annual lottery rounds instead of one.
4. If you want corporate finance, management or accounting in an operating company, compare Houston, Dallas, Chicago and Charlotte before New York: Houston, Chicago and Dallas have between 24 and 27 Fortune 500 headquarters, Charlotte has Bank of America and Truist, and one-bed rent is below $1,500 in all four.
5. If you want consulting for the public sector, prefer Washington, but expect a smaller federal base after the 2025–26 cuts (312,500 federal jobs by May 2026).
6. If you want life sciences, prefer Boston (117,108 jobs, first among US biopharma clusters) then Philadelphia (88,000, fifth).
7. If your priority is the cheapest rent for a given pay, Houston ($1,273 rent, $5,634 pay) and Chicago ($1,310, $6,115) beat Dallas, Atlanta and the coastal cities.
8. If you hold a European master's only, do not plan a US career without a sponsor or a US degree; prefer a US master's (MIT MFin, Duke MMS-type programmes) or a transfer inside a multinational.
9. If a single employer is the goal (Amazon, Bank of America, Exxon Mobil), choose the city by its headquarters, not by the city's ranking.

## 8. Gaps and claims to verify

1. New York finance is rated from industry and city statistics (SIFMA, the New York City Comptroller); the State Comptroller's securities-industry report refused automated reads.
2. H-1B odds for entry-level wage levels are DHS projections; USCIS has not published FY2027 counts by level.
3. Ratings for the seven business and computing occupations rest on BLS employment in one matching occupation per family (software developers, financial and investment analysts, data scientists, management analysts, accountants and auditors, logisticians, market research analysts and marketing specialists): at least 4% of the US total is rated strong and at least 2% present. These measure the market, not graduate jobs.
4. Metro GDP is derived from the BEA county series on 2023 area definitions (the BEA discontinued its own metro series); Virginia independent cities are combined with their counties by the BEA, so the Washington sum uses BEA's combined areas.
5. Pay is the BLS all-occupation mean; rent is the Census survey's median gross rent of one-bedroom rented homes, not asking rents; HUD fair market rents could not be read.
6. Named employers for Los Angeles, Boston, Seattle, Washington and San Francisco are thin because several employer and city-agency pages refused automated reads (Boston Planning and Development Agency, Axios, State Street, Fidelity, Vanguard, Microsoft's filings).
7. Startup Genome's full top-40 table is an image: only ranks stated in its text were used (Boston and Los Angeles as third and fourth in North America).
8. Fortune 500 counts by metro differ between sources (New York 62 vs 49; Houston 27 vs 26); both are shown.
9. No source read measured demand by city for economics, analytics, AI (outside the Bay Area), computer science or big data, so those stay "not rated".
