# Poland: country brief

Today is 3 October 2026. Tags: `[data]` official statistics or government pages, `[employer-stated]`, `[practitioner consensus]` (published indices and the library's syntheses), `[anecdotal]`.

## 1. Bottom line

- Poland's graduate market is multipolar. Warsaw is the largest by far: 119,200 information-and-communication workers (24% of Poland's 496,800) and 113,800 finance-and-insurance workers (28% of 405,700) in the metropolitan region in 2021 (Eurostat). Among the 25 European capital regions that report these figures, Warsaw is fourth for ICT and second for finance, after Paris [data].
- Next come Kraków (65,800 ICT workers), Katowice (49,400 ICT, 31,600 finance, the second-largest finance count after Warsaw) and Wrocław (47,800 ICT); then Gdańsk (32,000), Poznań (26,800) and Łódź (23,200) [data].
- The characteristic employer is the business-service centre. At the end of Q1 2025, 488,700 people worked in 2,081 centres; Warsaw has 429, Kraków 324 and Wrocław 258, and Warsaw and Kraków each employ more than 100,000 (ABSL, via the library) [data]. 74.1% of centres use intelligent process automation, and Big Four partners describe junior transactional roles shrinking [practitioner consensus].
- Pay is modest against Western Europe but the cities differ: in 2025 the average monthly gross wage was PLN 9,371 nationally, PLN 11,678 in Warsaw, 11,404 in Kraków, 10,148 in Wrocław and 9,367 in Łódź (Statistics Poland) [data]. A junior specialist in a centre earned a mean base of €1,744 a month in 2024 (ABSL citing Mercer, via the library) [data].
- Standing: Warsaw is 5 nationally in business services, IT, software and finance, 3 in Europe for business services, IT and finance (2 for software), and 2 or 1 at world scale. The Global Financial Centres Index 40 ranks Warsaw 78th and third in Eastern Europe, and the Warsaw Stock Exchange says it is the largest exchange in Central and Eastern Europe [practitioner consensus, employer-stated]. Startup Genome values Warsaw's ecosystem at $3 billion and Kraków's at $1 billion against a European average of $14.3 billion [practitioner consensus].
- For a European master's student, Poland is a place to work in finance operations, risk, IT and technology roles inside a named centre or bank, often in a Western European language; it is not a market for client-facing investment banking or consulting entry, which this round did not find evidence for.

## 2. The economy and the graduate labour market

**Size.** Poland had 36.75 million people in 2023 and GDP of €654.6 billion in 2022 (Eurostat) [data]; the library puts it as the sixth-largest EU economy (JLL, October 2025). Unemployment was 3.1% in 2025, among the lowest in the EU (Eurostat) [data].

**Structure.** ICT specialists were 4.5% of employment in 2025 (778,800 people), below the EU's 5.0% (Eurostat) [data]. The business-service sector contributes an estimated 5.7% of GDP in 2025 and employs over 480,000 (JLL, 20 October 2025; ABSL's 2025 figure is 488,700) [data].

**Graduates and youth.** In 2025, 91.2% of recent tertiary graduates aged 20-34 were in work (EU 85.3%); youth unemployment was 12.2% (EU 15.2%); 45.2% of 25-to-34-year-olds had a degree (EU 44.8%) (Eurostat) [data].

**Language.** 19.6% of service-centre staff are foreigners, 8.3% of those Italians; most centres pay a monthly language allowance of PLN 600 to 2,000 (ABSL, citing Mercer) [data]. A Western European language is a real salary premium in the centres.

**Entry pay.** See above; there is no Poland-wide graduate pay survey read. After tax a single worker on the average wage keeps 71.7% of gross, and on junior pay a central one-bedroom flat leaves about €190 a month in Warsaw and about €500 in Wrocław (author calculation on Numbeo rents, via the library) [data]. Rent was not re-read by city on 3 October (Numbeo refused access).

**How hiring works.** Centres hire in cohorts by language; no graduate calendars were read (gap).

**Permits.** EU citizens register residence with the voivode after three months; non-EU graduates of Polish full-time studies get one nine-month permit to look for work and are permanently exempt from work permits (official guide: visas_immigration/poland/poland_visas_immigration_guide.md).

## 3. Hubs

### Warsaw

**What it is.** 3,269,510 residents in the metropolitan region (2023), GDP €99.7 billion (2021), average gross pay PLN 11,678 a month (Eurostat; Statistics Poland) [data].

**Sectors and named employers.** Service centres (429), Goldman Sachs' technology and engineering base [practitioner consensus], Bending Spoons (office opened September 2026) [employer-stated], Bolt (one of four key hubs) [employer-stated], CD PROJEKT (registered in Warsaw) [employer-stated], and the Warsaw Stock Exchange [employer-stated].

### Kraków

**What it is.** 1,543,724 residents (metro, 2023), GDP €28.7 billion (2021), average pay PLN 11,404 (city, 2025).

**Sectors and employers.** Service centres employing nearly 108,000 people (Q1 2025), HSBC's centre in 11 languages [employer-stated]; ICT 65,800 jobs and finance 28,600 [data].

**Standing:** business 5 / 3 / 2; IT 4 / 2 / 1; finance 4 / 2 / 1. The ecosystem value is $1 billion [practitioner consensus].

### Wrocław

**What it is.** 671,206 residents (metro), €16.6 billion GDP, average pay PLN 10,148. 258 service centres; ICT 47,800 jobs, fourth in Poland. Standing: business 4 / 2 / 1, IT 4 / 2 / 1. Lower rents than Warsaw and Kraków [library, via Numbeo].

### Katowice (Upper Silesia)

2,535,354 residents (metro), €44.6 billion GDP, average pay PLN 10,794. ICT 49,400 jobs (third in Poland), finance and insurance 31,600 (second). Standing: IT 4 / 2 / 1, finance 4 / 2 / 1. No named employer was read.

### Gdańsk, Poznań, Łódź

Eurostat counts ICT jobs of 32,000, 26,800 and 23,200 and finance jobs of 20,000, 18,700 and 15,000 (2021); they are the fifth to seventh largest. Average pay PLN 10,956, 10,285 and 9,367. Populations (metro, 2023) 1,368,405, 1,249,495 and 1,038,261. Standing: IT 4 / 1 / 1 for Gdańsk, 3 / 1 / 1 for Poznań and Łódź. No family is rated for demand because no named employer or ranking was read.

## 5. Hypotheses tested

1. **"Kraków is the centre of Polish business services."** Partly supported: its centres employed nearly 108,000 people in Q1 2025 and ABSL puts it first for highly qualified staff (2024 report), but Warsaw has the most centres (429 against 324). The two are joint leaders [data].
2. **"Warsaw is a regional finance capital."** Supported for Eastern Europe (GFCI third behind Astana and Cyprus; the largest exchange in the region by its own statement) but not at European scale: 78th of 117 in GFCI 40 [practitioner consensus].
3. **"Service centres are shrinking the entry rung."** Partly supported: 74.1% use intelligent process automation and Big Four partners describe fewer junior transactional roles [practitioner consensus], while ABSL still reports about 488,700 jobs and Eurostat 91.2% employment for recent graduates.

## 6. Common mistakes and myths

- **Poland is not only Warsaw.** Kraków, Wrocław and Katowice each have ICT workforces of 47,000 to 66,000.
- **Average wage is not junior wage.** The PLN 11,678 Warsaw average includes senior staff; the junior base in centres was €1,744 a month in 2024.
- **Metropolitan job counts date from 2021.** Treat the shares as indicative.

## 7. Decision rules

1. If you speak a Western European language and want a first finance, risk or IT-support job, look at the named centres in Kraków, Warsaw and Wrocław (HSBC and similar).
2. If you want product or engineering work, compare Warsaw (largest ICT base) with Kraków and Wrocław, and apply to named tech employers (Bolt, Bending Spoons, CD PROJEKT).
3. If you want a capital-markets or investment-banking career, do not choose Poland for entry; Warsaw is the regional exchange centre but 78th globally.
4. If you are non-EU and graduate in Poland, use the nine-month permit once and have an offer lined up.
5. If cost matters, rents are lower in Wrocław than in Warsaw; check current rents because they were not re-read on the day.
