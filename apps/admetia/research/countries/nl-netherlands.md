# Netherlands: country brief

Today is 3 October 2026. Tags: `[data]` official statistics or government pages, `[employer-stated]`, `[practitioner consensus]` (published indices and specialist synthesis), `[anecdotal]`.

## 1. Bottom line

- The Netherlands is the continent's most English-friendly large job market: 7.8% of Dutch job postings say Dutch is not required, the highest share among the large European markets (Indeed Hiring Lab, 10 Oct 2024, via places/countries-and-cities.md) [data]. Amsterdam hosts about 40% of the international graduates who are still working in the country five years after graduating (Nuffic 2025) [data]. Only 25% of international graduates are still in the country five years on (Nuffic, 15 May 2025) [data].
- Amsterdam is the national leader (5 of 5) in finance, IT and business, but a recognised secondary centre, not a top European one (3 of 5): it is eighth among Western European financial centres in the Global Financial Centres Index 40 (September 2026) [practitioner consensus] and has the seventh-most jobs in both information and communication (114,000) and finance and insurance (67,000) among the 152 metropolitan regions in Eurostat's table (2021-22; German and British regions are missing from it) [data].
- Rotterdam is the one place where the Netherlands is first in Europe: 397.3 million tonnes of goods in 2024, the most of any EU port, followed by Antwerp-Bruges at 244.2 million (Eurostat) [data]. The port authority counts about 182,000 jobs, directly and indirectly, in the Rotterdam-Rijnmond area [employer-stated].
- Eindhoven is one employer's story: ASML reports more than 44,000 employees (FTEs) in its 2025 annual report and has its global headquarters in Veldhoven [employer-stated]. It is a good choice for engineers and computer scientists, a narrow one for business graduates.
- Utrecht is the quiet second: Eurostat puts it second in the Netherlands, after Amsterdam, for jobs in information and communication (60,800 in 2023) and finance and insurance (37,000), with the national railway's head office and Utrecht University [data, employer-stated]. The Hague is government and international law: 37% of its jobs are in public administration, defence, education and health [data].
- Pay is high for what it buys in Europe but not in absolute terms: employees under 30 in Dutch firms with 10 or more staff earned a mean €34,109 gross in 2022 (€45,349 for professionals) [data]. A one-bedroom flat in the centre is about €2,266 a month in Amsterdam against about €1,506 in Eindhoven (Numbeo, crowd-sourced) [anecdotal]. Non-EU graduates face a €3,122 gross monthly threshold after the orientation year (see the record).
- For a European master's student: go to Amsterdam for trading, payments, consulting and headquarters roles, to Rotterdam for logistics, shipping and energy, to Eindhoven for chip-equipment engineering. Do not expect the Hague to be a graduate-employer market beyond the civil service and international courts.

## 2. The economy and the graduate labour market

**Size.** GDP was €1,170.6 billion in 2025; unemployment was 3.9% overall and 8.8% among 15-to-24-year-olds (Eurostat nama_10_gdp, une_rt_a) [data]. The population was 18,044,027 on 1 January 2025 (Eurostat demo_pjan) [data].

**Structure.** Four ideas matter for graduates: a finance and trading cluster in Amsterdam (Optiver, IMC and Flow Traders have their headquarters there, per the library's countries-and-cities file [practitioner consensus]); a payments and fintech cluster (Adyen was founded in Amsterdam in 2006 and says it has 4,000+ employees [employer-stated]); a world-scale port and process-industry system around Rotterdam; and a chip-equipment and high-tech cluster around Eindhoven.

**Graduates.** In 2025 the employment rate of 20-to-34-year-olds with a tertiary degree was 92.7%, and 91.8% for those who finished within the last five years, the highest of the five countries covered in this round (Eurostat edat_lfse_24) [data]. A graduate who looks for work finds it; the question is whether the work is in English and in the right city.

**Entry pay.** Eurostat's Structure of Earnings Survey 2022 gives mean annual gross earnings for employees under 30 in firms with 10 or more staff, public administration excluded: €34,109 for all occupations and €45,349 for professionals, against €50,942 for all ages (earn_ses22_28) [data]. These are means for all under-30s, including people without degrees, so they understate a master's graduate's start and overstate a part-time worker's. No pay series by city exists in the sources read; the Atlas shows no wage metric for Dutch hubs.

**How hiring works.** The recruiting calendar and the 30% tax facility are in the library (getting-in/recruiting-calendar.md; the record's `nl-30` claim). Trading firms and the large banks recruit on fixed autumn cycles; no calendar was found for Rotterdam's port employers or ASML beyond their careers pages (gap).

**Language and permits.** Dutch is not required in 7.8% of postings and about 5% are written in English (Indeed Hiring Lab) [data]; the other 90% of postings want Dutch in some measure. EU citizens need no permit; non-EU graduates use the one-year orientation year, then a recognised sponsor and €3,122 gross a month (2026) as a highly skilled migrant (IND, via the library) [data]. See `visas_immigration/netherlands/netherlands_visas_immigration_guide.md` for the single source of truth on visas, work rights and residence rules.

### Amsterdam

**What it is.** The financial, creative and headquarters centre: 3,397,323 residents in the metropolitan region, GDP €201.1 billion (2021), a one-bedroom flat about €2,266 a month. Greater Amsterdam (NUTS 3 NL32B) has 1,144,700 jobs: 91,300 in information and communication, 60,100 in finance and insurance and 297,600 in professional, scientific, technical and administrative services [data].

**Employers.** Optiver, IMC, Flow Traders (trading firms); Adyen (payments); the University of Amsterdam (over 44,000 students and 6,200 employees [employer-stated]); a start-up ecosystem Startup Genome values at $83 billion and ranks joint 23rd in the world in 2026 [practitioner consensus]. ABN AMRO reports 21,995 internal FTEs, but its page does not state a head-office location, so it is not cited as an Amsterdam claim.

### Rotterdam and Delft

**What it is.** Europe's busiest port and its industry: 1,868,227 residents in the metropolitan region, GDP €95.2 billion (2021), rent about €1,627. Groot-Rijnmond has 831,500 jobs, 46,200 of them in manufacturing [data]. Delft (TU Delft) and the Rotterdam School of Management (RSM) sit in the hub; the calculator lists RSM's three master's programmes and TU Delft's computer science MSc.

**Employers and port.** The Port of Rotterdam Authority has about 1,440 employees and puts the port's employment at about 182,000 jobs and its added value at €23.3 billion, 2.2% of Dutch GDP [employer-stated]. It handled 428.4 million tonnes and 14.2 million TEU in 2025 [data]. Eurostat's 2024 table puts it first among EU ports by a wide margin [data].

**Standing.** Logistics 5 / 5 / 2. National 5 and Europe 5: Rotterdam is the largest EU port by gross weight of goods, with 397.3 million tonnes against 244.2 million for the next (Eurostat, 2024). The table covers the EU only, so Norwegian, British and Turkish ports are not compared. World 2: no world ranking was read.

### Eindhoven (Brainport)

**What it is.** The high-tech region around ASML: 803,180 residents, GDP €46.4 billion (2021), rent about €1,506. The Eindhoven region (NUTS 3 NL414) has 502,100 jobs, 80,400 of them (16%) in manufacturing and 16,300 in information and communication [data]. Eurostat's 2021 counts put the metropolitan region's ICT and finance jobs far below Utrecht's, so the hub is rated for computing roles only through ASML.

**Employers.** ASML (headquarters at De Run 6501, Veldhoven; more than 44,000 employees (FTEs), 143 nationalities, 2025 annual report [employer-stated]; plans a campus for 20,000 more staff, Brainport, 21 May 2024). Brainport Eindhoven lists Eindhoven University of Technology, Fontys, Avans and Tilburg University as the region's universities [employer-stated]. Philips, NXP and VDL were not read.

**Standing.** Software 3 / 2 / 1 and computer science 3 / 2 / 1. ASML is a very large named employer, but its employment is mostly engineering and the sourced figures stop at "present"; no city or regional ranking for software jobs was read.

### Utrecht

**What it is.** The central city of the Randstad: 1,387,643 residents, GDP €83.3 billion (2021), rent about €1,678. The Utrecht region (NUTS 3 NL350) has 891,800 jobs, 60,800 in information and communication, 37,000 in finance and insurance and 177,800 in professional and administrative services [data].

**Employers.** NS (head office at Laan van Puntenburg 100) [employer-stated]; Utrecht University (over 38,000 students, over 8,800 staff) [employer-stated]. Rabobank's page refused automated access, so its Utrecht head office is not cited.

**Standing.** IT 4 / 2 / 1 and finance 4 / 2 / 1. National 4: second in the Netherlands after Amsterdam on both counts. Europe 2: 19th and 18th in Eurostat's table of 152 metros. World 1.

### The Hague

**What it is.** The seat of government and of international courts: 1,150,797 residents, GDP €56.0 billion (2021). The agglomeration (NUTS 3 NL361) has 514,200 jobs, 191,400 of them (37%) in public administration, defence, education and health, 20,000 in information and communication and 12,300 in finance and insurance [data].

**Employers.** About 200 international organisations, including the International Court of Justice, Europol and the International Criminal Court (The Hague International Centre) [employer-stated]; the ICC's permanent premises are at Oude Waalsdorperweg 10 [employer-stated]. Shell's head office could not be cited from a page read.

**Standing.** IT 3 / 2 / 1 (fourth in the Netherlands for ICT jobs at NUTS 3 level), shown for completeness; no family is rated.

## 5. Hypotheses tested

1. **"The Netherlands is Amsterdam."** Partly supported. Amsterdam has 40% of the international graduates who stay and a seventh-in-Europe ICT and finance count, but Rotterdam is first in Europe in its industry and Eindhoven is built on one global employer. Holds for finance, IT and business roles for English speakers; fails for logistics and chip equipment.
2. **"Amsterdam is a top-five European finance centre."** Not supported. GFCI 40 ranks it 26th in the world and eighth in Western Europe, and Eurostat's metro table puts it seventh for finance jobs without German or British metros. Evidence: nl-gfci, nl-ams-rank.
3. **"Everyone stays."** Not supported. Only 25% of international graduates are still in the country five years later, and EEA graduates stay at about half the non-EEA rate (20.3% against 38.5%) (Nuffic) [data]. The Netherlands is a launchpad, not a destination, for Europeans.
4. **"A Dutch employer will hire you in English."** Partly supported. Only 7.8% of postings say Dutch is not required. It holds in tech, trading and international headquarters; it fails in the civil service, retail and most mid-sized firms.

## 6. Common mistakes and myths

- Treating the Randstad as one labour market: Utrecht is second for ICT and finance jobs and is half an hour from Amsterdam by train, so a second city is a real option.
- Assuming Eindhoven needs Dutch: it is the most international hub, but the hiring is engineering-led; business graduates compete for a small number of roles.
- Quoting averages: the €34,109 mean for under-30s covers all education levels and sectors in firms of 10 or more people, not a master's start.
- Reading rent as a salary signal: Amsterdam's €2,266 is crowd-sourced and the metropolitan GDP is not wages.

## 7. Decision rules

1. If you want trading, payments or an international headquarters and you do not speak Dutch, prefer Amsterdam (5 of 5 nationally for finance, IT and business).
2. If you want logistics, shipping, energy or process-industry operations, prefer Rotterdam (first among EU ports by tonnage).
3. If you are a computer scientist or engineer and want one very large employer, prefer Eindhoven; if you are a business graduate, check specific ASML or supplier roles before moving.
4. If rent decides the first year, compare Amsterdam (about €2,266) with Utrecht (about €1,678) and Rotterdam (about €1,627): the train link costs less than the rent gap.
5. If you are non-EU, plan the orientation year and the €3,122 sponsor threshold before choosing a city: the sponsor list matters more than the hub.
6. If you want public policy, courts or international law, prefer The Hague (about 200 international organisations).
7. If you expect to stay beyond five years, treat the Netherlands as one of the few places with a high graduate employment rate (91.8% of recent tertiary graduates in work) but plan for Dutch.

## 8. Gaps and claims to verify

1. No pay series by city; entry pay by field not found. Statistics Netherlands publishes wages by sector, not by city.
2. Rabobank, ING, Shell, Philips, NXP and ABN AMRO head-office or headcount claims were not read on pages that state them.
3. Numbeo rents are from August 2026 Internet Archive snapshots (Numbeo blocked direct access); The Hague has no rent.
4. Eurostat's metro table (2021-22) omits German, British and Swiss regions, so the "seventh in Europe" ranks are among 152 regions, not among all European cities.
5. The orientation-year and HSM thresholds are the library's, read on 30 September-2 October 2026.
