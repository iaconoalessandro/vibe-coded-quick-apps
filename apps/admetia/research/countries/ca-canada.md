# Canada: country brief

Today is 3 October 2026. Tags: `[data]` official statistics and government pages, `[employer-stated]`, `[practitioner consensus]` (published indices, specialist and trade-press synthesis), `[anecdotal]`. Routes in the Atlas are given for EU/EEA/Swiss and UK passports only (they are identical).

## 1. Bottom line

- **Canada has one finance and corporate market, Toronto, and four specialist ones.** Statistics Canada counts 3,736,700 people employed in the Toronto area in 2025, with 455,000 in finance, insurance, real estate, rental and leasing and 175,200 in professional occupations in finance, against 73,800 in Montréal and 60,100 in Vancouver (Table 14-10-0468) [data]. Fortune's 2026 Global 500 lists seven Canadian companies headquartered in Toronto among those checked, including Royal Bank of Canada (rank 110), Toronto-Dominion (140), Scotiabank (290) and CIBC (357) [data].
- **Toronto is also the largest tech market, and the only one in a world top 15.** CompTIA projects more than 414,000 tech jobs in the Toronto area in 2025, nearly 11% of its workforce against 6.8% for Canada [data]; Startup Genome's 2026 report puts Toronto-Waterloo 13th in the world, up seven places [data].
- **Montréal is the artificial-intelligence city and the cheapest big one.** Montréal International counts more than 48,000 experts with AI skills and names Mila the world's largest academic AI research centre [employer-stated]; the average one-bedroom rent in purpose-built flats was C$1,131 in 2025 against C$1,761 in Toronto (CMHC via Statistics Canada) [data]. French at work is the price (see section 2).
- **Calgary is where the energy head offices are.** Fortune's 2026 Global 500 lists Enbridge (335), Cenovus (465), Canadian Natural Resources (471) and Suncor (472) with Calgary headquarters [data], and 46,300 Calgarians worked in oil, gas, mining and forestry in 2025 against 5,000 in Toronto [data]. It is a business and engineering market, not a finance one: Calgary ranks 77th of 117 in the GFCI, last of the 14 North American centres.
- **Ottawa is the federal capital and Shopify's home.** 194,000 people worked in public administration in the Ottawa–Gatineau area in 2025, and 91,200 in natural and applied science professions, the highest share of employment of the five hubs (10.4%) [data]. Shopify is headquartered there with 8,100 employees (Fortune) [data].
- **The permit is generous; settling is hard.** Any public-university master's of at least eight months earns a three-year post-graduation work permit with no field-of-study rule, but university graduates need CLB 7 in all four language skills (IRCC, via places/beyond-europe.md) [data]; Ontario's Masters Graduate stream, which gave permanent residence without a job offer, closed on 30 May 2026 [data].
- **Pay is high, but rent narrows the gap.** Full-year full-time workers earned an average of C$97,300 in the Toronto area, C$94,100 in Vancouver, C$94,000 in Ottawa–Gatineau, C$87,700 in Calgary and C$84,300 in Montréal in 2024 (Statistics Canada, Table 11-10-0240) [data]. These are all-worker averages, not graduate pay.

## 2. The economy and the graduate labour market

**Size and structure.** Canada's labour force survey counted 21,173,000 people employed in August 2026, with unemployment at 6.4% (Statistics Canada, The Daily, 4 September 2026) [data]. Canada's net tech employment was 1.45 million in 2024, 6.8% of its workforce (CompTIA, 17 November 2025) [data]. The five hubs hold about 18.3 million people, using the July 2025 estimates for the metropolitan areas (Statistics Canada, Table 17-10-0148): Toronto 7,108,874, Montréal 4,597,837, Vancouver 3,088,036, Calgary 1,836,012 and Ottawa–Gatineau 1,700,014 [data].

**Output.** GDP at basic prices by metropolitan area stops at 2022 (Table 36-10-0468): Toronto C$522.4 billion, Montréal C$279.5 billion, Vancouver C$202.5 billion, Calgary C$130.0 billion and Ottawa–Gatineau C$105.8 billion (the Ontario part, C$88.0 billion, plus the Quebec part, C$17.8 billion) [data]. The year is four years old; treat the ranking, not the level, as the message.

**Youth and graduate unemployment.** Unemployment for 15-to-24-year-olds was 12.9% in August 2026, against 14.3% a year earlier and a pre-pandemic (2017–2019) average of 10.8%; in the Toronto area the all-age rate was 6.7%, down from a high of 9.0% in July 2025 (Statistics Canada, The Daily) [data]. No series for recent graduates of master's programmes was read.

**Entry pay by field.** Statistics Canada does not publish entry pay by city. Two benchmarks: Ivey's MSc class of 2023/24 had an 85% offer rate and a median base of C$75,000, with 30% going into financial services and 80% of the jobs of its 2023 business analytics class in the Toronto area (Ivey MSc Employment Report, via places/beyond-europe.md) [data]. Rotman's Master of Finance asks for two or more years of experience, so it is not a pre-experience degree (programme pages, via places/beyond-europe.md) [employer-stated]. The 2024 average employment income of full-year full-time workers is above the Ivey median in every hub because it covers all ages and ranks.

**How hiring works.** RBC's Canadian student page lists co-ops and internships and new-graduate rotational programmes, without dates on the page read (RBC careers) [employer-stated]. No dated recruiting calendar was found for the other banks, whose career pages loaded no content. Off-campus work during study is allowed for up to 24 hours a week in term and unlimited hours in scheduled breaks (IRCC, modified 15 April 2026) [data], which is how most students reach co-ops and internships.

**Language.** English in Toronto, Vancouver, Calgary and the Ontario side of Ottawa. In Québec, businesses with 25 to 49 employees had to register with the Office québécois de la langue française from 1 June 2025, as larger firms already did, and assess their use of French (BCF law firm summary of the Charter, 29 May 2025) [practitioner consensus]. For Montréal, plan for French; how far an employer may require it for a given job was not read on a primary page.

**Visa headline.** Full official guide and single source of truth: `visas_immigration/canada/canada_visas_immigration_guide.md`. A study permit with, from 1 January 2026, no provincial attestation letter for master's and doctoral students at public institutions; 49,000 of the 408,000 study permits planned for 2026 are set aside for them (IRCC, 25 November 2025, via places/beyond-europe.md) [data]. After graduating, the post-graduation work permit (3 years for master's $\ge 8$ months); then Express Entry (CEC CRS 518–523 in Aug–Sept 2026) or a provincial route. Detail is in the official guide and the Atlas route.

## 3. Hubs

Population is the Statistics Canada estimate for the census metropolitan area on 1 July 2025; GDP the 2022 figure for the same area; pay the 2024 average employment income of full-year full-time workers ÷ 12; rent the CMHC average one-bedroom rent in purpose-built rental buildings of three units or more, 2025. All are for the metropolitan area. Standing steps are [national, North America, world].

### Toronto

**What it is.** 7,108,874 residents, GDP C$522.4 billion, pay C$8,108 a month (C$97,300 a year), rent C$1,761. Statistics Canada counts 313,203 financial-services employees (Lightcast 2024, cited by the Ontario Ministry of Finance, 11 May 2026) against 132,381 in Montréal, and Toronto is home to all five of Canada's big banks and to TMX, the largest exchange operator in Canada [data]. **Employers.** Royal Bank of Canada, Toronto-Dominion, Scotiabank and CIBC (Global 500 ranks 110, 140, 290, 357), Manulife and Sun Life (277, 379), George Weston (332) are headquartered in Toronto per Fortune [data]; Fortune lists the Bank of Montreal's headquarters as Montréal. A Dallas Regional Chamber table (2025) counts eight Global 500 headquarters in Toronto, level with Chicago and Houston, behind New York (20) and London (16) [data]. **Universities.** Rotman and Schulich appear in the calculator's MBA list; Ivey (London, Ontario) sends 80% of its business analytics graduates to Toronto. **Ecosystem.** Toronto-Waterloo is 13th in Startup Genome 2026 [data].

**Standing.** Finance [5, 3, 2]: first in Canada by every count read; tenth of 14 North American centres and 41st of 117 in the GFCI (it fell from 29th), so regional 3 (secondary centre) and world 2 (present, small). Software [5, 3, 3] and IT [5, 3, 2]: the largest tech workforce in Canada; 13th in the world in Startup Genome, which supports a world 3 but not a 4. Business [5, 3, 3]: eight Global 500 headquarters, level with Chicago and Houston, behind New York, London, Paris, Tokyo and Beijing.

### Montréal

**What it is.** 4,597,837 residents, GDP C$279.5 billion, pay C$7,025 a month (C$84,300 a year, the lowest of the five), rent C$1,131 (27% below the next-lowest, Ottawa). 195,700 people in finance, insurance, real estate, rental and leasing, 73,800 in finance professions, 167,700 in natural and applied science professions, 253,900 in professional, scientific and technical services [data]. **Employers.** The Bank of Montreal (rank 273 by Fortune, headquarters listed as Montréal), Couche-Tard (185, Laval); Mila and IVADO; Google and Meta AI research centres; ServiceNow and Insilico Medicine are quoted in Montréal International's AI profile [employer-stated]. The GFCI puts Montréal 50th, second in Canada [data]. McGill Desautels is the MBA in the calculator.

**Standing.** Finance [4, 3, 2]: second in Canada on every count; 11th of 14 in North America. AI [4, 3, 2]: Mila and 48,000 experts support a national top handful, but no cross-city ranking was read. Software and IT [4, 3, 2]: 217,000 tech workers, second in Canada [practitioner consensus].

### Vancouver

**What it is.** 3,088,036 residents, GDP C$202.5 billion, pay C$7,842 a month (C$94,100 a year), rent C$1,807 (the highest of the five). 60,100 finance professionals (third in Canada), 134,000 in finance, insurance and real estate, 223,300 in professional, scientific and technical services, 82,400 in information, culture and recreation, 101,000 in transportation and warehousing [data]. **Employers.** lululemon's corporate address is in Vancouver [employer-stated]; the technology news site Techcouver reports about 150,000 tech workers, third in Canada [practitioner consensus]. GFCI: 75th, 13th of 14 in North America [data].

**Standing.** Finance [3, 2, 1]; software and IT [3, 2, 2]: third in Canada, but small beside the US West Coast, and no world ranking of the tech talent figure was read.

### Calgary

**What it is.** 1,836,012 residents, GDP C$130.0 billion, pay C$7,308 a month (C$87,700 a year), rent C$1,581. 46,300 in oil, gas, mining and forestry (4.7% of Calgary's 991,900 jobs), 68,000 in finance, insurance and real estate, 94,100 in natural and applied science professions, 142,300 in professional, scientific and technical services [data]. **Employers.** Enbridge, Cenovus, Canadian Natural Resources and Suncor [data]. Calgary Economic Development claims the highest head-office concentration per head in Canada (FP500, 2024) [employer-stated]. Startup Genome's July 2026 piece cites C$323.9 million of investment secured by Platform Calgary's members in 2025 [data]. GFCI: 77th, last of 14 in North America.

**Standing.** Business [4, 2, 1]: four Global 500 energy headquarters and the highest head-office density in Canada, but no comparison of Canadian head offices by city was read, so Calgary is not placed first nationally, and nothing sets it beside Houston, the US energy capital (27 Fortune 500 headquarters, see the United States brief).

### Ottawa

**What it is.** 1,700,014 residents (Ottawa–Gatineau), GDP C$105.8 billion, pay C$7,833 a month (C$94,000 a year), rent C$1,542. 877,300 employed, 194,000 of them in public administration, 91,200 in natural and applied science professions and 21,300 in finance professions [data]. **Employers.** Shopify (headquarters; 8,100 employees) [data]. The federal government is the largest employer by the public-administration count, but no federal graduate programme was read. Startup Genome calls Ottawa a "Bootstrap City" with direct access to federal decision-makers and procurement [data].

**Standing.** Software [3, 2, 1]: Shopify and a science-and-technology share of employment above the other four hubs, but no tech-workforce count for Ottawa was read.

## 5. Hypotheses tested

1. **"Montréal is the better value for a European graduate."** Partly supported. Rent is 36% below Toronto's and the average full-year pay is 13% below, so the monthly pay-to-rent ratio is about 6.2 against 4.6 in Toronto; but French is needed at work for most jobs and Montréal's finance sector is 42% the size of Toronto's by financial-services employees (132,381 against 313,203). Fails if the target is a bank or capital-markets job.
2. **"Calgary is Canada's energy finance centre."** Not supported. Calgary hosts four Global 500 energy headquarters but ranks 77th in the GFCI and has 30,800 finance professionals, less than a fifth of Toronto's 175,200 and 3.1% of local jobs against 4.7% in Toronto.
3. **"A Canadian master's guarantees a Canadian career."** Partly supported. The three-year permit is guaranteed by the master's (eight months or more, public university), but permanent residence is not: Ontario closed its no-job-offer master's stream on 30 May 2026, and Express Entry cut-offs were read only in secondary reports.
4. **"Toronto is a US-scale tech market."** Not supported. 414,000 tech jobs and 13th in Startup Genome are large for Canada, but GFCI and Startup Genome both put Toronto below the top ten in the world.
5. **"Ottawa is a government town with no private jobs."** Not supported: Shopify has 8,100 employees and the science-and-technology occupations are 10.4% of employment, the highest of the five. But the public-administration count (194,000) is the largest block.

## 6. Common mistakes and myths

- **Treating the five big banks as only a Toronto story.** Fortune lists the Bank of Montreal's headquarters as Montréal; its executive office is in Toronto, which no source read states.
- **Reading the 2022 GDP as current.** GDP by metropolitan area is published only to 2022.
- **Comparing average income of full-year full-time workers with entry pay.** Ivey's MSc median base was C$75,000; the average full-year full-time income in Toronto is C$97,300.
- **Assuming Rotman's Master of Finance is a pre-experience degree.** It asks for two or more years of experience; Ivey's MSc is the pre-experience route.
- **Assuming the work permit gives residence.** It does not; see the Ontario stream closure.

## 7. Decision rules

1. If you want banking, insurance or capital markets, prefer Toronto (all five big banks, 313,203 financial-services employees).
2. If you want software, data or IT and can handle a smaller market than the US, prefer Toronto first, Montréal second (414,000 and 217,000 tech jobs).
3. If you want AI research or applied AI, compare Montréal (Mila, 48,000 AI-skills experts) with Toronto-Waterloo (13th in Startup Genome).
4. If you can work in French and rent matters, prefer Montréal (C$1,131 against C$1,761).
5. If you want an energy company's corporate or finance function, prefer Calgary, and expect an engineering-led firm.
6. If you want public policy or a government-adjacent career, prefer Ottawa; note that Shopify offers a private-sector route there.
7. If you hold a European master's only, plan for a Canadian degree: the three-year permit needs a Canadian public institution and a programme of at least eight months.
8. If you need permanent residence, do not rely on a provincial no-job-offer master's stream in Ontario (closed 30 May 2026).
9. If you apply for co-ops and internships, start with the employer pages (RBC lists co-ops and new-graduate rotational programmes) and allow for no published dates.

## 8. Gaps and claims to verify

1. Express Entry cut-off scores come from secondary reports; IRCC's rounds page was blank when read.
2. The Toronto and Montréal financial-services counts come from an Ontario government bid document citing Lightcast data.
3. GDP by metropolitan area is published only to 2022; the Ottawa figure adds the Ontario and Quebec parts.
4. Pay is average employment income of full-year full-time workers (2024), not entry pay; rent is CMHC's average for purpose-built rental flats, not asking rents.
5. Montréal, Vancouver and Calgary tech employment comes from a news site reporting CompTIA; no Ottawa tech-workforce figure was read.
6. Fortune lists the Bank of Montreal's headquarters as Montréal, while the Ontario document counts all five big banks as Toronto-based.
7. Named employers for Vancouver, Montréal's finance sector and Ottawa are thin: bank, telecom and mining career pages loaded no content or refused automated reads, and no graduate programme dates were found.
8. Economics, accounting, management, marketing, analytics, data science and big data are not rated in any city.
9. French requirements for Montréal jobs rest on a law firm's summary; the Québec government pages could not be read.
10. The earlier Ottawa figures (688,200 employed, 82,000 in science professions, 16,800 in finance) were for the Ontario side only and were corrected to the whole metropolitan area.
