# Germany: country brief

Today is 3 October 2026. Tags: `[data]` official statistics or government pages, `[employer-stated]`, `[practitioner consensus]` (published indices and specialist synthesis), `[anecdotal]`. Pay figures are for **all** full-time employees of a state, not graduate starting salaries.

## 1. Bottom line

- Germany is not one graduate market but about six, and they do not overlap. Frankfurt is banking and supervision (73,900 bank employees in Q1 2025, almost 12% of Germany's 632,200; Helaba from Federal Employment Agency data) [data]. Munich is corporate headquarters (eight DAX companies in the city and region, more than any other city) [data]. Berlin is start-ups and AI. Stuttgart, Wolfsburg and Leipzig are cars; Hamburg is the port and aviation; Dresden is chips.
- Standing (national / Europe / world): Frankfurt finance 5/3/2; Munich business and management 5/3/2, software and IT 4/3/2; Berlin software and AI 5/4/2; Hamburg logistics 5/4/2; Dresden IT 4/4/2. No German city is above 2 of 5 at world scale in any family, and only Berlin (software, AI) and Dresden (IT) have a 4 in Europe. The reason is in the rankings: the Global Financial Centres Index 40 (September 2026) puts Frankfurt 29th in the world and ninth in Western Europe [practitioner consensus], and Startup Genome ranks Berlin 22nd in the world and third in Europe [practitioner consensus].
- Finance is smaller than the country's size suggests, and shrinking in rankings: Frankfurt fell 14 places in GFCI 40. Its strength is institutional (ECB, Bundesbank, BaFin, EIOPA, KfW and the new anti-money-laundering authority AMLA) rather than investment banking, which is rated only "present" there [data, employer-stated].
- Tech pays most and hires in English more often than other fields, but it is not the easy route it was: software development is no longer on the Federal Employment Agency's shortage list (2025 analysis), while the median full-time pay of IT occupations, €5,907 a month in 2024, is the highest of any occupational group [data].
- German is the real filter. Only 2.4-2.8% of German job postings say German is not required, among the lowest shares in Europe (Indeed Hiring Lab, October 2024) [data]. The exceptions are in tech, international finance and the Berlin start-up scene.
- Hiring runs through internships taken while you are still enrolled; a voluntary internship of more than three months after graduation must pay the minimum wage, so employers prefer the student's compulsory one. Strategy consulting uses the internship as the interview (BCG offers binding full-time offers to top interns) [data, employer-stated].
- Pay is high but the tax wedge is large: a single employee in Munich keeps about 62% of €60,000 gross, the lowest share of the library's compared cities except Milan [practitioner consensus]. Rent is the real difference between hubs: about €1,500 a month for a one-bedroom flat in central Munich against about €570 in Essen and €667 in Dresden (Numbeo, crowd-sourced) [anecdotal].
- Practical rule: choose the hub by the family, not by the country. A finance graduate with German goes to Frankfurt (or Munich for insurers and corporate finance); a computing graduate without German goes to Berlin or Munich; an engineering-minded business graduate goes to Stuttgart, Munich or Wolfsburg; a logistics graduate to Hamburg.

## 2. The economy and the graduate labour market

**Size and structure.** Germany's GDP was €4,530 billion in 2025 (€4,387 billion in 2024), the largest in the EU (Eurostat, nama_10_gdp, updated 2 October 2026) [data]. Its industry is spread across many mid-sized cities rather than concentrated in a capital: Bonn, Wolfsburg and Stuttgart each host a headquarters city's worth of employers for a population of a few hundred thousand. The record's sectors are automotive and suppliers, banking and insurance, software and ICT, semiconductors, logistics and ports, aerospace, chemicals and consumer goods.

**Graduates and youth.** Unemployment among 15-to-24-year-olds was 5.9% in 2023, 6.6% in 2024 and 7.1% in 2025 (Eurostat, une_rt_a), rising but low by European standards; overall unemployment was 3.1%, 3.5% and 3.8%, and for tertiary-educated 25-to-29-year-olds 3.1%, 3.9% and 4.5% (Eurostat, une_rt_a and lfsa_urgaed) [data]. A measure for recent graduates alone was not read (gap).

**Pay.** The Federal Employment Agency's pay statistics for 31 December 2024 give a median monthly gross pay of €4,013 for full-time employees subject to social insurance (€3,796 the year before). By state the range runs from €3,294 in Mecklenburg-Vorpommern to €4,527 in Hamburg; by district from €2,965 in the Erzgebirgskreis to €5,855 in Ingolstadt. By occupational group, IT and other ICT occupations have the highest median (€5,907), software development at expert level €6,097; by industry, finance and insurance pays €5,860 (Federal Employment Agency, Analyse zur Entgeltstatistik 2024, October 2025) [data]. These are pay levels for everyone in full-time work, much of it experienced; a graduate's starting salary is lower. No source read gives starting pay by field and city (gap).

**How hiring works.** Large employers recruit graduates through a mandatory internship (Pflichtpraktikum) or working-student job (Werkstudent) during the master's, a thesis written with a company, and then a direct entry offer. A voluntary internship longer than three months after graduation must pay at least the minimum wage (Mindestlohngesetz §22) [data]. Consulting is the clearest case: BCG Germany/Austria runs binding-offer programmes in which top interns receive a full-time offer without further interviews [employer-stated]. The library's recruiting-calendar file covers the dates.

**Language.** German is expected. In Indeed Hiring Lab's analysis only 2.4-2.8% of German job postings say German is not required, among the lowest shares in Europe, and the exceptions cluster in tech and international finance [data].

**Permits.** EU, EEA and Swiss citizens need no visa, residence permit or work permit; they register their address within two weeks (Bundesmeldegesetz §17). Non-EU students may work 140 days a year, get up to 18 months after graduating to look for a job, and may apply for the Blue Card at €50,700 gross a year in 2026 (€45,934.20 in shortage occupations or for degrees under three years old) [data]. All immigration, visa and registration rules are consolidated in `visas_immigration/germany/germany_visas_immigration_guide.md`. See the record's route for the full sequence.

## 3. Hubs

Method for every hub. **Population and GDP** are Eurostat metropolitan regions (met_pjanaggr3, population on 1 January 2023; met_10r_3gdp, GDP at current prices, 2021, the latest year published for these regions). Metropolitan regions are larger than the cities: Frankfurt's is 2.78 million people, the city itself about 760,000. **Pay** is the state median (see above), the same source for every hub, so a city's own pay differs from its state's. **Rent** is Numbeo's crowd-sourced average for a one-bedroom flat in the city centre, 2026 [anecdotal]. **Jobs by sector** are Eurostat employment by NUTS 3 region (nama_10r_3empers, 2023). Employer statements are from the employers' own pages.

### Frankfurt am Main

**What it is.** Germany's financial centre. Metropolitan population 2.78 million; GDP €160.8 billion (2021); Hessen median pay €4,325 (2024); one-bedroom flat in the centre about €1,178. The city has 761,090 jobs, of which 259,390 (34%) are in finance and insurance, real estate and professional and business services and only 39,890 in manufacturing (2023) [data].

**Sectors and named employers.** The ECB, the Bundesbank, BaFin (supervising banks, insurers and securities trading), EIOPA (the EU insurance supervisor) and KfW sit in the city (IHK Frankfurt) [data]. The EU Anti-Money Laundering Authority AMLA has its seat there, started work in summer 2025 and plans about 430 staff by the end of 2027 [data]. Deutsche Bank is headquartered in Frankfurt with 89,879 employees at the end of 2025 [employer-stated]. Deutsche Börse's headquarters is in Eschborn, just outside the city limits, with more than 3,100 employees [employer-stated]. Frankfurt Airport (Fraport) hosts about 80,000 jobs at some 500 organisations [employer-stated]. About 73,900 people worked in Frankfurt's banks in Q1 2025, with about 75,500 expected by the end of 2026 [data].

**Universities.** Goethe University and the Frankfurt School of Finance and Management are the obvious draws; this brief did not source their rankings (gap).

### Munich

**What it is.** Corporate Germany's most concentrated city. Metropolitan population 2.98 million; GDP €213.4 billion (2021); Bayern median pay €4,166; centre flat about €1,500, the highest rent among the hubs. The city has 1,203,650 jobs: 340,320 in finance and insurance, real estate and professional and business services, 327,110 in trade, transport, hospitality and information and communication, 113,320 in manufacturing (2023) [data].

**Sectors and named employers.** Eight DAX companies have their headquarters in Munich and its region (Allianz, BMW, Infineon, MTU Aero Engines, Munich Re, Scout24, Siemens, Siemens Energy), more than in any other city; Düsseldorf follows with four (City of Munich, April 2026) [data]. Google is renovating the Arnulfpost building for up to 2,000 staff and calls Munich a key hub for development, research and product innovation [employer-stated]. Employment in the city's ICT sector has nearly doubled since 2014 (City of Munich, 2025) [data].

**Start-ups.** Startup Genome's 2026 report puts Munich's ecosystem value at $71 billion (Europe's average $14.3 billion, the world's $25 billion), with $2.3 billion of seed and Series A funding and $10 billion of exits in 2021-2025 [practitioner consensus].

### Berlin

**What it is.** The capital, Germany's start-up and AI centre rather than its corporate or banking centre. Metropolitan population 5.48 million (the metropolitan region is larger than the city); GDP €217.9 billion (2021); Berlin state median pay €4,198; centre flat about €1,321. Berlin has 2,190,900 jobs, of which 170,560 are in information and communication, 43,450 in finance and insurance and 432,460 in professional, scientific, technical and administrative services (2023) [data].

**Start-ups and AI.** Berlin holds 43% of the value of Germany's start-up ecosystem, with more than 1,600 venture-funded companies, and counts more than 9,000 AI experts, placing itself among Europe's top three locations for frontier AI research (Berlin Senate for Economics, 19 February 2026) [data, a self-assessment]. Startup Genome's 2026 report ranks Berlin 22nd among the world's start-up ecosystems and third in Europe, also third in Europe for talent and for its AI-native cluster, with an ecosystem value of $89.5 billion [practitioner consensus]. Zalando SE is registered in Berlin [employer-stated]. GFCI 40 puts Berlin 45th for finance.

### Hamburg

**What it is.** The port. Metropolitan population 3.42 million; GDP €179.5 billion (2021); Hamburg state median pay €4,527, the highest state; centre flat about €1,146. The city has 1,348,210 jobs, 109,100 in manufacturing, 87,670 in information and communication, 45,240 in finance and insurance (2023) [data].

**Logistics.** The Port of Hamburg handled 8.3 million TEU and 114.6 million tonnes in 2025, Germany's largest seaport (Port of Hamburg Marketing, 19 February 2026) [data]. Eurostat's 2024 tonnage (97.0 million) makes it the third-busiest port in the EU after Rotterdam (397.3 million) and Antwerp-Bruges (244.2 million) [data]. About 48,700 people work in Hamburg's aviation industry; Airbus has its largest German site there with about 18,000 staff and Lufthansa Technik nearly 10,000 (Hamburg Ministry for Economic Affairs, 10 June 2025) [data]. The Otto Group is registered in Hamburg [employer-stated]. Startup Genome values the Hamburg ecosystem at $6 billion [practitioner consensus].

**Standing.** Logistics 5 / 4 / 2: national 5 (the largest German seaport), Europe 4 (third in the EU by tonnage, far behind Rotterdam, which is why not 5), world 2. IT 3 / 2 / 1 on 87,670 ICT jobs and a small ecosystem. Ratings: logistics dominant, IT strong, business present.

### Stuttgart

**What it is.** The car makers' region. Metropolitan population 2.82 million; GDP €155.7 billion (2021); Baden-Württemberg median pay €4,356; centre flat about €1,118. The city has 548,450 jobs, 77,320 (14%) in manufacturing and 140,560 in finance and insurance, real estate and professional and business services (2023) [data].

**Employers.** Mercedes-Benz, Porsche, Bosch, Mahle, Eberspächer, Mann+Hummel and Vector Informatik have their headquarters in the Stuttgart Region, which the regional development agency calls the most important focus of Germany's car industry; 118,000 people work directly in its automotive cluster [data]. Bosch is registered in Gerlingen-Schillerhöhe, outside the city. Startup Genome values the ecosystem at $6 billion [practitioner consensus]. GFCI 40 lists Stuttgart as an associate financial centre only.

**Standing.** Management 4 / 3 / 2 and business 4 / 2 / 1: a national 4 on the cluster of seven named headquarters (Munich has more DAX headquarters); Europe 3 for management on the cluster's size (118,000 direct automotive jobs); no ranking supports more. These steps are judgements from the claims. Ratings: business and management strong, software present.

### Rhine-Neckar (Mannheim, Walldorf, Heidelberg, Ludwigshafen)

**What it is.** Enterprise software and chemicals. Metropolitan population 1.21 million (Mannheim-Ludwigshafen region); GDP €56.1 billion (2021); centre flat in Mannheim about €843. Mannheim city has 249,430 jobs, 41,640 in manufacturing; Heidelberg 129,740; the Rhein-Neckar district 247,100 (2023) [data].

**Employers.** SAP SE is registered in Walldorf and reports 110,000 employees from 157-plus countries, total revenue of €36.8 billion (non-IFRS) in 2025 and 100-plus development locations [employer-stated]. BASF's Ludwigshafen site has around 33,000 employees, 125 production plants and calls itself the largest integrated chemical complex in the world [employer-stated]. The University of Mannheim business school is the draw for business students (rankings not sourced here).

**Standing.** Software 4 / 3 / 2 on SAP (national top four behind Berlin and Munich; a recognised European centre because Europe's largest software company is here, but no city ranking supports a 4); IT 3 / 2 / 1. Ratings: business, IT and software present.

### Düsseldorf

**What it is.** Corporate headquarters of the Rhineland and consumer goods. Metropolitan population 1.58 million; GDP €95.6 billion (2021); Nordrhein-Westfalen median pay €4,038; centre flat about €960. The city has 576,590 jobs, 175,900 (31%) in finance and insurance, real estate and professional and business services (2023) [data].

**Employers.** Four DAX companies (City of Munich count) include Henkel, registered in Düsseldorf, with more than 5,300 staff at its headquarters site and about 50,000 worldwide [employer-stated]; Rheinmetall has its head office there and says it employs around 34,000 people at 161 sites in more than 30 countries (July 2026) [employer-stated].

### Wolfsburg

**What it is.** A company town. Wolfsburg has 131,830 jobs, 71,080 (54%) in manufacturing (2023) [data]. The Braunschweig-Salzgitter-Wolfsburg metropolitan region has 1.01 million people and GDP €57.5 billion (2021); Niedersachsen's median pay is €3,832. No reliable rent was read.

**Employers.** Volkswagen AG is registered in Wolfsburg [employer-stated]; the Wolfsburg plant has about 70,000 employees, built 490,000 vehicles in 2023 and is the headquarters of Volkswagen Passenger Cars (Volkswagen newsroom, March 2024) [employer-stated].

**Standing.** Management 3 / 2 / 1: one dominant employer, not a diversified graduate market. Rating: management present.

### Dresden

**What it is.** Europe's largest microelectronics location. Metropolitan population 1.35 million; GDP €46.4 billion (2021); Sachsen median pay €3,388 (the lowest of the states compared); centre flat about €667. The city has 350,030 jobs, 36,920 in manufacturing [data].

**Employers.** Saxony's ICT and microelectronics sector has about 3,650 companies and 82,500 employees, 49.5% of them in software; one in three chips made in Europe comes from there; Bosch, Infineon, GlobalFoundries and, from 2027, TSMC (ESMC) run fabs in Dresden (Wirtschaftsförderung Sachsen) [data]. Trade press reports that Infineon opened its €5 billion Smart Power Fab in Dresden in July 2026, creating 1,000 new direct jobs [practitioner consensus].

**Standing.** IT 4 / 4 / 2 (Europe 4: the official source calls it Europe's largest microelectronics location, which is a cross-country statement); computer science 4 / 3 / 2; software 3 / 2 / 1. Ratings: IT, software and computer science strong.

## 5. Hypotheses tested

1. **"Choose the city by the family, not by 'Germany'."** Supported. Each family has one named national centre in the record (finance Frankfurt, business Munich, software and AI Berlin, logistics Hamburg, chips Dresden), and the shares are large: Frankfurt has 12% of national bank employment. It fails for graduates who accept a smaller employer in a second-tier city.
2. **"Frankfurt is Europe's second banking centre after London."** Not supported. GFCI 40 (September 2026) ranks it 29th globally and ninth in Western Europe, behind Zurich, Geneva, Luxembourg, Lugano, Paris, Copenhagen and Amsterdam. It holds institutions, not the largest markets business.
3. **"Berlin is the place for English-speaking graduates."** Partly supported. Berlin is third in Europe for talent and AI in Startup Genome, and the Indeed analysis puts tech and international finance among the few areas with postings without German. It fails for non-tech business roles, where German is expected, and for pay (€4,198 state median, below Hamburg and Baden-Württemberg).
4. **"High German pay translates into high savings."** Partly supported only. Pay in finance and IT is the highest by industry and occupation, but a single employee keeps about 62% of €60,000 gross in Munich and Munich rent is more than double Essen's. The advantage over cities like Dresden is large in gross pay (€3,388 against €4,325) but partly offset by rent.
5. **"Cars are shrinking, so avoid Stuttgart and Wolfsburg."** Insufficient evidence in this record. The library's industrial file tracks automotive job cuts; this brief did not measure graduate hiring there.

## 6. Common mistakes and myths

- Treating metropolitan figures as city figures: Berlin's 5.5 million is a region; the Ruhr's 5.1 million is spread over a dozen cities. [data]
- Assuming a state's median pay is a city's: Frankfurt, Stuttgart and Munich are among the top-ten districts for pay, so their city medians are above their states'; the Federal Employment Agency lists only ten districts [data].
- Assuming English is enough: 2.4-2.8% of postings say German is not required [data].
- Waiting until after graduation for an internship: a voluntary internship longer than three months must then pay the minimum wage, which pushes employers towards students still enrolled [data].
- Reading "headquarters" as "jobs": Deutsche Börse's headquarters is in Eschborn, Bosch's registration is in Gerlingen; the employers sit in the region, not the city [employer-stated].
- Treating software as a shortage occupation: it is not on the 2025 list [data].

## 7. Decision rules

1. If you want banking, regulation or central banking and speak German, prefer Frankfurt (national 5 in finance; the ECB, BaFin and AMLA hire here).
2. If you want corporate strategy, insurance or a DAX headquarters job, prefer Munich first, Düsseldorf and Stuttgart second (eight DAX headquarters in Munich, four in Düsseldorf).
3. If you work in software or AI and do not speak German, prefer Berlin (Startup Genome: third in Europe), then Munich (Google's 2,000-seat expansion).
4. If you want logistics or aviation, prefer Hamburg (the largest German port; 48,700 aviation jobs).
5. If you want car-industry management, prefer Stuttgart, Wolfsburg or Leipzig (see the cluster sizes above); accept a smaller peer group and one dominant employer.
6. If chips or embedded software, prefer Dresden; rent is a third of Munich's.
7. If rent matters more than status, the Ruhr (flat about €569) or Leipzig (about €687) buy the most; Munich (€1,500) the least.
8. If you are non-EU, treat the internship as the permit's runway: 140 working days a year, 18 months after graduating to search; the Blue Card salary threshold is €50,700 in 2026.
9. If you plan a consulting entry, apply for the internship; the binding offer comes from the internship, not a later round.
10. If you choose by pay, read the state median as a floor, not an offer: IT median €5,907 and finance €5,860 are for all full-time staff.

2. Several state medians (Hessen, Bayern, Baden-Württemberg, Nordrhein-Westfalen, Niedersachsen, Sachsen) were read off the Federal Employment Agency report's chart; Berlin and Hamburg and the national and extreme values are in its text.
3. Eurostat metropolitan GDP is for 2021 and population for 2023 (latest published); numbers will have moved.
4. Large employers' own sites refused automated access (Allianz, Siemens, Munich Re, Mercedes-Benz, E.ON, Rewe, Lufthansa, DHL): headcounts are missing.
5. CyberForum's figures (1,200 members, 30,000 jobs) are from a secondary report.
6. No city ranking supports European headquarters standing; the 3 for Munich business is a judgement.
7. University and business-school rankings were not sourced (Goethe, Frankfurt School, Mannheim, TUM, KIT).
8. Rent is Numbeo crowd-sourced data; the Wolfsburg figure was not available.
