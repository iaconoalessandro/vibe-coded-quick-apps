# Saudi Arabia: country brief

Today is 3 October 2026. Tags: `[data]` official statistics, government pages and published indices, `[employer-stated]`, `[practitioner consensus]`, `[anecdotal]`. Routes in the Atlas are given for EU/EEA/Swiss and UK passports only. The UK Foreign Office advises against all but essential travel near the Yemen border and reports Houthi missiles and drones aimed at Saudi cities, Riyadh among them, since 13 July 2026 (FCDO, 20 September 2026, https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/saudi-arabia) [data]. Read it before any decision.

## 1. Bottom line

- **This is a market for experienced hires, not for fresh European graduates.** The Public Investment Fund's Graduate Development Program is "aimed at" and open only to Saudi nationals (PIF, read 2 October 2026); SABIC's student and graduate programmes in the region "attract and nurture young Saudi talents"; Aramco's page for international applicants asks for "a minimum of five to 10 years of applicable experience" and names no graduate scheme for non-Saudis (Aramco, read 3 October 2026) [employer-stated].
- **Localisation quotas close the junior consulting and marketing seats.** Since 25 March 2024, 40% of roles such as business consultant, financial-advisory specialist and project manager in consulting firms must be held by Saudis; from January 2026, 60% in marketing and sales professions, 30% in 46 engineering professions and 70% in 12 procurement professions (labour ministry, via places/gulf-and-central-eastern-europe.md) [data].
- **If you go, go to Riyadh, through a multinational's regional office.** Foreign companies that want government contracts must base a regional headquarters in the capital; the investment minister reported 180 licences by 8 November 2023 against a target of 160 for the year, and the state news agency later headlined 450 foreign investors licensed (Arab News; Saudi Press Agency) [data]. A headquarters must hire at least 15 people, three of them at C-level, and is exempt from Saudisation for 10 years (DLA Piper, via the library) [practitioner consensus]. Those offices, PwC Middle East and BCG Saudi among them, are where non-Saudi juniors may appear, but PwC's Saudi postings ask for existing work rights without sponsorship (library) [employer-stated].
- **Riyadh is the only Saudi financial centre in the global index.** The Global Financial Centres Index 40 (September 2026) ranks it 46th of 117, up 15 places, and fourth in the Middle East and Africa; Jeddah is not listed (Z/Yen and CDI) [data].
- **The Eastern Province is Aramco's.** Aramco is headquartered in Dhahran; Fortune's 2026 Global 500 ranks it fifth in the world with 76,664 employees (Fortune) [data].
- **The labour market is tight for Saudis, loose for expatriates.** In the second quarter of 2025 unemployment was 2.8% overall and 6.8% for Saudis, and 52.8% of employed Saudis worked in the private sector (World Bank, from labour-ministry data) [data]: the state is steering Saudis into private jobs, which is the pressure behind the quotas.
- **Every job is employer-sponsored.** EU citizens need a sponsored work permit in every Gulf state and residence is tied to the contract (library, §1 and §3) [data].

## 2. The economy and the graduate labour market

**Size and structure.** Nominal GDP was SAR 4,703 billion in 2024; crude oil and natural gas were 19.4% of it, wholesale and retail 9.2%, construction 7.7% and public administration and defence 7.4%; real GDP grew 2.7%, with non-oil activities up 6.0% (GASTAT, Annual National Accounts 2024) [data]. The population was estimated at 35.3 million in mid-2024, of whom 44.4% were non-Saudis; non-Saudis were 89.9% of the 15-to-64 age group (GASTAT, Population Estimates 2024) [data].

**Employment by region.** In the second quarter of 2026 the Riyadh region's largest employing activities were construction (1,403,894 employees), administrative and support services (937,517) and wholesale and retail trade (678,141), with 7,672,179 registered employees and a Saudi unemployment rate of 4.2%; the Eastern Region's were construction (928,796), manufacturing (323,110) and trade (260,954), with Saudi unemployment at 5.3% (DataSaudi) [data]. These are whole-economy figures, dominated by labour-intensive work; they say nothing about graduate roles.

**Youth and graduate unemployment.** No graduate series was read. The World Bank notes that 35.8% of Saudis in the working-age population had tertiary education in 2025 against 24.4% of expatriates [data].

**Entry pay by field.** No employer-stated figure for expatriate graduates. The library records post-MBA consulting packages in Riyadh (BCG about $304,000 total) from a practitioner guide, not graduate pay (careers/consulting.md) [practitioner consensus].

**How hiring works.** Government-linked employers run national graduate schemes. Multinationals and consultancies post graduate and student roles in Riyadh, but for Saudi positions PwC states that the candidate must be eligible to work without PwC sponsorship (library) [employer-stated]. Expect to enter as an experienced hire or an intra-group transfer.

**Language of work.** English in multinationals and consultancies; Arabic matters for public-sector and government-contract work. No source read measured it.

**Visa headline.** All visas, Iqama procedures, Qiwa contracts, Premium Residency and Study in Saudi options are verified in the single source of truth: [`visas_immigration/saudi_arabia/saudi_arabia_visas_immigration_guide.md`](../../visas_immigration/saudi_arabia/saudi_arabia_visas_immigration_guide.md).

## 3. Hubs

Populations are the 2022 census as given by Saudipedia, a government encyclopedia, for the city (area: city), rounded. GASTAT publishes no regional GDP, wage or rent series, so no other metric is shown. Standing steps are [national, Middle East, world].

### Riyadh

The capital: about 6.9 million people in 2022, and 8,591,748 in the Riyadh region (DataSaudi) [data]. It hosts the Public Investment Fund, government entities and, by rule, the regional headquarters of foreign firms that want public contracts (sa-rhq, sa-rhq-count). PIF's graduate programme has enrolled 1,033 people over nine cohorts, for Saudis only [employer-stated].

**Standing.** Finance [5, 4, 2]: the only Saudi centre in the GFCI (national 5); third in the Middle East after Dubai (9th) and Abu Dhabi (13th) (regional 4); 46th of 117 is a mid-table centre, not a top-15 one (world 2) [data]. Business [5, 3, 2]: the headquarters rule makes it the national centre (5); no claim compares it with Dubai's regional offices, so the regional step stays at 3. Software [5, 4, 2]: Startup Genome puts Riyadh third in MENA behind Tel Aviv and Dubai (regional 4) [data]; it is outside the global top 40.

### Jeddah

The Red Sea port and commercial centre, about 3.7 million people. The Saudi Ports Authority and DP World are expanding the container terminal at Jeddah Islamic Port, a SAR 3 billion investment raising capacity from 1.8 million to four million TEU (Argaam, 7 March 2025) [data]. Jeddah is not in the GFCI. Logistics is rated present on that one named project, standing [3, 2, 1].

### Dammam and Dhahran

The Eastern Province: about 1.4 million people in Dammam; 5,125,254 in the region. Aramco is headquartered in Dhahran (Fortune Global 500 2026 rank 5; 76,664 employees). Business is present [4, 3, 2]. Jubail's petrochemical complexes are in the same province but no source on their staffing was read.

## 5. Hypotheses tested

1. **"Riyadh's headquarters rule creates entry jobs for Europeans."** Partly supported: licences are numerous, but an office must hire only 15 people, and PwC's own Saudi postings ask for existing work rights. Holds for experienced hires and transfers; fails for fresh graduates.
2. **"Saudi consulting is the Gulf's best-paid door for juniors."** Insufficient evidence: the pay figures read are post-MBA; the 40% quota reserves the main junior titles for Saudis.
3. **"The giga-project boom means graduate demand."** Not supported by anything read: the largest activities by employees are construction and support services, and the employers' graduate pages are for nationals.

## 6. Common mistakes and myths

- Reading the number of regional-headquarters licences as a number of jobs: the minimum is 15 staff each and the counts are licences, not operating offices [practitioner consensus].
- Treating Aramco, SABIC and PIF as European-graduate employers: their own pages say otherwise [employer-stated].
- Assuming the UAE playbook carries over: Saudi positions at PwC require existing work rights, UAE ones may be sponsored [employer-stated].

## 7. Decision rules

1. If you are a graduate without five years' experience, do not plan a Saudi first job around Aramco, PIF or SABIC (their international hiring is for experienced people or their schemes are for Saudis).
2. If you want the Gulf as a first job, compare Dubai first: its free zones list non-national employers, and Saudi consulting quotas reserve junior seats.
3. If a Riyadh offer comes from a multinational's regional office, ask whether the role counts under the Saudisation quota and who sponsors the permit before accepting.
4. If a posting says "must be eligible to work without sponsorship", it is not for you unless you already hold Saudi work rights.
5. If you accept, read the FCDO and Farnesina pages dated within the last month, because the advice changed on 20 September 2026.
6. If you are choosing between Riyadh and Dhahran, Riyadh has the headquarters offices and the finance firms; Dhahran is Aramco-centred and Aramco's international hiring is for experienced engineers and professionals.
