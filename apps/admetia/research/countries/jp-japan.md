# Japan: country brief

Today is 3 October 2026. Tags: `[data]` official statistics, `[employer-stated]`, `[practitioner consensus]` (published indices), `[anecdotal]` (crowd-sourced). Yen (¥) unless stated.

## 1. Bottom line

- Tokyo is the hub for a business or finance graduate: it holds 43.5% of the loans of domestically licensed banks (March 2022) and 2,391 of the 3,174 foreign-affiliated companies in Japan, with 15.8% of the country's workers (Tokyo Metropolitan Government) [data], and had 26 Fortune Global 500 headquarters in 2025, second only to Beijing's 47 [data].
- Tokyo is sixth in the world in the Global Financial Centres Index 40 (September 2026) and fourth in Asia/Pacific; Osaka is 20th; Fukuoka is an unranked associate centre; no other Japanese city is listed (Z/Yen, Long Finance) [practitioner consensus].
- For start-ups and AI, Startup Genome 2026 ranks Tokyo 12th in the world and fifth in Asia, with a top-ten global rank for talent strength; the other five hubs have no readable ranking [practitioner consensus].
- Nagoya is the logistics and manufacturing hub: its port is the largest in Japan by total cargo (156.71 million tons in 2024), and Toyota's head office is at nearby Toyota City, Aichi, with 73,133 employees [data, employer-stated].
- Ordinary graduate hiring is regulated by a national calendar: for the class of 2027 the government again asks employers to start publicity from 1 March, selection from 1 June and formal offers from 1 October (Cabinet Office, 24 Mar 2026) [data]. Of the class of March 2026, 98.0% of university students had a job on 1 April [data].
- Entry pay is modest and uniform: new university graduates earned ¥262,300 a month on average in 2025 (graduate degree ¥299,000), and Tokyo's general-worker average of ¥418,300 is 23% above the national ¥340,600 [data].
- For a European the headline routes are in the record: J-Find (up to two years to look for work, for graduates of top-100 universities in two of three rankings), and the post-study work statuses (JASSO, Immigration Services Agency) [data]. How much Japanese ordinary hiring requires was not researched.

## 2. The economy and the graduate labour market

**Size.** Prefectural GDP (nominal, fiscal 2023, Cabinet Office): Tokyo Metropolis ¥125,018.5 billion, Aichi 46,091.1, Osaka 44,992.4, Kanagawa 37,331.3, Fukuoka 21,238.7, Kyoto 11,510.4; the cities of Yokohama (¥15,213.0 billion) and Nagoya (¥15,021.7) are in the same table [data].

**Pay.** Scheduled monthly cash earnings of general workers (both sexes, 2025, MHLW wage survey): national ¥340,600, Tokyo 418,300, Kanagawa 368,600, Osaka 348,900, Aichi 341,600, Kyoto 337,400, Fukuoka 314,300; only Tokyo, Kanagawa, Aichi and Osaka were above the national figure [data]. This excludes bonuses and overtime and covers all ages. New graduates in 2025: university ¥262,300 (men 264,900, women 259,700, up 5.6%), graduate school ¥299,000, junior college and technical college ¥235,500, vocational school ¥230,700, high school ¥207,300 [data].

**Graduate labour market.** 98.0% of university students due to graduate in March 2026 had a job on 1 April 2026, unchanged on a year earlier; junior colleges 97.4% (MHLW and the Ministry of Education) [data]. This counts students who sought work and is not a measure of how hard it is for a foreigner.

**How hiring works.** Large employers recruit a cohort a year ahead through the shūkatsu calendar: publicity from 1 March before the graduating year, selection from 1 June, formal offers from 1 October, with an exception for students assessed through qualifying specialist internships (Cabinet Office, 24 Mar 2026) [data]. No employer's own graduate page was read.

**Language.** Graduates of Japanese universities need the top Japanese-language test level (N1) or a Business Japanese score of 480 for a broader work status (JASSO) [data]. Whether employers require Japanese in ordinary hiring was not read.

**Visa headline.** Citizens of Italy, the EU and the UK may enter Japan visa-free for up to 90 days for tourism or business with no work permitted; British, German, Austrian and Irish citizens can apply to extend this by 90 days, while Italian citizens cannot (MOFA) [data]. The Italy-Japan Working Holiday Programme entered into force on 1 April 2026 (18–30 years, 1 year, 500 quota, free of consular fees) (MOFA) [data]. Students may work 28 hours a week with permission; graduates can change to job hunting for six months (extendable once to 12 months) and then to the Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services status (JASSO, ISA) [data]. J-Find lets graduates of top-100 universities (in two of QS, THE, ShanghaiRanking within five years) stay up to two years with ¥200,000 in funds (ISA) [data]. The Highly Skilled Professional (HSP) route offers permanent residence in 1 to 3 years. Full verified immigration rules are consolidated in `visas_immigration/japan/japan_visas_immigration_guide.md`.

## 3. Hubs

Metrics in the Atlas: population is the city (resident register, 1 January 2026, MIC; Tokyo is the 23 special wards summed); GDP and wage are the prefecture (area "region"), so Nagoya, Yokohama and Kyoto sit in larger areas than their cities.

### Tokyo
- **What it is.** 9.80 million residents in the 23 wards (the Tokyo Metropolis has 14.08 million) [data]; prefecture GDP ¥125,018.5 billion, wage ¥418,300 a month.
- **Employers.** 2,391 foreign-affiliated companies; the banks with 43.5% of national lending; 26 Fortune Global 500 headquarters; Toyota Motor (a Tokyo head office in Bunkyo, 1-4-18 Koraku) and Sumitomo Corporation (dual head offices in Tokyo and Osaka since 1970) [data, employer-stated].
- **Standing.** Finance 5-4-4: first in Japan by the loan share; GFCI 4th in Asia/Pacific (top handful) and 6th in the world (New York and London are the benchmark 5s). Business 5-4-4: Fortune count second to Beijing.
- **Start-ups.** Startup Genome 12th in the world, 5th in Asia, 3rd in Asia for funding momentum, 5th in Asia for AI-native clusters [practitioner consensus]. Software and AI are not rated because no source gives Tokyo's share of national software jobs.
- **Rent.** ¥202,375 a month (Numbeo, crowd-sourced, Oct 2026) [anecdotal].

### Osaka
- **What it is.** 2.80 million in the city; prefecture GDP ¥44,992.4 billion; wage ¥348,900; labour force of 1.13 million (2020) [data].
- **Employers.** Sumitomo Corporation (second head office in Osaka); the pharmaceutical and manufacturing base noted by JETRO.
- **Standing.** Finance 4-3-2 (GFCI 20th, 8th in Asia/Pacific); business 4-3-2 (prefecture GDP third after Tokyo and Aichi).
- **Rent.** ¥110,667 (Numbeo) [anecdotal].

### Nagoya
- **What it is.** 2.31 million in the city; Aichi GDP ¥46,091.1 billion, the second largest prefecture; wage ¥341,600, above the national average [data].
- **Employers.** Port of Nagoya (largest in Japan by total cargo; 156.71 million tons in 2024); Toyota Motor (head office in Toyota City; 73,133 employees, 390,927 consolidated, at 31 March 2026) [data, employer-stated].
- **Rating and standing.** Logistics rated strong (one official statistic); standing 5-3-2: first in Japan by cargo, but no cross-country port comparison was read, so the regional step is held at notable.
- **Rent.** ¥83,650 (Numbeo) [anecdotal].

### Yokohama
- **What it is.** 3.75 million in the city, Japan's second largest; Kanagawa GDP ¥37,331.3 billion; wage ¥368,600, second highest of the six [data].
- **Employers.** Nissan Motor (global headquarters at 1-1 Takashima, Nishi-ku) [employer-stated].
- **Standing.** Business 3-2-1.
- **Rent.** ¥185,000 (Numbeo; few reports behind it) [anecdotal].

### Fukuoka
- **What it is.** 1.62 million; Fukuoka GDP ¥21,238.7 billion; wage ¥314,300, the lowest of the six [data].
- **Standing.** Finance 3-2-1 (GFCI associate centre, 111 assessments, mean 690); business 3-2-1.
- **Rent.** ¥75,627 (Numbeo) [anecdotal].

### Kyoto
- **What it is.** 1.37 million in the city; Kyoto Prefecture GDP ¥11,510.4 billion; wage ¥337,400 [data].
- **Employers.** Nintendo (head office in Minami-ku; 8,666 consolidated employees at March 2026) [employer-stated].
- **Standing.** Business 2-1-1.
- **Rent.** ¥89,153 (Numbeo) [anecdotal].

## 5. Hypotheses tested

1. **"Tokyo is where the money and the foreign firms are."** Supported: 43.5% of bank loans, 75.3% of foreign-affiliated companies, 26 Fortune HQs, GFCI 6th [data, practitioner consensus]. Fails for pay only if you compare with cost: rent in Tokyo is about 1.8 times Osaka's on Numbeo [anecdotal].
2. **"A Japanese master's is the way in for a European."** Partly supported: the graduate statuses and the 28-hour work rule exist, but J-Find needs no Japanese degree, only a top-100 university, so a European from a ranked university may not need to study in Japan [data].
3. **"Regional cities pay much less."** Partly supported: Fukuoka's ¥314,300 is 75% of Tokyo's, but Aichi (¥341,600) and Osaka (¥348,900) are within about 2.5% of the national average [data].
4. **"Japan's strong graduate employment rate means foreigners find work easily."** Not supported: the 98.0% rate counts students seeking jobs in a domestic calendar and says nothing on foreigners or language [data].

## 6. Common mistakes and myths

- Reading Tokyo's prefecture wage (¥418,300) as entry pay; the new-graduate average is ¥262,300.
- Comparing Tokyo-Metropolis and city populations: the 23 wards are 9.80 million, the Metropolis 14.08 million.
- Assuming the 90 days on arrival allow any work (FCDO).
- Missing the shūkatsu calendar: formal offers start 1 October of the graduating year, with publicity from 1 March the year before.

## 7. Decision rules

1. If you want finance or a corporate role and can use Japanese, prefer Tokyo, because loans, foreign firms and headquarters are concentrated there.
2. If you want logistics, trade or automotive supply chains, look at Nagoya (port, Toyota) before Tokyo.
3. If you hold a degree from a top-100 university in two of the three rankings, use J-Find rather than a Japanese master's unless the programme itself is the goal.
4. If you plan to join a large Japanese firm's graduate intake, work to the calendar: publicity from 1 March, selection from 1 June.
5. If pay matters more than city, compare the ¥262,300 national starting average with rent: Tokyo's one-bedroom rent (¥202,375, crowd-sourced) is about 77% of that.
6. If you are British, do not work on the 90-day entry permission.
