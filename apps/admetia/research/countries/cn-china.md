# China: country brief

Today is 3 October 2026. Tags: `[data]` official statistics, `[employer-stated]`, `[practitioner consensus]` (published indices), `[anecdotal]` (crowd-sourced). Figures are in yuan (CNY) unless stated.

## 1. Bottom line

- China is a market for people who already read Chinese or are ready to learn it; the Atlas has no source on language requirements, so treat the language as the first filter and verify it with each employer (not researched).
- Beijing, Shanghai and Shenzhen are the three hubs where a graduate's field and the city line up. Beijing leads Asia on Startup Genome 2026 (6th in the world, first in Asia, first for AI-native clusters) and had 47 Fortune Global 500 headquarters in 2025, more than Tokyo (26) and New York (14) combined [practitioner consensus, data].
- Shanghai is the finance city: GFCI 40 (September 2026) ranks it 5th in the world and third in Asia/Pacific after Hong Kong and Singapore; the city reports 1,813 licensed financial institutions, over 30% of registered institutions foreign, and a financial industry output of 897.97 billion yuan in 2025 [practitioner consensus, data].
- Shenzhen is the electronics, software and AI city: Tencent and Huawei are both headquartered there, software and information services passed 800 billion yuan in scale, and the city counts more than 2,600 AI enterprises above designated size; it is also 8th in the GFCI and 19th in Startup Genome 2026 [data, employer-stated, practitioner consensus].
- Guangzhou and Shanghai are the logistics hubs by volume: Guangzhou's port handled 28.05 million TEU in 2025 and its express deliveries exceeded 20 billion items; Shanghai's port handled 55.06 million TEU, 53.0% of it transhipment [data].
- The entry door is narrow but real: a foreigner with a master's from a Chinese university or a well-known foreign one, within a year of graduating, can be hired without work experience if the average grade is at least 80 out of 100 (or B+/B) and pay is not below the local average wage; the first work permit lasts one year (2017 ministries' notice, on the Beijing government site) [data].
- Read the UK Foreign Office warning before you go: broad national security laws, a risk of arbitrary detention including British nationals, and exit bans linked to business disputes (FCDO, 27 Aug 2026) [data].
- Pay figures are averages for all ages, not entry pay: urban non-private units averaged 129,441 yuan a year in 2025 nationally (about 10,800 a month), 248,752 in information and software and 211,164 in finance (National Bureau of Statistics) [data].

## 2. The economy and the graduate labour market

**Size and structure.** The nine hubs together are the core of the country's output: in 2025 their GDP ranged from 5,670.9 billion yuan (Shanghai) and 5,207.3 (Beijing) down to 2,214.7 (Wuhan) (city bulletins) [data]. Services dominate in Shanghai, Beijing and Hangzhou; manufacturing and trade in Suzhou, Guangzhou, Wuhan and Chongqing (see §3). The Chengdu-Chongqing economic zone was expected to reach 8.6 trillion yuan in 2024, over 6.5% of the national total, and accounts for over 10% of the country's car production and electronic-information output (GoChengdu, on an NDRC briefing, Jan 2025) [data].

**Pay.** Urban non-private units paid 129,441 yuan on average in 2025 (up 4.3%), the East region 149,902 and the West 114,989; sectors: information transmission, software and IT services 248,752, finance 211,164, scientific research and technical services 182,064, manufacturing 113,594 (NBS, 15 May 2026, tables 1 and 2) [data]. City releases read for the same statistic: Shenzhen 191,367 (15,947 a month), Guangzhou 166,790 (13,899), Suzhou 146,353 (12,196) and Chengdu 131,874 (10,990, as republished by Chengdu Bendibao from the bureau's release) [data]. No page was readable for Shanghai, Beijing, Hangzhou, Wuhan and Chongqing. These are averages for every age and level, which sit well above what a new graduate earns; no source on entry pay was read.

**Unemployment.** The surveyed urban unemployment rate averaged 5.2% in the first half of 2026 (5.0% in June), 5.1% in the 31 large cities and 4.1% for ages 30 to 59 (NBS, 16 Jul 2026) [data]. The youth (16 to 24, non-student) rate was seen only in a search summary (14.9% for June 2026) and is under Claims to verify.

**Language of work.** Not researched. Do not assume English is enough in any hub except at foreign firms; verify per employer.

**How hiring works.** Not researched in this round: no graduate-scheme page of a named employer in the nine cities was read. The route for a foreign graduate is the 2017 notice quoted above; a student on a study visa who wants an internship or part-time job needs the school's consent and a note on the residence permit before starting (Beijing government, Study in Beijing FAQ, 2021) [data].

**Visa and entry headline.** Citizens of 35 European countries (including Italy) and the UK holding ordinary passports can enter China without a visa for up to 30 days until 31 December 2026 for business, tourism, visits, exchange or transit (NIA, MFA); study, paid work or a longer stay requires an appropriate visa applied for in advance. Full verified rules in visas_immigration/china/china_visas_immigration_guide.md.

## 3. Hubs

Metrics in the Atlas are for the whole municipality (area "region"), as the bulletins give them: year-end resident population, preliminary 2025 GDP, wages of urban non-private units divided by 12 where the city's release was read, and Numbeo rent. A Chinese municipality includes large rural areas, which is why Chongqing (31.9 million) or Chengdu (21.5 million) look larger than their built-up cities.

### Shanghai
- **What it is.** 24.85 million residents; GDP 5,670.9 billion yuan in 2025 (Shanghai Municipal Bureau of Statistics, bulletin) [data].
- **Sectors and employers.** Finance: 1,813 licensed institutions; the Shanghai Futures Exchange (options on all non-ferrous metals); the IMF Shanghai Regional Center (inaugurated 2025); Guotai Haitong Securities (Guotai Junan and Haitong merged in 2025) (Shanghai government) [data]. Information, software and IT services produced 713.99 billion yuan, up 15.3%, the fastest-growing services (Shanghai government, Jan 2026) [data]. Port: 55.06 million TEU, airports 135.1 million passengers [data].
- **Standing.** Finance 5-4-4: the leading Chinese centre (GFCI 5th; Shenzhen 8th, Beijing 16th), third in Asia/Pacific so "major" regionally, 5th in the world but New York and London are the benchmark 5s. IT 4-4-3 and logistics 4-4-4 rest on the Startup Genome rank (11th in the world, 4th in Asia) and the port statistics.
- **Rent.** ¥7,047.62 a month, one-bedroom in the centre (Numbeo, crowd-sourced, Oct 2026) [anecdotal].

### Beijing
- **What it is.** 21.8 million residents; GDP 5,207.34 billion yuan (2025), passing 5 trillion for the first time; services 4,477.69 billion [data].
- **Employers.** 47 Fortune Global 500 headquarters (2025); 209 large AI models filed, nearly a third of China's total; research spending of 429.88 billion yuan by key enterprises, 320.11 billion of it in information, software and IT services; bank deposits of 27.1 trillion and loans of 12.3 trillion yuan; Xiaomi's smart factory and auto factory (Xiaomi's own site) [data, employer-stated].
- **Standing.** AI 5-5-4, software 5-5-4, IT 5-5-4 (Startup Genome: 6th in the world, first in Asia); finance 4-3-3 (GFCI 16th). AI is rated dominant on the statistics bureau's count of large models.
- **Rent.** ¥6,280.00 (Numbeo, Oct 2026) [anecdotal].

### Shenzhen
- **What it is.** 18.25 million residents; GDP 3,873.18 billion yuan; the average wage of urban non-private units 15,947 a month, the highest of the hubs where it was read [data].
- **Employers.** Tencent (headquartered in Shenzhen since 1998) and Huawei (headquarters at Bantian, Longgang; about 213,000 employees worldwide, 100% owned by employees) [employer-stated]; software and information services over 800 billion yuan; 2,600 AI enterprises; financial institutions 526.16 billion yuan of added value, up 12.1%; the port handled 35.41 million TEU [data].
- **Standing.** IT, software and AI 4-3-3 (Startup Genome: 19th, 7th in Asia); finance 4-4-3 (GFCI 8th, third in Fintech); logistics 4-3-3.
- **Rent.** ¥5,095.38 (Numbeo) [anecdotal].

### Guangzhou
- **What it is.** 19.10 million residents; GDP 3,203.946 billion yuan; wage 13,899 a month [data].
- **Employers.** The Port of Guangzhou (over 28 million TEU), Baiyun airport (83.59 million passengers), car, electronics and petrochemical makers (46.3% of large-industry added value); financial institutions 322.10 billion yuan [data]. No named company headquarters read.
- **Standing.** Logistics 4-4-4: express deliveries exceeded 20 billion items, the most in the country; port among the world's top six (government work report). Finance 4-3-2 (GFCI 28th). Startup Genome 38th.
- **Rent.** ¥3,611.11 (Numbeo) [anecdotal].

### Hangzhou
- **What it is.** 12.7 million residents; GDP 2,301.1 billion yuan [data].
- **Employers.** Core digital-economy industries 678 billion yuan, 29.5% of GDP; information, software and IT services 1,511.6 billion yuan of revenue, up 13.5%; finance 274.1 billion (Hangzhou bulletin text) [data]. No named employer headquarters read (Alibaba's page does not state its headquarters).
- **Standing.** IT and software 4-3-2 (Startup Genome 28th); finance 3-2-2 (GFCI 59th).
- **Rent.** ¥4,156.36 (Numbeo) [anecdotal].

### Suzhou
- **What it is.** 13.05 million residents; GDP 2,769.51 billion yuan; wage 12,196 a month [data].
- **Employers.** Large industrial enterprises with 4,896.64 billion yuan of output; goods trade of 2,811.93 billion yuan in 2025 (a record); integrated-circuit wafer output up 13.6%, industrial robots up 20.6% [data].
- **Standing.** Logistics 3-2-1 only; nothing rated, since the bulletins give output, not graduate hiring.
- **Rent.** ¥3,035.00 (Numbeo) [anecdotal].

### Wuhan
- **What it is.** 13.86 million residents; GDP 2,214.735 billion yuan [data].
- **Employers and universities.** High-tech manufacturers 26.2% of large-industry added value; 811,000 vehicles built; 225,300 postgraduate and 1.22 million undergraduate and college students [data].
- **Standing.** Finance 3-2-1 (GFCI 66th).
- **Rent.** ¥2,325.00 (Numbeo) [anecdotal].

### Chongqing
- **What it is.** 31.87 million residents in the municipality (year-end 2025, down 32,100); GDP 3,375.793 billion yuan (press and portal reports of the bulletin) [data].
- **Employers.** Software and IT-services firms above designated size grew revenue 16.2% and employed 72,300 people; output of new-energy vehicles rose 36.0% [data].
- **Standing.** IT 3-2-1.
- **Rent.** ¥2,806.67 (Numbeo) [anecdotal].

### Chengdu
- **What it is.** 21.54 million residents; GDP 2,476.36 billion yuan; wage 10,990 a month [data].
- **Employers.** Financial industry 240.52 billion yuan of added value; information, software and IT services 213.61 billion, up 9.6% [data].
- **Standing.** Finance 3-3-2 (GFCI 34th, ahead of Hangzhou and Wuhan); IT 3-2-1.
- **Rent.** ¥3,143.64 (Numbeo) [anecdotal].

## 5. Hypotheses tested

1. **"Shanghai is China's finance city and Beijing its tech city, so a finance graduate should go to Shanghai."** Supported on the indices: GFCI ranks Shanghai 5th and Shenzhen 8th, ahead of Beijing (16th); Beijing is 1st in Asia for start-ups and AI [practitioner consensus]. It holds for ranking, not for entry roles: no graduate-scheme page was read.
2. **"Shenzhen pays most."** Partly supported: of the four cities where the statistic was read, Shenzhen's 15,947 a month is highest, but Shanghai and Beijing were not readable, so it is not shown to lead [data].
3. **"A second-tier city is a cheaper way in."** Insufficient evidence: rent in Wuhan (¥2,325) is a third of Shanghai's (¥7,048) on Numbeo, but we found no entry-hiring evidence for the second-tier cities, and Chengdu's average pay (10,990) is about 69% of Shenzhen's [anecdotal, data].
4. **"The graduate work-permit rule is open to any European master's graduate."** Not supported: national circular 3/2017 (MOHRSS, MFA, MOE) requires a master's or above from a Chinese or Top 500 foreign university, application within 1 year of graduation, a grade average of at least 80 (or B+/B), and a job paying at or above the local average wage; see visas_immigration/china/china_visas_immigration_guide.md [data].

## 6. Common mistakes and myths

- Comparing municipality populations with city populations elsewhere. Chongqing's 31.9 million are mostly outside the built-up city.
- Reading the national average wage (129,441 a year) as a graduate salary. It is an all-ages average for urban non-private units.
- Treating the visa-free 30 days as work permission. It covers business, tourism and visits; study and paid work need a visa (FCDO).
- Assuming an internship is allowed on a study visa. It needs the school's consent and a note on the permit (Beijing government FAQ).

## 7. Decision rules

1. If you want finance and read Chinese, prefer Shanghai, then Shenzhen; both rank in the GFCI top ten, Beijing does not.
2. If you want AI or software, prefer Beijing, then Shenzhen: Beijing is 1st in Asia on Startup Genome 2026, Shenzhen 7th.
3. If you want logistics or trade, prefer Guangzhou or Shanghai: they carry the volumes (28.05 and 55.06 million TEU).
4. If you want to work after a master's in China, check the grade (average 80 of 100) and salary-floor conditions before choosing the city, because the floor is the local average wage.
5. If you are an EU (including Italian) or British citizen, do not rely on the 30-day visa-free entry for study or paid work (strictly prohibited), and note it runs only to 31 December 2026.
6. If you choose a second-tier city (Wuhan, Chongqing, Chengdu, Suzhou), look for a named employer first; the bulletins here show output, not graduate hiring.
7. Read the FCDO safety page before accepting a role that involves business disputes or data, because of exit bans and the breadth of national security laws.
