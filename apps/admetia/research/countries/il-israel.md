# Israel: country brief

Today is 3 October 2026. Tags: `[data]` statistics and published indices, `[employer-stated]`, `[practitioner consensus]`, `[anecdotal]`. Routes in the Atlas are for EU/EEA/Swiss and UK passports only. **Read the UK Foreign Office's travel advice first** (https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/israel, updated 22 July 2026): it advises against all travel to Gaza, within 500 m of its border, to parts of the northern West Bank and to areas near the northern border and the Golan Heights, advises against all but essential travel to most of the rest of the West Bank, and reports Iranian strikes in the region since 8 July 2026 [data].

## 1. Bottom line

- **Israel is a software, R&D and defence market, and Tel Aviv is where it is concentrated.** High-tech employed about 391,000 people in 2024, about 11.5% of employees; 51% of them (some 200,000) were in R&D roles (Israel Innovation Authority, April 2025) [data].
- **But employment has stalled.** The same report counts about 5,000 fewer high-tech employees in 2024 than in 2023, the first fall in at least a decade [data]. Intel Israel, with about 9,300 staff, has been cutting jobs, including at Kiryat Gat for the first time (Calcalist, 7 July 2025) [practitioner consensus].
- **Tel Aviv has the ecosystem.** Startup Genome's 2026 report puts it first in the Middle East and North Africa and in the global top five, with an ecosystem value of $250 billion; Israeli tech raised about $15.6 billion in 2025 [data].
- **Tel Aviv is a minor financial centre.** The Global Financial Centres Index 40 ranks it 94th of 117 (88th in March 2026); Dubai is 9th, Riyadh 46th, Doha 52nd, Istanbul 89th [data]. Ten of the 14 foreign banks' representative offices on the Bank of Israel's list are in Tel Aviv (2023) [data].
- **Pay differs sharply by city.** In the first half of 2025 the average monthly wage was NIS 22,359 in Tel Aviv and NIS 11,415 in Jerusalem, against NIS 15,098 nationally (National Insurance Institute) [data].
- **Jerusalem and Haifa are technology sites, not finance centres.** Har Hotzvim in Jerusalem hosts Intel, Mobileye and Rafael; Haifa has Elbit Systems' headquarters, Intel's R&D and the Technion [employer-stated].
- **Graduate and foreign expert work requires employer sponsorship.** Students on the A/2 visa are not allowed to work in the local economy; foreign professionals enter via the B/1 Foreign Expert route (PIBA Procedure 5.3.0041, minimum NIS 27,132/month in 2026) or Hi-Tech fast-track (Procedure 5.3.0043, which exempts STEM graduates of Israeli universities from the double-average wage threshold). Full rules: visas_immigration/israel/israel_visas_immigration_guide.md.

## 2. The economy and the graduate labour market

**Size and structure.** No GDP figure was read for this brief. The structure the sources show is high-tech: 391,000 employees in 2024, a share of about 11.5% that has held for several years, with headquarters roles down from 41% to 29% of high-tech jobs and R&D up from 37% (2012) to 51% [data].

**Youth and graduate unemployment; entry pay.** Not read. The only pay figures are all-employee averages by city (see §3).

**How hiring works.** Large employers publish vacancies on their own career pages. One snapshot: on 3 October 2026 Mobileye's page listed 187 open positions worldwide, 67 in Jerusalem, 33 in Ramat Gan, 25 in Haifa, 23 in Petah Tikva and one in Tel Aviv [employer-stated]. A snapshot of one employer, not a market measure.

**Language of work.** Not researched; Hebrew requirements are an open gap.

**Visa headline.** EU, EEA, Swiss and British visitors can enter without a visa for up to 90 days, but since 1 January 2025 all visa-exempt travellers require an electronic travel authorisation (ETA-IL), valid two years and costing NIS 25 via https://israel-entry.piba.gov.il/ before boarding; working without permission is strictly prohibited and leads to detention and deportation (PIBA) [data]. Students on the A/2 visa are generally not permitted to work, with a statutory exception under PIBA Procedure 5.8.0002 for master's and doctoral students employed by their university in part-time teaching [data]. Full immigration guide: visas_immigration/israel/israel_visas_immigration_guide.md.

### Tel Aviv

Population 495,230 (Tel Aviv-Yafo). Average monthly wage NIS 22,359 [data], about 48% above the national NIS 15,098.

**Sectors and employers.** Check Point gives its international corporate headquarters as 5 Shlomo Kaplan Street, Tel Aviv, with more than 7,000 employees, over 3,500 of them security experts (Check Point, About us) [employer-stated]. Foreign banks' representative offices include J.P. Morgan, BNP Paribas, Julius Baer and Pictet (Bank of Israel, 2023) [data]. Israel's start-up ecosystem is centred here (Startup Genome) [data]. Several large companies sit in the surrounding metropolitan area (Ramat Gan, Herzliya, Petah Tikva); this research counted no city-level employer totals.

**Universities.** Tel Aviv University (international office page read for its visa rules only).

**Standing.**
- **Software [5, 5, 4].** National 5: the centre of the national ecosystem and of Check Point. Regional 5: Startup Genome names it first in MENA. World 4: global top five by ecosystem value ($250 billion). Rated dominant (needs a `data` claim: Startup Genome, and the Innovation Authority's national figures). The gap: no source gives Tel Aviv's share of national high-tech jobs.
- **Finance [5, 2, 1].** National 5: the centre of the country's banking sector among the cities listed (10 of 14 representative offices); the Bank of Israel's list of supervised banks could not be read. Regional 2: GFCI 94th, behind Dubai, Abu Dhabi, Riyadh, Doha and Istanbul. World 1. Rated present.

### Haifa

Population 298,312. Israel's northern port city and home of the Technion.

**Employers and institutions.** Elbit Systems gives its headquarters as Haifa and says it employs over 21,000 people worldwide, with an order backlog of $32.0 billion at 30 June 2026 [employer-stated]. Intel has R&D centres in Haifa and Petah Tikva, under a round of layoffs [practitioner consensus]. Mobileye listed 25 openings in Haifa [employer-stated]. The Technion says it has more than 10,200 undergraduate and about 4,400 graduate students, and that many work in industry during their studies [employer-stated]. The Adani–Gadot group, owner of Haifa port since 2023, calls it the second-largest port in Israel [employer-stated].

**Standing.**
- **Computer science [3, 2, 1].** A recognised secondary site: Intel's R&D centre, Elbit's headquarters and Mobileye openings. Rated present, not strong: Intel is shrinking, and Elbit's and Mobileye's claims do not say they hire graduates in computing in Haifa.
- **Logistics [4, 2, 1].** The second port by its owners' own description; no ranking of Israeli ports was read. Rated present.

### Jerusalem

Population 1,028,366 (the Central Bureau of Statistics noted in June 2024 that it had passed one million). Average monthly wage NIS 11,415, among the lowest of the large cities (Globes, 21 December 2025, reporting the National Insurance Institute); Jerusalem had the most employees of any large city, 287,984 (National Insurance Institute) [data].

**Employers.** The Har Hotzvim Hi-Tech Park says its tenants include Intel, Mobileye, Rafael Advanced Defense Systems, Ophir Optronics, Elbit Systems Rokar and Alpha Tau, alongside medium-sized firms and start-ups [employer-stated]. Mobileye's careers page listed 67 openings in Jerusalem, the most of any of its sites [employer-stated]. The Hebrew University has three campuses in Jerusalem (its own page).

**Standing.**
- **Software [3, 2, 1].** Rated strong on two employer-stated claims (the park's tenants and Mobileye's 67 openings), not on a headcount. National 3: a recognised secondary centre behind Tel Aviv; regional 2 and world 1: no ranking of Jerusalem was read.

## 5. Hypotheses tested

1. **"Tel Aviv is the Middle East's leading software hub."** Supported: Startup Genome's 2026 regional ranking places it first, and it is in the global top five [data].
2. **"Israeli tech is a growing graduate market."** Contradicted for headcount: high-tech employment fell by about 5,000 in 2024 and Intel is cutting [data; practitioner consensus]. The fall may be a pause; one year of data cannot show the trend.
3. **"Tel Aviv is a financial centre like Dubai."** Not supported: 94th in the GFCI against Dubai's 9th [data].
4. **"A European graduate can work in Israeli tech on a study visa."** Not supported: A/2 students are generally not allowed to work in the local economy; tech employment requires employer sponsorship via the B/1 Foreign Expert route (PIBA Procedure 5.3.0041/5.3.0043) [data].

## 6. Common mistakes and myths

- Reading 391,000 high-tech jobs as 391,000 vacancies: it is the stock, and it fell in 2024.
- Comparing Tel Aviv's average pay (NIS 22,359) with entry pay: it is the average of all employees, including senior staff.
- Treating "Start-up Nation" as an open graduate market without work permits: employer sponsorship under B/1 is mandatory, requiring NIS 27,132/month base pay in 2026 unless graduating from an Israeli university STEM program under Procedure 5.3.0043.
- Counting vacancy snapshots (Mobileye, 187 worldwide) as demand: they change daily.

## 7. Decision rules

1. If you want software or cybersecurity and can obtain a permit through an employer, look first at Tel Aviv and its metropolitan area, then Jerusalem (Har Hotzvim) and Haifa.
2. If you want a financial-services career, treat Israel as a small market; the Gulf and London are the comparisons in this guide.
3. Check whether the employer is shrinking: Intel has cut jobs for several rounds.
4. Compare city pay with city rents before accepting: Tel Aviv pays about twice Jerusalem on average, but no rent figure was read.
5. Read the FCDO page the week you travel; it excludes several areas.
6. Ask the employer about B/1 work permit sponsorship before applying; verify current wage thresholds and rules in visas_immigration/israel/israel_visas_immigration_guide.md.
