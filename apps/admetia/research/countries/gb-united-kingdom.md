# United Kingdom (Atlas brief, round 5, 3 October 2026)

Tags follow the shared brief: [data], [employer-stated], [practitioner consensus], [anecdotal]. The record is `data/atlas/gb.js`; every claim id mentioned is in that file; the quotes and arithmetic behind each claim are in `research/verification/round-5b.md`.

## 1. Bottom line

- **London is the UK's only first-tier hub for finance, and one of two in the world.** The Global Financial Centres Index 40 (September 2026) ranks London second in the world after New York and first in Western Europe [practitioner consensus]. The City of London alone had 676,000 workers in 2024, 225,000 of them in financial services [data].
- **London also leads Europe in tech.** Startup Genome (GSER 2026) ranks it third in the world and first in Europe; Dealroom (2026) fourth and first in Europe [practitioner consensus]. Three quarters of UK venture capital raised in 2025 went to London start-ups [data].
- **Outside London, pick the city for the family, not the other way round.** Edinburgh is the asset-management city (42,000 finance and insurance jobs, the most outside London among 14 big-city council areas; 6,000 in fund management against 1,500 in the next, Liverpool) [data]. Manchester, Leeds and Birmingham are large professional-services and digital markets; Glasgow and Cardiff are finance and insurance back-office cities [data].
- **Pay outside London is 8 to 26% lower.** ONS median full-time workplace pay in 2025 is £49,692 a year in London and £36,700–£45,800 in the other eleven hubs; rents range from £913 (Liverpool) to £2,332 (London) a month [data]. London's premium comes from the career ladder, not the first year (see money/salaries-and-roi.md).
- **The graduate market is tight.** 140 applications per graduate vacancy and graduate vacancies down 8% in 2025 (ISE) [practitioner consensus]. International postgraduate-taught leavers fare worst: 13% unemployed and seeking work against 3% for UK leavers [data, library].
- **For EU readers, the UK is a visa country.** The Graduate visa gives 24 months to applications made by 31 December 2026 and 18 months from 1 January 2027; Skilled Worker needs £41,700, or £33,400 for new entrants [data]. Irish citizens are the exception [data].
- **Cambridge is a technology-and-research cluster, not a graduate-finance city.** It has the most R&D jobs of the big-city council areas outside London (9,000) and Dealroom calls it first in Europe for density [data, practitioner consensus]; entry-level business jobs there are thin.

## 2. The economy and the graduate labour market

**Size and structure.** Financial and related professional services account for over 2.4 million jobs in Great Britain, two thirds of them outside London, and the UK was the world's largest net exporter of financial services in 2024 (£92.6 billion) (City of London Corporation, July 2026) [data]. London holds 35% of Great Britain's 844,000 jobs in computer programming and consultancy, 33% of the 923,000 in head-office and management consultancy, 29% of 846,000 in legal and accounting, and three quarters of the 77,000 in fund management (ONS BRES 2024) [data; shares are arithmetic on the counts]. Nationally, tech roles are 6.4% of jobs; in London more than 7% [data].

**Labour market now.** The ONS labour market bulletin of 15 September 2026 puts the employment rate (16–64) at 75.1% and unemployment at 4.9% for May to July 2026, the latter up 0.2 points on the year [data]. Graduate Outcomes 2022/23 shows graduate unemployment up from just under 5.5% to 6.1%, and professional-level employment down from 75.1% to 71.9% (Jisc, Spring 2026) [data, via the library].

**Entry pay.** ISE's employer surveys give an average graduate salary of £33,000 (up 2%) and £24,000 for apprentices [practitioner consensus]. The Department for Education's LEO data give median earnings of UK-domiciled level-7 business and management graduates of £35,100 at one year, £41,400 at three and £53,400 at ten [data, library]. London Business School's MiM class of 2024 reported a UK mean base of £44,675 [data, library]. These are different populations; the LBS figure is an elite benchmark, not a typical outcome.

**How hiring works.** Large employers run graduate schemes and summer internships with early deadlines. London banking applications open as early as July and close between October and December for internships the following summer, and many roles are filled on a rolling basis before the deadline [employer-stated / practitioner consensus]. HSBC, Barclays and KPMG UK each publish student and graduate routes (HSBC: a students-and-graduates section; Barclays: graduate and internship programmes in Technology, Investment Banking, Capital Markets; KPMG: Audit, Consulting, Tax & Law, Technology & Engineering) [employer-stated]. The one-year MSc timing problem is in getting-in/recruiting-calendar.md.

**Language.** English; no local-language requirement.

**Visa headline.** Student visa for every EU, EEA and Swiss student (with the immigration health surcharge); study work limited to 20 hours a week in term time; Graduate visa without a job offer (24 months to 31 December 2026, 18 months after); Skilled Worker at £41,700 or the going rate, new entrants £33,400; High Potential Individual visa for graduates of listed universities, which excludes UK universities and Bocconi, HEC and St Gallen [data]. Single source of truth: `visas_immigration/united_kingdom/united_kingdom_visas_immigration_guide.md` and `places/visas-and-work-rights.md §1`.

## 3. Hubs

**Metric basis (same for all 12 hubs).** Population: ONS mid-2025 estimates for the local authority (Greater London for London) [data]. GDP: ONS Regional GDP by local authority, 2023, current prices (2024 data withhold Bristol, Cardiff and Glasgow) [data]. Pay: ONS ASHE 2025, workplace analysis, full-time employees, **median** annual gross pay divided by 12 (the mean is distorted by high earners: London £70,275 mean against £49,692 median; Cambridge £66,351 against £45,762) [data]. Rent: ONS Price Index of Private Rents, August 2026, average monthly rent of all property types, because ONS publishes no one-bedroom figure for cities; Edinburgh and Glasgow are the Lothian and Greater Glasgow rental-market areas [data]. All are `area: city` (local authority), except London, which is Greater London for population, GDP and rent and the London region for pay.

**Standing basis.** Standing steps are [national, regional (Europe), world]. A 4 or 5 beyond the country needs a ranking. Outside London, national 4s are set by the ONS rank among 14 big-city council areas (strong means first to third with at least 5,000 jobs); regional and world steps are kept low (2 and 1) unless a ranking says more.

### London
- **What it is.** Greater London, 9.12 million people, £617.9 billion of GDP (2023), median full-time pay £4,141 a month, average private rent £2,332 [data].
- **Sectors and employers.** Banking, fund management, insurance, legal, consulting, accountancy, technology. Registered offices on Companies House: HSBC Holdings (8 Canada Square), Barclays (1 Churchill Place), London Stock Exchange Group (10 Paternoster Square), Deloitte UK (1 New Street Square), KPMG UK (15 Canada Square), Revolut (30 South Colonnade) [data]. The City of London: 69,000 in banking, 29,000 in fund management, 48,000 in management consultancy, 26,000 in accountancy; one in five of Great Britain's finance jobs [data]. 39,266 tech establishments [data].
- **Ecosystem.** GFCI 40: London 2nd (757 points against New York's 761); GSER 2026: third, $437 billion ecosystem value against a $25 billion global average; Dealroom 2026: fourth; $23.7 billion of UK venture capital in 2025, 75% to London [data / practitioner consensus].
- **Universities and schools.** LBS, LSE, Imperial, UCL, King's; the calculator lists 15 London programmes.
- **Standing.** Finance 5/5/5 (GFCI plus City counts: London is the world benchmark with New York). Software 5/5/4 and IT 5/5/4 (GSER third, Dealroom fourth, 35% of GB computer programming jobs). AI 5/5/3 (first in Europe in the AI-native cluster; third at world scale since San Francisco and others lead). Management, accounting and business 5/3/3 (a third of GB consultancy jobs; the regional 3 reflects that Paris, Frankfurt and others are large peers with no ranking read).
- **Demand.** Finance dominant; business, management, accounting, software, IT and AI strong; banking dominant; asset management, investment banking, financial consulting and venture capital strong.
- **Cost.** Highest in the UK; on LBS-level pay, rent takes the greater part of net income (money/salaries-and-roi.md).

- ONS 2024: 373,000 jobs; 42,000 in finance and insurance (the most of the 14); 6,000 in fund management; 25,000 in banking-type financial services; 18,000 in information and communication [data]. Scotland has over 160,000 finance professionals and £480 billion of assets managed [data].
- Employers: Baillie Gifford & Co (3 Haymarket Square), Aberdeen Group (1 George Street), NatWest Group (36 St Andrew Square), Lloyds Banking Group (The Mound)(Companies House), and Bank of Scotland and Scottish Widows (named in the Scotland finance claim) [data].

- **Standing.** Finance 4/3/2: second UK finance centre by every measure read, 11th in Western Europe (so not a top-five European centre: regional 3). Demand: finance strong; asset management strong; banking present.

## 5. Hypotheses tested

1. **"Outside London there is no graduate finance in the UK."** Partly supported. Edinburgh has 42,000 finance and insurance jobs and the UK's asset-management concentration (6,000 fund-management jobs against 59,000 in London), and Glasgow has 27,000; but London has three quarters of fund management and a fifth of Great Britain's finance jobs in the City alone [data]. Holds for front-office investment banking; fails for asset management, insurance and banking operations.
2. **"Manchester is the UK's second tech city."** Supported on counts, with a caveat. Manchester ranks second of 14 for information and communication jobs (25,000) and its ecosystem value is the largest after London's; Leeds has more programming jobs (18,000 against 17,000) [data]. The two are close; neither approaches London's 294,000 programming jobs.
3. **"The biggest city is the best market for every family."** Not supported: Birmingham has the most jobs of the 14 big-city council areas outside London (568,000) and leads head-office and consultancy (25,000), but Leeds has more information-and-communication jobs (26,000 against 20,000) and Edinburgh more finance jobs (42,000 against 23,000) [data]. Size matters less than sector mix.
4. **"Cambridge and Oxford are alike."** Not supported: Cambridge has 9,000 R&D jobs and 6,000 programming jobs, Oxford 4,000 and 3,500, and Dealroom ranks Cambridge's density third in the world [data / practitioner consensus].
5. **"The median salary in London is £70,000."** Not supported: that is the ONS mean (£70,275); the median full-time workplace pay is £49,692 [data].

## 6. Common mistakes and myths

- Reading London's mean pay as typical pay; use the median (£49,692 a year).
- Treating "the UK" as London: the eleven other hubs each produce between 1.5% (Oxford) and 6.4% (Leeds) of London's GDP, and their median pay is 8 to 26% below London's [data; percentages are arithmetic].
- Assuming EU citizens still have work rights; only Irish citizens do [data].
- Counting on the Graduate visa as a long-term plan: it is 18 months from January 2027 and cannot be extended [data].
- Mixing sources: ONS regional GDP for 2024 excludes Bristol, Cardiff and Glasgow, so 2023 is used for all.

## 7. Decision rules

1. If you want investment banking, asset management at scale, or Big Four/MBB consulting, prefer London (three quarters of GB fund management jobs; GFCI 2nd).
2. If you want asset management or insurance and pensions without London rents, prefer Edinburgh (rent £1,415 against £2,332).
3. If you want digital, media or a broad professional-services market at lower cost, prefer Manchester or Leeds (rent £1,373 and £1,145).
4. If you want large-company business, management or accounting roles outside London, prefer Birmingham (the most jobs, rent £1,099).
5. If you want insurance or banking operations, consider Cardiff or Glasgow (Admiral; 27,000 finance jobs) but expect less front-office exposure.
6. If you want research-led computing or life sciences, prefer Cambridge; budget for rent £1,805.
7. If you are an EU citizen without a UK degree, check the HPI list before planning on the UK (Bocconi, HEC, St Gallen are not listed).
8. If you study a one-year MSc in the UK, start applications in the summer before you arrive: banking deadlines run from October to December.
9. If you compare pay across cities, use the median and subtract the local rent, not the mean.

## 8. Gaps and claims to verify

1. No Oxford family is rated: no source of entry-level hiring was read.
2. AI and data-science demand outside London rests on ONS counts and start-up rankings, not a hub-by-family statistic.
3. Employer entries are Companies House registered offices, not headcounts; Sage and Greggs are registered in North Tyneside.
4. ONS publishes no one-bedroom city rent: the figure is the all-property average, and Edinburgh and Glasgow cover rental-market areas that include surrounding councils.
5. GDP is for 2023 because the 2024 edition withholds three cities.
6. The Western Europe rank of Edinburgh (11th) is my count of the GFCI 40 Table 8 list; Glasgow is not in that table.
7. The UK–EU Youth Experience Scheme has no agreed terms; it is not shown.
8. The "two thirds outside London" and the 2.4 million figure are the City of London Corporation's; TheCityUK's earlier figure is almost 2.5 million; I trust the newer July 2026 factsheet.
9. Sheffield, Nottingham and Bath are in the ONS comparison set of 14 but have no hub; Belfast is not in it.
