# Portugal (Atlas brief, round 5, 3 October 2026)

Tags: [data], [employer-stated], [practitioner consensus], [anecdotal]. The record is `data/atlas/pt.js`; the quotes behind each claim are in `research/verification/round-5b.md`.

## 1. Bottom line

- **Lisbon is Portugal's market for graduates in finance and IT.** Eurostat counts 79,860 people in information and communication in the Lisbon metropolitan region (60% of Portugal's 133,230) and 47,950 in finance and insurance (58% of 82,360), in 2021 [data]. Porto is a clear second, with 25,760 and 11,690.
- **At European scale it is a mid-sized centre.** Lisbon is 12th of 152 metropolitan regions for information and communication jobs and 14th for finance and insurance (own count of Eurostat's table) [data]. The Global Financial Centres Index 40 puts it 61st in the world and outside the Western European top 15 [practitioner consensus].
- **Pay is low and the capital is dear.** Employees in the municipality of Lisbon average €2,121 gross a month (2024), 35% above Portugal's €1,576, but new-lease rents are €17.22 per square metre against €9.50 nationally (81% above) [data]. Porto: €1,930 and €14.29.
- **Graduate programmes are real but tiny.** Jerónimo Martins took 11 people from more than 1,400 applications in 2025; Sonae's Contacto aims at more than 80 places; Galp's Generation Galp has fed more than 500 current employees since 1998; EDP's programme needs no Portuguese [employer-stated].
- **Language is employer by employer.** EDP says Portuguese is not required; Jerónimo Martins asks for fluent Portuguese and English [employer-stated].
- **Tax is the strong card.** IRS Jovem exempts 100% of income in the first year for people up to 35, tapering over ten years, worth about €7,200 at €45,000 in year one (library calculation from a Portal das Finanças ruling) [data].
- **Many graduates of the top school leave.** Nova SBE's master's in management is 2nd in the FT 2026 table with an international-mobility rank of 10, so its pay figure is not Portuguese pay [data].

## 2. The economy and the graduate labour market

**Size and structure.** Portugal's GDP was €242.3 billion in 2022 and 4.96 million people were in work in 2021 (Eurostat, met_10r_3gdp and met_10r_3emp) [data]. Of those jobs 133,230 (2.7%) were in information and communication, 82,360 (1.7%) in finance and insurance and 753,250 (15%) in manufacturing (arithmetic on Eurostat's counts). It is an economy of energy utilities, retail groups, banks, tourism and manufacturing more than of software; the software and shared-services niche is concentrated in two metropolitan regions.

**Labour market.** Seasonally adjusted unemployment was 5.7% in August 2026 (EU 6.1%), but 19.8% for under-25s (EU 15.4%) (Eurostat une_rt_m) [data]. In 2025, 83.9% of tertiary graduates aged 20–34 who left education up to three years earlier were in work, against 85.3% in the EU (Eurostat edat_lfse_24) [data].

**Entry pay.** No official entry salary by field was found. The best figure is INE's average monthly gross earnings (ganho) by workplace: €1,576 for Portugal in 2024, €2,121 for establishments in Lisbon and €1,930 in Porto (INE indicator 0012655) [data]; all employees, not graduates. The library's figure for all employees in the first quarter of 2026 is €1,611 (INE via press report) [data, secondary]. Sonae promises Contacto pay above the average starting pay of the general "técnico superior" career, without a number [employer-stated].

**How hiring works.** Large groups run annual programmes. Galp: Generation Galp is a one-year traineeship for people who have completed or are completing a master's, with screening, an online assessment, group dynamics and a pitch, a business case and a final interview; latest applications closed; professional internships of 6 to 12 months run all year [employer-stated]. Jerónimo Martins: a two-year Trainee Programme, a new edition began in January 2026, with applications opened in September 2025 [employer-stated]. Sonae: Contacto applications closed on 6 April 2026, assessment days in Porto and Lisbon in April, start in September [employer-stated]. EDP: an 18-month programme of three rotations from September, one of them abroad [employer-stated].

**Language.** Portuguese for most programmes; EDP's is open without it (EDP careers) [employer-stated].

**Visa headline.** Full official guide and single source of truth: `visas_immigration/portugal/portugal_visas_immigration_guide.md`. EU citizens register for a CRUE at the municipality after three months (€15; Lei 37/2006). Non-EU students may work alongside their studies (Lei 23/2007 art. 97). Master's and PhD graduates can obtain a 12-month post-study residence permit for job search or entrepreneurship without exiting Portugal (art. 122(1)(p); Directive 2016/801 art. 25). Manifestação de interesse was abolished by DL 37-A/2024.

**Metric basis.** Population: Eurostat metropolitan regions, 1 January 2023 (area: metro). GDP: Eurostat met_10r_3gdp, 2022 (metro). Pay: INE indicator 0012655, 2024, by municipality (area: city), gross monthly average, workplace basis. Rent: not shown as a metric, because INE publishes rent per square metre and Numbeo refused automated access.

## 3. Hubs

### Lisbon

**What it is.** The capital and the country's main concentration of head offices. The metropolitan region has 2,899,670 residents (2023), €87.4 billion of GDP in 2022 (36% of Portugal's) and 1,474,000 jobs in 2021 (Eurostat) [data]. Employees in the municipality average €2,121 a month gross (INE, 2024) [data].

**Sectors and named employers.** Headquarters were read in the Global LEI Index [data]: EDP (Avenida 24 de Julho 12), Galp (Avenida da Índia 8, Alcântara), Caixa Geral de Depósitos (Avenida João XXI 63), Banco de Portugal (Rua do Comércio 148), NOS (Rua Actor António Silva 9), Jerónimo Martins (Rua Actor António Silva 7), OutSystems (Rua Central Park 6) and REN (Avenida Estados Unidos da América 55). Graduate programmes: EDP Global Graduate Program, Generation Galp, the Jerónimo Martins Trainee Programme and Sonae's Contacto (assessment day in Lisbon) [employer-stated].

**Universities and schools.** Nova SBE: 2nd in the FT 2026 master's in management table, 100% employed at three months, 93% international students; Católica Lisbon 25th ($99,125 three-year average pay, 97% employed) [data, via the library]. FT pay is a purchasing-power, sector-weighted figure, not a Lisbon salary.

**Start-ups.** Startup Genome's GSER 2026 puts Lisbon's Ecosystem Value at $35 billion (global average $25 billion), with $316 million of early-stage funding in the second half of 2023 to 2025 against a global average of $554 million [practitioner consensus]. The funding is below the global average, so it is an ecosystem of value more than of capital.

**Cost.** INE's median rent of new leases in the 12 months to March 2026 was €17.22 per square metre in Lisbon (17,144 new contracts); the library's central one-bedroom rent from Numbeo was about €1,425 [data; anecdotal].

**Standing.**
- *IT (information and communication):* national 5, regional 3, world 2. First in Portugal by Eurostat's count (60% of jobs); 12th of 152 European metropolitan regions, so a notable centre but not a top-five one; GSER's value above the global average without the funding to be major globally.
- *Finance:* national 5, regional 2, world 2. First in Portugal (58% of finance and insurance jobs; Banco de Portugal and Caixa Geral de Depósitos headquarters). Eurostat's count puts it 14th in Europe, but that counts all finance and insurance jobs, and the GFCI 40 places it 61st in the world and outside the Western European top 15, so the lower reading is taken.
- *Business:* national 5, regional 2, world 1. Three large groups with headquarters (EDP, Galp, Jerónimo Martins) run management programmes; this is a judgement from named employers, not a statistic.

### Porto

**What it is.** Portugal's second metropolitan region: 1,774,104 residents (2023), €39.2 billion of GDP in 2022 (16% of Portugal's) and 829,750 jobs in 2021 (Eurostat) [data]. Employees at establishments in the municipality average €1,930 a month gross (INE, 2024) [data].

**Sectors and named employers.** Eurostat counts 25,760 jobs in information and communication (38th of 152 European metropolitan regions) and 11,690 in finance and insurance (51st) [data]. Banco BPI (Avenida da Boavista 1117), Millennium bcp (registered at Praça D. João I 28; its operational base may be elsewhere), Mota-Engil (Rua do Rego Lameiro 38) and Sonae (Lugar do Espido, Maia) are headquartered in the area [data]. Contacto's April assessment days include Porto [employer-stated].

**Universities and schools.** Católica Porto: 66th in the FT 2026 table, $55,831, 100% employed (response rate 51%) [data, via the library]; a low response rate and not a Lisbon comparator.

**Start-ups.** GSER 2026 ranks Porto 51st to 60th among emerging ecosystems, in the top ten in Europe for funding runway and the top 20 for its AI-native cluster [practitioner consensus].

**Cost.** INE's median new-lease rent was €14.29 per square metre in the 12 months to March 2026 (7,574 new contracts) [data]; 50% above the national median but 17% below Lisbon's.

**Standing.**
- *IT:* national 4, regional 2, world 1. Second in Portugal, but 38th of 152 European regions.
- *Finance:* national 4, regional 2, world 1. Second in Portugal; 51st of 152; two bank headquarters.

### Coimbra (not mapped)

Eurostat lists Coimbra with 439,940 residents (2023), €9.3 billion of GDP (2022), 3,650 jobs in information and communication and 1,810 in finance and insurance (2021) [data]. Too small to be a hub on these counts.

## 5. Hypotheses tested

**H1. "Lisbon pays a premium for its higher cost."** Not supported. Pay in the municipality is 35% above the national average (€2,121 against €1,576) but rent per square metre is 81% above the national median (€17.22 against €9.50); in Porto the premiums are 22% and 50% (INE) [data; arithmetic]. It holds only for readers with a pay well above the local average, such as the international programmes.

**H2. "Portuguese graduate programmes are a door for anyone."** Not supported. Jerónimo Martins received more than 1,400 applications for 11 places in 2025, about 1 in 130 [employer-stated; arithmetic]. It fails where the programme is bigger (Contacto, more than 80 places) or where the language rule is relaxed (EDP).

**H3. "Lisbon is a top European tech centre."** Partly supported. It is Portugal's leading IT market (60% of jobs) and 12th of 152 European metropolitan regions for information and communication jobs, which makes it notable rather than top-five (Eurostat, 2021) [data]. GSER's finding that its Ecosystem Value ($35 billion) is above the global average ($25 billion) while its early-stage funding ($316 million) is below ($554 million) shows a value-rich but capital-light ecosystem [practitioner consensus]. It fails if you read job counts as headcount at global firms, which these figures do not show.

**H4. "Porto is the cheap alternative."** Partly supported. Rent per square metre is 17% lower, but so is pay (9% lower) and Porto has a third of Lisbon's information and communication jobs (INE; Eurostat) [data].

## 6. Common mistakes and myths

1. **"The FT salary is Lisbon pay."** Nova SBE's $131,640 is a three-year, sector-weighted figure for alumni, many of whom work abroad [data, via the library].
2. **"Portuguese pay is the average wage."** INE's €1,576 is a mean over all employees and sectors, and by workplace, so it includes tourism and manufacturing; it says nothing on graduate entry pay.
3. **"Rent per square metre is the rent of a flat."** INE's figure is per square metre of all homes; a flat's rent depends on size [data].
4. **"All programmes are open to non-Portuguese speakers."** Only EDP says so; Jerónimo Martins asks for fluent Portuguese [employer-stated].
5. **"Lisbon is a financial centre like Frankfurt."** The GFCI 40 ranks it 61st in the world, outside the Western European top 15 [practitioner consensus].

## 7. Decision rules

1. If you want finance or IT in Portugal, aim at Lisbon first: it holds about 60% of the country's jobs in both (Eurostat, 2021).
2. If you speak no Portuguese, start from EDP, whose programme says Portuguese is not required, and check each other programme's language line.
3. If you apply to a retail or energy trainee scheme, treat it as a lottery with small odds (11 places from 1,400 applications at Jerónimo Martins) and apply to several.
4. If you plan to apply to Sonae's Contacto, expect an application window closing in early April and assessment days in April for a September start.
5. If you are under 35, check your IRS Jovem year-count before accepting an offer (library calculation).
6. If you compare Lisbon with Porto on cost, compare rent per square metre (€17.22 against €14.29) and pay (€2,121 against €1,930) together, not one alone.
7. If you want a Portuguese career, ask a school for pay split by country of work; the FT figures do not give it.
8. If you are a non-EU graduate hoping to switch from study to work, confirm the current route first; the open job-seeker visa has been replaced (practitioner reading).

## 8. Gaps and claims to verify

1. No source gives demand by role family or graduate entry pay by city; ratings rest on Eurostat's 2021 sector counts and named headquarters.
2. Eurostat's employment counts date from 2021, GDP from 2022 and population from 2023, the latest published for Portuguese metropolitan regions in the files read.
3. Headquarters come from the Global LEI Index and are sometimes the registered rather than the operational address (Millennium bcp).
4. INE's Porto first-quarter 2026 rent is only in a chart; the library's earlier press-reported €14.60 was not matched by the text of the INE release, which gives €14.29 for the 12 months to March 2026.
5. The two Católica figures and the Nova SBE figures are from the FT 2026 table as recorded in the library; the table was not re-read.
6. University rankings (QS 2027) could not be read from a primary source: two secondary pages disagree on whether Lisbon or Porto leads.
7. No page was read for the banks' programmes, the Big Four, OutSystems or shared-service centres.
8. RESOLVED (5 Oct 2026): A non-EU graduate can switch from study to work or obtain a 12-month post-study job-search permit under Lei 23/2007 art. 122(1)(p) and (o); see `visas_immigration/portugal/portugal_visas_immigration_guide.md`.
