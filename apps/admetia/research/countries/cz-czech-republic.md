# Czech Republic: country brief

Today is 3 October 2026. Tags: `[data]` official statistics or government pages, `[employer-stated]`, `[practitioner consensus]` (published indices and trade-body reports as reported by others), `[anecdotal]`.

## 1. Bottom line

- Prague is the Czech office economy. In 2022 the Prague metropolitan region held 91,610 information-and-communication jobs (50% of the country's 181,930) and 42,050 finance-and-insurance jobs (51% of 82,780) (Eurostat) [data]. Prague's GDP per person was 191.8% of the EU average in 2024, among the five highest regions in the EU (Eurostat news) [data].
- Brno is a distant second: 38,390 information-and-communication jobs (21% of the country) and 8,780 in finance; Ostrava is third with 15,240 and 6,060 (Eurostat, 2022) [data]. Brno has the technology employers worth naming: Kiwi.com has its headquarters there and Red Hat has two offices [employer-stated].
- Banking is a Prague career: Komerční banka, Česká spořitelna, Raiffeisenbank and MONETA Money Bank give Prague addresses as their headquarters or seat, and the Czech National Bank has its head office in Prague 1 [employer-stated].
- Pay: the average gross monthly wage in 2025 was CZK 62,723 in Prague, 48,467 in the South Moravian Region (Brno) and 44,241 in the Moravian-Silesian Region (Ostrava), against CZK 49,215 nationally (Czech Statistical Office, preliminary) [data]. A junior specialist in a service centre earned a mean base of €2,133 a month in 2024, the highest of four central European countries compared (ABSL citing Mercer, via the library) [data].
- Standing: Prague is 5 nationally in finance, IT and software but only 2 in Europe and 1 in the world: tenth of 25 capital regions for ICT jobs and fourteenth for finance jobs, 102nd of 117 in the Global Financial Centres Index 40, and a $8 billion start-up ecosystem against a European average of $14.3 billion [data, practitioner consensus].
- The business-service sector is large and international: over 400 companies and nearly 200,000 people, about 43% of them from outside the country, English the common language (ABSL, as reported on Expats.cz, April 2025) [practitioner consensus]. Prague and Brno shares of that are not published in anything read.
- For a European master's student, the Czech Republic suits a first job in a bank, a technology firm or a service centre in Prague, or software work in Brno; it does not offer capital-markets or investment-banking entry at European scale, and this round found no evidence on consulting or graduate-programme calendars.

## 2. The economy and the graduate labour market

**Size.** 10.91 million people in 2025 and GDP of €347.3 billion in 2025 (Eurostat nama_10_gdp and demo_pjan, updated September and October 2026) [data]. Unemployment was 2.8% in 2025, among the lowest in the EU against 6.0% for the EU (Eurostat une_rt_a) [data].

**Structure.** ICT specialists were 4.7% of employment in 2025 (248,200 people), slightly below the EU's 5.0% (Eurostat) [data]. In Prague 12.0% of main jobs are in information and communication (4.0% nationally), 6.9% in finance and insurance (2.4%) and 10.4% in professional, scientific and technical activities (5.0%) (Czech Statistical Office, Q4 2024) [data].

**Graduates and youth.** In 2025, 86.1% of tertiary graduates aged 20-34 who finished within three years were in work (EU 85.3%); unemployment among 15-to-24-year-olds was 10.4% (8.3% in 2023; EU 15.2%); 36.0% of 25-to-34-year-olds had a tertiary degree against the EU's 44.8% (Eurostat) [data].

**Entry pay.** No Czech graduate pay survey by field was read. The figure the Atlas holds is the Mercer 2024 junior-specialist base in business-service, IT and R&D centres, €2,133 mean (ABSL 2025, via the library) [data]; a single worker keeps 78.1% of gross at the average wage (Eurostat 2025, via the library) [data]. The regional averages above include senior staff.

**Language of work.** English is the common working language of the business-service sector, where about 43% of employees come from outside the country and up to 30 languages are used (ABSL, as reported on Expats.cz) [practitioner consensus]. Whether Czech is needed outside international firms was not researched.

**How hiring works.** Not researched: no graduate programme calendar or intake figure was read for any employer (gap).

**Permits.** EU citizens report their address to the Foreign Police within 30 days (Act 326/1999 Sb. § 93); registration certificates after 3 months are optional. Non-EU full-time university students work without a permit (Act 435/2004 Sb. § 98(j)), and Czech university graduates enjoy permanent free access to the labour market (Act 435/2004 Sb. § 98(m)) and a 9-month non-renewable job-search residence permit (Act 326/1999 Sb. § 42(3)) that can be converted into a non-dual Employee Card. All immigration, visa, work authorization and Blue Card rules are consolidated in `visas_immigration/czech_republic/czech_republic_visas_immigration_guide.md`.

## 3. Hubs

### Prague

**What it is.** 2,796,717 residents in the metropolitan region (2023), GDP €110.0 billion (2022) (Eurostat) and an average gross wage of CZK 62,723 a month (Czech Statistical Office, Prague region, 2025) [data]. Rent: not read (Numbeo refused automated access on 3 October 2026).

**Sectors and named employers.**
- Banking: Komerční banka (Na Příkopě 33, Praha 1), Česká spořitelna (Olbrachtova 1929/62, Praha 4), Raiffeisenbank (Hvězdova 1716/2b, Praha 4), MONETA Money Bank (BB Centrum, Vyskočilova 1442/1b, Praha 4) and the Czech National Bank (Na Příkopě 864/28, Praha 1) [employer-stated].
- Software: Gen Digital, which says its global workforce has dual headquarters in Prague and Tempe, Arizona [employer-stated]; JetBrains, whose contacts page lists its headquarters in Amsterdam and a JetBrains s.r.o. sales office for EMEA and APAC at Kavčí Hory Office Park, Praha 4 [employer-stated]; Red Hat (a WeWork office on Národní) and Kiwi.com (a core office on Rohanské nábřeží, Prague 8) [employer-stated].
- Business services: the national ABSL totals; no Prague-only count was read.

**Universities and start-ups.** No university ranking was read this round. Startup Genome's 2026 report values the Prague ecosystem at $8 billion, with $391 million of early-stage funding in H2 2023-2025, against a European average of $14.3 billion [practitioner consensus].

### Brno

**What it is.** 1,217,200 residents in the metropolitan region (2023), GDP €30.3 billion (2022) (Eurostat); average gross wage CZK 48,467 a month in the South Moravian Region, a region and not a city (Czech Statistical Office, 2025) [data].

**Sectors and employers.** Information and communication 38,390 jobs and finance and insurance 8,780, both second in the country (Eurostat, 2022) [data]. Kiwi.com gives its Brno address (Lazaretní 925/9) as its headquarters and says it has over 400 employees in five core locations [employer-stated]. Red Hat lists two Brno offices (Purkyňova 647/111 and 665/115) [employer-stated]. Startup Genome puts the Brno ecosystem at $1 billion [practitioner consensus].

**Standing:** IT 4 / 2 / 1, software 4 / 2 / 1, finance 4 / 1 / 1. National 4 because second in the country, no wider because the counts are small beside Warsaw (119,200 ICT jobs) and Kraków (65,800).

### Ostrava

**What it is.** 1,189,674 residents in the metropolitan region (2023), GDP €24.3 billion (2022); average gross wage CZK 44,241 a month in the Moravian-Silesian Region (2025). An industrial region: steel and manufacturing.

**Sectors and employers.** Information and communication 15,240 jobs and finance and insurance 6,060, both third in the country (Eurostat, 2022) [data]. Tietoevry's Czech careers page lists an open vacancy located in Ostrava [employer-stated]; MONETA Money Bank's correspondence address is in Ostrava-Hrabová [employer-stated]. No employer headcount was read.

**Standing:** IT 3 / 1 / 1, finance 3 / 1 / 1 (third in the country in both).

## 5. Hypotheses tested

1. **"Prague is Central Europe's finance centre."** Not supported at European scale: fourteenth of 25 capital regions for finance jobs, 102nd of 117 in GFCI 40, behind Warsaw (78th) [data, practitioner consensus].
2. **"Brno is the Czech Silicon Valley."** Partly supported: second in the country on both counts and home to Kiwi.com and Red Hat offices, but its 38,390 ICT jobs are under half of Prague's, and its $1 billion ecosystem value is small [data, practitioner consensus].
3. **"Czech graduates are easy to place."** Partly supported: 86.1% employment for recent graduates is just above the EU's 85.3%, and unemployment is low (2.8%), but the tertiary attainment rate (36.0%) is below the EU's (44.8%) [data].

## 6. Common mistakes and myths

- **Average regional pay is not entry pay.** Prague's CZK 62,723 includes all ages and seniorities; the Mercer junior base was €2,133 in service centres [data].
- **Business-service jobs are national figures here.** The 200,000 total is not split by city in anything read.
- **Metropolitan job counts date from 2022.** Treat the shares as indicative.

## 7. Decision rules

1. If you want a first job in banking, start with the Prague headquarters of the main banks and the Czech National Bank.
2. If you want software or product work, compare Prague (Gen Digital, JetBrains' sales office) with Brno (Kiwi.com, Red Hat) and look at each firm's careers page.
3. If you want investment banking or capital markets, do not choose the Czech Republic for entry.
4. If you want a business-service or finance-operations role and speak English plus another language, look at the international centres, but ask the employer for the city and the team, since no city split is published.
5. If you are non-EU and graduate in the Czech Republic, use the nine-month permit and have offers lined up.
