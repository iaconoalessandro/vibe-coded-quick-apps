## 1. Bottom line

- **Oman is the least documented Gulf market in this guide, and no European-graduate route was found.** Work needs a sponsored visa arranged before you start; in-country conversion from tourist visas is banned (ROP 31 Oct 2023) (see visas_immigration/oman/oman_visas_immigration_guide.md) [data]. No graduate page open to foreigners was read.
- **Muscat is where the banks are, but it is not a ranked financial centre.** The central bank's list gives Muscat-area addresses (Ruwi, Hai Al Mina, Madinat Al Fahal, Al Khuwair) for Bank Muscat, National Bank of Oman, Bank Dhofar, Oman Arab Bank, Sohar International and Ahli Bank, and for foreign banks such as Standard Chartered and HSBC (Central Bank of Oman) [data]. The Global Financial Centres Index 40 lists Muscat only as one of 22 associate centres, with 23 assessments in 24 months against the 150 needed (Z/Yen and CDI, September 2026) [data].
- **Sohar is the growth story, on the port's own numbers.** SOHAR Port and Freezone says throughput rose from 62 million tonnes (2019) to 72 million (2025), that it secured $968 million of new commitments in 2025, and that its freezone ranked third globally in fDi Intelligence's free-zone index (Oman Observer, 21 January 2026) [employer-stated]. For the first half of 2026 it reports 52 million tonnes (up 52%) and 545,000 TEU (up 40%), with OMR 2.62 billion of projects under way (Container News) [employer-stated].
- **The economy is growing slowly, led by non-oil activity.** Real GDP was OMR 39.30 billion in 2025, up 2.4%; non-oil activities rose 3.1% to OMR 28.70 billion, petroleum 1.1% to OMR 12.02 billion (Ministry of Economy bulletin, via Oman Observer, 30 March 2026) [data].
- **Nationals come first.** Bank Muscat describes its training as developing "the national cadre"; its two-year graduate development programme enrolled 57 of its own staff in 2023 (Times of Oman) [employer-stated]. Official Omanisation quotas are 90% in banking (Central Bank of Oman) and 20-50% in logistics (MTCIT); tech, sales and marketing roles are reserved for Omanis under Ministerial Decisions 235/2022 and 501/2024 (see visas_immigration/oman/oman_visas_immigration_guide.md).

## 2. The economy and the graduate labour market

**Size and structure.** 2025 real GDP OMR 39.30 billion; petroleum OMR 12.02 billion and non-oil OMR 28.70 billion, with manufacturing, logistics, tourism and services the growing non-oil activities (Oman Observer) [data]. The population was 5,359,557 at the end of 2025, 43.3% expatriates (NCSI, via Muscat Daily) [data].

**Youth and graduate unemployment; entry pay.** Not read.

**How hiring works.** Public and quasi-public employers recruit nationals; the private sector hires expatriates on sponsored permits, subject to Omanisation. Neither was verified from an official page.

**Language of work.** Arabic and English; not measured.

**Visa headline.** Citizens of 103 countries (including EU/EEA/Swiss and UK) can visit for up to 14 days without a visa; longer stays, work and study need a visa, and overstaying brings fines of 10 rials a day (ROP Decision 109/2026). Work visas require prior employer sponsorship and cannot be converted in-country (ROP directive 31 Oct 2023). Full details: visas_immigration/oman/oman_visas_immigration_guide.md.

## 3. Hubs

Populations are whole governorates (area: region) from the National Centre for Statistics and Information at 31 December 2025, as reported by Muscat Daily. No governorate GDP, pay or rent was found. Standing steps are [national, Middle East, world].

### Muscat

The capital and the Kingdom's largest governorate: 1,532,234 residents (936,383 expatriates, 595,851 Omanis). Banks: see §1. Bank Muscat's graduate programme is for its own staff and aimed at nationals.

**Standing.** Finance [5, 2, 1]: all of Oman's local banks are based here (national 5); not ranked in the GFCI (an associate centre), so regional 2 and world 1 [data].

### Sohar

An industrial port and free zone on the Batinah coast between Muscat and Dubai. The population figure is North Batinah governorate, 940,999; Sohar is a wilayat within it. Besides the port's statements above, it said in 2020 that it had attracted over OMR 10.4 billion (USD 27 billion) of investment since opening 16 years earlier (Times of Oman, 18 November 2020) [employer-stated].

**Standing.** Logistics [4, 3, 2]: a leading Omani port by the figures it publishes (national 4; no ranking of Omani ports was read); the freezone's third place in fDi Intelligence's index is a free-zone ranking, not a logistics-city one, so regional 3, world 2. The family is rated strong on three employer-stated claims, not on hiring.

## 5. Hypotheses tested

1. **"Sohar's investment boom creates European-graduate jobs."** Insufficient evidence: the figures are tonnes and investment commitments; no headcount or graduate hiring was read.
2. **"Muscat is a smaller Dubai for finance."** Not supported: Muscat is not ranked in the GFCI and no free-zone count was read.
3. **"Omanisation makes Oman closed to Europeans."** Partly supported: the sources read point to nationals-first hiring in banks, but no rule was read from an official page.

## 6. Common mistakes and myths

- Treating investment commitments (USD 968 million in 2025) as jobs [employer-stated].
- Reading "Muscat Securities Market" or local banks as proof of a graduate market: no graduate page open to non-Omanis was read.

## 7. Decision rules

1. If you want the Gulf as a first job, start with Dubai or Abu Dhabi; Oman has no graduate evidence for Europeans in this research.
2. If you consider logistics, ask the Sohar firm who sponsors the permit and whether the role counts toward Omanisation.
3. If an offer is from a bank, check whether the role sits under the high banking quota.
4. Check the FCDO page dated within the last month before travelling.
