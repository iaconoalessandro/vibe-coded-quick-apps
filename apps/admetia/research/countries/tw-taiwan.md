# Taiwan: country brief

Today is 3 October 2026 (Numbeo's Taipei rent was read on 4 October). Tags: `[data]` official statistics, `[employer-stated]`, `[practitioner consensus]` (published indices), `[anecdotal]` (crowd-sourced). New Taiwan dollars (NT$) unless stated.

## 1. Bottom line

- Taipei is the finance capital by head offices: 35 of Taiwan's 40 domestic banks and all 14 domestic property and casualty insurers have their head office there (Central Bank, lists updated 1 Oct 2026; counted by Admetia) [data]. Its pay is high but second to Hsinchu: NT$947,000 average annual total pay in 2024, NT$78,917 a month (DGBAS, 25 Nov 2025) [data].
- Hsinchu is the semiconductor and electronics hub: the Hsinchu Science Park Bureau oversees six parks with over 600 approved companies and more than 160,000 employees (HSPB) [data]. Hsinchu City pays most in Taiwan among the places read: NT$1,298,000 a year on average, NT$108,167 a month; the median is NT$902,000 (DGBAS) [data].
- Taichung is a manufacturing hub, with the Central Taiwan Science Park (219 manufacturers, 51,827 staff, sales over NT$936 billion in 2020) [data]. It pays least of the three: NT$673,000 a year, NT$56,083 a month, against NT$776,000 nationally [data].
- Entry pay is lower than city averages: new entrants earned NT$37,000 a month on average in 2024 (university graduates NT$34,000, graduate-school NT$52,000; median NT$33,000) (Ministry of Labor) [data].
- Taipei ranks 42nd in the Global Financial Centres Index 40 (September 2026), up nine places [practitioner consensus]; no ranking of Taiwanese start-up ecosystems could be read.
- The door for a European is unusually open after a Taiwanese degree: graduates whose residence is extended for job seeking may stay up to two years and work freely without a work permit (Workforce Development Agency, 4 Feb 2026) [data]. Afterwards a 70-point test rewards pay (up to 40 points) and Chinese ability (up to 30) [data].
- Language matters for the points test; whether employers expect Mandarin at entry was not researched.

## 2. The economy and the graduate labour market

**Size and structure.** Usual resident population on 1 January 2026: 23.71 million; Taichung 3.08 million, Taipei 2.44 million, Hsinchu County 0.675 million, Hsinchu City 0.509 million (DGBAS) [data]. No GDP by city or county was found; the statistics office does not appear to publish one, so the Atlas has no GDP for any hub.

**Pay.** Average annual total pay of full-time Taiwanese employees by workplace, 2024 (mean; median): Hsinchu City 1,298,000 (902,000); Hsinchu County 1,003,000 (736,000); Taipei City 947,000 (735,000); Taichung 673,000 (517,000); national 776,000 (585,000). The statistics office warns that the figures reflect each place's industry mix, such as finance, electronic components and computer and optical products [data]. New entrants (2024): NT$37,000 a month on average, up 6.4%; university graduates NT$34,000; graduate-school graduates NT$52,000; 91.2% of entrants hold a university degree or higher, led by manufacturing, wholesale and retail, and health care (Ministry of Labor) [data].

**Hiring and language.** Not researched, apart from the points test (Chinese ability up to 30 of 70 points).

**Visa headline.** British and Canadian citizens can visit 90 days, extendable once by 90 days; EU and Italian citizens can visit 90 days (strictly non-extendable). An online arrival card (TWAC) is due within three days before arrival. For professional employment, the standard white-collar threshold is NT$47,971 a month, while the Employment Gold Card (NT$160,000 a month or field criteria) provides an open 4-in-1 permit. Students need a work permit (max 20 hours a week in term). Graduates of Taiwanese universities can stay two years to look for work and work freely without a permit. The definitive, verified guide covering all 10 visa categories, fees, and requirements is in [visas_immigration/taiwan/taiwan_visas_immigration_guide.md](../../visas_immigration/taiwan/taiwan_visas_immigration_guide.md).

## 3. Hubs

Metrics in the Atlas: population is the city's usual resident population (1 January 2026, thousands); wage is 2024 average annual total pay by workplace city, divided by 12. No GDP (none found). Hsinchu County, which hosts much of the science park, is a separate unit and is not in the Hsinchu metrics.

### Taipei
- **What it is.** 2.44 million residents (down 24,000 on the year); NT$78,917 a month on average [data].
- **Employers.** The head offices of 35 of 40 domestic banks and all 14 domestic property and casualty insurers (Central Bank) [data]. No financial group page loaded with an address (Cathay, Fubon and CTBC could not be read).
- **Standing.** Finance 5-2-2: first in Taiwan by head offices; GFCI 42nd in the world and outside the Asia/Pacific top 15 read, so "minor" in both wider areas.
- **Rent.** NT$32,250 a month, one-bedroom in the centre (Numbeo, crowd-sourced, 4 Oct 2026) [anecdotal].

### Hsinchu
- **What it is.** 509,000 residents in Hsinchu City; NT$108,167 a month, the highest of the three; Hsinchu County adds 675,000 residents and NT$83,583 a month [data].
- **Employers.** Companies in the science parks: over 600, more than 160,000 staff (HSPB) [data]. TSMC's profile page did not state its head-office address and MediaTek's page did not load, so no firm is named.
- **Standing.** IT 4-2-2: ahead of Taichung's park on staff and firm counts (160,000 against 51,827), with no regional comparison read.
- **Rent.** Not available: Numbeo has no Hsinchu page.

### Taichung
- **What it is.** 3.08 million residents (the city includes rural districts); NT$56,083 a month, the lowest of the three [data].
- **Employers.** Manufacturers in the Central Taiwan Science Park (219 firms, 51,827 staff, 2020) [data].
- **Standing.** IT 3-2-1.
- **Rent.** NT$12,700 (Numbeo) [anecdotal], about 39% of Taipei's.

## 5. Hypotheses tested

1. **"Taipei pays the most because it is the capital and finance centre."** Not supported: Hsinchu City (NT$1,298,000) and Hsinchu County (NT$1,003,000) average more than Taipei (NT$947,000) [data].
2. **"Taiwan's graduate wages are close to the city averages."** Not supported: new entrants average NT$37,000 a month, under half of Hsinchu's NT$108,167 and 47% of Taipei's NT$78,917 [data].
3. **"A Taiwanese master's gives easy access to the labour market."** Partly supported: two years of permit-free work after graduation is unusually open, but the later points test depends on pay and Chinese [data].
4. **"Taichung is the cheap alternative."** Supported on cost and pay together: rent is about 39% of Taipei's and average pay is 71% of Taipei's, so the gap in pay is smaller than the gap in rent [anecdotal, data].

## 6. Common mistakes and myths

- Reading city average pay as entry pay: the 2024 entrant average is NT$37,000 a month.
- Treating Hsinchu City as the whole of the science park: Hsinchu County also hosts parts of it, and its pay is NT$83,583 a month.
- Assuming a student can work freely: students need a permit and are capped at 20 hours a week in term, with fines for working without one (WDA).
- Thinking the two-year post-graduation stay is open to non-Taiwanese degrees: it applies to graduates of Taiwanese institutions (WDA).

## 7. Decision rules

1. If you want banking or insurance, prefer Taipei, where 35 of 40 domestic banks are headquartered.
2. If you want semiconductors, electronics or hardware-adjacent business roles, look at Hsinchu first and Taichung second, and compare Hsinchu County too.
3. If you are budget-constrained and will earn the entrant average (NT$34,000 for a university graduate), Taichung's rent (about NT$12,700) is a third of Taipei's.
4. If you study in Taiwan, plan on the two-year job-seeking stay, then the 70-point test, in which Chinese ability can be up to 30 points.
5. If you are British, do not work on the 90-day visa-free entry.
