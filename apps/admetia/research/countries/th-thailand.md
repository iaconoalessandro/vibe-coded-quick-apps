# Thailand: where a European business or computing graduate should look

Tags: `[data]` official statistics; `[employer-stated]` an employer's own report; `[practitioner consensus]` a published ranking; `[anecdotal]` crowd-sourced. All pages read on 3 October 2026.

## 1. Bottom line

- **Thailand offers accredited university study and graduate work pathways.** The Non-ED Plus visa (effective 15 Jul 2024) exempts university students from re-entry permits, has institutional extensions, and grants a **1-year post-graduation stay to seek employment and convert to a Non-Immigrant B work visa in Thailand** (Royal Thai Embassy London, MFA) `[data]`; non-academic courses use the standard ED visa (90 days at a time). Full legal and administrative requirements are codified in `visas_immigration/thailand/thailand_visas_immigration_guide.md`.
- **Bangkok is the only hub with a finance market.** The Stock Exchange of Thailand showed a market capitalisation of THB 19.83 trillion on 2 October 2026 (SET) `[data]`; Kasikornbank reports 17,194 employees and 732 domestic branches at the end of 2025 (annex to its 56-1 report) `[employer-stated]`. The GFCI 40 ranks Bangkok 88th in the world, up 12 places (Z/Yen, Sep 2026) `[practitioner consensus]`.
- **Bangkok's economy is large**: THB 6,351,792 million in 2024 (provisional), THB 697,529 per person (NESDC) `[data]`.
- **Chonburi and the Eastern Economic Corridor are an industrial and data-centre story.** Chon Buri produced THB 1,234,303 million in 2024 (NESDC) `[data]`; on 6 May 2026 the Board of Investment approved data-centre projects in the corridor worth 46 billion baht (Chachoengsao) and 24.6 billion baht (Chonburi) `[data]`. Those are investment approvals, not graduate jobs.
- **No family is rated above "present"** and no pay figure is given; the Atlas says so rather than guess.
- **For a European, the practical order is Bangkok for finance or a corporate role at a multinational, and the corridor only for industrial or data-centre work.** Documented routes exist: corporate employment via Non-Immigrant B and Work Permit (minimum salary of 50,000 THB/month for European passports; 4:1 Thai employee ratio), BOI SMART Visa for tech roles, LTR for senior professionals, and DTV for remote work (foreign clients only). See `visas_immigration/thailand/thailand_visas_immigration_guide.md`.

## 2. The economy and the graduate labour market

**Size and structure.** The Atlas record's main sectors are tourism, automotive, agriculture and food, banking and financial services and electronics manufacturing. NESDC's 2024 provisional accounts put the Eastern region at THB 3,312,470 million and Bangkok and its vicinities at THB 8,830,323 million (Table, GPP 2024) `[data]`; the Bangkok Metropolis province alone produced THB 6,351,792 million.

**Entry pay and youth unemployment.** Not found. The labour-force survey's pay averages are by region, not by province; no graduate-pay survey was read.

**How hiring works.** Not researched.

**Language.** Not researched.

**Visa headline.** From 15 September 2026, EU (including Italian) and British citizens entering without a visa may stay up to 30 days, for tourism only and normally twice a calendar year by land; business travel needs a visa (Cabinet Resolution 19 May 2026, MFA Thailand, FCDO) `[data]`. Degree students at accredited universities use the **Non-ED Plus visa** (valid for the full degree duration without re-entry permits, plus 1 year post-graduation stay for employment search); non-academic courses use the standard ED visa (90 days at a time) (Royal Thai Embassy, London, MFA) `[data]`. Employment requires a Non-Immigrant B visa and Work Permit (minimum salary of 50,000 THB/month for Europeans) or BOI SMART/LTR channels. The Foreign Office advises against all but essential travel to Pattani, Yala and Narathiwat and four districts of southern Songkhla, and to areas within 20 km of the Cambodian border (FCDO) `[data]`. Full details in `visas_immigration/thailand/thailand_visas_immigration_guide.md`.

## 3. Hubs

Metric method: **population** and **GDP** are NESDC's provisional 2024 Gross Regional and Provincial Product table for the province (`region`): Bangkok Metropolis (population estimate 9,106 thousand) and Chon Buri (2,050 thousand). The population is NESDC's own estimate, used for GPP per capita, not the registered population. **Pay** and **rent** are left out.

### Bangkok

- **What it is.** The capital and home of the stock exchange, at 93 Ratchadaphisek Road, Din Daeng (SET website) `[employer-stated]`.
- **Finance.** SET market capitalisation THB 19.83 trillion (SET board) and THB 215.5 billion (mai board) on 2 October 2026 `[data]`; Kasikornbank's headcount `[employer-stated]`; GFCI 88th `[practitioner consensus]`.
- **Standing** (national / Asia-Pacific / world): finance 5 / 2 / 1 (the only Thai centre in the GFCI; no cross-city table lifts it in Asia-Pacific).
- **Rating:** finance present (unchanged; claims added).
- **Not read:** Bangkok Bank, Siam Commercial Bank, PTT (access refused or blocked), Kasikornbank's head-office address.

### Chonburi and the Eastern Economic Corridor

- **What it is.** The industrial corridor east of Bangkok, now attracting data centres: Bridge Data Centres in Chonburi (24.6 billion baht, 134 MW) and Skyline Data Center and Cloud Services in Chachoengsao (46 billion baht, 200 MW) (BOI press release 67/2569) `[data]`.
- **Standing:** IT 3 / 2 / 1, a judgement from the investment approvals. **No family rated.**

## 5. Hypotheses tested

**H1. "Thailand is easy to work in after a European master's."** Partly supported: while Thailand has no automatic job-search visa for holders of foreign degrees, European master's graduates can be hired via employer sponsorship under a Non-Immigrant B visa and Work Permit (legal minimum salary of 50,000 THB/month for Europeans) or fast-track BOI SMART/LTR visas. In contrast, local degree graduates in Thailand receive 1 year of post-study stay to search for jobs under the Non-ED Plus scheme `[data]`.

**H2. "The Eastern Economic Corridor creates graduate IT jobs."** Insufficient evidence: the source lists investment approvals, not hiring.

**H3. "Bangkok's size makes it a major finance centre."** Partly supported: the exchange is large (THB 19.83 trillion) but the GFCI ranks the city 88th `[data]`, `[practitioner consensus]`.

## 6. Common mistakes and myths

(a) Reading NESDC's 9.1 million as the registered population; (b) treating investment approvals as jobs; (c) assuming tourist-entry rules apply to study or work; (d) assuming the Hague Apostille Convention is already in force before 28 February 2027 (full consular legalization is required until then); (e) assuming Working Holiday visas exist for EU citizens (they do not).

## 7. Decision rules

1. If the target is a Thai finance job, prefer Bangkok and check the employer's own graduate page: no entry-hiring figure was read.
2. If you plan to study a degree in Thailand, enrol in an accredited university for a Non-ED Plus visa to benefit from re-entry exemption and 1 year of post-graduation job search.
3. If you rely on working in Thailand after graduating, ensure your employer meets the 4:1 Thai employee ratio and 2M THB capital requirement, or is BOI-promoted (One Bangkok TIESC service); European passport holders must be paid at least 50,000 THB/month.
4. Do not compare Thai provincial output with other countries' city output without noting the area (province).
