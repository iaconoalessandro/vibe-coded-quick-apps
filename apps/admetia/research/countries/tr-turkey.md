# Turkey: country brief

Today is 3 October 2026. Tags: `[data]` statistics and published indices, `[employer-stated]`, `[practitioner consensus]`, `[anecdotal]`. Routes in the Atlas are for EU/EEA/Swiss and UK passports only. **Read the travel advice**: the UK Foreign Office advises against all travel within 10 km of the border with Syria and reports Iranian strikes in the wider region since 8 July 2026 (FCDO, updated 21 September 2026, https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/turkey) [data]; the US State Department rates Türkiye Level 2 and warns of arbitrary detentions and exit bans (9 June 2026) [data].

## 1. Bottom line

- **Istanbul is where to look, for banking above all.** Of Turkey's 189,636 bank employees at the end of 2025, 83,582 (44%) worked in Istanbul and 18,621 in Ankara; the state banks Ziraat, Halkbank and Vakıfbank have head offices in the Istanbul Finance Centre and Akbank in Levent (Banks Association of Türkiye, May 2026) [data].
- **Istanbul is a national, not an international, financial centre.** The Global Financial Centres Index 40 ranks it 89th of 117 (101st in March), seventh of the eight Middle Eastern centres listed, behind Dubai (9th), Abu Dhabi, Riyadh, Doha, Kuwait City and Bahrain, and ahead of Tel Aviv (94th) (Z/Yen, September 2026) [data].
- **Istanbul has a real start-up ecosystem.** Startup Genome ranks it third among emerging ecosystems worldwide, after funding rose 423% in 2024 [data].
- **Industry and groups are in the Marmara and Aegean.** Koç Holding, headquartered in Istanbul, ranks 203rd on the Fortune Global 500 of 2026, with 120,219 staff [data]. Oyak Renault built 387,113 vehicles in Bursa in 2025 [employer-stated]. The Aegean Free Zone near Izmir exported $3.2 billion in 2025 and employs about 23,500 people [employer-stated].
- **Ankara is the capital and a defence centre.** Second for bank staff; Turkish Aerospace has its headquarters at Kahramankazan, with a research building at the METU Teknokent [employer-stated]. No graduate hiring evidence was read for it.
- **Work permits and residence.** Visas, work permits and residence rules are fully consolidated from primary sources in [visas_immigration/turkey/turkey_visas_immigration_guide.md](../../visas_immigration/turkey/turkey_visas_immigration_guide.md). Master's and PhD students can get a work permit from their first year; undergraduates only after the first year and only part-time (max 24 hours/week); Turkish university graduates can obtain a one-year job-search residence permit within six months of graduation (Law 6458, Art. 31/1-ı).
- **Pay, rent and graduate programmes were not read.** Compare offers locally; do not assume euro-level pay.

## 2. The economy and the graduate labour market

**Size and structure.** Population 86,092,168 at 31 December 2025, of which Istanbul 18.3% (TurkStat via BirGün) [data]. Economic output is concentrated: in 2024 Istanbul made 29.2% of GDP (TRY 13,010.7 billion), Ankara 10.5% (TRY 4,672.8 billion), Izmir 5.7% (TRY 2,562.8 billion), and the five largest provinces 53.0% (TurkStat via Capital) [data]. GDP per head was $24,452 in Istanbul, $24,031 in Ankara, $16,949 in Izmir and $15,014 in Bursa (TurkStat via Habertürk) [data]. The main sectors in the record are banking, manufacturing and automotive, tourism and construction.

**Youth and graduate unemployment; entry pay.** Not read on a dated, official page. News reports of the August labour-force release gave conflicting figures in the search results, so none is used.

**How hiring works.** Banks and groups recruit through their own graduate programmes; none was read. Students and recent graduates: internships through the university and Erasmus+ traineeships need no work permit (Study in Türkiye) [data]; other work needs a permit applied for by the employer.

**Language of work.** Turkish; English in multinationals, start-ups and the finance centre. Not measured.

**Visa headline.** British citizens can visit for up to 90 days in any 180; study and work need the right visa and permits (FCDO) [data]. A student residence permit does not include the right to work (Study in Türkiye) [data].

### Istanbul

Population 15,754,053 (province); GDP TRY 13,010.7 billion (2024); GDP per head $24,452.

**Sectors and employers.** Banking and capital markets (83,582 bank staff; the state banks' head offices at Ümraniye; Akbank at Levent); Koç Holding (headquarters; 120,219 staff; Fortune Global 500 203rd); a start-up ecosystem that Startup Genome ranks third among emerging ecosystems, fifth in Europe for affordable talent and top ten in Europe for funding in its 2024 edition [data].

**Universities.** Not researched beyond the record's student-permit sources.

**Standing.**
- **Finance [5, 2, 1].** National 5: 44% of the country's bank staff and the state banks' head offices [data]. Middle East 2: GFCI 89th, seventh of eight Middle Eastern centres. World 1. Rated dominant.
- **Software [5, 3, 2].** National 5: the leading ecosystem by Startup Genome's emerging-ecosystem ranking. Middle East 3: Tel Aviv is first in MENA (Startup Genome 2026), and Istanbul's European ranks put it among the larger secondary centres; no Middle East ranking was read for Istanbul. World 2: third among emerging ecosystems, not among the leaders. Rated strong on Startup Genome's published statistic, not on employer counts.

### Ankara

Population 5,910,320; GDP TRY 4,672.8 billion (2024); GDP per head $24,031.

The capital: 18,621 bank staff, second after Istanbul. Turkish Aerospace has its headquarters at Kahramankazan, a research building at the METU Teknokent and runs student-team and university-industry programmes [employer-stated]. Middle East Technical University, whose international office is a source for the permit rules, is here.

**Standing.** Finance [4, 2, 1]: second in Turkey for bank staff (rated strong on the count, not on named employers). Computing and defence are not rated: no source says Turkish Aerospace or ASELSAN hire graduates in a rated family here.

### Izmir

Population 4,504,185; GDP TRY 2,562.8 billion (2024); GDP per head $16,949.

The Aegean port city: 9,943 bank staff, third in Turkey. The Aegean Free Zone's operator says it exported $3.2 billion in 2025 (up 12%) on a trade volume of $6.332 billion, employed about 23,500 people and made 24% of Izmir's exports [employer-stated]. A 2012 magazine article on its companies was not used: it is stale.

**Standing.** Finance [3, 2, 1]: third in Turkey for bank staff. Logistics is not rated: no current port statistic was read.

### Bursa

Population 3,263,011; GDP per head $15,014, 13th of the 81 provinces. Total GDP not read.

Car-making city: Oyak Renault's factory built 387,113 vehicles in 2025, 336,336 of them passenger cars, and exported 271,994 (about 14% more than in 2024) [employer-stated]. Banks employ 4,714 people.

**Standing.** Finance [2, 1, 1] from the bank count only; no family is rated.

## 5. Hypotheses tested

1. **"Istanbul is the Middle East's financial centre."** Not supported: 89th in the GFCI, behind six Gulf centres [data].
2. **"Turkey's finance jobs are in Istanbul."** Supported: 44% of bank staff and the state banks' head offices [data].
3. **"Istanbul's start-up scene gives graduates jobs."** Partly supported: the ranking and funding growth are strong [data]; no headcount or graduate-hiring figure was read.
4. **"A European master's student can work while studying."** Partly supported: from the first year, but only through an employer's permit application [practitioner consensus].

## 6. Common mistakes and myths

- Treating a bank-staff count as a count of graduate vacancies.
- Reading Startup Genome's "emerging" ranking as a top-tier ranking: it compares emerging ecosystems.
- Assuming the Aegean Free Zone's 23,500 jobs are office or graduate jobs: the source does not say.
- Comparing Turkish lira pay with euro pay without an exchange rate and local prices: no pay series was read.

## 7. Decision rules

1. If you want banking, start in Istanbul (Levent, Ümraniye); then Ankara.
2. If you want start-ups, Istanbul; check the permit before accepting.
3. If you are a master's student, ask the employer to apply for the work permit from the first year; undergraduates wait until after year one.
4. If you want industry (cars), Bursa and the Marmara belt; no graduate scheme was read.
5. Read the FCDO and State Department pages the week you travel.
6. Do not rely on this brief for pay or rent: neither was read.
