# United Arab Emirates: country brief

Today is 3 October 2026. Tags: `[data]` official statistics and government pages, `[employer-stated]` (including free-zone authorities' own releases), `[practitioner consensus]` (published indices, recruiter guides, trade press), `[anecdotal]`. Routes in the Atlas are given for EU/EEA/Swiss and UK passports only. The UK Foreign Office reports strikes and retaliatory attacks by Iran in the region since 8 July 2026 and warns of flight cancellations and airspace closures, without advising against travel to the UAE as a whole (FCDO, updated 24 July 2026) [data]; read it before planning.

## 1. Bottom line

> **Single Source of Truth:** All United Arab Emirates immigration, visa, work permit, student logistics, and health insurance rules are centralized and verified in [`visas_immigration/uae/uae_visas_immigration_guide.md`](../../visas_immigration/uae/uae_visas_immigration_guide.md).

- **There are two markets, and Dubai is the larger finance one.** Dubai International Financial Centre counts 1,052 regulated firms and a workforce of 50,200 at the end of 2025 (DIFC Authority) [employer-stated]; Abu Dhabi Global Market counts 347 financial institutions, 171 asset and fund managers and 44,339 people (ADGM) [employer-stated]. The Global Financial Centres Index 40 (September 2026) ranks Dubai 9th of 117 and first in the Middle East and Africa, Abu Dhabi 13th and second [data]. Financial and insurance activities were 12% of Dubai's GDP in the first nine months of 2025 (Dubai Media Office) [data].
- **Dubai is also the technology and logistics centre; Abu Dhabi is the AI push.** Dubai Internet City has about 4,000 companies and more than 31,000 professionals, among them Google, Oracle, SAP, Amazon, IBM and Cisco (TECOM, February 2025) [employer-stated]. Startup Genome's July 2026 report ranks Dubai second in the Middle East and North Africa and Abu Dhabi fourth, with an AI-native ecosystem value of US$5.4 billion [data].
- **The best-known graduate programmes are for UAE nationals.** ADIA, Mubadala, Emirates NBD, FAB and ADNOC run graduate schemes for UAE nationals; non-nationals are mostly hired as experienced staff or transfers (employers' own pages, via places/gulf-and-central-eastern-europe.md) [employer-stated]. Emirates NBD's own careers page presents its graduate programmes inside its Emiratisation offer [employer-stated].
- **Private firms with 50 or more staff must add 2% Emiratis in skilled jobs each year; from 1 July 2026 each unfilled position costs AED 10,000 a month** (WAM quoting the labour ministry, 22 June 2026) [data]. This is the reason Europeans enter through open roles and transfers, not graduate intakes.
- **No income tax, but pay evidence is thin.** The UAE levies no personal income tax; VAT is 5% [data]. Michael Page's UAE Salary Guide 2026 says more than 60% of positions pay AED 10,000 to 40,000 a month [practitioner consensus]. No employer-stated entry pay for expatriate graduates was found.
- **Entry is easy; staying is employer-led.** EU citizens may visit for up to 90 days in any 180 (the agreement does not cover paid work); a jobseeker visit visa of 60, 90 or 120 days needs no sponsor, and graduates of a top-500 university qualify within two years of graduating [data]. A residence visa lapses after more than 180 days outside the UAE [data].

## 2. The economy and the graduate labour market

**Size.** The Emirate of Dubai had 4,248,200 residents at the end of 2024 and a GDP of AED 442.9 billion at constant prices (AED 540.7 billion at current prices) (Government of Dubai) [data]. The Emirate of Abu Dhabi had 4,135,985 residents in 2024, up 7.5%, and a real GDP of AED 1.2 trillion, of which non-oil activities were AED 644.3 billion, a record 54.7% (Statistics Centre Abu Dhabi) [data]. These are emirate figures, not city figures, and the GDP bases are not identical.

**Structure.** Dubai's economy produced about AED 355 billion in the first nine months of 2025, up 4.7%, with finance and insurance at AED 42.8 billion [data]. Jebel Ali handled 15.5 million TEU in 2024, its highest since 2015 (DP World) [employer-stated]. The Emirates Group is headquartered in Dubai (Fortune Global 500 rank 474, 130,919 employees worldwide) [data].

**Youth and graduate unemployment.** No UAE series for graduate unemployment was read.

**Entry pay by field.** None from an employer. Michael Page's guide puts most positions between AED 10,000 and AED 40,000 a month, which covers all seniority [practitioner consensus].

**How hiring works.** Graduate schemes at the biggest employers are for nationals; the expatriate route is experienced hires and intra-company transfers. Students may only work through an employer-held permit (part-time permit, or a three-month student training permit) [data]. The standard employment visa is applied for by the employer and valid for two years, renewable [data].

**Language of work.** English in finance, technology and logistics; no source read measured it.

**Visa headline.** A student visa sponsored by the university; no study-to-work route that does not need an employer is documented beyond the jobseeker visit visa and the Golden residence for graduates of a top-100 university with a bachelor's GPA of at least 3.5 within two years of graduating [data].

## 3. Hubs

Population and GDP are for the emirate (area: region), from the emirates' own statistics offices. No average-pay or rent figure from an official source was found for either emirate, so neither is shown. Standing steps are [national, Middle East, world].

### Dubai

**What it is.** 4,248,200 residents (emirate, end of 2024); GDP AED 442.9 billion at constant prices. DIFC: over 290 banks and capital-markets firms, 135 insurers, over 500 wealth and asset managers, and 1,677 AI, fintech and innovation companies, up 35% in 2025 [employer-stated]. Dubai Internet City generates 65% of Dubai's technology-sector output [employer-stated]. Named employers: DIFC firms, Google, Oracle, SAP, Amazon, IBM and Cisco at Internet City, DP World at Jebel Ali, the Emirates Group, Emirates NBD (30,000 employees). Startup Genome ranks Dubai second in the region (ecosystem value US$30 billion, US$1.14 billion of Series A) and 12th among emerging ecosystems worldwide [data; the global rank is reported by news sites].

**Standing.** Finance [5, 5, 3]: DIFC leads the UAE by workforce; first in the Middle East and Africa in the GFCI; 9th of 117 worldwide, which is a top-15 centre but not a top-five one. Software and IT [5, 4, 2]: Internet City's weight nationally; second in the Middle East and North Africa behind Tel Aviv (Startup Genome); among the emerging, not the top-40, ecosystems worldwide. Logistics [4, 3, 2]: DP World calls Jebel Ali the region's leading hub, a self-description, so the regional step is kept at 3.

### Abu Dhabi

**What it is.** 4,135,985 residents (emirate, 2024; the Abu Dhabi Region alone is about 2.8 million); real GDP about AED 1.2 trillion, 54.7% non-oil. ADGM: 347 financial institutions, 171 asset and fund managers, 44,339 people at the end of 2025, up more than half in a year [employer-stated]. Hub71: 390 start-ups that have raised more than US$2.7 billion, with programmes for AI, climate tech, digital assets and life sciences [employer-stated]. Startup Genome ranks Abu Dhabi fourth in the region, with a US$73 billion ecosystem value, and in the 41–50 band of emerging ecosystems [data]. ADIA, Mubadala and ADNOC run graduate programmes for UAE nationals.

**Standing.** Finance [4, 4, 3]: second in the UAE and in the region in the GFCI, and 13th in the world. AI [4, 4, 2]: fourth in the region, 41–50 among emerging ecosystems worldwide.

## 5. Hypotheses tested

1. **"Abu Dhabi is the finance centre because it has the sovereign funds."** Not supported for a graduate: ADGM's workforce (44,339) is 12% below DIFC's (50,200) and the GFCI ranks Abu Dhabi four places lower, and the sovereign funds' schemes are for UAE nationals. Holds for asset-owner roles reached as an experienced hire.
2. **"Graduate schemes are the way in."** Not supported for Europeans: the named schemes are for UAE nationals, and the 2% annual Emiratisation duty explains why.
3. **"The UAE's low tax means high net pay."** Partly supported: no income tax and VAT at 5%, but pay evidence is thin and Dubai's rent figure could not be sourced; Italians who do not register with AIRE are presumed to remain Italian tax residents (Italian foreign ministry, 2024, via places/gulf-and-central-eastern-europe.md) [data].
4. **"Dubai is as strong in technology as in finance."** Not supported at world scale: it is 9th of 117 in the GFCI but outside the Startup Genome top 40 (12th among emerging ecosystems). Holds regionally, where it is first in finance and second in start-ups. Internet City's 65% share of Dubai's technology output is the zone's own figure.

## 6. Common mistakes and myths

- **Applying to a UAE sovereign-fund graduate programme without checking nationality rules.** ADIA, Mubadala, Emirates NBD, FAB and ADNOC schemes are for UAE nationals.
- **Treating free-zone firm counts as jobs.** DIFC counts 1,052 firms and 50,200 workers; the firm count is not a vacancy count.
- **Believing a visit visa lets you work.** The EU–UAE waiver does not cover paid work.
- **Letting the residence visa lapse.** It is cancelled automatically after more than 180 days outside the UAE.

## 7. Decision rules

1. If you want banking, wealth or asset management and are not a UAE national, prefer Dubai (DIFC, 50,200 workers), reached as an experienced hire or transfer.
2. If you want AI or a start-up role, compare Abu Dhabi (Hub71, 390 start-ups) with Dubai (second in the region).
3. If you want a UAE graduate scheme, first check nationality: the best-known ones are for UAE nationals.
4. If you are an EU or UK citizen visiting to look for work, use the jobseeker visit visa (60, 90 or 120 days), not a tourist visit.
5. If you plan to stay, keep your residence visa alive (no more than 180 days outside).
6. If you are Italian, register with AIRE and keep proof that your life has moved.
