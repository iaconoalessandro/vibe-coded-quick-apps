# Hong Kong: country brief

Today is 3 October 2026. Tags: `[data]` official statistics, `[employer-stated]`, `[practitioner consensus]` (published indices), `[anecdotal]`.

## 1. Bottom line

- Hong Kong is a top-three financial centre: GFCI 40 (September 2026) ranks it third in the world (rating 756, one point behind London), first in Asia/Pacific and first globally for fintech (Z/Yen, Long Finance, 2026) [practitioner consensus].
- Finance made up 26.2% of GDP in 2024 and employed 265,000 people, 7.2% of all employment; banking alone made up 18.9% of GDP with 96,100 staff (Census and Statistics Department, Dec 2025) [data].
- The exchange is the visible engine: 2,686 listed companies, market capitalisation HK$47,392.5 billion, and IPO funds raised of HK$285.8 billion in 2025, more than three times the 2024 figure of HK$88.0 billion (HKEX Market Statistics 2025) [data].
- The permit is the easiest in Asia for a recent graduate: IANG gives 24 months with no job offer and no quota to graduates of a Hong Kong degree; the Top Talent Pass offers the same 24 months, subject to a quota, to graduates of top-100 universities (record claims, Immigration Department) [data].
- The catch is pay and retention. The median monthly wage of employees was HK$21,200 in May to June 2025 (HK$51,300 at the 90th percentile) (C&SD, Mar 2026) [data]; HKUST's taught-postgraduate survey found 65.5% of MSc and MA graduates had left or gone home, with employed graduates in banking and finance at a median of HK$30,000 [data].
- For a European master's student: Hong Kong suits finance (banking, markets, asset management, insurance) and air cargo logistics, ideally entered via a summer internship. It is a weak choice for a general business or computing graduate.

## 2. The economy and the graduate labour market

**Size and structure.** Four key industries produced 58.2% of GDP in 2024 (value added HK$1,811.9 billion of a GDP of HK$3,112.2 billion) and 42.3% of employment: financial services, trading and logistics, professional services and tourism (C&SD, Dec 2025) [data]. Trading and logistics employed 555,200 people, 15.0% of employment, 170,700 of them in logistics (same source) [data]. Population: 7,527,500 at mid-2025 (provisional; unchanged on 2024) (C&SD, 14 Aug 2025) [data].

**Pay.** Employee median monthly wage HK$21,200 (3.5% up on 2024); 25th percentile HK$15,300, 75th HK$33,000 (C&SD AEHS 2025) [data]. HKUST's school-reported median for employed MSc and MA graduates is HK$25,000 (banking and finance HK$30,000), but 21.8% of respondents were in full-time work and 65.5% had left or gone home (HKUST survey 2025 via places/beyond-europe.md §5.2) [data]. Analyst pay at investment banks is higher than these medians; no primary source for it was read.

**Language.** Whether front-office finance needs Mandarin or Cantonese was not verified from a primary source; MIT's finance report shows non-Chinese-speaking graduates do get Hong Kong offers (places/beyond-europe.md §5.2) [data, small sample].

**How hiring works.** Banks and insurers recruit summer interns and graduate trainees, and the library's recruiting calendar applies. Cathay Pacific lists a 36-month Cathay Cargo Graduate Trainee Programme and a Digital and IT track for Hong Kong and the Greater Bay Area (Cathay careers) [employer-stated].

**Visa headline.** Italian and EU citizens can visit for up to 90 days visa-free, British citizens for six months; neither can work or study on visitor status (Cap. 115 s. 41). Hong Kong no longer accepts BN(O) passports for immigration clearance (FCDO) [data]. See the single source of truth: `visas_immigration/hong_kong/hong_kong_visas_immigration_guide.md`.

## 3. Hub: Hong Kong

**What it is.** A territory of 7.5 million people with an economy built on finance, trade and logistics. Metrics are for the territory ("city" in the Atlas): population 7,527,500 (mid-2025), GDP HK$3,112.2 billion (2024), median monthly wage HK$21,200 (employees, May to June 2025). No rent figure: Numbeo returned HTTP 429 on every request and no official rent series for a one-bedroom flat was found.

**Employers.**
- Hong Kong Exchanges and Clearing: the exchange itself, with 2,686 listed companies (HKEX) [data].
- AIA: "the largest independent publicly listed pan-Asian life insurance group", present in 18 markets, total assets US$345 billion at 31 December 2025, listed in Hong Kong under code 1299 (AIA Annual Report 2025) [employer-stated].
- Cathay Group: graduate trainee programmes in cargo (20 freighters, 46 destinations), digital and IT, engineering and legal (Cathay careers) [employer-stated].
- Banks: 96,100 staff (C&SD). I could not read HSBC, Standard Chartered, Hang Seng Bank or BOCHK pages, so none is listed by name.

**Universities.** HKUST's MSc in Finance costs HK$460,000 in total for the 2027-28 intake (places/beyond-europe.md §5.2) [data]. HKUST, HKU and CUHK are the usual routes into IANG.

**Start-ups.** Startup Genome ranked Hong Kong 27th in 2025, the biggest rise in the top 40; it is not named in the 2026 key findings, so its current rank is unknown [practitioner consensus].

## 5. Hypotheses tested

1. **"A Hong Kong master's is the cheapest ticket to Asian finance."** Partly supported. The permit is the easiest in Asia (24 months, no quota, no job offer), but the HKUST survey shows most graduates leave and median pay for employed MSc and MA graduates is HK$25,000 [data].
2. **"Hong Kong is more dependent on finance than Singapore."** Supported on the shares read: finance 26.2% of GDP (C&SD) against about 200,000 finance jobs in Singapore (MAS); the two shares are on different bases (value added versus jobs), so this is a pointer only [data].
3. **"Hong Kong's rank has slipped against mainland centres."** Not supported by GFCI 40: Hong Kong stays third and Shanghai (5th), Shenzhen (8th) and Beijing (16th) rank below it [practitioner consensus]. Startup Genome shows Chinese ecosystems (Beijing 6th, Shanghai 11th, Shenzhen 19th) ahead of Hong Kong's 2025 rank of 27th.

## 6. Common mistakes and myths

- Treating HKUST medians as a finance salary; they mix every MSc and MA programme (places/beyond-europe.md §5.2).
- Assuming the Top Talent Pass is unlimited; the page read says it is subject to an annual quota, first come first served (Immigration Department).
- Assuming a BN(O) passport clears immigration in Hong Kong; it no longer does (FCDO).
- Counting the part-time work exemption as permanent; it is a trial measure still in effect "until further notice" (CUHK careers office, Feb 2026) [data].

## 7. Decision rules

1. If you want finance and can get a summer internship in Hong Kong, prefer a one-year Hong Kong master's, because IANG then gives 24 months without a sponsor (Immigration Department).
2. If you graduated from a top-100 university elsewhere, consider the Top Talent Pass instead, but apply early because of the quota.
3. If your target is a general business or marketing job, do not choose Hong Kong for the labour market: median pay is HK$21,200 and no source read shows entry demand outside finance and logistics.
4. If you hope to work in air cargo, look at Cathay's 36-month Cargo programme, not at generic logistics ads.
5. If you need front-office finance and speak neither Mandarin nor Cantonese, test the claim with the bank's recruiters; the language requirement is unverified.
6. If you hold a British passport and expect to arrive on a BN(O) document, do not: use your ordinary passport and the right permit.
