# Denmark: country brief

Today is 3 October 2026. Tags: `[data]` official statistics or government pages, `[employer-stated]`, `[practitioner consensus]` (published indices and specialist synthesis), `[anecdotal]`.

## 1. Bottom line

- Denmark has one dominant graduate market, Copenhagen. Eurostat counts 77,000 of the country's 123,000 information-and-communication jobs (about 63%) and 48,000 of its 75,000 finance and insurance jobs (about 64%) in the Copenhagen metropolitan region in 2022 (counts rounded to thousands), and the region produced €178.8 billion of Denmark's €380.6 billion GDP (47%) [data].
- Copenhagen is rated 5 nationally, 3 in Europe and 2 in the world for software, IT and finance. The finance step rests on the Global Financial Centres Index 40 (September 2026), which ranks Copenhagen 25th of 117, up 19 places, and seventh among European centres [practitioner consensus]. Stockholm, not Copenhagen, is the Nordic start-up leader: Startup Genome's 2026 Top 40 has Stockholm 23rd and calls it the standout European performer.
- Aarhus is a clear second (19,000 ICT and 9,000 finance jobs in 2022), with the headquarters of Vestas and Arla; Aalborg and Odense are third and fourth for ICT and are university and industry towns, not graduate-employer clusters [data, employer-stated].
- Pay is higher in the capital region than elsewhere but the gap is modest: in 2025 standardised monthly earnings averaged DKK 57,773 in Region Hovedstaden against DKK 52,846 nationally and DKK 48,209 in Region Nordjylland (Statistics Denmark, LONS30) [data]. These are all-employee averages, not graduate starting pay, for which no primary source was found.
- Graduate hiring is structured and calendar-driven: Maersk's 18-month management trainee programme takes applications in January and February and starts in July; Carlsberg's and PFA's 24-month programmes take applications in December and January and start in September; Novo Nordisk opens applications for a September 2027 start on 6 December 2026 [employer-stated].
- The first year is the hard part. Danish graduates with a long higher education who finished in 2024 spent on average 23.3% of their first twelve months unemployed; Eurostat has 83.8% of recent tertiary graduates aged 20-34 in work in 2025, below the EU's 85.3% [data]. Novo Nordisk announced 9,000 job cuts in September 2025, about 5,000 in Denmark [practitioner consensus].
- For a European master's student: Copenhagen for finance, consulting, pharma-adjacent business roles and IT; a named programme (Maersk, Carlsberg, Novo, Nordea, PFA, the Big Four) is the realistic door; do not expect to arrive without a job offer and find the first year easy. Tax is the heaviest in the Nordics (about 65% of €60,000 kept).

## 2. The economy and the graduate labour market

**Size and structure.** Eurostat's metropolitan table gives Denmark a GDP of €380.6 billion in 2022 and 3.17 million people in work (2022); the Copenhagen region has 36% of residents (2.11 million of 5.93 million in 2023) and 47% of output (Eurostat met_10r_3gdp, met_pjanaggr3) [data]. The main sectors are pharmaceuticals and life sciences (Novo Nordisk, Lundbeck, Ferring in Medicon Valley, which counts more than 580 life-science companies, 9 universities and 32 hospitals across Greater Copenhagen and southern Sweden) [data], shipping and logistics (Maersk), energy and green technology (Vestas), banking (Danske Bank, Nordea) and consumer goods (Carlsberg, Arla) [employer-stated]. ICT specialists were 5.7% of employment in 2025 (EU 5.0%), joint tenth of 33 European countries; Sweden led with 8.9% (Eurostat isoc_sks_itspt) [data].

**Graduates and youth.** 83.8% of Danish tertiary graduates aged 20-34 who had finished in the last three years were in work in 2025 (EU 85.3%); unemployment among 15-to-24-year-olds was 13.8% (EU 15.2%) (Eurostat edat_lfse_24, une_rt_a) [data]. Statistics Denmark data via the employers' federation show 53% of international master's graduates working in Denmark two years after graduating, up from about 40% before 2019; at CBS the figure was 46%, and Italians were 9% of those employed [data]. See places/iberia-and-nordics.md for the full treatment.

**Entry pay.** No primary source for graduate starting pay by field was found. The only regional figure is the all-employee average from Statistics Denmark (LONS30, 2025): DKK 52,846 nationally, 57,773 in Hovedstaden, 50,316 in Midtjylland, 49,835 in Syddanmark and 48,209 in Nordjylland per standardised month [data]. Tax: a graduate keeps about 65% of €60,000, the lowest share among the Nordic and Iberian countries compared; the 27% scheme for key employees needs DKK 65,400 a month [data, author calculation in the library].

**How hiring works.** Graduate programmes are the visible route and run on a calendar: Maersk Regional Management Trainee (18 months, January-February applications, July start), Carlsberg Danmark (24 months, December-January, September), PFA (24 months, December-January, September), Copenhagen Municipality IT (18 months, March-April), Banedanmark (18 months, applications opening in March) (Jobbank.dk listings posted by employers) [employer-stated]. Nordea's 1.5-year programme starts in September and took 2026 applications from 9 to 28 February [employer-stated]. Danske Bank's early-careers page offers student jobs mainly in Copenhagen, Aarhus and Linköping and full-time roles after graduation but names no formal programme [employer-stated]. Jobbank.dk lists many more employers, among them Jyske Bank, Nykredit, Pandora, Deloitte, PwC, EY and KPMG.

**Language and permits.** Novo Nordisk's graduate programme does not require Danish [employer-stated]. From 1 October 2026 new non-EU students get a job-seeking permit of one year, down from three (PhD graduates retain three years); the Pay Limit scheme needs at least DKK 552,000 a year in 2026 (Supplementary Pay Limit DKK 446,000) [data]. EU citizens apply for an EU residence document within three months and then a CPR number. Single source of truth: `visas_immigration/denmark/denmark_visas_immigration_guide.md`.

## 3. Hubs

### Copenhagen

**What it is.** The capital and the only large graduate market: the metropolitan region has 2,109,733 residents (2023), €178.8 billion of GDP (2022) (Eurostat) and region-level average standardised pay of DKK 57,773 a month (2025) (Statistics Denmark) [data]. Rent: see the comparison table.

**Sectors and named employers.** Danske Bank has its address at Bernstorffsgade 40 and more than 20,000 employees across four countries; Maersk is at Esplanaden 50 and has more than 100,000 staff worldwide; Carlsberg is at J.C. Jacobsens Gade 1 (37,000 staff); Lundbeck is at Ottiliavej 9, Valby; Novo Nordisk is at Novo Allé 1, Bagsværd, in Greater Copenhagen; Netcompany was founded in Copenhagen in 2000 and has more than 9,500 professionals in more than 10 countries; Nordea runs a graduate programme in four Nordic countries [employer-stated]. Group headcounts are global, not Danish.

**Universities.** The Technical University of Denmark is 105th in the QS World University Rankings 2027, second in Denmark and 40th in Europe [employer-stated, DTU's own news]. CBS (the calculator's Danish school) was not read in a ranking for this brief.

**Start-up ecosystem.** Startup Genome's Copenhagen page shows $1.6 billion of seed and Series A funding in 2023-2025 and $8 billion of exits in 2021-2025, against regional averages of $531 million and $4.3 billion; Stockholm shows the same early-stage funding but $34 billion of exits [practitioner consensus].

### Aarhus

**What it is.** Denmark's second city: 928,096 people in the metropolitan region (2023), €50.7 billion of GDP (2022), regional pay DKK 50,316 (Region Midtjylland, 2025) [data]. 19,000 ICT jobs and 9,000 finance jobs in 2022, second in the country on both [data].

**Employers.** Vestas Wind Systems (Hedeager 42, with a graduate programme), Arla Foods (head office Sønderhøj 14, Viby J) and Danske Bank (student jobs in Aarhus) [employer-stated].

**Standing.** IT 4 / 2 / 1 and finance 4 / 2 / 1: second in the country; small at European scale.

### Aalborg

**What it is.** North Jutland's centre: 594,634 people in the metropolitan region, €28.9 billion of GDP, regional pay DKK 48,209, the lowest of the four regions [data]. Third in Denmark for ICT (8,000 jobs, 2022). Aalborg University has 17,700 students and more than 3,700 staff [employer-stated]. Standing: IT 3 / 1 / 1.

### Odense

**What it is.** Funen's centre: 505,026 people in the metropolitan region, €22.9 billion of GDP, regional pay DKK 49,835 [data]. 6,000 ICT jobs, fourth in the country. Odense Robotics says there are more than 160 robotics, automation and drone companies with 3,600 employees on Funen [employer-stated]; the University of Southern Denmark is 283rd in QS 2027. No family is rated; IT 3 / 1 / 1 as the fourth city.

## 5. Hypotheses tested

1. **"Copenhagen is Denmark's only real graduate market."** Supported for finance, ICT and headquarters: 63-64% of the two sectors' jobs and 47% of GDP. Fails for energy and food, where Aarhus holds the Vestas and Arla headquarters, and for robotics (Odense). Evidence: Eurostat, employers' pages [data, employer-stated].
2. **"Pay in Copenhagen is far above the rest of the country."** Partly supported: the regional average is 9% above the national figure (DKK 57,773 against 52,846) and 20% above Nordjylland, but graduate pay is not measured and tax compresses the net gap [data].
3. **"A Danish master's gives a foreign graduate a job in Denmark."** Partly supported: 53% of international master's graduates were working in Denmark two years on, 46% at CBS [data]; the first year is slow for Danes too (23.3% of it unemployed) [data]. It holds for those who time applications to the programme calendar; it fails for those who arrive without a Danish-speaking network.
4. **"Copenhagen is a Nordic start-up capital."** Not supported at this evidence level: Stockholm is 23rd in Startup Genome's Top 40 and is the stand-out; Copenhagen's early-stage funding is level with Stockholm's but its exits are a quarter of the size [practitioner consensus].

## 6. Common mistakes and myths

- Reading a group headcount (Maersk 100,000, Carlsberg 37,000, Danske 20,000) as Danish jobs; they are global figures.
- Treating Medicon Valley as a Danish figure: it counts companies across Greater Copenhagen and southern Sweden [data].
- Assuming Novo Nordisk is still hiring freely: 9,000 job cuts and a hiring freeze on non-critical roles were announced in September 2025 [practitioner consensus].
- Missing the programme calendar: the main graduate intakes close between December and April.
- Quoting the three-year job-seeking window: for permits applied for from 1 October 2026 it is one year [data].

## 7. Decision rules

1. If you want finance, consulting-adjacent or ICT work and speak no Danish, prefer Copenhagen: it holds about two-thirds of both sectors' jobs.
2. If the target is wind energy, food or an industrial group, look at Aarhus (Vestas, Arla) before assuming Copenhagen.
3. If you need a visa, treat the one-year search window as the clock and apply to calendar programmes (December to April) while still studying.
4. If you want the largest graduate-programme list, use Jobbank.dk and the employers' own pages, and note each programme's duration and start date before applying.
5. If net pay matters, compare after tax: a graduate keeps about 65% of €60,000, so a higher gross in Stockholm or Oslo is not automatically better.
6. If you are choosing between Copenhagen and Stockholm for start-ups, prefer Stockholm; for banking, pharma-adjacent business and shipping, Copenhagen.
7. If a role is in Aalborg or Odense, treat it as an employer-specific move (a named company or the university), not a market.
