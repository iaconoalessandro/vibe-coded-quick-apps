# Spain (Atlas brief, round 5, 3 October 2026)

Tags: [data], [employer-stated], [practitioner consensus], [anecdotal]. The record is `data/atlas/es.js`; the quotes behind every claim are in `research/verification/round-5b.md` (P39).

## 1. Bottom line

- **Madrid is the place for a business or finance graduate.** The Comunidad de Madrid has 171,900 people in finance and insurance and 259,400 in information and communication (Eurostat Labour Force Survey, 2025): 37% and 31% of Spain's totals and, after Île-de-France, the second-largest figures of the 255 and 257 European regions with data (the UK is not covered) [data]. It holds the Banco de España, the stock-exchange operator and the headquarters of Telefónica, Repsol, Endesa and Amadeus [data, Global LEI Index].
- **Barcelona is the technology and start-up city.** Cataluña has 192,300 people in information and communication, 23% of Spain's and third among 257 European regions [data]; Catalonia counted a record 2,403 start-ups in 2025 [data]; StartupBlink ranks Barcelona 33rd in the world, ahead of Madrid at 51st [practitioner consensus, via ACCIÓ].
- **The two cities are not equals in finance.** Madrid ranks 43rd (13th in Western Europe) in the Global Financial Centres Index 40; Barcelona is not ranked at all [practitioner consensus]. Cataluña has 75,300 finance and insurance jobs against Madrid's 171,900 [data].
- **Pay is modest and regional.** Mean gross annual earnings per worker in 2024 were €34,410 in the Comunidad de Madrid, €31,730 in Cataluña, €26,817 in the Comunitat Valenciana and €26,090 in Andalucía, against €29,540 for Spain (INE) [data]. The library's estimate is that a master's graduate earning about €26,600 keeps about €410 a month after a central one-bedroom in Madrid [practitioner consensus].
- **The graduate market is average, the headline unemployment is not.** In 2025, 84.9% of tertiary graduates aged 20 to 34 who left education up to three years earlier were in work, against 85.3% in the EU; but the unemployment rate was 10.0% in August 2026 (22.7% for under-25s) against 6.1% (15.4%) in the EU [data].
- **Outside the two big cities, choose by employer.** Bilbao has the BBVA and Kutxabank headquarters and Iberdrola's tower; Valencia has the port authority, CaixaBank's registered office and Mercadona; Seville, Málaga and Zaragoza are one- or two-employer markets in the sources read [data].
- **Spanish is the working language** for almost every graduate job: 5.8% of Spanish job postings waive it (Indeed Hiring Lab, 2024, via the library) [data]. The exceptions are international hubs, investment banking, trading and international consulting.

## 2. The economy and the graduate labour market

**Size and structure.** Spain's metropolitan regions produce most of its output: Eurostat puts the Madrid metropolitan region at €237.5 billion (19% of Spain's €1,222 billion in 2021) and Barcelona at €173.7 billion (14%) [data]. Employment is spread more evenly than output: the Eurostat Labour Force Survey counts 22.2 million people in work in 2025, of whom 839,600 in information and communication and 468,200 in finance and insurance [data]. The two sectors are far more concentrated than total employment: Madrid has 16% of all Spanish jobs but 31% of those in information and communication and 37% of those in finance and insurance (arithmetic on 3,545,500 / 22,202,900) [data].

**Graduate market.** Spain's recent-graduate employment rate is close to the EU's (84.9% against 85.3%, 2025) and well above Italy's (75.8%) or Greece's (64.0%) [data]. Entry pay is low against big-city rents outside consulting, banking and tech (the library's `es-pay` claim): about €26,600 in the first year for an average master's graduate, but about €45,000 in consulting or tech, which leaves about €1,385 a month after a central Madrid one-bedroom [practitioner consensus, library].

**How hiring works.** The large graduate programmes are run from Madrid. Santander's headquarters programme starts each September in Madrid, with applications for 2027 opening in March 2027 [employer-stated, library]. Banks keep their operational centres in Madrid, which also hosts the Bank of Spain and the stock exchange [data, Comunidad de Madrid, page last updated 2019].

**Visa and permit headline.** EU citizens register within three months. Non-EU students may work up to 30 hours a week, and a graduate can switch from a study stay to a residence-and-work permit without a new visa (RD 1155/2024). The Beckham regime (24% flat up to €600,000) is worse than normal taxation at €30,000 to €45,000 and only level at €60,000 [practitioner consensus, library]. See the single source of truth in `visas_immigration/spain/spain_visas_immigration_guide.md` and `places/iberia-and-nordics.md`.

## 3. Hubs

### Madrid

**What it is.** Spain's capital and corporate centre. Eurostat's metropolitan region has 6.87 million residents (1 January 2023) and €237.5 billion of GDP (2021) [data]. Mean gross annual earnings in the Comunidad de Madrid were €34,410 in 2024 (about €2,868 a month, INE) [data].

**Sectors and employers.** Banking, telecoms, energy, consulting and the public sector. Headquarters from the Global LEI Index: Telefónica (Gran Vía 28), Repsol (Calle Méndez Álvaro 44), Amadeus (Salvador de Madariaga 1), Endesa (Calle Ribera del Loira 60), the Banco de España (Calle de Alcalá 48) and Bolsas y Mercados Españoles (Plaza de la Lealtad 1) [data]. The Comunidad de Madrid says 65% of Spanish multinationals have their headquarters there (page last updated 2019) [data]. Santander, BBVA and Bankinter run operational centres [data]. Banco Santander's legal headquarters is in Santander (Cantabria), but its graduate programme starts in Madrid [data / employer-stated].

**Universities and business schools.** IE Business School (Master in Management, Master in Digital Marketing, MBA) is in Madrid; the calculator lists these programmes.

**Start-ups.** Startup Genome's GSER 2026 ranks Madrid third among the world's emerging ecosystems and ninth in Europe, with top-ten European placings for performance and talent strength [practitioner consensus]; StartupBlink puts it 51st in the world, second in Spain after Barcelona [practitioner consensus, via ACCIÓ].

**Standing (national / Europe / world).**
- *Finance 5 / 3 / 2.* National 5: 37% of Spanish finance and insurance jobs and the central bank, the stock exchange and bank headquarters. Europe 3: the second-largest regional job count after Île-de-France is large, but the GFCI 40 ranks Madrid 43rd and 13th in Western Europe (so a top-fifteen centre, not a top-five one). World 2.
- *IT 5 / 3 / 2 and software 5 / 3 / 2.* National 5 on 31% of information and communication jobs. Europe 3: the second-largest regional count, but the ecosystem rankings place Madrid ninth in Europe and 51st in the world (StartupBlink). World 2.
- *Business 5 / 3 / 2.* The regional-headquarters share and the corporate headquarters above.
- Same steps as Milan in the Italy brief, deliberately: both have a top-three European job count and a mid-table financial-centre rank.

**Cost of living.** No rent source could be read; see section 6.

### Barcelona

**What it is.** Spain's second city and technology centre: 5.80 million residents in the metropolitan region (2023), €173.7 billion of GDP (2021) [data]; Cataluña's mean gross annual earnings were €31,730 in 2024 (about €2,644 a month) [data].

**Sectors and employers.** Start-ups, software, health tech and tourism. Glovo has its headquarters on Calle Llull, SEAT at Martorell and Banco Sabadell at Sabadell, in the Barcelona area [data, Global LEI Index]. ACCIÓ counted 2,403 start-ups employing more than 30,500 people and raising €1.13 billion in 2025 [data]. The Eurostat survey counts 192,300 people in information and communication in Cataluña in 2025 [data].

**Universities and business schools.** Esade and IESE (Masters in Management and Finance, Marketing, MBAs) are in Barcelona; the calculator lists these programmes.

**Start-ups.** GSER 2026 puts Ecosystem Value at $16 billion, above the European average of $14.3 billion [practitioner consensus]; StartupBlink ranks Barcelona 33rd in the world and fifth in the EU in 2025 (ACCIÓ, February 2026) [practitioner consensus].

**Standing.** *Software 4 / 3 / 2 and IT 4 / 3 / 2.* National 4: the second-largest information and communication count in Spain and the best start-up ranking, but behind Madrid in size. Europe 3: third among 257 European regions on the Eurostat count and fifth in the EU in StartupBlink's ranking, but no source compares cities across all of Europe for software. World 2.

Valencia, Seville, Málaga, Bilbao and Zaragoza were added on 3 October 2026 from Eurostat's metropolitan tables; this round added regional sector counts, headquarters and pay.
### Valencia

2.66 million residents, €61.4 billion of GDP, 1.04 million jobs (2021) [data]. The Comunitat Valenciana has 68,500 jobs in information and communication and 35,600 in finance (2025) [data]. The Valencia Port Authority and Mercadona have their headquarters in the area, and CaixaBank gives Valencia as its registered headquarters [data, Global LEI Index]. GSER 2026 puts Valencia in the 61st-70th emerging range, up 22 places [practitioner consensus]. Rated *present* in logistics, finance and business; standing logistics 3 / 2 / 1.

### Seville

1.96 million residents, €40.7 billion of GDP [data]. Abengoa (Campus Palmas Altas) and Heineken España (Avenida de Andalucía) have their headquarters there [data]. Andalucía has 95,800 jobs in information and communication and 56,800 in finance (2025), shared with Málaga. Rated *present* in business; standing business 3 / 2 / 1.

### Málaga

1.75 million residents in the Málaga-Marbella region, €30.8 billion of GDP [data]. Unicaja Banco has its headquarters on Avenida de Andalucía [data]. Rated *present* in finance; standing finance 3 / 2 / 1.

### Bilbao

1.15 million residents, €35.4 billion of GDP [data]. BBVA (registered headquarters, Plaza de San Nicolás), Kutxabank (Gran Vía) and Iberdrola (Torre Iberdrola) are there [data]. The País Vasco's mean earnings, €35,170 a year or about €2,931 a month (INE, 2024), are above Madrid's among the six regions read [data]. GSER puts Ecosystem Value at $1 billion [practitioner consensus]. Rated *strong* in finance on two bank headquarters; standing finance 3 / 2 / 1.

### Zaragoza

0.98 million residents, €27.9 billion of GDP [data]. Pikolin and Saica Pack have headquarters in the city [data]. Aragón has 17,600 jobs in information and communication and 10,500 in finance [data]. Rated *present* in business; standing business 3 / 2 / 1. The record's "logistics centre" description is not rated: no logistics source was read.

## 5. Hypotheses tested

1. **"Barcelona is Spain's second finance market."** Not supported. Cataluña has 75,300 finance and insurance jobs against Madrid's 171,900, and Barcelona is not in the GFCI 40 at all [data / practitioner consensus]. Its strength is technology (192,300 jobs, third in Europe by region).
2. **"Madrid is Europe's second financial centre."** Not supported. It has the second-largest regional finance job count after Île-de-France in a data set that excludes the UK, but the GFCI 40 ranks it 43rd in the world (13th in Western Europe), and a job count includes a great deal of retail banking and insurance administration [data / practitioner consensus].
3. **"Pay is higher in the Basque Country than in Madrid."** Supported on the INE regional mean (€35,170 against €34,410), but the regional mean is not an entry salary and Bilbao's job market is far smaller (13,400 finance jobs in the whole País Vasco) [data].
4. **"The smaller hubs are not worth considering."** Partly supported: only one source per hub could be read, but each has a named headquarters (Valencia Port Authority, Unicaja, BBVA, Iberdrola, Pikolin). They are employer-led markets rather than sector hubs [data].

## 6. Common mistakes and myths

- **"Banco Santander is a Madrid bank."** Its legal headquarters is in Santander; the group's graduate programme starts in Madrid. BBVA's and CaixaBank's registered offices are in Bilbao and Valencia respectively, while their large operational sites are elsewhere [data].
- **"Spain pays like Italy."** The INE national mean (€29,540) is about €2,460 a month before the regional split; Italian pay figures in the Italy brief are on a different basis (MEF declarants), so the two are not directly comparable [data].
- **"Spanish is optional in tech."** Only 5.8% of postings waive it, and the exceptions are international hubs, investment banking, trading and international consulting [data, library].
- **"The 2025 regional job counts are exact."** They are survey estimates for whole autonomous communities, not city counts [data].

## 7. Decision rules

1. If you want a large graduate programme at a bank or corporate headquarters, start in Madrid (Santander's programme starts there each September).
2. If your target is software or a start-up, prefer Barcelona; if you also want financial-sector work in the same city, Madrid is better.
3. If your Spanish is below professional level, restrict yourself to investment banking, trading, international consulting and tech roles (5.8% of postings waive Spanish).
4. If you want the highest regional mean pay, look at the País Vasco and Madrid, but compare entry pay after rent (about €410 a month after a central Madrid one-bedroom on €26,600).
5. If you are a non-EU student, you can work 30 hours a week and switch to a work permit without a new visa (RD 1155/2024; check the record's route).
6. If you expect to claim the Beckham regime, check the five years without Spanish residence: a one-year master's in Spain can break it.
7. If you consider Valencia, Seville, Málaga, Bilbao or Zaragoza, name the employer first (Valencia Port Authority, Unicaja, BBVA, Kutxabank, Iberdrola, Pikolin) and treat the city as a one-employer market.

## 8. Gaps and claims to verify

1. No rent could be read for any Spanish hub (Numbeo refused automated access; no official one-bedroom rent by city was found). A Ministry of Housing reference-rent series (SERPAVI) exists and was not read.
2. Sector counts are for autonomous communities, not cities; Málaga's and Seville's are Andalucía's.
3. The Madrid headquarters share comes from a regional page last updated in 2019.
4. Employer headquarters are the Global LEI Index headquarters addresses, not headcounts. For BBVA and CaixaBank these are registered offices.
5. The Kutxabank description ("Basque savings-bank group") is general knowledge, not read on the record.
6. StartupBlink's ranking (33rd Barcelona, 51st Madrid) is quoted from ACCIÓ's report, not read at StartupBlink.
7. Recent-graduate employment rates are Eurostat survey estimates (national only).
