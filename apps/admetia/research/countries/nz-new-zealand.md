# New Zealand: where a European business or computing graduate should look

Tags: `[data]` official statistics; `[employer-stated]` an employer's own report; `[practitioner consensus]` a published ranking; `[anecdotal]` crowd-sourced. All pages read on 3 October 2026.

## 1. Bottom line

- **Auckland is the only hub with a big private-sector graduate market.** It holds 37.5% of New Zealand's GDP (NZ$161.8 billion of NZ$431.5 billion, year to March 2025; Stats NZ) `[data]`; financial and insurance services employed 42,200 people there in 2024 (Auckland Economic Monitor) `[data]`, and ANZ's New Zealand holding company has its principal place of business there (financial statements, 30 Sep 2025) `[employer-stated]`.
- **Wellington is the public-sector and software city.** 42.6% of the Public Service workforce is in the Wellington region, against 21.3% in Auckland (Public Service Commission, 30 June 2025) `[data]`; Xero's registered address is in Te Aro (annual report 2026) `[employer-stated]`. Startup Genome gives Wellington no rank and a smaller ecosystem than the Oceania average (GSER 2026) `[practitioner consensus]`.
- **Christchurch is a manufacturing and technology city**, with a tech sector of NZ$2.4 billion GDP and over 15,000 jobs, and Tait Communications and Seequent named as local global businesses (ChristchurchNZ, own page) `[data]`.
- **The permit is generous, the market is small.** A level 7 or higher degree studied full-time for 30 weeks or more in New Zealand can lead to a Post-Study Work Visa of up to three years (Immigration New Zealand) `[data]`. No source read gives graduate outcomes or entry pay for international master's graduates.
- **Start-up signal:** Startup Genome put Auckland in the emerging-ecosystems 31–40 range in 2026, the biggest climb in that top 40, by more than 75 places `[practitioner consensus]`.
- **Order of search for a European:** Auckland first for finance and tech; Wellington for public administration, policy and software; Christchurch only for technology with a manufacturing flavour.

## 2. The economy and the graduate labour market

**Size and structure.** New Zealand's GDP was NZ$431,457 million at current prices in the year to March 2025 (provisional); Auckland produced 37.5%, Canterbury 12.9% and Wellington 11.9% (Stats NZ, Regional GDP, Table 5) `[data]`. The population was 5,308,100 in the year to June 2025 (same table). The record's main sectors are banking and insurance, technology, tourism, agriculture and food, and the public sector. Auckland's city centre produces 37% of the country's financial-and-insurance GDP and 28% of its information-media and telecoms GDP, and nearly 40 of the top 200 tech firms are in or near it (Auckland Economic Monitor, July 2025) `[data]`.

**Entry pay.** No entry-pay or regional earnings figure was read. Stats NZ publishes regional earnings (linked employer-employee data) only through its data explorer, which could not be read; the Atlas therefore has no pay metric for New Zealand. Income tax runs from 10.5% to 39% over NZ$180,000 (Inland Revenue, rates from 1 April 2025) `[data]`.

**How hiring works.** Not read: no employer's graduate calendar was found. Ask each employer directly.

**Language.** English (no source read; no claim in the record).

**Visa headline.** A Fee Paying Student Visa costs from NZ$850, lasts up to four years and needs NZ$20,000 for each year of study; students may work 25 hours a week in term (Immigration New Zealand) `[data]`. British citizens can visit for up to six months with an NZeTA (FCDO, 19 Mar 2026) `[data]`. For the sovereign single source of truth covering all New Zealand visa routes (AEWV, SMC 6 points, Student, PSWV, Working Holiday, costs and legal requirements), see [`visas_immigration/new_zealand/new_zealand_visas_immigration_guide.md`](../../visas_immigration/new_zealand/new_zealand_visas_immigration_guide.md).

## 3. Hubs

Metric method: **population** is the Stats NZ estimated resident population of the territorial authority (Auckland, Wellington city, Christchurch city; `city`), provisional, 30 June 2025. **GDP** is regional GDP at current prices for the year to March 2025 (`region`: Auckland, Wellington and Canterbury). **Pay** and **rent** are left out: earnings by region could not be read, Numbeo returned HTTP 429, and the official rental-bond data are by area unit, not one-bedroom flats by city.

### Auckland

- **What it is.** 1,816,000 residents (the territorial authority; the region has the same figure) `[data]`; GDP NZ$161.8 billion.
- **Finance.** 42,200 jobs in financial and insurance services (2024, up from 40,900) `[data]`. ANZ New Zealand has its registered office and principal place of business at ANZ Centre, 23–29 Albert Street `[employer-stated]`.
- **Tech.** Nearly 40 of the top 200 tech firms are in or near the centre `[data]`; GSER 2026 emerging 31–40 `[practitioner consensus]`. Fisher & Paykel Healthcare gives an Auckland address and says it employs over 7,500 people worldwide, over 950 in research and development `[employer-stated]`.
- **Standing** (national / Asia-Pacific / world): finance 5 / 2 / 1; IT 5 / 2 / 1. Auckland is first in New Zealand on jobs and output; no cross-city table puts it above second tier in Asia-Pacific.
- **Ratings:** finance dominant (unchanged, with the ANZ claim added); IT strong (unchanged); banking present (new).

### Wellington

- **What it is.** 210,800 residents in the city (the region has 543,400) `[data]`; regional GDP NZ$51.3 billion. A regional report lists public administration, professional services and finance among its five largest industries (2020) `[data]`.
- **Public sector.** 42.6% of the Public Service workforce (2025) `[data]`.
- **Software.** Xero's registered address `[employer-stated]`; GSER gives Wellington an ecosystem value of $2 billion against an Oceania average of $5.6 billion `[practitioner consensus]`.
- **Standing:** management (public administration) 5 / 2 / 1; software 4 / 2 / 1; finance 3 / 2 / 1.
- **Ratings:** IT and software not rated → present (one named employer with its registered address in the city). Finance not rated: Kiwibank and the Reserve Bank were not read.

### Christchurch

- **What it is.** 419,200 residents in the city (Canterbury region 698,200) `[data]`; regional GDP NZ$55.5 billion; Canterbury is the second-largest manufacturing region, 14% of manufacturing GDP, with 40,000 manufacturing jobs `[data]`.
- **Tech.** NZ$2.4 billion GDP and over 15,000 jobs in tech; Tait Communications and Seequent named `[data]`.
- **Standing:** IT 4 / 2 / 1. **Rating:** IT present.

## 5. Hypotheses tested

**H1. "New Zealand is Australia with a friendlier post-study visa."** Partly supported. The visa can reach three years `[data]`, but the labour market is small: the three hubs' regional GDP adds up to NZ$268.6 billion, a third of New South Wales alone (A$855 billion, a different currency) `[data]`. No NZ graduate-outcome figure comparable with Australia's QILT was found.

**H2. "Wellington is the better city for a business graduate because it is the capital."** Not supported for private-sector hiring: its finance evidence is a regional list of industries from 2020, and the strongest employer evidence is Xero and the public service. It holds for public-administration and policy careers `[data]`.

**H3. "Auckland's start-up scene is the country's tech engine."** Partly supported: nearly 40 of the top 200 tech firms are in or near the centre and GSER shows a fast climb, but the ranking is in the emerging list (31–40) `[data]`, `[practitioner consensus]`.

## 6. Common mistakes and myths

(a) Reading the Auckland figures as "the city centre" when GDP is regional; (b) assuming the Post-Study Work Visa rules without checking NZQF levels: under INZ Operational Manual WD3.5, a Level 9 Master's studied full-time for 30+ weeks grants a fixed 3-year visa, while Level 7 Bachelor and Level 8 PGDip match course duration; (c) treating Wellington's 2020 industry list as current.

## 7. Decision rules

1. If the target is banking, insurance or consulting, prefer Auckland: 42,200 finance jobs and ANZ's New Zealand home.
2. If the target is policy or public administration, prefer Wellington: 42.6% of the Public Service.
3. If the target is SaaS or accounting software, look at Wellington (Xero) and Auckland.
4. If the target is hardware, communications or geoscience software, look at Christchurch (Tait Communications, Seequent).
5. Apply for the Post-Study Work Visa within three months of the student visa ending (Immigration New Zealand).
6. Do not compare New Zealand and Australian figures without converting currency (NZ$ and A$).
