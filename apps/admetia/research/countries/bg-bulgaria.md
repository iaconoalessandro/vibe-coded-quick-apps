# Bulgaria: country brief

Today is 3 October 2026. Tags: `[data]` official statistics or government pages, `[employer-stated]`, `[practitioner consensus]` (published indices, trade articles and trade-body reports), `[anecdotal]`.

## 1. Bottom line

- Bulgaria's graduate market is Sofia. In 2021 the Sofia metropolitan region held 104,550 information-and-communication jobs (84% of the country's 123,930) and 52,080 finance-and-insurance jobs (77% of 67,550) (Eurostat) [data]. Among the 25 European capital regions that report these figures, Sofia is eighth for ICT and tenth for finance.
- Plovdiv (5,350 ICT and 2,320 finance jobs) and Varna (4,840 and 2,830) follow at a great distance: Plovdiv is second for ICT jobs and Varna third, Varna is second for finance jobs and Plovdiv third, each an order of magnitude smaller than Sofia (Eurostat, 2021) [data]. No named employer was read for either city.
- The outsourcing sector is national: 833 companies (412 in IT, 388 in business processes, 33 in R&D) and 105,436 full-time employees in 2024, with employment up only 0.2% and revenue up 7.6% (AIBEST Annual Industry Report 2025) [data]. The sector's split by city was behind a registration form and was not read.
- Banking in Sofia: UniCredit Bulbank (registered address on Sveta Nedelya Square; 3,038 employees at the end of 2025) and First Investment Bank (head office on Tsarigradsko shose) [employer-stated].
- Pay: the 2024 average annual wage was BGN 38,728 in Sofia, 26,007 in Varna and 23,473 in Plovdiv, against 27,898 for the country (National Statistical Institute) [data]. A junior specialist in a service centre earned a mean base of €1,517 a month in 2024, the lowest of the five countries compared (ABSL citing Mercer, via the library) [data].
- Standing: Sofia is 5 nationally in IT, software and finance; 3 in Europe for IT, 2 for software and finance, and 1 in the world. The Global Financial Centres Index 40 ranks Sofia 115th of 117 and last in Eastern Europe and Central Asia [data, practitioner consensus].
- For a European master's student, Bulgaria is a market for IT, service-centre and bank jobs in Sofia on lower pay, with graduate employment above the EU average (90.0% against 85.3%); outside Sofia the evidence is only job counts.

## 2. The economy and the graduate labour market

**Size.** 6.44 million people in 2025 and GDP of €116.0 billion in 2025 (Eurostat nama_10_gdp and demo_pjan) [data]. Unemployment was 3.5% in 2025, below the EU's 6.0% (Eurostat une_rt_a) [data]. Bulgaria's wages are quoted in leva until the end of 2025; the euro replaced the lev on 1 January 2026, and this brief keeps the currency of each source.

**Structure.** ICT specialists were 4.8% of employment in 2025 (141,500 people) against 5.0% in the EU (Eurostat) [data]. The outsourcing sector's 105,436 employees are described above.

**Graduates and youth.** In 2025, 90.0% of tertiary graduates aged 20-34 who finished within three years were in work (EU 85.3%); unemployment among 15-to-24-year-olds was 13.1% (12.1% in 2023; EU 15.2%); 41.2% of 25-to-34-year-olds had a tertiary degree against the EU's 44.8% (Eurostat) [data].

**Entry pay.** No Bulgarian graduate pay survey by field was read. The Atlas holds the Mercer 2024 junior-specialist base, €1,517 a month in service centres (ABSL 2025, via the library) [data]. In 2024 the average annual wage in information and communication was BGN 68,972 in Sofia against 64,537 nationally, and in finance and insurance BGN 46,423 against 41,620 (National Statistical Institute) [data]: this is a sector average for all ages.

**Language of work.** Not researched.

**How hiring works.** Not researched: no graduate programme or calendar was read (gap).

**Permits.** EU citizens apply for a residence certificate within three months; non-EU students may work 20 hours a week (full-time during official holidays) and have nine months after graduating to find work. Single Permit (ЕРПР) and EU Blue Card (threshold €2,166/month) routes are consolidated from primary sources in `visas_immigration/bulgaria/bulgaria_visas_immigration_guide.md`.

## 3. Hubs

### Sofia

**What it is.** 1,619,690 residents in the metropolitan region (2023) and GDP of €33.3 billion (2021) (Eurostat) [data]; an average annual wage of BGN 38,728 in 2024, or BGN 3,227 a month (National Statistical Institute, Sofia (capital) district; annual ÷ 12) [data]. Rent: not read.

**Sectors and named employers.**
- Banking: UniCredit Bulbank (registered address 7 Sveta Nedelya Square; 3,038 employees at 31 December 2025) and First Investment Bank (111 P Tsarigradsko shose Blvd) [employer-stated].
- Software: SAP Labs Bulgaria, which a trade article of 19 October 2020 says started in 2000 and employed over 1,000 people [practitioner consensus]. This headcount is six years old.
- Business-process and IT outsourcing: the national AIBEST totals; no Sofia-only count was read.

**Start-ups.** Startup Genome's 2026 report values the Sofia ecosystem at $3 billion, with $63 million of early-stage funding in H2 2023-2025, against a European average of $14.3 billion [practitioner consensus].

### Plovdiv

**What it is.** 631,516 residents in the metropolitan region (2023), GDP €5.3 billion (2021) (Eurostat); average annual wage BGN 23,473 in 2024, or BGN 1,956 a month (National Statistical Institute, Plovdiv district) [data].

**Sectors.** Information and communication 5,350 jobs, second in the country; finance and insurance 2,320, third (Eurostat, 2021) [data]. Manufacturing, technology and food are the city's other sectors in the record. No named employer was read.

**Standing:** IT 4 / 1 / 1, finance 3 / 1 / 1.

### Varna

**What it is.** 430,847 residents in the metropolitan region (2023), GDP €4.3 billion (2021) (Eurostat); average annual wage BGN 26,007 in 2024, or BGN 2,167 a month (National Statistical Institute, Varna district) [data]. The Black Sea port.

**Sectors.** Information and communication 4,840 jobs and finance and insurance 2,830, the latter second in the country (Eurostat, 2021) [data]. No named employer was read, and no family is rated for demand because no hub other than Sofia reaches the 5,000-job threshold the record uses.

**Standing:** IT 3 / 1 / 1, finance 4 / 1 / 1.

## 5. Hypotheses tested

1. **"Bulgaria is a cheap IT hub."** Partly supported: junior service-centre pay is the lowest of five countries compared (€1,517) and Sofia's average annual wage is BGN 38,728, but ICT specialists are 4.8% of employment, near the EU's 5.0%, and the outsourcing sector's growth stalled in 2024 (+0.2%) [data].
2. **"Everything is in Sofia."** Supported by the job counts: Sofia has 84% of ICT jobs and 77% of finance jobs; the next cities have about 5,000 ICT jobs each [data].
3. **"Sofia is a regional finance centre."** Not supported: GFCI 40 ranks it 115th of 117, last of 14 in its region; its 52,080 finance jobs make it tenth among the 25 capital regions [data, practitioner consensus].

## 6. Common mistakes and myths

- **District averages mix all ages and sectors.** BGN 38,728 a year in Sofia is not an entry salary.
- **The currency has changed.** Sources dated 2025 or earlier are in leva; the euro has been the currency since 1 January 2026.
- **The SAP Labs headcount is from 2020.** A search summary quoted a larger 2026 figure that I did not read on a page.

## 7. Decision rules

1. If you want a first job in IT, software or finance in Bulgaria, start with Sofia; outside it the job counts are small.
2. If you want banking, start with the Sofia head offices (UniCredit Bulbank, First Investment Bank) and check their careers pages for graduate intakes.
3. If you compare a Bulgarian and a Polish or Czech service-centre offer, compare net pay and rent, not gross: Bulgaria's junior base is the lowest of the five countries.
4. If you want investment banking or capital markets, do not choose Bulgaria for entry.
5. If you are non-EU, use the nine-month search period after graduating, and have offers lined up.
