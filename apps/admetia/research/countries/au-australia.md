# Australia: where a European business or computing graduate should look

Tags: `[data]` official statistics; `[employer-stated]` an employer's own report; `[practitioner consensus]` a published ranking; `[anecdotal]` crowd-sourced. All pages read on 3 October 2026 unless a different date is given.

## 1. Bottom line

- **Sydney is the only hub that is both a finance city and a tech city.** Finance and financial services is the largest sector by jobs in the City of Sydney, with 125,002 jobs (2022), ahead of professional and business services (94,157) and ICT (38,895) (City of Sydney, Floor Space and Employment Survey 2022) `[data]`. Commonwealth Bank, Westpac and Macquarie all work from Sydney addresses (their FY25 and FY26 reports) `[employer-stated]`.
- **Melbourne is the second pole**, not a copy of Sydney: it is NAB's and Telstra's registered home, and Startup Genome ranks it 30th in the world and second in Oceania (GSER 2026) `[practitioner consensus]`. Finance is rated only "present" because no jobs-by-sector table for the city could be read.
- **The post-study record for a business master's is weak.** In 2025 only 53.3% of international postgraduate coursework graduates in business and management were in full-time work, against 92.2% of domestic graduates; median full-time salary A$65,000 against A$130,000 (QILT 2025, via the library) `[data]`. A coursework master's earns a two-year Temporary Graduate visa (subclass 485).
- **Pay is high in absolute terms and similar across states**: full-time adults' average weekly ordinary-time earnings were A$2,083.70 in May 2026 (about A$9,030 a month); Western Australia and the ACT are above A$2,200 a week (ABS) `[data]`. That is the average of all full-time adults, not a graduate starting salary.
- **Canberra and Adelaide are public-sector and defence cities.** 35.4% of the Australian Public Service (70,221 of 198,529) is in Canberra (APSC, 30 June 2025) `[data]`; South Australia's defence sector employs more than 14,000 (Skills SA) `[data]`. Both suit policy, economics and public-administration paths, not finance or tech.
- **Perth is the resources city.** Western Australia's mining sector had more than 136,000 on-site jobs in 2025 (WA government) `[data]` and Woodside is registered in Perth. Corporate jobs at resource headquarters exist, but no source read counts graduate hiring.
- **For a European, the order of search is Sydney, then Melbourne, then Brisbane for tech**; look at Perth only with a resources angle and at Canberra only if a public-sector career is the goal.

## 2. The economy and the graduate labour market

**Size and structure.** Australia's GDP was A$2,778,897 million at current prices in 2024-25; New South Wales produced A$855.4 billion, Victoria A$637.4 billion, Queensland A$531.0 billion, Western Australia A$458.8 billion, South Australia A$157.9 billion and the ACT A$59.4 billion (ABS, State Accounts 2024-25, Table 1) `[data]`. Growth was 1.4% in real terms; the ABS names health care, financial and insurance services, public administration and transport as the strong sectors (same release) `[data]`. The five largest sectors in the record are banking and financial services, professional and business services, technology, mining and resources and higher education.

**Entry pay.** The ABS average weekly ordinary-time earnings of full-time adults in May 2026 were A$2,083.70 nationally, up 3.7% in a year (NSW 2,108.80; Victoria 2,041.10; Queensland 2,039.70; South Australia 1,970.20; Western Australia 2,227.40; ACT 2,290.20) `[data]`. Monthly figures in the Atlas are the weekly figure times 52 divided by 12. No starting-salary survey by field was read (the Graduate Outcomes Survey microdata and the Graduate Careers' pages were not reached). A job board advertised Telstra graduate programmes at A$80,000 to 92,000 (search summary only, not verified) `[anecdotal]`.

**Graduate outcomes for internationals.** The QILT 2025 International Report gives the figures in the bottom line (library, `places/beyond-europe.md` §3.3) `[data]`. The gap between 53.3% and 92.2% is the single most important number for a European paying international fees for a coursework master's.

**Youth and graduate unemployment.** Not read in this round; see gaps.

**Language.** English. No employer's own recruiting calendar was read; a job board lists Telstra's 2026 graduate programmes as starting between 28 February and 30 March 2026 (search summary only) `[anecdotal]`.

**Visa headline.** Student visa (subclass 500) from A$2,500 per application from 1 July 2026 (Study Australia) `[data]`; work up to 48 hours a fortnight in term; a two-year subclass 485 for a coursework master's, under 35, applied for inside Australia, no extension (Home Affairs via the library) `[data]`. EU/Italian citizens enter on a free eVisitor (subclass 651) or Working Holiday (subclass 417, 18–35). Full official guide and single source of truth: `visas_immigration/australia/australia_visas_immigration_guide.md`.

## 3. Hubs

Metric method: **population** is the ABS estimated resident population of the greater capital city (`metro`), 30 June 2025 (Regional population 2024-25). **GDP** is gross state product at current prices for 2024-25, a **state or territory** figure (`region`), not the city's. **Wage** is the state's full-time adult average weekly ordinary-time earnings of May 2026 × 52 ÷ 12 (`region`, mean). **Rent** is left out: Numbeo returned HTTP 429 on every attempt and no official city-level rent series was read.

### Sydney

- **What it is.** Australia's largest city, 5,638,830 residents (ABS) `[data]`, in a state producing A$855.4 billion `[data]`.
- **Finance.** 125,002 jobs in finance and financial services in the City of Sydney local area in 2022 `[data]`. GFCI 40 ranks Sydney 24th in the world and tenth in Asia/Pacific, ahead of Melbourne's 32nd (Z/Yen, Sep 2026) `[practitioner consensus]`. Commonwealth Bank: results issued from Commonwealth Bank Place South, 11 Harbour Street; cash profit A$10,982 million in FY26; 51,714 full-time equivalent employees (Annual Review 2026) `[employer-stated]`. Westpac: head office 275 Kent Street; 35,236 full-time equivalent employees at 30 September 2025 `[employer-stated]`. Macquarie: 19,124 people in 30 markets, about half in Australia and New Zealand, with Sydney offices at 50 Martin Place and 1 Elizabeth Street (with London) housing about half of its people; its asset-management arm reports A$325.9 billion managed in Australia `[employer-stated]`.
- **Tech.** GSER 2026 ranks Sydney 26th globally and first in Oceania; Tech Central is described as a A$42 billion economy with more than 100,000 workers; ICT jobs in the City of Sydney area were 38,895 (2022) `[practitioner consensus]`, `[data]`.
- **Universities named by GSER:** UNSW Sydney, University of Sydney, Western Sydney University, Macquarie University and the University of Technology Sydney `[practitioner consensus]`.
- **Standing** (national / Asia-Pacific / world): finance 5 / 3 / 2 (first in Australia on jobs and GFCI; tenth in Asia/Pacific; 24th in the world); IT 5 / 3 / 2 and software 5 / 3 / 2 (first in Oceania, 26th in GSER); business 4 / 2 / 1 (professional services are large, but no table compares Sydney with Melbourne).
- **Ratings changed:** finance strong → **dominant** (data, GFCI and two named banks); software and banking → strong; asset management → present; business and IT strong (unchanged).
- **Not found:** graduate-programme pages for CBA, Macquarie (Macquarie's page loaded but gave no figures), Westpac (404) and ANZ (404); the big accounting and consulting firms were not read.

### Melbourne

- **What it is.** 5,435,590 residents, the largest annual increase of any Australian capital (up 105,030, or 2.0%, in 2024-25) `[data]`; Victoria produced A$637.4 billion.
- **Finance.** NAB: registered office on Bourke Street; 41,880 full-time equivalent employees; says it was ranked first graduate employer in banking and finance on Prosple's Top 100 for the fourth year (2025 annual report) `[employer-stated]`. GFCI 40: 32nd in the world, thirteenth in Asia/Pacific `[practitioner consensus]`.
- **Tech.** GSER 2026: 30th globally, second in Oceania; Victorian start-ups raised a record A$2.4 billion in 2025; more than half of Australia's enterprise-software and health-tech start-ups are in Victoria; Airwallex raised a record A$467 million round `[practitioner consensus]`. Telstra's registered office is on Exhibition Street `[employer-stated]`.
- **Standing:** finance 4 / 3 / 2; software 4 / 3 / 2.
- **Ratings:** finance and banking not rated → present (one named employer with its home in the city). Nothing else: the City of Melbourne jobs-by-industry table was blocked.

### Brisbane

- **What it is.** 2,833,524 residents (+2.1%); Queensland produced A$531.0 billion. The 2032 Olympic Games are named by GSER as driving transport investment `[practitioner consensus]`.
- **Employers.** Suncorp Group's registered office is Level 23, 80 Ann Street `[employer-stated]`.
- **Start-ups.** GSER 2026: emerging 51-60, fourth in Oceania; PsiQuantum's US$1 billion project `[practitioner consensus]`.
- **Standing:** software 4 / 2 / 1; finance 3 / 2 / 1. **Rating:** finance present.

### Perth

- **What it is.** 2,452,765 residents, the fastest-growing capital by percentage (+2.4%); Western Australia produced A$458.8 billion and has the highest state wage of the six (A$2,227.40 a week) `[data]`.
- **Employers.** Woodside Energy, registered office on Mount Street `[employer-stated]`; more than 136,000 on-site mining jobs in the state in 2025 (66,367 iron ore, 38,816 gold) `[data]`.
- **Standing:** business 3 / 2 / 1, a judgement from head-office presence; no family rated.

### Adelaide

- **What it is.** 1,491,015 residents; South Australia produced A$157.9 billion and has the lowest wage of the six (A$1,970.20 a week) `[data]`.
- **Employers.** South Australia's defence sector employs more than 14,000, with another 10,000 jobs expected over 20 years; AUKUS submarines are to be built at Osborne `[data]`. 13,648 APS staff work in Adelaide, fifth among the capitals `[data]`.
- **Standing:** management (public administration) 3 / 2 / 1. No family rated.

### Canberra

- **What it is.** 484,630 residents; the ACT produced A$59.4 billion and has the highest wage (A$2,290.20 a week) `[data]`.
- **Employers.** 70,221 APS employees, 35.4% of the service (APSC, Table 13, 30 June 2025) `[data]`.
- **Standing:** management (public administration) 5 / 2 / 1. No family rated: the APS graduate programme page was refused (access denied), so entry hiring is not documented.

## 5. Hypotheses tested

**H1. "Australia is a good place to build a career for a European with an Australian master's."** Partly supported. Pay is high and the 485 gives two years, but only 53.3% of international business and management postgraduates were in full-time work (QILT 2025) `[data]`. It holds for finance and tech candidates with a job offer lined up; it fails for a general management master's with no local network.

**H2. "Melbourne is the cheaper, smaller version of Sydney for finance."** Partly supported. Melbourne has lower state pay (A$2,041 against A$2,109 a week), a lower GFCI rank (32nd against 24th) and NAB rather than three banks and Macquarie in the sources read `[data]`, `[practitioner consensus]`. But its start-up ecosystem (30th) is close to Sydney's (26th). No rent source was read, so "cheaper" is untested.

**H3. "The mining boom makes Perth the best-paid graduate city."** Not supported on the evidence read. Perth sits in the state with the second-highest average pay, but that is a state average that includes site workers and says nothing about graduate offices; no graduate pay source for Perth was reached.

**H4. "Canberra is where a policy or economics graduate belongs."** Supported on scale, not on hiring. 35.4% of the APS is in Canberra `[data]`, but the graduate programme itself was not read.

## 6. Common mistakes and myths

(a) Reading state wages as city wages: the Atlas figures are for the whole state. (b) Reading the City of Sydney's 125,002 finance jobs as Greater Sydney's: it is a local-area count from 2022. (c) Assuming a master's earns permanent residence: the 485 cannot be extended and the applicant must find a job in two years. (d) Treating Prosple's graduate-employer ranking as a measure of how many graduates NAB hires: it is a student survey ranking quoted by NAB, `[employer-stated]`.

## 7. Decision rules

1. If the target is investment banking, asset management or banking, prefer Sydney: two of the big four banks (Commonwealth Bank, Westpac) and Macquarie are there, and finance is the largest sector by jobs in the City (125,002).
2. If the target is retail banking, telecoms or enterprise software, look at Melbourne as well as Sydney: NAB and Telstra are based there and Victoria holds more than half of Australia's enterprise-software start-ups (GSER).
3. If the target is quantum, cloud or government-linked tech, add Brisbane (GSER: PsiQuantum's project, 4th in Oceania).
4. If the target is policy, economics or regulatory work, prefer Canberra, and apply to the APS graduate streams directly; no EU-specific eligibility was read, so check nationality rules first.
5. If the target is resources, accept that corporate roles in Perth are few and engineering-led; no business-graduate hiring figure was found.
6. If you are paying international fees for a business master's and have no offer, treat the 485 as a two-year job search with a 53% full-time employment base rate.
7. Compare hubs with the same pay basis: use state averages, not city ones, and do not compare them with graduate medians.
8. Budget separately for the visa (student visa from A$2,500) and for health cover for the whole stay.
