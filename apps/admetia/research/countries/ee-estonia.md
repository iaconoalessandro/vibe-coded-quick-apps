# Estonia: country brief

Today is 3 October 2026. Tags: `[data]` official statistics or government pages, `[employer-stated]`, `[practitioner consensus]` (published indices and specialist synthesis), `[anecdotal]`.

## 1. Bottom line

- Estonia has one real graduate market, Tallinn. Eurostat counts 28,900 of Estonia's 36,500 ICT workers (79%) and 14,000 of its 16,800 finance and insurance workers (83%) in the Tallinn metropolitan region in 2022 (met_10r_3emp) [data]; in 2025 the city had 8,997 of the country's 12,085 telecom, programming and information-services firms (74%) and 2,712 of its 3,824 financial and insurance firms (71%) (Statistics Estonia, ER0308, 2025) [data]. Tartu city is second in ICT with 591 firms, then nothing close.
- Tallinn is the national leader (5 of 5) in software and IT, a recognised secondary European centre (3 of 5) and small at world scale (2 of 5). Startup Genome's 2026 report places it in the 31-40 band of its emerging ecosystems, outside the Top 40 mature list [practitioner consensus]; StartupBlink ranks Tallinn 53rd among world cities in 2026 [employer-stated, via a state agency]. The product owner's example holds: 5 nationally, 2-3 in Europe, much less beside Silicon Valley.
- Pay in tech and finance is far above the Estonian average: in 2025 information and communication averaged €3,651 a month gross (median €3,115) and finance €3,338, against €2,092 for the whole economy (Statistics Estonia, PA101) [data]. A Wise Product Academy role in Tallinn was advertised at €3,916 gross a month (2025 advert) [employer-stated].
- The market is small and not growing fast for juniors. The start-up sector had 17,084 employees in June 2026, +1% on the quarter (Superangel 500 from the Tax and Customs Board) [data]; the 20-to-29-year-old head count in ICT fell from 6,130 to 5,070 between 2022 and 2024 (Statistics Estonia) [data]; unemployment among 15-to-24-year-olds was 20.7% in 2025 (Eurostat) [data].
- The anchor employers are few and named: Wise (largest office, 2,419 staff in the Estonian register), Bolt (headquarters), Playtech, Pipedrive, Veriff, Skype's Microsoft unit, LHV and Swedbank, plus NATO's cyber-defence centre and Cybernetica for security.
- For a European master's student: go to Tallinn for software, fintech or a specific named employer, and use it as a first job or a base for a remote-friendly career; do not expect a deep corporate-finance, consulting or banking market. Tartu is a university and research town (Estonia's top-ranked university, QS 2027: 367th), not a graduate-employer cluster.

## 2. The economy and the graduate labour market

**Size.** Estonia's GDP was €39.8 billion in 2024, €29,036 a head, with Harju County (Tallinn and its suburbs) producing 63.2% of it (Statistics Estonia, RAA0050) [data]. The population was 1,360,745 on 1 January 2026 (Statistics Estonia, RV0291U) [data].

**Structure.** Information and communication employed 28,658 people and financial and insurance activities 14,124 in 2025 out of 592,126 employees in total (PA101) [data]. A wider count puts the ICT workforce at about 36,000 in June 2024 (Statistics Estonia, 17 Jul 2024) [data]. Eurostat counts ICT specialists at 6.8% of employment in 2025 (47,500 people), the fifth highest among the European countries it lists, after Sweden (8.9%), Luxembourg (8.7%), Finland (7.8%) and the Netherlands (7.2%); the EU average is 5.0% [data]. The start-up sector is Estonia's showpiece: the e-Residency programme counts 10 unicorns (Skype, Playtech, Wise, Bolt, Pipedrive, ID.me, Gelato, Zego, Veriff, Glia) and ranks Estonia top in the EU per head [employer-stated].

**Graduates and youth.** In 2025, 91.0% of tertiary graduates aged 20-34 who had finished in the last three years were in work (EU 85.3%), but unemployment among 15-to-24-year-olds was 20.7% (EU 15.2%), up from 17.3% in 2023; overall unemployment was 7.5% (Eurostat) [data]. A graduate who finds a job does well; finding it is harder than it was.

**Entry pay.** Statistics Estonia publishes sector deciles: in 2025 the lowest tenth of ICT employees earned under €1,246 and of finance employees under €1,656, so averages of €3,651 and €3,338 are not entry salaries (PA101) [data]. The only entry-level figure read is a Wise advert for a Tallinn Product Academy role at gross €3,916 a month plus a share package (advert removed 3 Nov 2025) [employer-stated]. Tax: income tax 22%, the first €700 a month tax-free from 2026; the employer adds 33% social tax (Tax and Customs Board) [data].

**How hiring works.** Wise runs a global graduate cycle: final-year students or people who graduated a year ago, all starting in September; interns are penultimate-year students for ten paid weeks in summer (Wise careers) [employer-stated]. No graduate calendar was found for Bolt, the banks or the Big Four (gap).

**Language and permits.** Estonian at level A2 is needed to extend an employment permit after five years; graduates of Estonian universities are exempt from the salary floor (€2,092, or €1,674 at a start-up, for applications from 5 March 2026) (Police and Border Guard Board; Interior Ministry 2018) [data]. EU citizens need only register their address. Full 10-case verified guide: visas_immigration/estonia/estonia_visas_immigration_guide.md.

## 3. Hubs

### Tallinn

**What it is.** The capital and, in practice, the only large employer market: 452,563 residents (1 Jan 2026, city), GDP €20.67 billion in 2024 (51.9% of Estonia's), €45,223 a head, average gross pay €2,451 a month in 2025 against €2,092 nationally (Statistics Estonia, RV0291U, RAA0050, PA107) [data]. One-bedroom flat in the centre about €722 a month (Numbeo, crowd-sourced) [anecdotal].

**Sectors and named employers.** Start-up register counts, end of June 2026: Wise 2,419, Bolt 878, Playtech 662, Microsoft Estonia (Skype) 393, Pipedrive 373, Coolbet 331, Veriff 301, Milrem 286, Cybernetica 241; the sector as a whole 17,084 (Superangel 500 from the Tax and Customs Board; board members excluded and group staff abroad not counted) [data]. Wise says its largest office is in Tallinn (Krulli) [employer-stated]. Bolt says its team of over 4,000 is based at the Tallinn headquarters and key hubs in London, Warsaw, Bucharest and Berlin (a global figure; the Estonian register shows 878 for the Bolt unit, so read the two as different things) [employer-stated]; Bolt reported €2.27 billion of revenue in 2025 and its first annual profit (Trade with Estonia, July 2026) [employer-stated]. Banking: Swedbank, SEB, Luminor, LHV and Coop Pank are the largest (Invest in Estonia) [data]; LHV has its registered office in Tallinn and more than 1,150 staff (annual report 2025) [employer-stated], and Swedbank gives a Tallinn address. Security and defence: NATO's Cooperative Cyber Defence Centre of Excellence (Filtri tee 5) and Cybernetica (Mäealuse 2/1) [employer-stated]. The Ülemiste City campus says 18,000 people work, study and live there [employer-stated].

**Universities.** QS 2027: Tallinn University of Technology (TalTech) 600th, Tallinn University 901-950th (Study in Estonia) [practitioner consensus]. No Tallinn business-school ranking was read.

**Start-up ecosystem.** StartupBlink 2026: Estonia 12th in the world and 5th in the EU, Tallinn 53rd among cities, Estonia 10th for fintech (Invest in Estonia, May 2026) [employer-stated]. Startup Genome 2026: Tallinn in the 31-40 band of emerging ecosystems; London's ecosystem value is $438 billion against Paris $169 billion and Berlin $89 billion [practitioner consensus].

### Tartu

**What it is.** Estonia's second city: 99,900 residents, GDP €3.51 billion (8.8% of the country; €34,803 a head), average gross pay €2,229 (Statistics Estonia) [data]; centre flat about €557 (Numbeo) [anecdotal].

**Sector and employers.** Second in ICT firms (591) but a long way behind Tallinn [data]. The University of Tartu presented its deep-tech spin-offs at Startup Day 2026, with the Tartu Science Park among founder support [employer-stated]; the university is 367th in QS 2027 [practitioner consensus]. StartupBlink ranks Tartu 421st in 2026, down nine places [employer-stated]. Playtech was founded in Tartu according to the e-Residency programme; the register does not say where its 662 staff sit, so they are not counted as Tartu jobs.

**Standing:** software 3 / 1 / 1. National 3: second largest city for ICT firms. Not rated for finance or business.

## 5. Hypotheses tested

1. **"Tallinn is a top-tier European tech hub for graduates."** Not supported. It leads Estonia and is a secondary European centre: outside Startup Genome's Top 40, 53rd in StartupBlink, 87th in the finance index. Holds where the target is Wise, Bolt or a start-up of that kind; fails for anyone expecting a deep local market of large employers.
2. **"The start-up boom means plentiful junior jobs."** Not supported. Sector employment is 17,084 and growing 1% a quarter; young ICT workers fell by about 1,060 in two years; youth unemployment is above the EU average [data].
3. **"A tech job in Tallinn pays much more than the local average, so it is worth the small market."** Supported on pay: ICT averages 1.7 times the national mean (€3,651 against €2,092). Conditions: it is an average, the bottom tenth earns under €1,246, and the only entry figure read is one advert. Whether it beats western-European offers after rent was not tested (insufficient evidence).
4. **"Estonia is a fintech centre, so finance graduates should go."** Partly supported. The fintech ranking (41st, up 27 places, GFCI 40) is good, but the banking market is five lenders, and Wise's roles are software and product, not banking. A finance graduate should aim for product, risk or analytics roles inside fintech, not a bank graduate scheme [practitioner consensus plus data].

## 6. Common mistakes and myths

- **e-Residency is not residence.** It gives e-services and company registration, not a right to live or work [data].
- **Start-up headline numbers are not Estonian jobs.** Bolt's "over 4,000" is global, the register shows 878 for the Estonian unit.
- **Averages are not entry pay.** Tenth-decile pay in ICT is under €1,246.
- **"English is enough" is untested here.** No source read measures it; the one formal language rule is A2 Estonian after five years on a permit.

## 7. Decision rules

1. If your target is software, product or data inside a scale-up, prefer Tallinn: it holds 74% of the country's ICT firms.
2. If your target is investment banking, private equity or consulting, prefer another country: no source read shows any Estonian entry market in them.
3. If you are an EU citizen, register your address and get the ID card before you start; no permit is needed.
4. If you are non-EU and studied in Estonia, use the graduate exemption from the salary floor; otherwise check the €2,092 floor (€1,674 at a start-up).
5. If you apply to Wise, aim at the September graduate cycle and apply as a final-year student.
6. If you want a research or deep-tech path, use Tartu and its spin-offs, and expect few large employers.
7. If you need a flat on a graduate salary, Tallinn centre rent is about €722 (crowd-sourced) against average gross pay of €2,451; budget before you accept.

## 8. Gaps and claims to verify

1. A Tallinn-only employment figure for ICT or finance: Eurostat's is for the metropolitan region (Harju County) and for 2022; Statistics Estonia counts firms by municipality, jobs only by county or country.
2. The Startup Genome position of Tallinn is a band, not a number; the StartupBlink city rank was read in a state agency's report, not on the ranking site (blocked).
3. SEB and Luminor headquarters were not read; their staff numbers were not read.
4. No graduate programme page for Bolt, the banks or the Big Four.
5. Entry salary: one job advert only.
6. Playtech's relationship to Tartu is from the e-Residency programme's article, not from Playtech.
