# Luxembourg: country brief

Today is 3 October 2026. Tags: `[data]` official statistics or government pages, `[employer-stated]`, `[practitioner consensus]` (published indices and specialist synthesis), `[anecdotal]`.

## 1. Bottom line

- Luxembourg is a finance market with one city. Its fund industry held €6.69 trillion of UCITS assets and €9.07 trillion including alternative funds in June 2026, up about 20% in twelve months (ALFI statistics, via places/countries-and-cities.md) [data]. The Atlas rates finance, and asset management within it, as dominant.
- It is a European top-four centre and a world top-twenty one: the Global Financial Centres Index 40 (September 2026) ranks Luxembourg 17th in the world, fourth in Western Europe after London, Zurich and Geneva, and second in the world for investment management, behind Hong Kong [practitioner consensus].
- The labour market is small and finance-heavy: 510,990 jobs in 2023, of which 54,220 are in finance and insurance and 22,730 in information and communication (Eurostat) [data]. By metropolitan region it has the 11th-most finance and insurance jobs of 152 in Eurostat's table, just behind Brussels (63,000 against 53,040) [data].
- Pay is high: employees under 30 in firms with 10 or more staff earned a mean €48,369 gross in 2022, and under-30 professionals €60,726 (Eurostat SES), against €38,114 in Belgium and €34,109 in the Netherlands [data]. A one-bedroom flat in the centre costs about €2,000 a month (Numbeo) [anecdotal], so net purchasing power is lower than the pay suggests.
- Beyond funds, the named public employers are the European Investment Bank (more than 4,000 employees [employer-stated]) and the financial regulator CSSF (over 950 [employer-stated]); the start-up ecosystem is small (value $4 billion against a European average of $14.3 billion, Startup Genome 2026) [practitioner consensus].
- For a European master's student: go for fund administration, asset management, compliance and risk, regulation and the EU bank; do not expect technology or consulting roles of the size of Frankfurt or Amsterdam. Work is multilingual and English is common in finance (library).
- For non-EU master's and PhD graduates from Luxembourg universities, the 2023 law grants a 12-month job-search residence permit with no ADEM market test upon conversion to work; all immigration rules are consolidated in `visas_immigration/luxembourg/luxembourg_visas_immigration_guide.md` [data].

## 2. The economy and the graduate labour market

**Size.** Population 681,973 on 1 January 2025; GDP €89.5 billion in 2025; unemployment 6.5% overall in 2025 and 21.6% among 15-to-24-year-olds in 2024 (the 2025 youth figure was not in the extract read) (Eurostat demo_pjan, nama_10_gdp, une_rt_a) [data].

**Structure.** Of 510,990 jobs in 2023 (Eurostat nama_10r_3empers): 115,420 in public administration, defence, education and health; 90,490 in professional, scientific, technical and administrative services; 54,220 in finance and insurance; 33,050 in manufacturing; 22,730 in information and communication [data]. Finance and insurance is therefore about 11% of jobs, and the professional services around it (audit, tax, legal, fund administration) are the next layer. The regulator CSSF says it has over 950 employees; the European Investment Bank more than 4,000, in Luxembourg and around the world.

**Graduates.** In 2025 the employment rate of 20-to-34-year-olds with a tertiary degree was 90.5%; for those who finished within the last five years it was 88.2% (Eurostat edat_lfse_24) [data]. Residents of the country are a small part of the workforce: commuters from France, Germany and Belgium fill many jobs, but no primary source for their number was read (see section 8).

**Entry pay.** Eurostat's Structure of Earnings Survey 2022 (firms with 10 or more staff, public administration excluded): mean annual gross earnings of €48,369 for employees under 30 and €60,726 for under-30 professionals, against €74,542 for all ages (earn_ses22_28) [data]. The Atlas pay metric is the all-age mean (€74,542 ÷ 12 = €6,212 a month) for the whole country; it is four years old and is not a graduate figure.

**How hiring works.** Fund administrators, banks and the Big Four recruit graduates throughout the year through their own career pages; no published calendar was read. The CSSF and the EIB recruit through their own competitions and traineeships (not researched in detail).

**Language and permits.** Work is multilingual; English is common in finance (record summary). EU citizens must declare arrival within 8 days and register at the commune within three months (law of 29 August 2008, art. 8; law of 19 June 2013); non-EU students may work up to an average of 15 hours a week outside study time (art. 57); non-EU master's/PhD graduates get a 12-month post-study job-search permit with ADEM exemption upon conversion (art. 59 and 67-4, law of 7 August 2023) [data]. See verified details in `visas_immigration/luxembourg/luxembourg_visas_immigration_guide.md`.

### Luxembourg City

**What it is.** The only hub: 681,973 residents (the country), GDP €82.1 billion (2023), a one-bedroom flat about €2,000 a month. The metropolitan-region table (`met_10r_3emp`) has Luxembourg 11th of 152 regions for finance and insurance jobs (53,040) and 45th for information and communication (21,830) [data].

**Employers.** Luxembourg-domiciled funds (€9.07 trillion including alternative funds); the European Investment Bank (more than 4,000 employees); the CSSF (over 950); the start-up ecosystem (value $4 billion, $271 million of seed and Series A funding in H2 2023 to 2025, $1 billion of exits in 2021 to 2025 [practitioner consensus]). ## 5. Hypotheses tested

1. **Luxembourg is a top-five European financial centre.** Supported by a published index: fourth in Western Europe in GFCI 40, behind London, Zurich and Geneva. Holds for funds and investment management; fails for banking (12th in the world) and for trading.
2. **Luxembourg is a place for generalist business graduates.** Partly supported. 90.5% of tertiary graduates are in work, and professional services are 90,490 jobs, but the evidence on named graduate schemes is thin (not read). It fails if you want technology: information and communication is 22,730 jobs.
3. **High pay makes Luxembourg the best place to start.** Partly supported: the under-30 mean is €48,369 against €38,114 in Belgium and €34,109 in the Netherlands (Eurostat SES 2022), but rent is about €2,000 against €1,209 in Brussels and €2,266 in Amsterdam (Numbeo, anecdotal), and the pay is four years old.

## 6. Common mistakes and myths

- Treating "Luxembourg finance" as banking: its distinction is funds (second in the world for investment management in GFCI 40), not trading or investment banking (banking 12th) [practitioner consensus].
- Reading the national mean (€74,542 a year) as an entry salary: under-30s earned €48,369 in the same survey [data].
- Assuming a start-up scene: $4 billion of ecosystem value is below a third of the European average [practitioner consensus].

## 7. Decision rules

1. If your target is fund administration, asset management, fund compliance or risk, choose Luxembourg; it is the only hub here and its standing in investment management is world top-two (GFCI 40).
2. If your target is investment banking or trading, choose London, Frankfurt, Paris or Amsterdam; Luxembourg ranks 12th for banking.
3. If your target is technology, choose another country: 22,730 information and communication jobs, no named large employer read.
4. If you want regulation or EU public finance, apply to the CSSF and the EIB, which state 950+ and 4,000+ employees.
5. If you are a non-EU student, study there only if the programme is a five-year university cycle: the art. 59 permit is tied to it.
6. Compare offers by net pay after rent: about €2,000 for a one-bedroom flat in the centre (anecdotal) against €1,209 in Brussels takes back part of the pay advantage; run the sum on your own offer.
