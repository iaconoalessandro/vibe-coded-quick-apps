# Iceland: country brief

> **Single Source of Truth:** All Icelandic immigration, visa, residence permit and post-study rules are consolidated and verified in [`visas_immigration/iceland/iceland_visas_immigration_guide.md`](../../visas_immigration/iceland/iceland_visas_immigration_guide.md).

Today is 3 October 2026 (verified 5 October 2026). Tags: `[data]` official statistics or government pages, `[employer-stated]`, `[practitioner consensus]` (published indices), `[anecdotal]`.

## 1. Bottom line

- Iceland is a very small market (235,518 people in employment in June 2026) and Reykjavík is its only graduate hub: 139,804 residents in the city and 251,912 (64% of the country) in the capital region on 1 January 2026 (Statistics Iceland) [data].
- The graduate-sized sectors are finance and ICT, and they are small: 6,610 people in finance and insurance and 9,104 in information and communication in June 2026 (Statistics Iceland, register, national) [data]. Tourism, at about 9.5% of hours worked, is bigger but is not a graduate-pipeline sector (Statistics Iceland) [data].
- Finance is the strongest family. Landsbankinn (Reykjastræti 6) and Arion Bank (Borgartún 19) give Reykjavík addresses; Íslandsbanki is in Kópavogur, in the capital region [employer-stated]. Finance and insurance pay a mean of ISK 1,372 thousand a month to full-time employees, 30% above the all-sector mean of ISK 1,058 thousand (2025) [data].
- On the international scale the hub is small: the Global Financial Centres Index 40 lists Reykjavik only as an associate centre, with too few assessments to rank [practitioner consensus], and Startup Genome values its start-up ecosystem at $3 billion against a European average of $14.3 billion [practitioner consensus].
- Graduates who find work do well: 92.0% of recent tertiary graduates aged 20-34 were employed in 2025 (EU 85.3%), and youth unemployment was 9.5% (EU 15.2%) (Eurostat) [data].
- Permits matter more than the labour market for non-EU students: from 8 July 2026 graduates get 18 months, not three years, to find work (full guide in `visas_immigration/iceland/iceland_visas_immigration_guide.md`).
- For a European master's student: Iceland is a destination for a specific job (a bank, a games or energy employer) or a short stay, not a market to land in without an offer.

## 2. The economy and the graduate labour market

**Size and structure.** 394,324 residents (1 Jan 2026), 225,700 people aged 16-74 employed in 2025, unemployment 4.3% nationally and 4.9% in the capital region against 3.2% elsewhere; 49.0% of employed 25-64-year-olds had a tertiary education (Statistics Iceland, labour market 2025) [data]. Tourism accounted for 8.8% of GDP in 2025 (tourism satellite accounts) [data]. Other sectors the record names: fisheries and seafood, energy and aluminium, technology and data centres.

**Pay.** Full-time employees earned a mean total of ISK 1,058 thousand a month in 2025 (median 948); in information and communication ISK 1,098 thousand (median 961); in finance and insurance ISK 1,372 thousand (median 1,176) (Statistics Iceland, VIN02003) [data]. There is no regional pay table, so the hub carries no pay metric. ISK figures are not converted here.

**Graduates.** Eurostat 2025: employment of recent tertiary graduates 92.0%; youth unemployment 9.5% (2023: 8.8%) [data]. Iceland has no ICT-specialist share in Eurostat's 2025 table.

**Hiring.** No graduate calendar or programme page was read for any Icelandic employer (gap). Whether employers expect Icelandic was not researched.

## 3. Hubs

### Reykjavík

**What it is.** The capital and the only hub: 139,804 residents in the city, 251,912 in the capital region (Statistics Iceland, MAN02005) [data]. No GDP or pay figure is published for it.

**Sectors and employers.** Banking: Landsbankinn, Arion Bank, Íslandsbanki (Kópavogur) [employer-stated]. Games and software: CCP Games, with studios in Reykjavik, London and Shanghai [employer-stated]. Energy: Landsvirkjun, Katrínartún 2 [employer-stated]. Nationally there are 9,104 ICT and 6,610 finance workers [data]; the city's share is not published, but the capital region has 64% of the population.

## 5. Hypotheses tested

1. **"Iceland is a tech hub because of its start-ups."** Not supported: $3 billion of ecosystem value and $94 million of early-stage funding in H2 2023-2025 are far below the European average [practitioner consensus].
2. **"A small market means low graduate pay."** Not supported on the data read: sector pay in finance is ISK 1,372 thousand a month. Cost of living was not tested, and no entry-level figure was read (insufficient evidence).
3. **"Iceland is easy to stay in after a master's."** Partly supported for EU/EEA citizens (free movement), not for others: the post-study window fell from three years to 18 months on 8 July 2026 [data].

## 6. Common mistakes and myths

- **Iceland is in the EEA, not the EU**: EEA citizens register a domicile within three months; the rules are not the EU's own.
- **Tourism is not a graduate career**: it is 9.5% of hours but the record finds no graduate-employer evidence in it.
- **Íslandsbanki is not in Reykjavík city**: its address is in Kópavogur, a neighbouring municipality.

## 7. Decision rules

1. If you want finance in Iceland, target the three banks; they are the only named finance employers read.
2. If you are an EEA citizen, register your domicile within three months of arriving.
3. If you are non-EU, plan for 18 months after graduating, not three years.
4. If you want software, accept a very small market: one named employer was read (CCP Games).
5. If pay matters, compare ISK pay with your own rent estimate; do not convert at a remembered rate.
