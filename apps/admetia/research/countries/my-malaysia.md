# Malaysia: where a European business or computing graduate should look

Tags: `[data]` official statistics; `[employer-stated]` an employer's or agency's own page; `[practitioner consensus]` a published ranking; `[anecdotal]` crowd-sourced. All pages read on 3 October 2026.

## 1. Bottom line

- **Kuala Lumpur is the finance and corporate hub.** CIMB says it grew from its headquarters in Kuala Lumpur to around 33,000 employees (Integrated Annual Report 2025) `[employer-stated]`; Deutsche Bank (Malaysia) and Bank of America Malaysia have their head offices there (Bank Negara Malaysia directory) `[data]`. The GFCI 40 ranks Kuala Lumpur 39th in the world and Labuan 44th (Z/Yen, Sep 2026) `[practitioner consensus]`.
- **Pay in Kuala Lumpur is the highest in the three hubs**: the median formal-sector monthly wage was RM4,391 in December 2025, against RM3,500 in Penang and RM2,982 in Johor (DOSM) `[data]`.
- **Penang is an engineering city with a business and computing fringe.** Penang's economy grew 7.3% to RM130.3 billion in 2025, led by electrical, electronic and optical products up 12.7% (DOSM) `[data]`; Intel Malaysia, founded there in 1972, covers manufacturing, product design and development and global shared services (MIDA) `[employer-stated]`.
- **Johor is the data-centre hub**: it grew fastest of all states in 2025 (8.0%, to RM171 billion) on data centres (DOSM) `[data]`; Malaysia recorded RM385.7 billion of data-centre investment from 2021 to the first half of 2026, with operators present "particularly in Greater Kuala Lumpur and Johor" (MIDA) `[data]`. Those investments create more construction and operations jobs than graduate IT roles.
- **The work-rights step is the filter.** Full rules are verified in visas_immigration/malaysia/malaysia_visas_immigration_guide.md. International students may work only in term breaks, at most 20 hours a week, in restaurants, petrol kiosks, mini markets and hotels, and not as cashiers, with prior Immigration Department approval (EMGS) `[data]`; from 1 June 2026 the lowest Employment Pass category needs RM5,000 a month, up from RM3,000 (ESD Announcement 266, Ministry of Home Affairs) `[data]`. A 12-month Graduate Pass exists for 32 countries (UK, DE, FR, etc.) but excludes Italian and several EU citizens; no job-search permit exists for foreign-degree holders.
- **For a European, search Kuala Lumpur first** for banking and corporate roles, Penang for design and shared-services roles at multinationals, and Johor only for cloud and data-centre operations.

## 2. The economy and the graduate labour market

**Size and structure.** Kuala Lumpur's economy grew 5.2% in 2025 to RM265.1 billion and was the second-largest contributor to the national economy after Selangor; services were more than 90% of it, and finance, insurance, real estate and business services grew 5.3% (DOSM, GDP by State 2025, 1 Jul 2026) `[data]`. Penang produced RM130.3 billion and Johor RM171 billion, nearly 10% of the national economy (same release) `[data]`. National sectors in the record: banking and financial services, electronics manufacturing, oil, gas and petrochemicals, trade and logistics, tourism.

**Entry pay.** DOSM gives median monthly wages of formal-sector employees by state (above); the national median was RM3,167 in December 2025. These are all employees, not graduates. No graduate starting-salary survey was read.

**How hiring works.** Not researched: no graduate calendar read.

**Language.** Not researched.

**Visa headline.** Italian, EU, EEA, Swiss and British citizens can visit for tourism or business without a visa and normally stay up to 90 days (Malaysian Embassy Rome, JIM) `[data]`; all foreign visitors must submit the Malaysia Digital Arrival Card (MDAC) online within 3 days before arrival. The record's route section and visas_immigration/malaysia/malaysia_visas_immigration_guide.md have the full student-work, DE Rantau and Employment Pass detail. The UK Foreign Office advises against all but essential travel to the islands and dive sites off eastern Sabah (FCDO) `[data]`.

## 3. Hubs

Metric method: **population** is the state or federal territory, published by DOSM to the nearest 0.1 million (Kuala Lumpur 2.1 million, Pulau Pinang 1.8, Johor 4.2; `region`), 2025. **GDP** is GDP by state, 2025 (`region`). **Wage** is the state's median monthly wage of formal-sector employees, December 2025 (`region`, median). **Rent** is left out: Numbeo returned HTTP 429 and no official city rent series exists in the sources read.

### Kuala Lumpur

- **What it is.** The capital federal territory: 2.1 million residents, RM265.1 billion output, services above 90% `[data]`.
- **Finance.** CIMB (headquartered in the city; its tower is Menara CIMB at Kuala Lumpur Sentral; around 33,000 employees) `[employer-stated]`; Deutsche Bank (Malaysia) Berhad on Jalan Sultan Ismail and Bank of America Malaysia Berhad in Menara Merdeka 118 `[data]`. GFCI 40: 39th, up three places `[practitioner consensus]`.
- **Start-ups.** GSER 2026: emerging 31–40 and 19th in Asia, after slipping more than 15 places `[practitioner consensus]`.
- **Standing** (national / Asia-Pacific / world): finance 5 / 2 / 1; IT 5 / 2 / 1.
- **Ratings changed:** finance and banking present → **strong** (three named banks with head offices in the city plus GFCI).
- **Not read:** Maybank (its new head office, Menara Merdeka Maybank, was reported by press; the annual report would not download), Petronas (the financial report would not download), Public Bank.

### Penang (George Town)

- **What it is.** Malaysia's electrical and electronics hub: 1.8 million residents, RM130.3 billion output `[data]`.
- **Employers.** Intel Malaysia (1972; manufacturing, design and development, shared services) `[employer-stated]`; Infineon (Phase 2, Bayan Lepas Free Industrial Zone) `[employer-stated]`; Micron lists Penang as a manufacturing location `[employer-stated]`. No headcount was read on an official page.
- **Standing:** IT 4 / 2 / 1. **Rating:** IT not rated → present (Intel's design and shared-services functions; one named employer with a major site).

### Johor Bahru

- **What it is.** The state across from Singapore: 4.2 million residents, RM171 billion output `[data]`.
- **Employers.** Data-centre operators named by the investment authority: AWS, Microsoft, Google, Bridge Data Centres, DayOne, AirTrunk, Vantage `[data]`.
- **Standing:** IT 4 / 2 / 1. **Rating:** IT strong (unchanged, with the MIDA claim added). Caveat in the record: the jobs created are mostly construction and operations.

## 5. Hypotheses tested

**H1. "Malaysia is a cheap place to build a career after a European master's."** Not supported on the evidence read. Students' work rights are narrow, European master's graduates have no job-search route (and the local Graduate Pass excludes Italy), and an Employment Pass now needs RM5,000 a month (RM 10,000 for Cat II, RM 20,000 for Cat I) while the national median formal wage is RM3,167 `[data]`.

**H2. "Johor's data-centre boom means IT graduate jobs."** Partly supported. Output and investment are very large `[data]`, but the roles are largely construction and operations; no source counts graduate IT hiring.

**H3. "Penang's chip boom is a business-graduate market."** Insufficient evidence: the sources read describe manufacturing output and one employer's functions, not hiring by role.

## 6. Common mistakes and myths

(a) Treating a state wage as a graduate wage; (b) comparing the population figures, which are rounded to 0.1 million, to a city-level figure elsewhere; (c) believing any foreign degree holder can stay to search for work: the Graduate Pass is restricted to 32 countries (excluding Italy) and requires a Malaysian university degree; (d) relying on informal immigration advice instead of the single source of truth in visas_immigration/malaysia/malaysia_visas_immigration_guide.md.

## 7. Decision rules

1. If the target is banking or corporate finance, prefer Kuala Lumpur: CIMB, Deutsche Bank and Bank of America are headquartered or have their Malaysian head offices there.
2. If you hold a European passport and intend to work after a master's, plan for an Employment Pass (RM5,000 a month for Cat III, RM10,000 for Cat II, RM20,000 for Cat I from 1 June 2026) sponsored by an ESD-registered employer from the first day: no graduate job-search route exists for Italian and most EU passports (see visas_immigration/malaysia/malaysia_visas_immigration_guide.md).
3. If the target is hardware design or shared services at a multinational, look at Penang (Intel Malaysia).
4. If the target is cloud operations, look at Johor, but expect operations roles.
5. Compare hubs with the same pay basis (median formal wage by state).
