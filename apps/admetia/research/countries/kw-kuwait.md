# Kuwait: country brief

Today is 3 October 2026. Tags: `[data]` statistics, published indices and government pages, `[employer-stated]`, `[practitioner consensus]`, `[anecdotal]`. Routes in the Atlas are for EU/EEA/Swiss and UK passports only. On 20 August 2026 the UK Foreign Office stopped advising against all but essential travel to Kuwait but still warns of regional attacks (FCDO, https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/kuwait) [data]; read the current page.

## 1. Bottom line

- **Kuwait is a market for nationals and sponsored specialists, and the evidence for Europeans is the thinnest of the six Gulf records.** No graduate route open to foreigners was found.
- **The two national graduate schemes read are for Kuwaitis.** The Kuwait Investment Authority's eleven-month Fresh Graduate Training Program (more than 600 alumni since 1995; ten-week international assignment) is "designed for high-performing nationals" and takes applicants no older than 26 (KIA) [employer-stated]. Kuwait Petroleum Corporation's fresh-graduate campaigns for non-engineering majors are "restricted to nationals" (KPC) [employer-stated].
- **Banking is in Kuwait City.** The central bank lists five Kuwaiti conventional banks, among them National Bank of Kuwait (Sharq), Gulf Bank (Qibla) and Burgan Bank (Sharq), all with head offices in the capital's central districts (Central Bank of Kuwait, read locally from the page) [data].
- **It is a mid-table financial centre.** The Global Financial Centres Index 40 (September 2026) ranks Kuwait City 60th of 117 (67th in March), behind Dubai (9th), Abu Dhabi (13th), Riyadh (46th) and Doha (52nd) (Z/Yen and CDI) [data].
- **Every job is employer-sponsored.** EU citizens need a sponsored work permit in every Gulf state, with residence tied to the contract (library) [data].

## 2. The economy and the graduate labour market

**Size.** The 2021 census counted 4,385,717 people; the 2025 estimates read in search results (4,881,254, of whom about 31% Kuwaiti) were not on a page I could open and are not used (Central Statistical Office via City Population) [data]. No GDP figure was read from an official page: the national statistics bureau and the IMF refused automated reads. Press reports put 2024 growth at about -1% on OPEC+ output cuts, with non-oil activity recovering; not used as a claim.

**Structure.** Oil and gas dominate public revenue; the public sector is the main employer of Kuwaitis; banking and trade are the other large sectors. None of this was measured on a page I read, so the Atlas rates only finance, and only as present.

**Youth and graduate unemployment, entry pay.** Not read.

**How hiring works.** Government and sovereign employers recruit Kuwaiti graduates through announced campaigns and national programmes. Private banks and multinationals hire expatriates on sponsored permits. No Kuwaitisation quota was read.

**Language of work.** Arabic in government; English in banks and multinationals. Not measured.

**Visa headline.** British citizens need a visa and can get one on arrival or online; EU citizens need a sponsored work permit (see the Atlas record) [data].

## 3. Hubs

### Kuwait City

The capital and the only city mapped. Population: 574,839 in the Capital governorate in the 2021 census, which contains Kuwait City (area: region, a governorate, so it is not a city-proper count). Farwaniya (1,110,560) and Hawalli (926,170) are larger governorates in the same urban area, so the figure understates the built-up area.

**Standing, [national, Middle East, world].** Finance [5, 3, 2]: the only Kuwaiti centre in the GFCI (national 5); fifth among Middle Eastern centres in the index after Dubai, Abu Dhabi, Riyadh and Doha (regional 3, held down because only a handful of centres are listed and the step needs a notable rather than a major rank); 60th of 117 is present but small (world 2) [data].

**Employers.** National Bank of Kuwait, Gulf Bank, Burgan Bank (head offices); the Kuwait Investment Authority and Kuwait Petroleum Corporation (national schemes for Kuwaitis) [employer-stated].

## 5. Hypotheses tested

1. **"A sovereign fund's graduate programme is a way in for Europeans."** Not supported: KIA's is for nationals, as are PIF's (Saudi Arabia), QIA's (Qatar), ADIA's (UAE).
2. **"Kuwait's banks hire European graduates directly."** Insufficient evidence: no bank's graduate page was read.
3. **"GFCI 60th means a larger market than Doha."** Not supported: Doha ranks 52nd and has a free zone with over 4,700 firms.

## 6. Common mistakes and myths

- Reading "Kuwait Investment Authority" as a route into asset management for non-Kuwaitis: its own page says "nationals" [employer-stated].
- Applying through KPC for an engineering job: engineers apply through Kuwait Oil Company or Kuwait National Petroleum Company, and the campaigns are for nationals [employer-stated].

## 7. Decision rules

1. If you have no Kuwaiti nationality, do not plan around KIA or KPC.
2. If you want a Gulf first job, compare Dubai, Abu Dhabi and Riyadh first; Kuwait has the least documented graduate market.
3. If you receive a Kuwaiti offer from a bank, ask who sponsors the permit, what the contract says about notice, and whether the role counts toward a Kuwaitisation quota.
4. Check the FCDO and Farnesina pages dated within the last month.
