# Austria: country brief

Today is 3 October 2026. Tags: `[data]` official statistics or government pages, `[employer-stated]`, `[practitioner consensus]` (published indices and specialist synthesis), `[anecdotal]`.

## 1. Bottom line

- Austria is Vienna plus three industrial regional capitals. Vienna has 1,127,400 jobs (Eurostat, 2023), more than the next three regions together (Linz-Wels 388,100, Graz 295,900, Salzburg 226,400) [data].
- Vienna is the business hub rather than the finance hub: more than 412 international companies run international headquarters from Austria, 45% of them in Vienna (Austrian Business Agency) [data], but the Global Financial Centres Index 40 (September 2026) ranks Vienna only 81st in the world, below Zurich (10th), Paris (23rd), Frankfurt (29th), Munich (58th) and Milan (62nd) [practitioner consensus]. National leader in business and finance, minor in Europe.
- Industry is where the regional capitals are strongest: Linz-Wels has 75,700 jobs in manufacturing, 20% of its total, against 5% in Vienna; voestalpine is headquartered in Linz and reports 48,800 employees worldwide [data, employer-stated]. AVL, a mobility-technology group, is headquartered in Graz with 12,000 employees [employer-stated]. Porsche Holding Salzburg, Europe's largest automotive distributor, is headquartered in Salzburg [employer-stated].
- Pay is modest by DACH standards and low outside the capital's finance: employees under 30 in firms with 10 or more staff earned a mean €37,371 in 2022, and under-30 professionals €47,987 (Eurostat SES) [data]; the median gross annual income of all employees was €36,022 in Vienna, €40,845 in Upper Austria and €39,557 in Styria in 2024 (Statistik Austria, part-time included) [data].
- Graduates find jobs, with a catch for the very young: 89.2% of tertiary graduates aged 20 to 34 were employed in 2025, unemployment was 5.7% overall and 11.5% for 15-to-24-year-olds [data].
- Language is the entry condition: McKinsey's recruiters say fluent German is mandatory for the Germany and Austria offices [employer-stated]; English works in international headquarters and some start-ups.
- For a European master's student: Vienna for headquarters, consulting and banking roles that need German (WU is the programme in the record); Linz or Graz for engineering and industrial management; do not expect Salzburg to be a graduate market beyond tourism, trade and Porsche Holding.

## 2. The economy and the graduate labour market

**Size.** Population 9,197,213 on 1 January 2025; GDP €518.2 billion in 2025; unemployment 5.7% overall and 11.5% among 15-to-24-year-olds in 2025 (Eurostat demo_pjan, nama_10_gdp, une_rt_a) [data].

**Structure.** Vienna is a services city (368,100 jobs in public administration, education, health and other services; 342,900 in trade, transport, hospitality and information and communication; 286,100 in finance, real estate, professional and administrative services; 59,200 in manufacturing). Linz-Wels and Graz are industrial (manufacturing 75,700 and 45,200 jobs); Salzburg is trade, tourism and services (74,800 jobs in trade, transport, hospitality and ICT, 27,000 in manufacturing) [data]. Eurostat's tables give no finance-only or ICT-only split for Austrian regions (the metropolitan table has no values for them), so those families are rated on named employers only.

**Graduates.** In 2025 the employment rate of 20-to-34-year-olds with a tertiary degree was 89.2%, and 88.8% for those who finished within the last five years (Eurostat edat_lfse_24) [data]. The library's file says 90.8% of recent tertiary graduates in Austria were in work in 2025, among the highest rates in the EU (Eurostat, via places/countries-and-cities.md) [data]; the two figures differ because they use different age and years-since-leaving cuts.

**Entry pay.** Eurostat's Structure of Earnings Survey 2022 (firms with 10 or more staff, public administration excluded): mean annual gross earnings of €37,371 for employees under 30, €47,987 for under-30 professionals, €52,526 for all ages (earn_ses22_28) [data]. Statistik Austria's payroll-tax data for 2024 give the median gross annual income of employees by state (apprentices excluded, part-time and part-year work included): Austria €39,121, Vienna €36,022, Upper Austria €40,845, Styria €39,557, Salzburg €37,351 [data]. Annual figures include the 13th and 14th monthly payments; the Atlas divides by 12.

**How hiring works.** Large employers recruit through their own graduate pages; no calendar was read. Consulting expects fluent German (McKinsey, via e-fellows, 1 Aug 2025) [employer-stated].

**Language and permits.** German is the language of most corporate roles. Anyone who takes up accommodation registers within three days (Meldegesetz §3); EU, EEA and Swiss citizens staying more than three months apply for a registration certificate within four months; non-EU students may work 20 hours a week with an employer-obtained permit; after an Austrian degree the permit can be renewed for 12 months to look for work and a matching job leads to a Red-White-Red Card for graduates with no labour-market test [data]. See `visas_immigration/austria/austria_visas_immigration_guide.md` for the complete verified immigration and permit guide.

## 3. Hubs

Population and GDP are Eurostat NUTS 3 regions (`demo_r_pjangrp3`, population 1 January 2025; `nama_10r_3gdp`, GDP 2023): Wien (the city), Linz-Wels, Graz and Salzburg und Umgebung, `area: region`. Pay is Statistik Austria's median annual income of employees of the federal state, ÷ 12, `area: region`. Rent is Numbeo's average for a one-bedroom flat in the city centre (crowd-sourced, `anecdotal`), read through the Internet Archive's snapshots.

### Vienna

**What it is.** The capital and headquarters city: 2,028,289 residents, GDP €120.0 billion (2023), median pay in the state €3,002 a month (annual €36,022 ÷ 12), a one-bedroom flat about €1,168. The programmes in the record are WU's Strategy, Innovation and Management Control and Quantitative Finance.

**Employers.** International headquarters (Boehringer Ingelheim, Siemens, Henkel, Takeda, BMW Group among 412+ companies [data]); Raiffeisen Bank International (headquarters in Vienna [employer-stated]); a start-up ecosystem Startup Genome values at $13 billion, below the European average of $14.3 billion, with $516 million of seed and Series A funding in H2 2023 to 2025 and $2 billion of exits in 2021 to 2025 [practitioner consensus].

**Standing.**

**Re-rating.** Finance: not rated → present, on Raiffeisen Bank International's headquarters (at-rbi). Business stays strong; management stays present.

### Linz

**What it is.** Upper Austria's industrial capital: 617,181 residents in Linz-Wels, GDP €40.7 billion, median pay in the state €3,404 (the highest of the four), rent about €729. 388,100 jobs, 75,700 of them in manufacturing [data].

**Employers.** voestalpine, a steel and technology group headquartered in Linz (48,800 employees worldwide, €15.1 billion revenue in 2025/26) [employer-stated]. The region's 461,300 jobs and €41.4 billion GDP in the 2021 metropolitan table are kept in the record.

**Standing.** Business 3 / 2 / 1: second Austrian region by jobs and home of a 48,800-employee group; no source says how many entry posts go to business graduates, so no family is rated for demand.

### Graz

**What it is.** Styria's capital: 469,968 residents, GDP €27.7 billion, median pay in Styria €3,296, rent about €701 (the lowest of the four). 295,900 jobs, 45,200 in manufacturing [data].

**Employers.** AVL List, headquartered in Graz, with 12,000 employees at more than 90 locations and a stated focus on electrification, software, artificial intelligence and automation [employer-stated].

**Standing.** Software 3 / 2 / 1: a named employer headquartered in Graz states a software focus; there is no statistic by family, so demand for software is present, not strong.

### Salzburg

**What it is.** A smaller region of trade, tourism and services: 378,982 residents, GDP €25.5 billion, median pay in the state €3,113, rent about €1,275. 226,400 jobs, 74,800 in trade, transport, hospitality and ICT [data].

**Employers.** Porsche Holding Salzburg: Europe's largest automotive distributor, roots in Salzburg, operations in 29 countries, more than 36,900 employees worldwide, and the company with the highest revenue in Austria in 2025 by its own account [employer-stated].

**Standing.** Business 2 / 1 / 1: fourth Austrian region by jobs among those read and one headquarters; demand present.

## 5. Hypotheses tested

1. **Vienna is the finance centre of central Europe.** Not supported by GFCI 40 (81st in the world); supported only as the headquarters of Raiffeisen Bank International and the base for regional offices. Holds for CEE-facing banking; fails for trading, asset management and wholesale finance.
2. **Vienna pays more than the industrial regions.** Not supported: Vienna's median annual income (€36,022) is below Upper Austria's (€40,845) and Styria's (€39,557), though Vienna's mean (€43,464, same file) is higher than Salzburg's and the median is pulled down by part-time work. Fails if you compare full-time graduate offers.
3. **German is optional in Austria.** Not supported for consulting (McKinsey: fluent German mandatory) and most corporate roles; partly supported for international headquarters and start-ups.
4. **Graz is a software city.** Insufficient evidence: one employer's focus statement (AVL) and 45,200 manufacturing jobs, no statistic by family.

## 6. Common mistakes and myths

- Treating Vienna as a Frankfurt: GFCI 40 ranks Frankfurt 29th and Vienna 81st [practitioner consensus].
- Reading the median annual income as a graduate salary: it covers all employees including part-time, and Vienna's median is lowest because of that mix [data].
- Assuming the non-EU route needs a quota: the Red-White-Red Card for graduates has no labour-market test, but the pay must at least match comparable Austrian graduates [data].

## 7. Decision rules

1. If you target headquarters, consulting or CEE-facing roles and read German, choose Vienna.
2. If you target banking in Vienna, check that the employer states a Vienna headquarters (Raiffeisen Bank International does); expect a regional bank, not a trading floor.
3. If you target engineering or industrial management, choose Linz (voestalpine) or Graz (AVL) over Vienna.
4. If you target automotive distribution, choose Salzburg (Porsche Holding) and compare it with Wolfsburg and Stuttgart in Germany.
5. If you are non-EU and study in Austria, plan on the 12-month search permit and the Red-White-Red Card for graduates.
6. If rent decides, Graz (€701) and Linz (€729) are about 40% cheaper than Vienna (€1,168) for the same one-bedroom flat in the centre (Numbeo, anecdotal), and Linz's median pay is higher.
