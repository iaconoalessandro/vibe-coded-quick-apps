# Singapore: country brief

Today is 3 October 2026. Tags: `[data]` official statistics or government pages, `[employer-stated]`, `[practitioner consensus]` (published indices and specialist synthesis), `[anecdotal]`.

## 1. Bottom line

- Singapore is one hub, and it is a top-four financial centre: the Global Financial Centres Index 40 (September 2026) ranks it fourth in the world and second in Asia/Pacific, one rating point behind Hong Kong (Z/Yen, Long Finance, 2026) [practitioner consensus].
- Finance employs close to 200,000 people in over 2,500 licensed institutions, but over 80% of the workforce is local and nine in ten net new finance jobs in 2018 to 2023 went to locals (MAS, 10 Mar 2025) [data]. A European enters through a graduate programme or an internship, not the open market.
- Tech is bigger than it looks: 222,170 tech professionals in 2025, and 58.7% of tech and media staff work outside the infocomm sector (IMDA, May 2026) [data]. A banking, logistics or consulting employer is also a tech employer.
- Shipping is the third pillar: the port handled a record 44.66 million TEU in 2025, the city kept first place in the Xinhua-Baltic shipping-centre index and has more than 200 international shipping groups (MPA, 13 Jan 2026) [data].
- The permit is the filter, not the labour market. The Employment Pass floor is S$5,600 a month at 23 or below (S$6,200 in financial services), rising to S$6,000 (S$6,600) on 1 January 2027 (MOM, Apr 2026) [data]. Local business and economics graduates of SMU report median pay of S$4,350 to S$4,600 (SMU, 2025) [data], so the floor sits above typical entry pay outside finance and tech.
- For a European master's student: choose Singapore for finance, tech or shipping with a named employer or an internship lined up; do not move for a general business job.

## 2. The economy and the graduate labour market

**Size and structure.** GDP at current prices was S$789.5 billion in 2025, up 5.0% (Ministry of Trade and Industry, Economic Survey of Singapore 2025) [data]. Services grew 4.3%; wholesale trade and information and communications grew fastest, both 6.1% (same report) [data]. The population was 6.11 million in June 2025, of whom 4.20 million were citizens or permanent residents (Population in Brief 2025) [data]. 1.91 million were non-residents, so about three in ten people in the city hold some kind of work pass or dependant status.

**Pay.** The median gross monthly income of full-time employed residents was S$5,775 in 2025 (employer pension contributions included); for degree holders it was S$9,038 (Ministry of Manpower, Labour Force in Singapore 2025) [data]. For fresh graduates, SMU's 2025 survey (the six universities run it jointly) gives median gross pay of S$4,600 in business management, S$4,500 in economics, S$4,350 in accountancy and S$5,400 in information systems, for those in full-time permanent jobs about six months after finishing (SMU annex, 2025) [data]. A trade-press report of the joint survey gives S$4,500 as the all-university median (Human Resources Online, 5 Mar 2026; not read on a university page) [secondary]. Note that these are bachelor's graduates, mostly Singaporean; no coursework-master's outcomes are published (places/beyond-europe.md §4.2) [data].

**Unemployment.** The resident unemployment rate for professionals, managers, executives and technicians was 2.8% in 2025 (MOM, Labour Force 2025) [data]. No youth or graduate-specific rate was extracted.

**Language.** English is the common language of work, and the language most often spoken at home by 48.3% of residents aged five and over (Census 2020) [data].

**How hiring works.** Large banks and tech firms run graduate programmes and summer internships, and the library's recruiting calendar (getting-in/recruiting-calendar.md) applies. The Work Holiday Pass (18 to 25, six months, 2,000 places, tied to where you studied) is one way in without a sponsor (MOM) [data].

**Visa headline.** European passports need no entry visa. After studying in Singapore a graduate can apply for a Long-Term Visit Pass to look for work; otherwise the route is the Employment Pass with its salary floor and the 40-point COMPASS test. Detail: the Atlas record's route section.

## 3. Hub: Singapore

**What the city is.** A city-state with the country's whole economy. Population 6,110,000 (June 2025, total); GDP S$789.5 billion (2025); median gross monthly pay S$5,775 (2025, residents); a one-bedroom flat in the centre S$3,804 a month on Numbeo (crowd-sourced, 2 Oct 2026) [anecdotal]. Because it is a city-state all four metrics are national figures and area is "city".

**Sectors and employers.**
- *Finance.* Over 2,500 licensed institutions and close to 200,000 jobs (MAS) [data]. DBS says it is headquartered and listed in Singapore, present in 19 markets, with total assets of SGD 897 billion (DBS Annual Report 2025) [employer-stated]. Banking is rated strong. I could not read OCBC, UOB, GIC or Temasek (Temasek returned "access denied"), so they are not listed.
- *Technology.* 222,170 tech professionals (IMDA). Sea Limited, founded in Singapore in 2009, runs Garena, Shopee and Monee and calls Shopee the largest pan-regional e-commerce platform in Southeast Asia, Taiwan and Brazil (Sea, 3 Mar 2026) [employer-stated]. Grab's website gives a Singapore office address but nothing on headcount or graduate schemes, so it is not listed.
- *Shipping.* The Maritime and Port Authority reports 44.66 million TEU in 2025 (up 8.6%), the DNV-Menon "leading container port" title, and 35 maritime companies opening or expanding in the year, for more than 200 international shipping groups (MPA) [data].

**Universities and business schools.** NUS, NTU and SMU are on the Ministry of Manpower's top-tier list (20 qualification points under COMPASS), with INSEAD's MiM and MBA also in the calculator (places/beyond-europe.md §4.1) [data].

**Start-ups.** Startup Genome's 2026 report ranks Singapore eighth in the world and second in Asia (Startup Genome, Singapore page) [practitioner consensus].

**Standing.** Steps are national, Asia-Pacific, world.

## 5. Hypotheses tested

1. **"Singapore's pay floor, not its points system, is what stops a fresh European graduate."** Supported. The Employment Pass floor of S$5,600 to S$6,200 is above SMU's median entry pay of S$4,350 to S$4,600 for business, economics and accountancy, and close to the S$5,400 for information systems [data]. It fits finance and tech, where the pass salary is below typical offers.
2. **"Singapore finance is a closed shop."** Partly supported. Over 80% of finance staff are local and nine in ten net new jobs went to locals (MAS), but the sector still has about 200,000 jobs and expat-facing programmes; the data do not show how many graduate hires are foreign [data].
3. **"Singapore is a tech city only for tech firms."** Not supported. 58.7% of tech and media staff work outside the infocomm sector (IMDA), so tech skills are valued across banking, logistics and consulting [data].
4. **"Rankings overstate Singapore against Hong Kong."** Insufficient evidence. GFCI 40 separates the two by one rating point, within the noise of a survey-based index [practitioner consensus].

## 6. Common mistakes and myths

- Quoting the all-NUS median (about S$4,750) as what a European master's graduate will earn; it is a bachelor's, all-nationality figure (places/beyond-europe.md §4.2) [data].
- Assuming a Singapore degree grants a work pass; it grants a job-search visit pass, then the same salary floor applies [data].
- Assuming the CPF pension applies to you: foreign employees, Employment Pass holders included, do not pay in (CPF Board) [data].
- Treating "world's leading container port" as a claim about jobs; it is a ranking of the port (MPA).

## 7. Decision rules

1. If your target is banking, markets or asset management, prefer Singapore with an internship first, because the permit floor is lowest relative to offers in financial services (S$6,200, MOM).
2. If you hold a top-tier degree (NUS, NTU, SMU, Oxford, Cambridge, LSE, Imperial, TUM and others), expect 20 COMPASS qualification points, so the salary floor is the binding test (MOM C2 list, via places/beyond-europe.md).
3. If your degree is from a school not on the list (Bocconi, Mannheim, RSM were not found), plan for 10 points and a thinner margin on the other criteria.
4. If you want logistics or maritime, prefer Singapore over other Asian ports for the breadth of shipping groups (200+), but check that the employer will sponsor.
5. If you are a UK or EU 18-to-25 graduate of a university in France, Germany, the Netherlands, Switzerland or the UK, consider the Work Holiday Pass (six months) as a trial period, not a career route.
6. If a salary offer is below the floor, do not count on sponsorship: ask about the quota and local-hiring rules before accepting.

## 8. Gaps and claims to verify

1. The all-university 2025 graduate median of S$4,500 was read only in trade press.
2. No OCBC, UOB, GIC, Temasek, Grab or consulting-firm employer pages were read.
3. The duration of the graduate Long-Term Visit Pass is not stated on the ICA page.
4. The Numbeo rent is crowd-sourced; no official rent series was read.
5. No published outcomes for coursework master's graduates in Singapore.
6. Whether foreign graduate hires form a measurable share of finance entry hiring.
