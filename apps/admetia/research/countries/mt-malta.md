# Malta (Atlas brief, round 5, 3 October 2026)

Tags: [data], [employer-stated], [practitioner consensus], [anecdotal]. The record is `data/atlas/mt.js`; the quotes behind each claim are in `research/verification/round-5b.md`.

## 1. Bottom line

- **Malta is a one-city labour market built on two sectors.** Online gaming (302 licensed companies, 15,039 full-time equivalents working in Malta at the end of 2025) and financial services (about 6.2% of the workforce) are the distinctive employers (Malta Gaming Authority; MFSA, 2026) [data].
- **Finance and IT are bigger here than in the EU.** In 2025 finance and insurance were 5.1% of jobs (EU 2.3%) and information and communication 4.8% (EU 3.5%), together about one job in ten (Eurostat) [data].
- **Pay in finance is above average.** The MFSA says financial-sector pay is about 36% above the national average [data]. Eurostat's 2022 survey gives mean monthly gross earnings of €2,749 in information and communication and €2,781 in finance and insurance against €2,080 for all sectors, and €1,832 for under-30s [data].
- **Recent graduates find work.** 92.8% of tertiary graduates aged 20 to 34 who left education up to three years earlier were in work in 2025 (EU 85.3%); unemployment was 3.4% in August 2026, and 9.7% for under-25s (EU 15.4%) [data].
- **Accountancy and banking are the entry doors named in sources.** Deloitte Malta's International Graduate Programme is for accounting graduates, KPMG says it houses over 600 professionals in Pietà, and four banks (Bank of Valletta, HSBC Malta, APS, Lombard) are headquartered in the built-up core [employer-stated; data].
- **It is not a major financial centre.** The Global Financial Centres Index 40 ranks Malta 63rd in the world and outside the Western European top 15 [practitioner consensus].
- **For non-EU students**: 20 hours a week of work with a licence, and at least nine months after graduating to look for a job [data].

## 2. The economy and the graduate labour market

**Size and structure.** Malta had 574,250 residents on 1 January 2025 and a GDP of €23.1 billion in 2024; the island of Malta (without Gozo) has 93% of the people and 96% of the output (Eurostat) [data]. In 2025, 338,190 people were in employment (Eurostat) [data]. Gaming's gross value added was about €1,422 million, 6.3% of output, with an estimated 19,150 jobs directly or indirectly tied to the sector, about 6.5% of the workforce (MGA, annual report 2025) [data]. Financial and insurance activities produced about €1.3 billion, 7.3% of total value added, against about 4% in the euro area (MFSA) [data].

**Labour market.** Unemployment: 3.4% (August 2026; EU 6.1%) and 9.7% for under-25s (EU 15.4%). Recent-graduate employment: 92.8% (EU 85.3%) (Eurostat) [data].

**Entry pay.** The best figure is Eurostat's structure of earnings survey for 2022, for firms with ten or more employees: mean monthly gross earnings €2,080 for all, €1,832 for under-30s, €2,749 in information and communication, €2,781 in finance and insurance, €2,566 in professional, scientific and technical activities. Mean annual: €30,960, €39,711 and €39,899 [data]. This is older than 2023 and covers all employees, not graduates; no figure for tertiary-educated staff is published for Malta.

**How hiring works.** Deloitte Malta's International Graduate Programme: for people who have recently graduated or are about to graduate in accounting, work in Audit & Assurance in Malta, a salary package, a relocation allowance and a permanent contract; the page gives no pay or dates [employer-stated]. KPMG Malta runs a Graduate Recruitment Programme within an office of over 600 professionals [employer-stated]. The MFSA had 45 students in its Pre-Graduate Programme and works with the University of Malta on a postgraduate course in financial regulation and compliance [data].

**Language.** Maltese and English are the official EU languages; the programmes read are in English [data; employer-stated].

**Tax.** For 2026 a single resident pays 0% on the first €12,000, 15% to €16,000, 25% to €60,000 and 35% above; employee and employer each pay social security of 10% of salary, at a fixed €55.93 a week for annual salaries above €29,084 (PwC tax summaries, reviewed 30 September 2026) [practitioner consensus].

**Visa headline.** EU and EEA nationals staying over three months register with Identità. Non-EU students on courses at level 5 or above may work up to 20 hours a week with an employment licence, and graduates may apply for a permit of at least nine months to look for work (EU Immigration Portal, April 2025) [data]. Detail: visas_immigration/malta/malta_visas_immigration_guide.md.

**Metric basis.** Population and GDP: Eurostat NUTS 3 region MT001, the island of Malta (area: region); population on 1 January 2025 is 532,997 and GDP for 2024 is €22.1 billion. Pay: the national Eurostat figure (area: region), because no regional split exists. Rent: not shown.

## 3. Hub

### Valletta and the harbour towns

**What it is.** Malta's only mapped hub: the built-up core around the capital's two harbours, where the head offices, banks, regulators and gaming offices sit. The record's addresses range from Valletta and Floriana to Sliema, Pietà, Birkirkara and Santa Venera; statistics are for the island or the country because none is published for these towns alone.

**Sectors and named employers.**
- *Gaming:* 302 MGA-licensed companies with 15,039 full-time equivalents; Kindred Group plc is headquartered at The Centre, Tigné Point, Sliema [data], and FDJ UNITED says its Malta site supports the group's online betting and gaming operations [employer-stated].
- *Banking and markets:* Bank of Valletta (Cannon Road, Santa Venera), HSBC Bank Malta (116 Archbishop Street, Valletta), APS Bank (APS Centre, Tower Street, Birkirkara), Lombard Bank Malta (67 Republic Street, Valletta), the Central Bank of Malta and the Malta Stock Exchange (both at Castille Place, Valletta) [data, Global LEI Index].
- *Regulation:* the MFSA supervised 2,413 authorised entities in 2025 with 575 staff, 90 of them international from 32 countries [data].
- *Professional services:* KPMG Malta (over 600 professionals in Pietà) and Deloitte Malta's audit graduate programme [employer-stated].

**Start-ups and universities.** No Startup Genome ecosystem page exists for Malta and no university ranking was read.

**Standing.**
- *Finance:* national 5, regional 2, world 2. It is the only hub and holds the sector. In Europe it is minor: the GFCI 40 ranks Malta 63rd in the world and outside the Western European top 15, and 17,100 finance and insurance jobs are small in absolute terms despite a share of employment more than double the EU's.
- *IT:* national 5, regional 2, world 1. The only hub; 16,250 jobs in information and communication (4.8% of employment); Malta has no ranking in start-up tables read here.

**Ratings.** Finance dominant (7.3% of value added, 6.2% of the workforce, 2,413 supervised entities); information technology strong (Eurostat share of jobs above the EU's); banking strong (four bank headquarters and the central bank); accounting present (KPMG and Deloitte, with no headcount for graduate intake). Other families are not rated: no source measures them.

## 5. Hypotheses tested

**H1. "Malta is an online-gaming island for graduates."** Partly supported. Gaming has 15,039 full-time equivalents in Malta, but the MGA does not split them by role, and finance employs 17,100 people against 16,250 in information and communication (MGA; Eurostat) [data]. It holds for IT and operations; it fails if the reader assumes the sector hires business graduates at scale.

**H2. "Finance in Malta pays much more than elsewhere in the country."** Supported. MFSA: about 36% above the national average; Eurostat 2022: €2,781 against €2,080 monthly (+34%) [data; arithmetic].

**H3. "Gaming is shrinking."** Not supported on 2025 data: licensed companies fell from 316 (2023) to 302 (2025) but full-time equivalents rose from 13,404 to 15,039, and the MGA calls the change consolidation (MGA, annual report 2025) [data].

**H4. "Malta is a major financial centre."** Not supported. GFCI 40 rank 63rd, fintech 89th [practitioner consensus].

## 6. Common mistakes and myths

1. **"A licensed company employs people in Malta."** The MGA counts 3,974 of the 15,039 full-time equivalents on activities not licensed by it (type B) [data].
2. **"Malta pay is the EU average."** The 2022 survey gives €2,080 a month for all employees, €1,832 for under-30s [data].
3. **"Malta has an official graduate pay table."** None was found; the Big Four pages give no figure [employer-stated].
4. **"Bigger share of finance jobs means a bigger financial centre."** Malta's finance and insurance jobs are 17,100, small beside any large European centre (Eurostat; GFCI) [data].

## 7. Decision rules

1. If you want finance in Malta, look at the banks, the regulator and the audit firms: they publish headquarters addresses and programmes; there is no large pool of graduate schemes.
2. If you are an accounting graduate wanting to work abroad in the EU, check Deloitte Malta's International Graduate Programme first (relocation allowance).
3. If you want gaming, use the regulator's list of licensed companies and ask each employer for the role split; the regulator counts jobs, not roles.
4. If you compare Malta with larger countries on pay, use under-30 pay (€1,832 in 2022) and treat it as a national mean, not a graduate salary.
5. If you are a non-EU student, plan for 20 hours a week with a licence and a nine-month permit after graduating.
6. If you need a deep market in one family (investment banking, consulting at scale), do not choose Malta (GFCI 63rd; one hub).

## 8. Gaps and claims to verify

1. No rent: Numbeo refused access and the Housing Authority's rent tool is an interactive app; no official one-bedroom rent was read.
2. Pay is a 2022 survey for the country; no split for graduates, tertiary education or the harbour towns.
3. National Statistics Office pages (registered employment, labour force survey, earnings) blocked automated access, so employment is Eurostat's national-accounts figure.
4. Kindred's Malta site and gaming role is read on a corporate news item only; no gaming employer's careers page was read.
5. No PwC, EY or bank graduate page was read; no intake sizes or dates for Deloitte or KPMG.
6. The hub's geography is the island's built-up core, not an official area.
