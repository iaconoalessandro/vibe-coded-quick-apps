# Switzerland: country brief

Today is 3 October 2026. Tags: `[data]` official statistics or government pages, `[employer-stated]`, `[practitioner consensus]` (published indices and specialist synthesis), `[anecdotal]`.

## 1. Bottom line

- Switzerland pays the most in Europe. In 2024 the Federal Statistical Office (BFS) put the median gross monthly wage of a full-time post at CHF 7,024, and at CHF 10,533 for employees with a university degree and CHF 9,288 for a university of applied sciences degree [data]. Eurostat's 2022 survey gives a mean of €70,488 a year for employees under 30 and €83,890 for under-30 professionals, nearly double Belgium's €38,114 [data].
- Zurich is the national centre for finance and the only Swiss hub with a world-class claim: the Global Financial Centres Index 40 (September 2026) ranks it 10th in the world and second in Western Europe after London, sixth for banking and for investment management [practitioner consensus]. The canton counts more than 103,400 finance jobs, one worker in ten [data]. Google's largest development centre outside the US is there [practitioner consensus].
- Geneva (12th in the GFCI, third in Western Europe) and Lugano (21st, fifth) are the two other ranked centres; Geneva and Lausanne add commodity trading, EPFL and IMD, Lugano a banking and trading niche in the Italian-speaking south.
- Basel is a pharma and logistics city (Roche and Novartis headquarters, the Bank for International Settlements, about 30% of Swiss exports by river, air and rail) and Zug is a commodity, wholesale and head-office canton: its largest private employers include Roche Diagnostics (2,833 employees in the canton), Glencore (1,175) and Partners Group (569).
- Bern, St. Gallen and Lucerne are mid-sized markets rated only on economic weight: second, sixth and seventh of Switzerland's seven metropolitan regions by GDP in Eurostat's tables. No family is rated for them.
- Graduates find jobs: 90.2% of tertiary graduates aged 20 to 34 were employed in 2025, and unemployment was 4.9% (8.8% for 15-to-24s) [data]. The cost is high: a one-bedroom flat in the centre is about CHF 2,467 in Zurich and CHF 3,180 in Zug (Numbeo) [anecdotal].
- For a European master's student: EU citizens move freely (register within 14 days); non-EU graduates face quotas (8,500 for 2026) and the economic-interest test, and Swiss graduates get a six-month search permission. Language of work was not researched; do not assume English suffices outside finance and engineering.

## 2. The economy and the graduate labour market

**Size.** Population 9,051,029 on 1 January 2025; GDP €925.9 billion in 2025; unemployment 4.9% overall and 8.8% among 15-to-24-year-olds in 2025 (Eurostat demo_pjan, nama_10_gdp, une_rt_a) [data].

**Structure.** Five clusters matter: banking, wealth management and insurance in Zurich (banks 43% and insurers 37% of the canton's financial value added [data]); pharma and life sciences in Basel (over 800 companies in the cluster [data]); commodity trading around Lake Geneva, in Zug and in Ticino (a trade body of 215+ members, about 2.3% of Swiss GDP [employer-stated]); engineering and technology (ETH, EPFL, Google); and the federal capital in Bern. Zug's cantonal statistics count 131,705 jobs in 2023, 14,693 of them in wholesale trade, 7,781 in head-office management and consultancy, 7,093 in IT services and 9,656 in financial services and activities auxiliary to finance [data].

**Graduates.** In 2025 the employment rate of 20-to-34-year-olds with a tertiary degree was 90.2%, and 90.1% for those who finished within the last five years (Eurostat edat_lfse_24) [data]. BFS, via the library, puts the median of Swiss university economics master's graduates at CHF 87,100 one year after graduating [data].

**Entry pay.** BFS Swiss Earnings Structure Survey 2024 (published 25 November 2025): median gross monthly wage CHF 7,024; the Zurich region CHF 7,502, north-western Switzerland CHF 7,156, central Switzerland CHF 7,092, Lake Geneva CHF 6,998, Espace Mittelland CHF 6,964, eastern Switzerland CHF 6,623, Ticino CHF 5,708. The BFS release itself names only Zürich and Ticino; the other regions are from a Bilanz report of the same survey [data]. Eurostat's 2022 survey gives under-30 employees a mean of €70,488 a year in firms of 10 or more (€83,890 for professionals). At €60,000 gross a single employee in Zurich keeps about 84% (library calculation); health insurance is separate: a basic premium in Zurich city is about CHF 459 a month for 19-to-25-year-olds [data].

**How hiring works.** No graduate-programme calendar was read for Swiss banks, insurers or the pharma groups. EU citizens do not need a prior labour-market test or sponsor, but must register with their commune before starting work to obtain an L or B permit; non-EU graduates of Swiss universities get a six-month search permission that cannot be extended; permits for non-EU nationals are capped at 8,500 for 2026 (4,500 B and 4,000 L), plus 3,500 for UK nationals; an employer can skip the priority test only if the job is of predominant scientific or economic interest (see single source of truth in `visas_immigration/switzerland/switzerland_visas_immigration_guide.md`) [data].

## 3. Hubs

Population and GDP for Zurich, Basel, Geneva and Lausanne (summed), Bern, St. Gallen and Lucerne are Eurostat metropolitan regions (`met_pjanaggr3`, population 1 January 2023; `met_10r_3gdp`, GDP 2021, in euro), `area: metro`. Pay is the BFS 2024 median of the large region the hub sits in, `area: region`. Rent is Numbeo's average for a one-bedroom flat in the city centre (crowd-sourced, `anecdotal`), read through the Internet Archive's snapshots. Zug and Lugano have no metropolitan region: their population is the canton of Zug (133,723, end 2024) and the city of Lugano (68,507, 31 December 2024), and neither has a GDP metric. The Geneva and Lausanne hub has no rent (two cities, CHF 2,321 and CHF 1,882).

### Zurich

**What it is.** The financial centre and Switzerland's largest city region: 1,579,967 residents, GDP €140.8 billion (2021), median pay in the Zurich region CHF 7,502, rent CHF 2,467. Zürich's canton counts 103,400 finance jobs generating CHF 32.8 billion [data].

**Employers.** Banks and insurers (their headcounts were not read), Google (largest development centre outside the US), and a start-up ecosystem Startup Genome values at $44 billion against a European average of $14.3 billion, with $2.4 billion of seed and Series A funding in H2 2023 to 2025 and $7 billion of exits in 2021 to 2025 [practitioner consensus]. ETH Zurich is the programme in the record (MSc Computer Science).

### Basel

**What it is.** Pharma and Rhine-port logistics: 724,230 residents, GDP €64.9 billion, median pay in north-western Switzerland CHF 7,156, rent CHF 1,643. Roche was founded there in 1896 [employer-stated]; the canton counts a life-sciences cluster of over 800 companies, the Bank for International Settlements and about 30% of Swiss exports [data].

**Standing.** Logistics 4 / 2 / 1: national 4 on the export share (no ranking against Zurich airport or Geneva was read, so no 5); no European port ranking applies to a Rhine port. Demand strong for logistics, present for finance, unchanged.

### Geneva and Lausanne

**What it is.** The Lake Geneva region: 1,344,545 residents in the two metropolitan regions, GDP €110.5 billion, median pay CHF 6,998. Commodity trading (215+ trade body members around the lake, in Zug and in Ticino), international organisations, private banking, EPFL and IMD. Startup Genome values Greater Lausanne's ecosystem at $18.7 billion and says its institutions matriculate 35,000 students [practitioner consensus].

**Standing.** Finance 4 / 4 / 3: Geneva is second in Switzerland and third in Western Europe in GFCI 40 (12th in the world), 11th for banking. Demand present for finance on the commodity-trading claim; programmes: EPFL MSc Computer Science, IMD MBA.

### Bern, St. Gallen and Lucerne

Eurostat gives Bern 1,051,437 residents and GDP €78.6 billion (second in Switzerland), St. Gallen 581,726 and €42.2 billion, Lucerne 469,271 and €30.7 billion; median pay CHF 6,964 (Espace Mittelland), CHF 6,623 (eastern Switzerland) and CHF 7,092 (central Switzerland); rent CHF 1,551, CHF 1,463 and CHF 1,748. No source with jobs by sector was read, so no family is rated; the standing entries in business (Bern 3 / 2 / 1, St. Gallen and Lucerne 2 / 1 / 1) use economic weight as a proxy and say so.

### Zug (new)

**What it is.** A canton of 133,723 residents (end 2024) with 131,705 jobs (2023) and a GDP the BFS puts at CHF 192,958 per inhabitant (2022, provisional) against CHF 90,131 for Switzerland [data]. Rent CHF 3,180 (the highest of the hubs), median pay in central Switzerland CHF 7,092.

**Employers.** Roche Diagnostics International (2,833 employees in the canton), Siemens (2,121), AMAG (1,447), Glencore (1,175), Partners Group (569), Zuger Kantonalbank (560) [data]. Finance demand present (Partners Group and the cantonal bank), standing finance 3 / 2 / 1.

### Lugano (new)

**What it is.** The financial centre of Ticino: 68,507 inhabitants, 17,579 registered businesses of which 3,459 in finance (31 December 2024; businesses, not jobs) [data]. Median pay in Ticino CHF 5,708 (the lowest of the regions), rent CHF 1,409. GFCI 40 ranks it 21st in the world, fifth in Western Europe and eighth in the world for banking [practitioner consensus].

**Standing.** Finance 4 / 4 / 2: third among the Swiss centres GFCI ranks (after Zurich and Geneva), fifth in Western Europe and the top five in Europe, 21st in the world. Demand finance strong, on the register's count of finance businesses and the GFCI banking rank; no employer was named.

## 5. Hypotheses tested

1. **Zurich is a top-five European financial centre.** Supported: second in Western Europe in GFCI 40. It is not a top-five world centre (10th); Zurich ranks 6th for banking. Holds for banking, insurance (15th in the world sector table) and wealth management; fails for trading.
2. **Switzerland's high pay is offset by cost.** Partly supported. Under-30 pay is about twice Belgium's (Eurostat SES) but rent in Zurich (CHF 2,467) and Zug (CHF 3,180) is high, and health insurance is paid separately (about CHF 459 a month for the young in Zurich). The library calculation says Zurich keeps 84% of gross at €60,000, the highest of its compared cities.
3. **Ticino is a cheap way into Swiss finance.** Partly supported: Ticino's median is CHF 5,708 against CHF 7,502 in Zurich and Lugano's rent is CHF 1,409, but Lugano is a small market (68,507 inhabitants) and its pay is the lowest of the regions.
4. **Commodity trading is Geneva's, not Zug's.** Insufficient evidence: the trade body counts members "mainly around Lake Geneva, in Zug and in Ticino"; Glencore alone employs 1,175 in Zug's canton, but no equivalent figure was read for Geneva.

## 6. Common mistakes and myths

- Assuming Swiss pay means a Swiss net salary: social contributions, income tax at source for foreigners and health premiums are separate (library).
- Treating every hub as Zurich: St. Gallen's median is CHF 6,623 and Ticino's CHF 5,708 against CHF 7,502 [data].
- Believing quotas apply to EU graduates: they cap non-EU permits only (8,500 for 2026).

## 7. Decision rules

1. If you want banking, insurance, wealth management or asset management and have a free-movement passport, choose Zurich first and Geneva second.
2. If you want pharma commercial, supply-chain or finance roles, choose Basel; logistics demand there is strong.
3. If you want commodity trading, look at Geneva and Zug, and check the employer's own page: Glencore states more than 1,000 posts in Zug's canton.
4. If you want a Swiss start-up or research role, compare Zurich ($44 billion ecosystem) and Greater Lausanne ($18.7 billion with EPFL).
5. If you are a non-EU student, plan on the six-month search permission and the quota: an employer must show the post is of predominant scientific or economic interest.
6. If rent decides, compare net pay after housing: Zug (CHF 3,180) and Zurich (CHF 2,467) against Bern (CHF 1,551), St. Gallen (CHF 1,463) and Lugano (CHF 1,409) (anecdotal).

## 8. Gaps and claims to verify

1. Headcounts for UBS, Zurich Insurance, Swiss Re (403 on 3 October 2026), ETH Zurich, EPFL and the Swiss universities were not read; the language of work was not researched.
2. The BFS release names only Zürich and Ticino; the other regions' medians come from Bilanz's report of the same survey (secondary).
3. Eurostat has no Swiss regions in its jobs-by-sector tables; Bern, St. Gallen and Lucerne are rated only on economic weight.
4. Zug and Lugano have no metropolitan figures and no GDP metric; Zug's GDP per head (CHF 192,958) is provisional 2022 data from the canton's yearbook.
5. The 2027 quotas are decided each November; the 2026 figures are shown.
