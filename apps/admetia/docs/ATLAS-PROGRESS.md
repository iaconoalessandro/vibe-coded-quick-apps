# Atlas progress

The Atlas is the world map at `map.html`: 46 selectable countries, a quick overview of each,
a passport-aware country page, and a zoomable hub map for every country. This file
is the checklist; it is updated as each country lands.

**Files.** `map.html` · `js/page-map.js` · `data/atlas/index.js` (the 46 countries and the
vocabulary) · `data/atlas/geo.js` (shapes, generated by `tools/atlas-geo.js` from Natural Earth)
· `data/atlas/<id>.js` (one record per country, English and Italian) · `tests/atlas-test.js` ·
Atlas checks in `tests/i18n-test.js` · verification log in `research/verification/round-4a.md`
onwards, with one P number per country in `research/verification/claims-to-verify.md` §4.2.

## Method, in short

- **Every statement is a claim** with one tag (`data`, `employer-stated`, `practitioner consensus`,
  `anecdotal`), a source (an https page, or a research brief when the library already verified
  it) and the date it was read. The test fails on an untagged, unsourced, undated or unused claim.
- **Sources are notes**: on the country page a claim shows only a note number, [3]; its tag, linked
  source and read date are listed once in "Sources" at the foot of the page, grouped by section
  (each hub its own group). Numbers follow the reading order and are redrawn when a hub or the
  passport changes. A chart's figures from one publisher share one note naming every hub. Topics
  (Working there, Visas and permits) are a ledger: topic in the margin, text beside it.
- **Visas and permits** is a section of each country page, not a separate page. Its content lives
  in `data/atlas/visas/<id>.js`, condensed from `research/visas_immigration/<country>/`: one row
  per situation (studying, internship, job search, first skilled job, research, working holiday,
  visits, staying for good, tax relief) with a verdict (free, open to you, needs a sponsor,
  limited, closed), key figures and a "watch out" line, then traps and the "not settled yet" list
  from the open questions. Every sentence carries the register ids it rests on; the Sources
  block at the foot of each file is generated from `<country>_sources.md` by
  `node tools/visas-sources.js <id>` (the test fails if it is out of step). Each file states
  when it was checked and when it is due for review; past that date the page warns the reader.
  Figures a review found wrong or unconfirmed are left out or listed as open.
- **First weeks** lives in the same visa file (`arrival`), from the same guide and register: the
  same seven steps for every country (before you leave, register where you live, your residence
  document, personal and tax number, health cover, bank account and digital ID, keeping your
  status), each answered for every passport in scope, and redrawn with the passport switch. A step
  the guide gives no register id for is marked `none` and the page says the research has not
  covered it yet; the test fails if any step is left unanswered, and prints the `none` list.
- **Life there** comes from `data/atlas/life.js`, generated by `node tools/atlas-life.js` (responses
  cached in `.cache/atlas-life/`, git-ignored): one source per topic for all 46 countries, so pages
  compare. Every row on the page is marked **Whole country** or **By city**. By city, read at each
  hub's city-centre coordinates: climate (NASA POWER, 2001–2020 monthly means), daylight (NOAA solar
  equations from latitude), air (CAMS PM2.5 via Open-Meteo, first 14 days of each month of 2025,
  against the WHO 5 µg/m³ guideline), time zone (IANA), English where EF scores the city. Whole
  country: English (EF EPI), price level (IMF WEO, GDP at market rates over GDP at PPP, US = 100),
  homicides per 100,000 (UNODC via the World Bank), weekly hours worked (ILOSTAT), out-of-pocket
  share of health spending and life expectancy (World Bank); crime recorded by the police (UNODC: serious
  assault and robbery from counts over UNODC's own population, theft and burglary rates; England and Wales
  for the UK, marked on the page), shown with its five-year change and never ranked, because countries
  define and record offences differently; and how locals come across (InterNations Expat Insider, Ease
  of Settling In ranks), worded as foreign residents' perception, never as a trait of a people. A source with no figure for a country
  (Taiwan in the World Bank and ILO series, for instance) shows as such; nothing is filled in.
  Round 6c added three rows (log: `research/verification/round-6c-life-there.md`): **language in
  daily life** (whole country: official languages and the share who speak them, Unicode CLDR),
  **health-care quality** (whole country, added to Health care: UHC service coverage index, doctors
  and beds per 1,000, premature death from chronic disease; World Bank) and **office culture** (whole
  country, `data/atlas/culture.js`, not generated: legal minimum of paid leave and public holidays, and
  one guide sentence each on the working day and office style, English and Italian). Office culture has
  no single source, so each field cites its own page, and a field no page supported is left out (gaps
  listed in the log). `tests/atlas-test.js` checks the shape of all three.
- **Demand levels** are judgements from claims, by a fixed rule:
  - *dominant*: an official or statistical source names the hub as the national centre for the
    family (needs a `data` claim);
  - *strong*: a statistic, or two or more claims about named employers with an HQ or major site;
  - *present*: at least one such employer;
  - *marginal*: a source says entry hiring is small;
  - anything else is `gap` and shows as "not rated".
- **Scope**: European countries get visa content for EU/EEA/Swiss, UK, US and other passports;
  countries outside Europe for EU/EEA/Swiss and UK passports only. The test fails if a
  non-European visa file carries US or other-passport content, or leaves an in-scope passport
  with nothing to read.
- **Hub coordinates** are city centres, rounded to 0.01°; the test checks each lies inside its
  country's shape bounds.
